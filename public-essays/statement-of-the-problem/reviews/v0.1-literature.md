## 0. What the reader will do with the first sentence

"Physics lacks a precise explanation of what the observer is" will be filed by a trained reader into one of six existing bins within a sentence or two. In rough order of likelihood:

1. **Bell's complaint about "measurement"** (the measurement problem). This is the right bin. Bell asked "What exactly qualifies some physical systems to play the role of 'measurer'?" (Bell 1990). The essay is a proposed answer to that question.
2. **Consciousness and the observer** (Wigner 1961; the von Neumann–Wigner reading). This is the wrong bin. Note 1 blocks it, but the main text does not, and "the observer" alone invites it.
3. **Observer-relative facts** (QBism, relational QM, the Wigner's-friend no-go theorems). This is partly the right bin. They share the starting point but make the observer an agent or any system, and none defines it as an object with a law of motion.
4. **Observers as physical systems inside quantum theory** (quantum reference frames, Page–Wootters, the 2023–2025 quantum-gravity work on observer-dependent algebras). This is the right instinct in the wrong setting. The observer is a physical subsystem, but a quantum one on a given background.
5. **Wolfram's "observers like us".** The words are closer than a physicist expects: branching, a space of states laid out by branching ("branchial space"), and QM and GR from the nature of the observer. The reputational cost of the association is real.
6. **Müller's "law without law".** This is the closest in structure: observer states, and a chance of the next observer state given the current one alone. There is no geometry and no physical object.

The one-line answer to the essay's own question "(If it has been — what is its name?)": no programme we know combines these aims, which *Mathematical Foundations* v0.2 §11 also says. The nearest in structure is Müller (2020), the nearest in vocabulary is Wolfram (2020, 2023), and the nearest in the probability move is Strayhorn (2008) with Sebens–Carroll (2018) and Albert–Loewer (1988).

The two words that cost the most on first read are **"local"** (bullet 3, and again in "LHV") and **"LHV"** itself. Sections 4 and 7 explain why.

## 1. "Physics lacks a precise explanation of what the observer is"

**Reader connects to.**
- Bell, "Against 'measurement'" (1990): the "shifty split" of the world into system and apparatus, and the "measurer" question quoted above.
- The Heisenberg cut, and Wigner's consciousness reading (Wigner 1961, **unverified**, not re-checked).
- Wheeler's observer-participancy, "Law without law" (in Wheeler and Zurek (eds), *Quantum Theory and Measurement*, Princeton 1983, pp. 182–213).
- Gell-Mann and Hartle's IGUS (information gathering and utilizing system). Their definition is verbatim: "A measurement is a correlation with variables in a quasiclassical domain. An 'observer' (or information gathering and utilizing system) is a complex adaptive system that has evolved to exploit the relative predictability of a quasiclassical domain" (Gell-Mann and Hartle 1990, arXiv:1803.04605). See also Hartle, "The physics of now" (2005), where IGUSes are simple physical models with memory along a world line.
- The Wigner's-friend line: Frauchiger–Renner (2018), Brukner (2018), and Local Friendliness (Bong et al. 2020).
- Recent quantum gravity: an observer is added as a physical system with a clock, and the algebra of observables depends on it (Chandrasekaran, Longo, Penington and Witten 2023; Witten 2024; De Vuyst, Eccles, Höhn and Kirklin 2025, "Gravitational entropy is observer-dependent").

**Fit.**
- It **holds** with Bell and with Gell-Mann–Hartle. Both say the observer must be a physical system inside the theory and that textbook QM does not say which systems qualify. Tying sentence 1 to that complaint tells the reader which gap is meant.
- It **partly holds** with the quantum-reference-frame and quantum-gravity observer work. The instinct is the same (the observer is a physical subsystem, with a clock or a world line). But there the observer is a quantum system on a given spacetime, and it is added so that observables are well defined, not modelled as an object in a space of observers.
- It **misleads** toward consciousness (Wigner) and toward QBism, where the observer is an agent who uses the theory. QBism does not seek a physical definition of the agent (Fuchs, Mermin and Schack 2014). A reader who files the essay there will expect a view about minds or beliefs.

**Terms.**
- Adopt: "the observer as a physical system inside the theory". "Embedded observer" is also used this way in the reference-frame and Wolfram literatures. Borrowing Bell's word "measurer", or naming his question, costs one clause and fixes the bin.
- Avoid in the opening: "consciousness", "mind", "agent" (QBism), and "perspective" (which carries relational-QM and reference-frame baggage). Note 1 already says the observer is "of the same kind as the things physics already describes". Promoting that half-sentence into the main text would do the work.

## 2. "The observer is a physical object in a space of possible observers"

**Reader connects to.**
- **Gielen and Wise (2013)**, who own the phrase "observer space": the 7-manifold of future-directed unit 4-velocities, defined abstractly with no spacetime assumed (arXiv:1210.0019). Paper 1 §9 already firewalls this. The collision is the same here.
- Wolfram's **branchial space** (states on different branches of history laid out by common ancestry) and rulial space (Wolfram 2020; "Observer Theory", 2023).
- Barbour's **Platonia**, the space of relative configurations of the whole universe, each instant a "time capsule" (Barbour 1994; *The End of Time*, 1999).
- Müller's **observer states**, finite bit strings with no physical carrier (Müller 2020, Postulates 3.1–3.2).
- Everett's observers, modelled as automata with memory sequences (Everett 1957); Tegmark's "self-aware substructures" (SAS) (Tegmark 2008).
- In philosophy, **centred worlds** (Lewis 1979) and **observer-moments** (Bostrom, *Anthropic Bias*, 2002, **unverified**, not re-checked).

**Fit.**
- **Gielen–Wise misleads.** It is a different object, as Paper 1 §9 says. A reader who knows the term will assume one before reading on.
- **Barbour partly holds.** Each point of Platonia contains records, as the observer's state does in note 7. But Barbour's points are configurations of a whole universe, not observers, and Barbour has no motion (time is abolished). The essay has motion.
- **Müller holds in structure and misleads in substance.** Müller has a space of observer states with a transition law, but the observer is informational, not a physical object, and there is no geometry.
- **Centred worlds partly hold.** If one observer is shared by many worlds (Paper 1's lock), a philosopher will read an observer as a class of centred worlds with the same local content. That is a useful handle for philosophers.
- **Observer-moments partly hold.** Under note 4 each step of a history is an observer, which is what Bostrom calls an observer-moment. The term brings self-sampling (anthropic) baggage.
- **Risk.** Any theory that makes observers local physical objects and later puts a measure on them will be asked the Boltzmann-brain question (Albrecht and Sorbo 2004). Müller claims his framework dissolves it. Paper 1 §9 already separates the programme from the cosmological measure problem.

**Terms.**
- "Space of possible observers" is clear, so keep it. "Observer space" needs a one-clause disambiguation at first use, for example "(not Gielen and Wise's observer space of 4-velocities)". "Observer-state space" is an alternative that avoids the collision.
- Avoid "configuration space": it brings in Bohm and the configuration-space locality move (see §7).
- Use "observer-moment" only if the anthropic literature is wanted as a neighbour.
- None of these terms decides what the observer is, which note 1 leaves open.

## 3. "The internal state of the observer contains a complete representation of the external universe"

**Reader connects to.**
- **Records**: Zurek's quantum Darwinism, where observers learn about pointer states by intercepting redundant fragments of the environment (Zurek 2003 RMP; Zurek 2009).
- Gell-Mann–Hartle's IGUS schemata, and Hartle's memory-register models (Hartle 2005).
- Barbour's **time capsules**: static structures holding what we read as records of a past (Barbour 1999).
- Everett's memory sequences (Everett 1957).
- Tegmark's **frog and bird perspectives**, the "inside view" of an observer versus the outside view of the whole structure (Tegmark 2008). This is not Dyson's "Birds and Frogs" (Dyson 2009), a different distinction with the same animals.
- Müller, where the external world emerges from the statistics of observer states (Müller 2020, §4).

**Fit.**
- The **weak reading** (note 7: whatever the observer takes to be true of the world is fixed by its own state) **holds**. It is the shared idea of records in Zurek, Gell-Mann–Hartle, Hartle and Barbour.
- The word **"complete" misleads.** In foundations, "complete" is EPR's word ("Can quantum-mechanical description of physical reality be considered complete?", 1935). Jarrett used "completeness" for what is now called outcome independence (Jarrett 1984). A Bell-literate reader will hear hidden-variable completeness.
- **"Representation"** brings in philosophy-of-mind baggage (intentionality) that note 1 does not want.
- The **strong reading** (the state picks out the universe) is Tegmark's hope of reconstructing the bird from the frog, and Barbour's time capsules taken literally. Whether the essay means that is David's question (c), so both readings are carried below.

**Terms (both options carried; (c) is open).**
- Weak reading: "the observer's state fixes all its records of the world" or "its internal model of the world". "Records" is the shared term in four neighbouring programmes.
- Strong reading: keep "complete representation", but know that "complete" will be heard in EPR's sense. "Determines the external universe" says the strong claim without that baggage.

## 4. "Motion is local but not deterministic"

**Reader connects to.**
- **Stochastic processes.** Note 6 ("which next observers are allowed, and how they are weighted, depends only on the current observer") is the **Markov property**.
- **Müller**, Postulate 3.1(ii): "Being in some observer state x now, there is a well-defined chance of being in some other observer state y next." This is notes 5 and 6 almost word for word.
- **Nelson's stochastic mechanics** (Nelson 1966), a Markov diffusion in configuration space.
- **Barandes's stochastic-quantum correspondence** (Barandes 2023/2025; 2023 "The stochastic-quantum theorem"): QM is equivalent to classical stochastic processes on configuration space that are generically *indivisible*, which is to say not Markov.
- Spontaneous collapse (Ghirardi, Rimini and Weber 1986); Bell-type jump processes (Dürr, Goldstein, Tumulka and Zanghì 2005).
- Many-minds, where each mind follows a stochastic trajectory (Albert and Loewer 1988).
- Wolfram's multiway systems, where rewriting is non-deterministic and all branches are kept (Wolfram 2020).
- Rideout–Sorkin sequential growth (2000); Belnap's branching space-times (1992).

**Fit.**
- **Müller holds** almost verbatim for the rule. His observer state can carry its own memory (in Postulates 3.2 the next state is x followed by one more bit), which is one way a Markov rule on observers can still see history. That point may help the essay.
- **Barandes partly holds and gives a warning.** It shows classical probability plus non-deterministic motion can reproduce QM. But on configuration space the processes must be non-Markov in general. That does not rule out a Markov rule on observer space: it is a different space, and an observer state can carry memory. A reader who knows Barandes will still ask.
- **Nelson misleads.** It is the standard cautionary tale: Markov in configuration space, nonlocal in physical space.
- **"Equations of motion" misleads** if note 4's weak reading is meant. Every physicist hears differential equations and a time parameter, which note 4 disowns.

**Terms (both options carried; (a) is open).**
- If an ordered succession under a rule is meant (note 4): say "transition rule" or "rule of succession", and "a non-deterministic transition rule" for bullet 3.
- If continuous motion is meant: "equations of motion" is fine. The established term for the non-deterministic version is a stochastic differential equation, or a diffusion (Nelson).
- For note 6's "local": say "**Markov**" or "memoryless" (depends only on the current observer). Keep the word "local" for the Bell sense and always add "in observer space". Bare "local" is heard as spatial nearness and as Bell locality, and note 6 means neither.
- Keep "weights" generic. "Transition probabilities" presupposes per-step weights, which bears on (M1)/(M2).

## 5. "Measurement is branching"

**Reader connects to.**
- **Everett**; DeWitt's popular "splitting" ("Quantum mechanics and reality", 1970); Wallace's decoherence branches (2012).
- **Decoherent histories**: branches as coarse-grained histories (Griffiths 1984; Gell-Mann and Hartle 1990, 1993), and the set-selection objection (Dowker and Kent 1995).
- Wolfram's multiway branches and branchial space.
- Tegmark's "frog branching", which the frog perceives as apparent randomness (Tegmark 2008).
- **Belnap's branching space-times**: indeterminism as branching histories with "choice points", classical and with no quantum state (Belnap 1992). Its analysis of Bell correlations as "modal funny business" (Müller, Belnap and Kishida 2008) is an existing classical-branching vocabulary for claim 7.

**Fit.**
- "Branch" is Everett's word. A reader will file bullet 5 as many-worlds and then look for the Everett probability debate. That is the desk-reject slot this programme already met in 2008 (the SHPMP rejection, where Referee 2 judged the paper as an Everett paper). Note 8 disowns identification with decoherence branches, but the main text does not.
- **Belnap partly holds.** It gives classical branching of histories with one history realised, which is the chance reading.
- **Everett partly holds.** All branches are real there, which is the co-existence reading.
- Which reading the essay means is open (MF §6 and §9; the chance and co-existence readings of v0.6). So "branch" alone quietly picks one.

**Terms (both options carried).**
- Co-existence reading: "branch point", defined as in note 8, with one clause, "a branching of the allowed histories, not yet Everett's worlds".
- Chance reading: "choice point" or "fork" (Belnap).
- Avoid "splitting" and "worlds" in the opening in either case.

## 6. "Probability is entirely classical, needs no additional assumptions, and gives the Born rule, truly derived and not circular"

**Reader connects to.**
- **Kolmogorov probability**; typicality in Bohmian mechanics (Dürr, Goldstein and Zanghì 1992).
- The **Everett probability programme**: Deutsch–Wallace decision theory (Wallace 2012); self-locating uncertainty (Sebens and Carroll 2018); Vaidman's measure of existence (2012).
- **Branch counting** and its objections: Wallace's point that the count depends on how branches are cut; Saunders's equal-amplitude counting (Saunders 2021).
- **Zurek's envariance** (Zurek 2003 PRL; Zurek 2005), and the circularity critique of it (Schlosshauer and Fine 2005).
- Gleason's theorem (1957); the operational redundancy of the measurement postulates (Masanes, Galley and Müller 2019).
- **QBism**, with Born as a coherence norm on personal probabilities (Caves, Fuchs and Schack 2002).
- Barandes (classical probabilities over configurations); Albert and Loewer; Strayhorn (2008); Stoica (Paper 1 §9).

**Fit.**
- **"Classical" has three readings.** (i) Kolmogorov probability, which fits note 9. (ii) Classical physics, which misleads. (iii) Bayesian (QBism), which misleads, because QBist probabilities are personal. Beside "LHV", "classical" will also be heard as "hidden-variable", the reading SHPMP Referee 1 made.
- **"No additional assumptions"** will be pressed. Any probability over infinitely many histories needs a choice of measure (Norton 2008 on infinite lotteries; per-step weighting and counting at a cut differ, Burda et al. 2009). Note 9 already defers this. The main text reads stronger than the note.
- **"Truly derived, not merely justified" and "not circular"** step onto contested ground. Every well-known candidate (Deutsch–Wallace, Zurek, Sebens–Carroll, Gleason-based arguments) has been charged with circularity (for Zurek, Schlosshauer and Fine 2005). That helps the essay if the reader hears it as entering a named debate. It hurts if, after "branches", the reader hears a claim to solve many-worlds probability. That is the 2007 Everett-vs-decision-theory framing, and the programme does not want it.

**Terms.**
- Adopt: "ordinary (Kolmogorov) probability over the observer's possible futures".
- Avoid: bare "classical", "branch counting", and "egalitarian".
- Carry both weighting options in neutral words, as note 9 does: "weighting each step" and "counting whole histories".
- For Born, "derived from the structure of the space of observers" is clearer than "truly derived". Naming circularity as the standard objection tells the reader which debate "not circular" answers. No derivation is claimed (note 10).

## 7. "It is an LHV theory, local in observer space rather than spacetime, so Bell does not bite"

**Reader connects to.**
- Bell (1964); Jarrett's factorisation into "locality" and "completeness" (1984); relativistic GRW, which is Bell-nonlocal with no signalling and no preferred frame (Tumulka 2006).
- **Bohm is local in configuration space.** This is the objection most likely to come first. Bohm's dynamics is local in configuration space and nonlocal in physical space, and everyone counts it as Bell-nonlocal.
- **Programmes that say Bell does not bite by relocating something:**
  - Everettian locality (Deutsch and Hayden 2000; Brown and Timpson 2016)
  - QBism, whose introductory paper is subtitled "an application to the locality of quantum mechanics" (Fuchs, Mermin and Schack 2014)
  - relational QM (Martin-Dussaud, Rovelli and Zalamea 2019; Adlam and Rovelli 2023)
  - superdeterminism (Hossenfelder and Palmer 2020)
  - Barandes's "causally local" formulation (Barandes 2024, arXiv only)
- **Local Friendliness**: Bong et al. (2020); Cavalcanti and Wiseman (2021); Wiseman, Cavalcanti and Rieffel (2023); with Frauchiger–Renner (2018) and Brukner (2018).
- **Müller §7**, which asks what Bell correlations look like in an observer-state framework once the emergent world carries "locality structure".
- Belnap, Müller and Kishida's "funny business" (2008).

**Fit.**
- **Where it holds.** Bell's theorem says nothing about a Markov rule on a space that is not spacetime. By the same token, the Schrödinger equation is local in Hilbert space. In that narrow sense the claim is true, and easily. All the substance sits in how Bell's settings, outcomes and shared variable map onto observer space (note 12; MF §9).
- **Where it misleads (the word).** In every textbook, "LHV" means a model that factorises in spacetime, the kind Bell excludes. Note 12 redefines it. The reader will not have read note 12 and will hear either a contradiction or the Bohm move ("local in my space, nonlocal in yours"). The Bohm move is honest if it is meant (MF §9, first row: Bell-nonlocal in spacetime and local in observer space, which Bell does not exclude). But then "LHV" is the wrong name for it.
- **Where it misleads (the theorem that bites next).** Local Friendliness is the theorem that targets theories in which observers are physical systems. It needs no quantum formalism. It rules out Absoluteness of Observed Events together with Local Agency (plus no superdeterminism). Because the essay's observer is a physical system whose outcomes are its own states, a referee who knows Bong et al. will aim LF, not Bell, at it. Carrying both readings:
  - **Co-existence reading** (all branches real): it takes the Everett, relational-QM and QBism exit by denying single absolute outcomes, and it inherits their costs.
  - **Chance reading** (one branch occurs): Absoluteness of Observed Events holds, so Local Agency or measurement independence must fail in spacetime.
  - Either way, the essay's rhetorical question gains a precise answer. Which of these the essay takes is the (M1)/(M2) and (d) territory, and is not answered here.

**Terms.**
- Adopt the Bell-literature names so the reader can locate the claim:
  - "Bell locality" (factorisability)
  - "parameter independence" and "outcome independence"
  - "measurement (statistical) independence"
  - "single outcomes" or "absoluteness of observed events"
  - "local agency"
- Avoid "LHV", or gloss it in the main text as "hidden-variable, and local in observer space, not in Bell's spacetime sense".
- "Local in observer space" is the phrase to keep. It becomes precise once one Bell premise is named as the one reread. The essay need not pick it here, but should say that one will be picked.

## 8. "QM (and perhaps Einstein's equations) is recovered as an approximation"

**Reader connects to.**
- **Emergent QM**: 't Hooft's cellular automaton interpretation (arXiv:1405.1548; Springer 2016, **book details not re-checked**); Nelson; Barandes; Müller §7.
- **Wolfram and Gorard**, who claim both QM and Einstein's equations from hypergraph rewriting (Wolfram 2020; Gorard 2020a, 2020b). This is the closest in ambition.
- Reconstruction programmes (Höhn 2017; Masanes, Galley and Müller 2019).
- For Einstein: Jacobson's "equation of state" (1995); causal sets (Bombelli et al. 1987).

**Fit.**
- This is an aim only (note 11), so there is nothing to test.
- **The association risk is tonal.** The reader will compare the essay to Wolfram, whose physics claims are widely regarded as unproven. They will also compare it to 't Hooft, who accepts superdeterminism to get past Bell, and will then ask which Bell premise the essay reads.
- "Derived, not assumed" for Einstein is Jacobson's framing, which helps if it is cited.

**Terms.**
- Adopt: "recovered as an effective description in a limit", which is standard, or "in a limit" in place of "in the approximation".
- "Emergent" is fine but vague.
- Which limit is open (note 11).

## 10. Desk-reject notes (reader's side)

1. **"LHV … so Bell does not bite" with no named premise.** A Bell-literate referee stops here. The fix is terminological (§7), and it makes no choice.
2. **"Branches" plus "Born rule truly derived".** Together these are read as a many-worlds probability paper, the 2008 SHPMP slot. The fix is to say "branching of allowed histories" and name the debate (§5, §6).
3. **The Wolfram association.** The overlap in words (branching, a space of branches, QM and GR from the observer) is large. One sentence saying what is not shared (a deterministic multiway system; no object; no probability rule) would pre-empt it.
4. **Local Friendliness, not Bell, is the sharper test** for an observer-as-physical-system theory (§7). The essay does not mention it. A referee in the Wigner's-friend line will.

## (b) Top five terminology recommendations for the opening paragraph (lines 9–17)

1. **Name the gap in the reader's words.** Say "the observer as a physical system inside the theory" and point to Bell's question of which physical systems count as a "measurer". This puts sentence 1 in the measurement-problem bin and keeps it out of the consciousness and agent bins. It makes no choice.
2. **Disambiguate "observer space" at first use.** Keep "space of all possible observers", and add one clause separating it from Gielen and Wise's observer space of 4-velocities, or use "observer-state space".
3. **Replace bare "local" in bullet 3 with "Markov" or "memoryless" (depends only on the current observer).** Keep "local" for the Bell sense, always as "local in observer space". This is the single change that most reduces misreading in §7.
4. **Choose the branching word knowingly (both options carried).** Use "branch point" if the co-existence reading is meant, or "choice point" / "fork" (Belnap) if the chance reading is meant. Either way, add "of the allowed histories" so bullet 5 is not read as a many-worlds claim.
5. **Say "ordinary (Kolmogorov) probability" instead of "entirely classical".** This avoids the classical-physics and hidden-variable readings, and leaves note 9's weighting choice open.

Runners-up: "transition rule" in place of "equations of motion" if note 4's succession reading is meant ((a) open); and "records" in place of "complete representation" if note 7's weak reading is meant ((c) open).
