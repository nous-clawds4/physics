## 2. Hidden framework assumptions in #148

**2.1 "where it can go depends only on where it is now."**
- *Presumes:* a position ("where"), a present moment ("now"), and the Markov property itself. Plain wording does not remove the Markov property: it is still a structural claim about the rule. My #147 item 3 moved it into the text from note 6. (G) already flags that David may drop it.
- A reader will bring up Barandes's non-Markovian ("indivisible") stochastic-quantum correspondence either way.
- *Both options:*
  - **Keep:** ", and what it can change into depends on nothing but the observer itself". This is as open on Q-D5/T1 as before, since whether arrival data count as part of "the observer itself" is T1. It no longer shares "where it is" with the locality sentence.
  - **Drop:** delete the clause. MF §4's observer-only row then holds the property.

**2.2 "The rule restricts where the observer can go."**
- *Presumes:* locations in a space.
- *Replacement:* "The rule restricts what the observer can change into without being fully deterministic". "Without being fully deterministic" is kept (Q-D1).

**2.3 "It moves to observers near where it is."**
- *Presumes:* the observer occupies observer space instead of being one of its elements, and something persists through change (T1/Q-D5).
- *Optional replacement:* "It changes into observers near the one it is, whatever 'near' turns out to mean mathematically."

**2.4 "branch points, or choice points: places where the observer has more than one allowed way to go on."**
- *Presumes:*
  - "Points" and "places": isolated, discrete branching events. A continuous stochastic rule (MF §3, stochastic-ODE row) branches everywhere and has no branch points.
  - "Go on": continuation along a path.
  - "Branch" and Belnap's "choice point": a tree with no rejoining, whereas MF §2's digraph allows paths to merge.
- *Replacement:* "a model of measurement as branch points, or choice points: situations in which the observer is allowed to change in more than one way, not assumed to be Everett's worlds;"

**2.5 "a complete representation of the external universe."**
- *Presumes:* one universe. Note 7 declines "one whole universe", and v0.6 has many worlds sharing one patch.
- This is #139 (c), so both options are given: keep "of the external universe", or use "of the world outside it" (note 7's wording). "Complete representation" stays, as (G) decided.

**2.6 "The hidden variable is the observer's own state."**
- *Presumes:* a state space, and that state and observer can differ. MF T1's "enlarge the state" option is exactly where they come apart.
- *Both options:* "The hidden variable is the observer itself." (state = observer), or keep "the observer's own state" (the state may carry arrival data). T1 stays open.

**2.7 "ordinary (Kolmogorov) probability."**
- *Presumes:* countable additivity (Kolmogorov's Axiom VI). This excludes a uniform probability over countably many whole histories (Norton 2018).
- It does not decide (M1) vs (M2): per-step weights, and a count at a finite cut, can both be countably additive. But it narrows the options.
- *Both options:* keep "(Kolmogorov)" if countable additivity is intended, or write "ordinary probability over the observer's possible futures" and leave additivity to MF §7.

**2.8 "classical spacetime".**
- *Presumes:* a spacetime is given alongside observer space, although the Einstein aim allows spacetime to be derived.
- No essay change, since Bell's setting needs spacetime. Add a row to the MF §0 table: "| 'spacetime', 'classical spacetime' | a spacetime given alongside observer space (the Einstein aim allows it to be derived) | 14 |".

## 4. Reader trip-wires (psi.inc audience)

1. **"teleport".** Quantum teleportation (Bennett et al. 1993) is standard, and the word sits two sentences from Bell. Option: "the observer doesn't teleport (in the everyday sense, not quantum teleportation)".
2. **"local, but not in the textbook sense".**
   - The first objection: Bohmian mechanics (Bohm 1952) is also local in its configuration space and Bell-nonlocal in spacetime. In v0.3 §9's "Locality, reread" row, append "(the same shape as Bohmian mechanics: local in configuration space, nonlocal in spacetime)". This names a precedent and picks no row.
   - Readers will also ask which of the essay's two properties plays the role of Bell locality. Bell's local causality has both flavours: dependence only on what lies in the past light cone. That is #139 (d) and is not answered here. v0.3 §9 could list both properties as candidates.
3. **"complete".** v0.3 §9 cites Jarrett's "completeness" (outcome independence), which is unrelated to the essay's "complete representation". Fix in the §9 table: "(Jarrett's decomposition; his terms 'locality' and 'completeness', unrelated to note 7's 'complete representation')".
4. **Uncited names in the essay.** Bong et al. gets a year; Bell and Gielen–Wise do not.
   - "Bell put it as a question ('Against "measurement"', 1990):"
   - "not Gielen and Wise's (2013) 'observer space' of 4-velocities". Do not gloss "4-velocities" as velocities in a given spacetime: their abstract case has none.
5. **"(Kolmogorov)".** Philosophers of probability will read countable additivity (§2.7).
6. **"choice point".** This is Belnap's term (1992); readers who know it hear the chance reading, which is intended as one of the two. If §3.5 is taken, add Belnap to v0.3's references.
