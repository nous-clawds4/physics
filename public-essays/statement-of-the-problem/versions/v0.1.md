# Statement of the Problem

David Strayhorn

**Status.** v0.1, awaiting David's sign-off. The essay text below is David's `thread-worth-pulling.md`, verbatim, with bracketed markers added. Each marker points to a note that gives the weakest reading of that word and says what the word does not commit to. Those choices are deferred to the companion essay *Mathematical Foundations*. Change control is in this folder's `README.md`.

---

The spirit of this program is based on the idea that physics lacks a precise explanation of what the observer is. Progress requires that we supply that explanation.

Specifically:
- a precise definition of the observer as a physical object [1]
- a precise mathematical definition [2] of the space [3] of all possible observers
- equations of motion [4] of the observer through observer space; equations that restrict what motion is allowed without being fully deterministic [5]; movement through observer space is a local [6] process
- a precise specification of how the internal state of the observer contains a complete representation of the external universe [7]
- a model of measurement as branches [8] in the allowed movements through observer space
- a probability interpretation that is entirely classical and requires no additional assumptions [9]; and yet, one from which the Born rule is truly derived, not merely justified [10]; a program that shows the Born rule to be inevitable, inescapable, and not via circular reasoning.

That's it. That's the thread worth pulling. The question should not be: why is this a thread worth pulling. The question should be: how in the name of all that is holy should this NOT be considered a thread worth pulling? Certainly, there is an aesthetic appeal to its ontology. So why has this program not been attempted? (If it has been -- what is its name?) The best explanation for the failure to pull on this particular thread is that it feels like a local hidden variable theory of the sort that is excluded by Bell's Inequality. Our first task is to present the case that this ontology is NOT excluded by Bell's Inequality. Make no mistake: we will not argue that quantum mechanics is wrong, or that Bell's Theorem is wrong. On the contrary: we take QM and Bell quite seriously; as seriously as anyone can claim to take them. Our ultimate goal is a theory from which quantum mechanics is recovered in the approximation [11]. (And perhaps Einstein's equations as well: derived, not assumed.)

Our argument is simply that the proposed model is not excluded by Bell. It is a LHV theory [12], but it is local in observer-space, not in classical spacetime. It is almost trivially true (that it is not excluded by Bell) [13], once the distinction between observer space and spacetime are made mathematically precise [14]. Much confusion will therefore be avoided by the constant reminder that observer space and spacetime are not the same thing. Most mistakes will be the result of applying results in one of those spaces and applying those results without justification to the other space.

## Notes: the weakest reading of each word

Each note gives the reading the essay commits to, then what it leaves open.

1. **Observer as a physical object.** The observer is a thing in the physical world, of the same kind as the things physics already describes, not something outside physics. *Does not commit to* what that thing is (for example, a patch of geometry, a brain, or a record).
2. **Precise mathematical definition.** There is a definition that can be written down exactly and checked. *Does not commit to* which mathematical structure it uses.
3. **Space.** The set of all possible observers. *Does not commit to* a topology, a distance, a manifold structure, a dimension, or a measure on that set.
4. **Motion, equations of motion, movement.** A history of an observer is an ordered succession of observers, and a rule says which successions are allowed. *Does not commit to* continuous paths, differential equations, a time parameter, or whether every step carries elapsed proper time.
5. **Restrict without being fully deterministic.** From a given observer, the rule may allow more than one next observer, and it forbids others. *Does not commit to* any particular law saying which next observers are allowed.
6. **Local.** Which next observers are allowed, and how they are weighted, depends only on the current observer. *Does not commit to* nearness in any distance or topology on observer space, and says nothing about locality in spacetime.
7. **Internal state; complete representation of the external universe.** Everything the observer takes to be true of the world outside it is fixed by the observer's own state. *Does not commit to* the observer's state picking out one whole universe uniquely, or to any particular map from observer to universe.
8. **Branch; measurement.** A branch point is an observer from which more than one next observer is allowed. A measurement is modelled as such a point. *Does not commit to* identifying these branches with decoherence branches, to where branch points occur, or to how many branches there are.
9. **Entirely classical; no additional assumptions.** The probabilities are ordinary probabilities over an observer's possible futures, and they come from the structure already given (the space and the allowed motions) rather than from a separate probability postulate. *Does not commit to* which way of turning that structure into numbers is correct (for example, weighting each step versus counting whole histories), or to the claim that no choice at all is needed.
10. **Born rule truly derived; inevitable; not circular.** This is the aim: the Born rule should follow from the structure, without being assumed at any step. *Does not commit to* a route, and no derivation is claimed here.
11. **Recovered in the approximation.** Quantum mechanics (and perhaps Einstein's equations) should appear as a limit or approximation of the theory. *Does not commit to* which limit.
12. **LHV (local hidden variable) theory.** The hidden variable is the observer's own state, and "local" is meant in the sense of note 6. *Does not commit to* how Bell's formal setting (settings, outcomes, a shared variable) maps onto observer space.
13. **Not excluded by Bell; almost trivially true.** Bell's theorem assumes locality in spacetime. The claim is that its conclusion does not carry over to locality in observer space. *Does not commit to* a particular model in which this is exhibited; none is exhibited here.
14. **The distinction made mathematically precise.** Observer space and spacetime are different sets with different structure, and results proved in one do not transfer to the other without an argument. *Does not commit to* what the relation or map between the two is.
