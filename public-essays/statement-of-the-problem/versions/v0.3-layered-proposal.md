Physics lacks a precise explanation of what the observer is. Progress requires that we supply that explanation.

Quantum mechanics predicts what observers will see, but never says which physical systems count as observers. Bell put it as a question: "What exactly qualifies some physical systems to play the role of 'measurer'?" What is missing is the observer as a physical system inside the theory: a thing of the same kind as everything else physics describes, not a mind or an agent standing outside the physics.

Those two paragraphs are layer 1, held absolutely. The rest of the program follows one layer at a time, each held a little less strongly than the one before. Nothing past layer 1 is sacred.

**Layer 2. Observer space, and how the observer changes.** We need a precise definition of the observer as a physical object, and a precise mathematical definition of the collection of all possible observers, which we call observer space. The name does not say what kind of mathematical object it is, and it is not Gielen and Wise's "observer space" of 4-velocities. We need a precise specification of how the internal state of the observer contains a complete representation of the external universe. We need equations of motion for the observer, or at least a rule saying how the observer is allowed to change. The rule restricts where the observer can go without being fully deterministic. The intuition is that the observer doesn't teleport: it moves to observers near where it is, whatever "near" turns out to mean mathematically. And we need a model of measurement as branch points, or choice points: places where the observer has more than one allowed way to go on, not assumed to be Everett's worlds.

**Layer 3. Probability from the structure itself; the Born rule derived.** We need a probability interpretation that uses only ordinary (Kolmogorov) probability over the observer's possible futures and requires no additional assumptions. And yet it must be one from which the Born rule is truly derived, not merely justified: a program that shows the Born rule to be inevitable, inescapable, and not via circular reasoning. Our ultimate goal is a theory from which quantum mechanics is recovered as an approximation (and perhaps Einstein's equations as well: derived, not assumed).

That's it. That's the thread worth pulling. The question should not be: why is this a thread worth pulling? The question should be: how in the name of all that is holy should this NOT be considered a thread worth pulling? Certainly, there is an aesthetic appeal to its ontology. So why has this program not been attempted? (If it has been, what is its name?)

**Layer 4. Local in observer space, not in spacetime.** The best explanation for the failure to pull on this thread is that it feels like a local hidden-variable theory of the sort that Bell's inequality excludes. Our first task is to present the case that this ontology is NOT excluded by Bell. Make no mistake: we will not argue that quantum mechanics is wrong, or that Bell's theorem is wrong. On the contrary, we take QM and Bell as seriously as anyone can claim to take them.

Our argument is simply that the proposed model is not excluded by Bell. It is a hidden-variable theory, and it is local, but not in the textbook sense. The hidden variable is the observer's own state. "Local" means local in observer space, in the sense of layer 2: the observer doesn't teleport. It does not mean local in classical spacetime, which is the locality Bell's theorem assumes. That the model is not excluded by Bell is almost trivially true, once the distinction between observer space and spacetime is made mathematically precise. Readers will also aim a sharper theorem at it: Local Friendliness (Bong et al., 2020), the no-go theorem built for theories in which observers are physical systems. The same case has to be made there. Much confusion will be avoided by the constant reminder that observer space and spacetime are not the same thing. Most mistakes will come from proving a result about one and applying it, without justification, to the other.

**Layer 5. The mathematical foundations.** Making all of this precise is the work of the companion essay, *Mathematical Foundations* (`public-essays/mathematical-foundations/`): what kind of object observer space is, what "near" means, whether change is continuous or comes in steps, and whether where the observer can go depends only on where it is now. Its choices are always open to question.

**Layer 6. The specific constructions.** The papers make specific choices: Paper 1, "The Observer as a Physical Object" (`papers/observer-space-ontology.md`), and the framework paper (`public-papers/observer-space-framework/versions/v0.6-prose.md`). They are the most provisional layer, and the first to give up.
