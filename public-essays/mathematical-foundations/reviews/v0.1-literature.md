## R1. Fix the §9 list of what an exhibiting model needs (required)

§9 says a model would need "(2) a relation between them that does not smuggle in one world". v0.6 §8 item 2 says the opposite. It says a sketch needs "an embedding of both in one world with spacelike-separated anchors, which selects an ensemble member and so is E-smuggling". v0.6 names the smuggling as a cost the sketch must carry. It does not name avoiding it as a requirement. Item (4) also adds "shown to fail in observer space" to v0.6's "a named Bell premise". That is a fair gloss, but it isn't what v0.6 says.

**Replace with:**
> (1) two observers as vertices; (2) an embedding of both in one world with spacelike-separated anchors, which selects an ensemble member and so smuggles in one world, a cost v0.6 names; (3) a setting-dependent correlation rule; (4) a named Bell premise (see the premise table below).

## R2. Add the Bell-premise alternatives to §9 (required)

"Local in observer space" is not itself one of Bell's premises. To say Bell does not exclude the model, you have to say which premise fails once the model is mapped onto a Bell experiment. That is exactly the kind of choice Foundations exists to lay out. Today §9 mentions measurement independence only in passing, as a cost. **Add a second small table, "Which Bell premise fails":**

| Premise dropped | Known programme | Fits | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single outcomes: each measurement has one outcome (absoluteness of observed events) | Everettian local accounts: Deutsch and Hayden, *Proc. R. Soc. A* 456, 1759 (2000); Brown and Timpson, "Bell on Bell's theorem: the changing face of nonlocality", in Bell and Gao (eds), *Quantum Nonlocality and Reality* (CUP 2016), arXiv:1501.03521; Wallace, *The Emergent Multiverse* (OUP 2012), ch. 8 | the co-existence reading (arms co-exist) | the claim becomes the known Everett-style locality move carried into observer space, not something new. The stronger extended-Wigner's-friend no-go, "Local Friendliness" (Bong et al., *Nature Physics* 16, 1199 (2020)), must also be faced |
| Measurement (statistical) independence | superdeterminism: Hossenfelder and Palmer, *Front. Phys.* 8, 139 (2020) | the "settings are state" row | the conspiracy objection. The rule would have to correlate settings with the hidden variable |
| Parameter or outcome independence (Jarrett, *Noûs* 18, 569 (1984)) | Bohm; stochastic collapse (GRW) | the chance reading, if it reproduces QM statistics | nonlocal in spacetime, which is what note 13 aims to avoid |
| No retrocausation | retrocausal models: Wharton and Argaman, *Rev. Mod. Phys.* 92, 021002 (2020) | none in v0.6 | a time-symmetric rule; v0.6 has none |

Say in one line that v0.6 picks none of these, and that its adopted co-existence reading points to the first row. This keeps note 13 honest without choosing for it. It also removes the main desk-reject risk. A referee who reads "LHV, local in another space, not excluded by Bell" and finds no named premise stops reading.

## R3. Add a nearest-programmes paragraph (required)

Proposed text, about 170 words, as a short final section or after the status paragraph:

> **Nearest existing programmes.** We know of no programme that combines all of these choices: an observer defined as an analytic metric germ, a non-deterministic rule on the space of such observers, classical probability over their futures, and Bell locality read in that space. Each ingredient has close neighbours. Observer states as the primary objects, with the chances of the next observer state fixed by the current state alone, are Müller's "law without law" (*Quantum* 4, 301 (2020)), which uses algorithmic probability and no geometry. "Observer space" as a geometry with spacetime reconstructed from it, or not, is Gielen and Wise (*J. Math. Phys.* 54, 052501 (2013)), deterministic and without branching. Stochastic motion of observer states is the many-minds view (Albert and Loewer, *Synthese* 77, 195 (1988)). A classical measure on discrete growth fixed by structural conditions is Rideout–Sorkin sequential growth (*Phys. Rev. D* 61, 024002 (2000)). Counting and typicality over branches are Strayhorn (*Ann. Fond. L. de Broglie* 33, 211 (2008)), Sebens and Carroll (*Brit. J. Phil. Sci.* 69, 25 (2018)) and Vaidman, and Bohmian typicality is Dürr, Goldstein and Zanghì (*J. Stat. Phys.* 67, 843 (1992)). Locality without single outcomes is Deutsch–Hayden and Brown–Timpson. The combination has no established name.

This answers the Problem essay's question without claiming the programme was never attempted. It says only that we know of no instance.

## R4. Missing alternatives (required: first three bullets; the rest recommended)

- **Gielen–Wise abstract observer space (§1, §2, §10). Required.** Their observer space is not only 4-velocities on a given metric. The paper defines "abstract observer space geometries for which no underlying spacetime is assumed", reconstructs spacetime as a quotient when integrability holds, and otherwise treats spacetime as "an observer-dependent, relative concept" (arXiv:1210.0019). So:
  - §1's "Event + 4-velocity in a given spacetime (✗14)" is only their induced case.
  - Add a row to §2 and §10: "Cartan geometry on a 7-dimensional observer space, spacetime reconstructed or not (Gielen–Wise 2013)". Buys: a worked geometry in which spacetime is secondary, which is close to note 14. Costs: it is smooth, deterministic and has no branching, and its points are 4-velocities, not metric germs.
  - Note the name collision once. v0.6's §10 table row ("a 7-manifold of 4-velocities on a given metric") has the same slip and should be fixed in the next framework version.
- **A classical measure fixed by structure (§7). Required.** Add a row: "transition probabilities fixed by symmetry and causality conditions (Rideout–Sorkin classical sequential growth)". Buys: weights forced by axioms, not chosen, which is a direct candidate for note 9. Costs: needs such conditions stated on $(V,R)$, and none are. v0.6 already cites it as a precedent for forced weights, so the essay should too.
- **The reading of the weight (§7). Required.** Typicality appears only in §8. Add one line under the §7 table saying that a measure still needs a reading. The co-existence or typicality reading (Dürr–Goldstein–Zanghì; Sebens–Carroll self-locating uncertainty; Vaidman) is adopted in v0.6. The chance reading (many-minds, Albert–Loewer) is defined but not adopted. §9 uses both, so §7 should introduce them.
- **Space (§2), recommended:**
  - a locally finite partial order (causal sets: Bombelli, Lee, Meyer and Sorkin, *PRL* 59, 521 (1987)), a natural strengthening of the digraph row that v0.6's no-Zeno clause already half imposes;
  - a configuration space of records (Barbour, *Class. Quantum Grav.* 11, 2853 and 2875 (1994), "time capsules"), also for the §5 "records" row;
  - finite observer states as strings (Müller 2020), which trades geometry for algorithmic probability.
- **Motion (§3), recommended:**
  - multiplicity of histories from global consistency rather than a local rule (billiard balls in spacetimes with closed timelike curves: Echeverria, Klinkhammer and Thorne, *Phys. Rev. D* 44, 1077 (1991));
  - classical indeterminism from non-Lipschitz equations (Norton's dome, *Phil. Sci.* 75, 786 (2008)). Worth one clause, because analyticity rules this route out: real-analytic equations have unique solutions. That is the same reason §3's first row has a unique successor.
- **Probability (§7), recommended:** algorithmic prior (Müller 2020). Excluded as a separate postulate (✗9), but it is the nearest programme that gets next-state chances from the current state alone.

## Nits (not HOLD)

1. **§2 mixes what the points are with what structure sits on them.** Bare lumps, the jet space and the oriented space $V$ are choices of points. Fermi topology, manifold and digraph are structures on those points. A two-part table (points; structure) would make the alternatives genuinely distinct, and v0.6's choice would read as "oriented $V$ plus digraph plus Thm 3 curves".
2. **§7's "Coincidence" row is a condition, not a rule.** Move it into the T2 text, where it already is.
3. **§3: "meets note 5 only if multiple next observers come from elsewhere (v0.6 §10)".** v0.6 §10 says only that, with type (ii) abandoned, Paper 1's non-uniqueness is false under the CWS singleton. The "from elsewhere" hedge is the essay's own, and could be attributed as such or dropped.
4. **§2: "quotient not known to be Hausdorff (Props. 14–15)".** Prop. 14 is about closure; only Prop. 15 is the Hausdorff statement.
5. **§1: "Material system (brain, record)"** could cite the standard neighbours (Everett relative states; Zurek, *Nature Physics* 5, 181 (2009)) in the reference pass.
