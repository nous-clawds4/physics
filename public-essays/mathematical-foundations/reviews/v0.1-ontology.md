## Verdict

No law of $R$, no Born or Einstein claim, and Paper 1's object is untouched. Four places quietly strengthen or reinterpret a note, and each is fixed by a sentence or a cell (R1, R2, R4, R5):

T2 leans slightly: it applies note 9 to EPP1 but not to the count's named cut (R3). One Literature addition would pick a Bell premise for David, and a Bell sentence already in #140 has the same problem (R6, R7). None of the required items needs the Problem essay to change.

## Q2. Placement

**Foundations → Problem (strong opinions in Foundations):** none needs to move.
- §5's "Here sits the infinite-jet, real-analytic reading" is the nearest case. It is v0.6's locked choice, not a new commitment, so rewording it is enough (N1).
- The §4 and §9 Bell sentences are consequences of Foundations alternatives, not opinions. They must be conditional on a mapping (R7). As bare statements they would conflict with note 13, and under the README that conflict goes to David, not into Foundations.

**Problem notes → Foundations (hidden math):** four places. Each becomes a question for David (Q-D1 to Q-D4); no fix is required in #139.
- **Note 5, "may allow".** Permission or existence? (Q-D1.)
- **"Next observer" in notes 5, 6, 8, 9.** This is discrete-successor language. Note 4 says it does not commit to continuous paths, but a continuous curve has no "next" point, so the notes quietly assume a successor relation. (Q-D2.)
- **Note 6, "and how they are weighted".** Weights that depend only on the current observer define a Markov kernel, which is a per-step measure. Under (M2) the conditional weight of an arm is $\lvert C\cap\mathrm{desc}(a)\rvert/\lvert C\cap\mathrm{desc}(v)\rvert$. That depends on the cut $C$, and so on depth or accumulated $\tau$ from the root, unless the cut is anchored at the current observer. Note 9 leaves "each step versus whole histories" open. So note 6 as written leans toward (M1), and the two notes pull apart. (Q-D3.)
- **Note 13 names only the locality premise.** It says Bell assumes spacetime locality and that the conclusion does not carry over to observer-space locality. It is silent on Bell's other premises (single outcomes, measurement independence). (Q-D4.)

## Q3. Tensions

**T1 (note 6 vs the arrival flag).** The T1 block is fair.
- It cites C6 (1 arm after a jump, at least 2 after a segment), so $(O^u,a)$ provably differs from $O^u$.
- It keeps the options open.
- It says plainly that $a$ is history data, not germ data.

Two places elsewhere undo that fairness: the §4 table (R1) and the §9 table (R2). Both treat the enlarged state as the observer. The third option, "Carry the tension … note 6 holds only for the enlarged state", reads like the first option restated (N4).

**T2 (per-step vs counted weight).** No choice is made ("not settled here"). But the framing is one-sided:
- EPP1 is presented as the thing that strains note 9.
- The count row has "cut is extra" in its costs, but its note cell says only "9", with no T2 flag.
- The note-6 point above, which strains (M2), is absent.

A reader comes away thinking (M2) is the note-9-clean option. v0.6 and #139 question (b) both hold that (M1)/(M2) are unchosen. R3 makes T2 symmetric.

## Q4. Literature's additions against the Problem essay

- **Lit R1 (E-smuggling as a named cost): fits.** Embedding both observers in one world is exactly the step that note 14 says needs an argument. No change.
- **Lit R3 (nearest programmes, "no established name"): fits.** It answers the essay's "(If it has been -- what is its name?)" without claiming the programme was never attempted. One wording point: the combination it names is v0.6's (analytic germ), and note 1 does not commit to that (N5).
- **Lit R4 (Gielen–Wise abstract observer space; Rideout–Sorkin forced weights; reading of the weight): fits.** Rideout–Sorkin is a direct candidate for note 9's "from the structure already given". Taking Lit R4 alongside R4 below is consistent: both de-flag §1's event + 4-velocity row as a note-14 violation.
- **Lit R2 (which Bell premise fails): does not fit as written.** It is right to add the table, but three things would pick a premise for David or contradict his framing.
  1. **No row for the essay's own framing.** The essay says "It is a LHV theory, but it is local in observer-space, not in classical spacetime." That is: Bell's locality premise is stated in spacetime, the model claims no spacetime locality, and its locality is note 6's. None of Lit R2's four rows says this. Row 3 (parameter or outcome independence) is the nearest, and it is written as the Bohm/GRW case.
  2. **Row 3's cost cell misreads note 13.** "Nonlocal in spacetime, which is what note 13 aims to avoid" is not what note 13 says. Note 13 does not claim spacetime locality; the essay text explicitly disclaims it ("not in classical spacetime"). The real cost is different: a referee will file the model with Bohm unless the mapping shows no signalling and no preferred frame.
  3. **"Its adopted co-existence reading points to the first row."** Together with #140's "v0.6's only route for the Bell Hope", this makes single-outcome failure the only route on the page. That is the Everett-style move, and it sits badly with "It is a LHV theory". Hidden-variable theories normally keep single outcomes. v0.6's arms are oriented observers, not decoherence branches, and each history sees one outcome. So "co-existence ⇒ single outcomes fail" is itself a mapping choice, and note 12 leaves the mapping open.

  R6 gives wording that keeps the table unchosen and consistent with note 13. Whether David means "locality, reread" (with single outcomes kept) or accepts single-outcome failure is Q-D4.

## Questions for David (Problem notes; not required fixes)

- **Q-D1 (note 5).** Does "without being fully deterministic" require at least one observer with two or more allowed next observers, or only permit it? Note 5 says "may allow". Foundations' ✗5 flags and v0.6's $R\ne\emptyset$ both read it as existence.
- **Q-D2 (notes 4–6, 8–9, "next observer").** Does "next" mean a discrete step (a successor relation), or does it also cover continuation along a continuous path? Note 4 leaves continuity open, but "next" presupposes steps. v0.6's answer is "the next vertex", with segments between.
- **Q-D3 (note 6 vs note 9, weights).** Note 6 says the weights depend only on the current observer. That is a per-step rule, and it leans toward (M1). Note 9 leaves step versus count open. Which governs? This sharpens #139 question (b).
- **Q-D4 (notes 12–13, which premise).** "LHV, local in observer space, not in classical spacetime" reads as Bell's locality premise reread, with single outcomes kept. Is that the intent? Or is failure of single outcomes, via co-existing arms, an acceptable route? Note 13 is silent, and the Foundations table stays unchosen either way (R6).
- **Q-D5 (notes 6 and 12, the arrival flag).** Should the arrival flag count as part of "the current observer" or "the observer's own state"? If yes, the observer exceeds Paper 1's oriented germ. If no, v0.6's rule (A) strains note 6. This is T1, and it sits next to #139 question (a).
