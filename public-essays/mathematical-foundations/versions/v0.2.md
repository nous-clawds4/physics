# Mathematical Foundations

David Strayhorn

**Status.** v0.2 (revises v0.1). Strong opinions, weakly held; expected to change often.

*Statement of the Problem* (v0.1, cited "Problem, note N") commits each mathematical word only to its weakest reading. For each deferred word, this essay lays out the alternatives (buys, costs, Bell), and marks which one the framework paper uses: *Observer space and graph joins*, v0.6 prose (`public-papers/observer-space-framework/versions/v0.6-prose.md`), cited "v0.6 §n". The Problem essay wins any conflict. No law of motion, Born derivation, limit or Bell-evading model is claimed.

Results (`papers/`): `geodesic-lifts.md` (Thm 3, Prop. 3.2), `patch-edge.md` (Thm 7), `type-ii-adopted.md` (Prop. 13), `obs-as-a-space.md` (Prop. 15, Thm 16), `neighborhood-uncountable.md` (Thm 17).

Legend: **✗n** = contradicts note n's weakest reading; **bold** v0.6 entry = used in v0.6 (a fact, not an endorsement); HV = hidden variable; Cn = v0.6 Appendix A pin.

## 3. Motion and the non-deterministic rule (Problem, notes 4–5)

Weakest reading: a history is an ordered succession of observers, with a rule on which are allowed (note 4). From a given observer the rule *may* allow more than one next observer, and it forbids others (note 5). The ✗5 flags read the essay's "without being fully deterministic" as "some observer has more than one allowed next observer"; that reading is open. Continuity, time parameter, proper time per step, and whether "next" presupposes discrete steps are open.

| Alternative | Buys | Costs | Bell | v0.6 | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Continuous curves only (geodesic lifts, world switching) | analytic (Thm 3) | unique successor (Prop. 3.2, Thm 7) | one history | type-(i) baseline, **§4.1** | **✗5** if the whole rule |
| **Mixed**: proper-time segments joined by jumps that carry no $\tau$ | geodesic motion between decisions | not a $\tau$-curve; needs alternation + no-Zeno (§5.3) | branching at jumps | **§4.2 items 2, 7–8** | 4, 5 |
| Digraph rule: $R\ne\emptyset$ (existential), neighbourhood law, or germ readout | countable steps | not derivable (Prop. 13); continua (Thm 17); readout unmotivated (§5.4) | countable outcomes | **§4.2** ($R\ne\emptyset$ only) | 4, 5 |
| Differential inclusion, stochastic or non-Lipschitz ODE (Norton's dome) | calculus | needs a manifold; analyticity bars the dome | stochastic process | no | 4, 5 |
| $R=V\times V$ | trivial | forbids nothing | none | no | **✗5** |

No law of $R$ is adopted. Type (ii) is abandonable. If it is abandoned, Paper 1's non-uniqueness is false under the singleton baseline (v0.6 §10).

## 4. Locality in observer space (Problem, note 6)

Weakest reading: which next observers are allowed, and how weighted, depends only on the current observer; no nearness, nothing about spacetime.

| Alternative | Buys | Costs | v0.6 | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Observer-only**: allowed next observers and weights are a function of the current observer $O^u$ | exactly note 6 | the observer must hold everything the rule reads | v0.6's Obs-local (§9) is defined on the state $(O^u,a)$, so it meets this row only under T1's "enlarge the state" option | 6 |
| Topological: neighbourhood targets, continuous paths | continuity | topology (Thm 16); continua (Thm 17) | type (i) only | 6, strengthened |
| Metric (bounded jumps) or differential (rule reads path jets) | quantitative | no distance or manifold given | segments (differential) | 6, strengthened |
| Spacetime locality | Bell's notion | not note 6 | no | not note 6 |

Bell: every notion but the last is about $V$, not spacetime. Under a mapping in which each wing's observer evolves by its own stochastic rule from a shared past state, a per-observer stochastic rule is what Bell constrains (§9).

**Tension T1: jumps without proper time versus "a local process".** v0.6's type-(ii) jumps carry no proper time and are discontinuous in oriented observer space (v0.6 §3, §4.2 item 2). Against note 4: no conflict (proper time per step is open). Against topological locality: conflict, since no continuous path in $V$ joins a jump's ends; but that is a strengthening of note 6. Against observer-only locality: it turns on what "the current observer" includes. Under arrival rule (A) the arm set depends on $(O^u,a)$, with $a$ recording arrival by segment or jump (v0.6 §4.2 item 7): 1 arm after a jump, at least 2 after a segment (C6).

Options, all open:
- **Enlarge the state.** Count $(O^u,a)$ as the current observer; note 6 holds. But $a$ is history data, not germ data (v0.6 §2): the observer then exceeds Paper 1's oriented germ, or "state" and "observer" come apart.
- **Drop (A).** v0.6's examined alternatives: a bound on consecutive jumps (law-like), or a ban on jumps onto vertices (kills the worked instance). With no arrival rule, jump chains pile up at one accumulated time (C5).
- **Carry the tension.** Keep (A), and keep the observer as $O^u$. Note 6 is then openly strained, since the rule also reads $a$.

## 5. Internal state as a complete representation (Problem, note 7)

Weakest reading: everything the observer takes to be true of the outside world is fixed by its own state. Neither a unique universe nor an observer-to-universe map is committed to.

| Alternative | Buys | Costs | v0.6 | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Infinite jet, real-analytic** | fixes the patch $P(O)$ | one patch, not one universe | **§2** | 7 |
| Finite jet / $C^\infty$ germ | weaker regularity | surroundings not fixed (Borel's lemma; flat perturbations) | no | 7, trivially |
| Whole-universe state (Cauchy data) | one universe | not local to the observer | no (§2) | 7, stronger |
| Records in matter (Barbour's "time capsules") | usual picture | needs matter, correlation model | cartoon (§6) | 7 |

This is v0.6's reading: one jet fixes the unique simply-connected patch, but not one universe, since many analytic worlds contain the same lump (v0.6 §2). The map from what the jet fixes to what the observer takes to be true is itself open (note 7).

## 6. Branch and measurement (Problem, note 8)

Weakest reading: a branch point is an observer from which more than one next observer is allowed; a measurement is modelled as one.

| Alternative | Buys | Costs | v0.6 | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Arms = continue + jump targets**, finite | exact count | needs $R$; vertices on a chosen slice $V^\tau$ (§5.1), none on Minkowski/dS/ESU | **§4.2, §5.1** | 8 |
| Continuum of targets | natural | uncountable (Thm 17) | no | 8 |
| Decoherence branches | standard QM | imports the wave function | no (§7) | 8 |

Bell: arms are the candidate outcomes. Excluded by v0.6 results: delayed forks among analytic continuations (Prop. 3.2, Thm 7) and world endings (v0.6 §5.2). Where branch points occur, and how many, is open.

## 7. Classical probability without additional assumptions (Problem, note 9)

Weakest reading: ordinary probabilities over an observer's futures, from the space and allowed motions, not a separate probability postulate. The method, and whether any choice is needed, are open.

| Alternative | Buys | Costs | Bell | v0.6 | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Per-step equal weights (EPP1): $1/\lvert A\rvert$ per arm | no free numbers | a named postulate; unequal weights on co-existing histories | per-observer process | **§7** (working postulate; (M1)/(M2) unchosen, §9) | 9 (T2) |
| Count at a named cut (variant: maximal-entropy walk) | Paper 1's count | cut is extra; cut-dependent; the walk's squared-eigenvector density is a circularity hazard | outcome counting | (M2) §9, unchosen | 9 (T2) |
| Weights forced by symmetry/causality conditions (Rideout–Sorkin) | forced, not chosen | no conditions stated on $(V,R)$ | open | precedent (§7) | 9 |
| Ensemble-size weight (EPP2) | uses worlds | ensemble vote | none | foil | 9 (extra structure) |
| Free kernel, algorithmic prior (Müller), or $\lvert a\rvert^2$ inserted | fits data | a separate probability postulate | standard stochastic HV | no | **✗9**, ✗10 |

A measure still needs a reading: co-existence (typicality), a measure over co-existing histories, adopted in v0.6 and the route v0.6 names for the Bell Hope (v0.6 §9; neighbours Dürr–Goldstein–Zanghì, Sebens–Carroll, Vaidman); or chance, one history followed, defined but not adopted (neighbour: many-minds, Albert–Loewer).

**Tension T2: per-step weights versus "no additional assumptions".**

EPP1 has no adjustable numbers, but v0.6 names it a postulate, which strains note 9. On a named cut $C$, with $D(v)$ the product of out-degrees above $v$, per-step weighting gives $1/D(v)$ and counting gives $1/\lvert C\rvert$. They agree iff $D$ is constant on $C$ (in the limit, iff siblings balance); agreement is not monotone in depth and can fail at proper-time cuts (v0.6 §7; C12–C15). Where they agree, no choice is needed.

The count is not free of additions either. Its named cut is structure beyond the space and the allowed motions (note 9). Its implied next-step weight, $\lvert C\cap\mathrm{desc}(a)\rvert/\lvert C\cap\mathrm{desc}(v)\rvert$, depends on the cut, and so on where the history started, unless the cut is anchored at the current observer. That strains note 6's "how they are weighted … depends only on the current observer", as EPP1's postulate strains note 9. Each option strains a note; neither is chosen. Which note governs weights is open.

EPP1 also cannot be tuned: with $m$ jump targets per decision the continue arm has weight $(1+m)^{-n}$ after $n$ decisions, so geodesic motion needs sparse decisions or observationally null jumps (v0.6 §7).

## 8. Aims: Born rule, and recovery in an approximation (Problem, notes 10–11)

These are aims only. No route, derivation or limit is exhibited.

**Note 10.** A derivation would need at least a uniform, motivated rule for $R$ (v0.6 §5.4), weights fixed by structure (§7), a map from arms to outcomes, and no hand-drawn $\lvert a\rvert^2$ tree (v0.6 §8).

**Note 11.** Candidate limits: deep trees (§7), coarse-graining over branch points, observationally null jumps. Einstein's equations are not assumed (v0.6 §2).

## 9. Local hidden variable, and not excluded by Bell (Problem, notes 12–13)

Note 12: the HV is the observer's own state; "local" as in note 6. Note 13: Bell's theorem assumes locality in spacetime; its conclusion does not carry over to locality in observer space. No model is exhibited.

| Mapping | Buys | Costs | v0.6 | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **HV = observer state $O^u$.** Under (A) the rule also reads the flag $a$; whether $a$ is part of "the observer's own state" is T1 (§4), not settled here | note 12 as stated | settings/outcomes map open | **§9** | 12 |
| Shared variable at a spacetime source, outcomes set locally in spacetime | standard | Bell's own model, excluded | no | **✗12**, ✗13 |

**Which Bell premise fails.**

| Premise declined | Known programme | Fits | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Locality, reread: Bell's premise is stated in spacetime; the model claims only observer-space locality (note 6) | the Problem essay's framing ("local in observer-space, not in classical spacetime") | either reading, once a mapping is given (note 12) | filed with the parameter/outcome row unless the mapping shows no signalling and no preferred frame; no mapping given |
| Single outcomes | Everettian locality (Deutsch–Hayden; Brown–Timpson; Wallace) | co-existence reading, if co-existing arms count as outcomes (a mapping choice) | the known Everett move; faces Local Friendliness (Bong et al.) |
| Measurement independence | superdeterminism (Hossenfelder–Palmer) | settings as state | conspiracy objection |
| Parameter or outcome independence (Jarrett) | Bohm; GRW | chance reading: what Bell constrains, under the per-wing mapping of §4 | nonlocal in spacetime with signalling or a preferred frame; note 13 claims no spacetime locality, so the cost is those, not nonlocality as such |
| No retrocausation | Wharton–Argaman | none | a time-symmetric rule |

Which premise fails depends on how Bell's settings, outcomes and shared variable map onto observer space, and note 12 leaves that open. This essay lists the rows and picks none; neither does v0.6.

An exhibiting model would need at least (v0.6 §8): (1) two observers as vertices; (2) an embedding of both in one world with spacelike-separated anchors, which selects an ensemble member and so smuggles in one world, a cost v0.6 names; (3) a setting-dependent correlation rule; (4) a named Bell premise. None is exhibited.

## 10. Observer space and spacetime (Problem, note 14)

Weakest reading: different sets with different structure; results do not transfer without argument. The map is open.

| Relation | Buys | Costs | v0.6 | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Occurrence**: a lump occurs at points of many worlds ($E_W$) | no map assumed | many-to-many | **§2** | 14 |
| Projection $\gamma=\pi_W(\sigma)$ | curves in $V$ (Thm 3) | lifetimes world-dependent (Prop. 3.2) | **§3** | 14 |
| Bundle over one given spacetime | concrete | misses other worlds' observers; the projection supplies the transfer argument, so the Bell burden moves to the mapping (note 12) | no | **✗3**; note 14 allowed |

Bell: spacetime separation of the wings is not separation in $V$. Concretely, the Fermi-slice topology (Thm 16) lives on observer space; spacelike-separated Minkowski events are one oriented observer (C9, C28); nearby events at different Schwarzschild radii are different observers.

## 11. Nearest existing programmes

We know of no programme that combines the Problem essay's aims with v0.6's choices: an analytic germ as observer, a non-deterministic rule on the space of observers, classical probability over their futures, and Bell locality read in that space. Neighbours: next-state chances from the current observer state alone (Müller, algorithmic, no geometry); observer space as a geometry (Gielen–Wise; deterministic, no branching); stochastic observer states (many-minds, Albert–Loewer); a structurally fixed measure on discrete growth (Rideout–Sorkin); counting or typicality over branches (Strayhorn 2008; Sebens–Carroll; Vaidman; Dürr–Goldstein–Zanghì); locality without single outcomes (Deutsch–Hayden; Brown–Timpson). The combination has no established name.
