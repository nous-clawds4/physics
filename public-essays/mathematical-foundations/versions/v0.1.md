# Mathematical Foundations

David Strayhorn

**Status.** v0.1. Strong opinions, weakly held; expected to change often.

This essay is the companion to *Statement of the Problem* (v0.1; cited here as "Problem, note N"). That essay says what the program is trying to do. For each mathematical word in it, the essay gives the weakest reading it commits to and defers the rest. This essay takes each deferred word in turn and lays out the mathematical alternatives. For each alternative it says what the choice buys, what it costs, and what it would mean for the Bell argument. It marks which alternative the current framework paper uses, *Observer space and graph joins* v0.6 prose (`public-papers/observer-space-framework/versions/v0.6-prose.md`, cited as "v0.6 §n"). It also flags any alternative that would contradict a weakest reading. Where the two essays conflict, the Problem essay wins. Nothing here claims a law of motion, a Born-rule derivation, a limit, or a model that evades Bell.

Supporting results are cited by file: `papers/geodesic-lifts.md` (Thm 3, Prop. 3.2), `papers/patch-edge.md` (Thm 7), `papers/type-ii-adopted.md` (Prop. 13), `papers/obs-as-a-space.md` (Props. 14–15, Thm 16) and `papers/neighborhood-uncountable.md` (Thm 17).

Legend: **✗n** means the alternative contradicts the weakest reading of Problem note n. **v0.6** marks the alternative the framework currently uses.

## 1. The observer and its precise definition (Problem, notes 1–2)

The weakest reading asks for two things. The observer is a physical thing, of the kind physics already describes (note 1). It has an exact, checkable definition (note 2).

| Alternative | Buys | Costs | Bell | v0.6 | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Oriented metric germ**: the infinite jet of a real-analytic Lorentzian metric at $p$, plus a future unit timelike $u$; equivalently the simply-connected analytic patch $P(O)$ from local continuation (not a completed 4-manifold) | purely geometric; exact; rigid (§5 below) | analyticity excludes generic smooth metrics; matter fields open | the hidden variable would be jet data plus $u$ | **v0.6 §2** (Paper 1's object) | 1, 2, 7 |
| Finite jet or $C^\infty$ germ | weaker regularity | does not fix its surroundings (§5 below) | as above, but less data | no | 1, 2 |
| Material system (brain, detector, record) as a matter configuration | familiar | needs matter fields and a definition of "system" | the hidden variable is a matter state | no (matter open, v0.6 §6) | 1 |
| Event plus 4-velocity in a given spacetime | standard kinematics | observer space becomes a bundle over one spacetime | the hidden variable is located in spacetime; Bell applies directly | no (v0.6 §2 distinguishes it) | ✗14 (see §10) |
| Abstract agent or undefined primitive | nothing | no physical carrier, or no definition | undefined | no | **✗1**, **✗2** |

Gauge beyond germ isotropy is left open: two presentations are the same lump when a pointed analytic diffeomorphism relates them (v0.6 §2).

## 2. Space (Problem, note 3)

The weakest reading is the set of all possible observers. It has no topology, distance, manifold structure, dimension or measure.

| Alternative | Buys | Costs | Bell | v0.6 | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bare set of lumps | exactly note 3 | no nearness, no measure | "local" can only mean state-only (§4 below) | the underlying set | 3 |
| Jet/germ space: an inverse limit of finite jet spaces | analytic *curves* are defined (Thm 3) | on all presentations, the quotient topology is not known to be Hausdorff (Props. 14–15) | allows limits of states | curves only | 3 |
| Topological space: Fermi-slice quotient $\mathcal P_F/O(3)$ | Hausdorff neighbourhoods of lumps (Thm 16); neighbourhood laws become stateable | every open ball minus the curvelet is uncountable (Thm 17), so neighbourhood laws yield continua | allows topological locality (§4 below) | available, not used by any rule | 3 |
| Infinite-dimensional manifold | differential equations, tangent vectors | no atlas is known; not declared | allows differential locality | no | 3 |
| Graph or digraph $(V,R)$ | discrete succession, counting | needs a relation $R$, whose law is open | allows arm counting, outcome counting | **v0.6 §4.2** (the incidence of $R$) | 3, 5, 8 |
| **Oriented observer space $V$**: $O^u$ with $u$ taken modulo germ isotropy | makes arm sets physical (v0.6 C9) | the flat lump is one point for all $u$ | the hidden variable lives in $V$ | **v0.6 §2** | 3, 6 |
| Jet bundle of one fixed spacetime | concrete | misses observers of other worlds | spacetime structure transfers | no | **✗3** ("all possible") |

v0.6 uses $V$ as a set, with the analytic-curve structure of Thm 3 and the incidence of an unspecified $R$. It uses no topology in any rule. No measure on $V$ is used, or needed, at this level.

## 3. Motion and the non-deterministic rule (Problem, notes 4–5)

The weakest reading has two parts. A history is an ordered succession of observers, and a rule says which successions are allowed (note 4). From some observer the rule allows more than one next observer, and it forbids others (note 5). Continuity, differential equations, a time parameter, and proper time on every step are all left open.

| Alternative | Buys | Costs | Bell | v0.6 | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Continuous curves only: geodesic lifts, world switching | analytic, parameter-free (Thm 3) | the successor is unique: one branch class (Prop. 3.2; Thm 7; v0.6 §4.1) | deterministic single history | the type-(i) baseline, **v0.6 §4.1** | **✗5** if it is the whole rule |
| Discrete succession: paths in a digraph | counting, graph tools | the law of $R$ is open (Prop. 13) | outcome counting is defined | part of v0.6 | 4, 5 |
| **Mixed**: segments of proper time joined by jumps that carry no proper time | keeps geodesic motion between decisions; non-deterministic at jumps | a history is no longer a curve parametrised by $\tau$; clause (2) needs strict alternation and no-Zeno (v0.6 §5.3) | branching happens at jumps | **v0.6 §4.2 items 2, 7–8** | 4 (open on proper time), 5 |
| Differential inclusion or stochastic equation on a manifold | calculus | needs manifold structure (§2 above) | a stochastic version is a local stochastic process | no | 4, 5 |
| Rule that allows everything ($R=V\times V$) | trivial | forbids nothing | none | no | **✗5** ("forbids others") |

For the rule itself, the candidate forms include:
- a relation $R$ with $R\ne\emptyset$ as an existential postulate. It is not derivable from locked data (Prop. 13; v0.6 §4.2), and it asserts no branch point at any named observer.
- a neighbourhood law, stateable by Thm 16 but continuum-valued by Thm 17;
- a readout of the germ, such as the Kretschmann rule of v0.6 §5.4, which is stated and uniform but unmotivated.

No law of $R$ is adopted here. Type (ii) is abandonable, but abandoning it leaves only the first row. That row meets note 5 only if some other source of more than one allowed next observer is found (v0.6 §10).

## 4. Locality in observer space (Problem, note 6)

The weakest reading: which next observers are allowed, and with what weights, depends only on the current observer. Nearness is not required, and spacetime locality is not implied.

| Alternative | Buys | Costs | Bell | v0.6 | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **State-only**: the rule is a function of the current state | exactly note 6; no topology needed | "the current state" must contain everything the rule reads | Bell constrains a per-observer stochastic version (§9 below) | **v0.6 §9** ("Obs-local") | 6 |
| Topological: next observers lie in a neighbourhood; paths are continuous | continuity, as in the slogan "movement is a local process" | needs a topology (Thm 16); neighbourhood targets are continua (Thm 17) | "near" means near in $V$, not in spacetime | type (i) only | 6 (strengthening) |
| Metric: jumps are bounded in a distance | quantitative nearness | no distance is given; compare the "observationally null" condition (J2) in v0.6 §7 | as above | no | 6 (strengthening) |
| Differential: the rule reads only jets of the path at the point | ODE-like | needs manifold structure | as above | the geodesic equation on segments | 6 (strengthening) |
| Spacetime locality | Bell's own notion | not what note 6 says | Bell's theorem applies | no | not note 6 |

**Tension T1: jumps without proper time versus "a local process".**

In v0.6, type-(ii) jumps carry no proper time and are discontinuous in oriented observer space (v0.6 §3, §4.2 item 2). Against note 4 this is no conflict, since note 4 leaves proper time per step open. Against topological locality it is a conflict: no continuous path in $V$ joins a jump's source to its target. Topological locality is a strengthening of note 6, not its weakest reading.

Against state-only locality, the question is what "the current observer" includes. Under v0.6's arrival rule (A), the arm set depends on the pair (oriented germ $O^u$, arrival flag $a$). The flag records whether $O^u$ was reached by a segment or by a jump (v0.6 §4.2 item 7). The same germ has one arm after a jump and at least two after a segment (v0.6 C6). Three options stay open:

- **Enlarge the state.** Count $(O^u,a)$ as the current observer. Note 6 then holds. But the flag is history data, not germ data (v0.6 §2 defines lock-side as excluding history data). So either the physical observer becomes more than Paper 1's oriented germ, or "state" and "observer" separate.
- **Drop (A).** The rule then reads $O^u$ alone. The alternatives v0.6 examined are a bound on consecutive jumps, which is law-like, and forbidding jumps onto vertices, which kills the only worked instance. With no arrival rule at all, jump chains pile up at one accumulated time (v0.6 C5, §4.2).
- **Carry the tension.** Keep (A), and state that note 6 holds only for the enlarged state.

## 5. Internal state as a complete representation (Problem, note 7)

The weakest reading: everything the observer takes to be true of the outside world is fixed by its own state. It is not required that the state pick out one whole universe, and no map from observer to universe is fixed.

| Alternative | Buys | Costs | Bell | v0.6 | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Infinite jet, real-analytic** | the jet fixes the whole patch $P(O)$ by analytic continuation; the representation reaches beyond the point | generic smooth geometry excluded; matter open; one patch, not one universe (below) | whatever the observer "knows" is a function of its own state | **v0.6 §2** | 7 |
| Finite jet or $C^\infty$ germ | weaker regularity | does not fix the surroundings: by Borel's lemma every formal series is the jet of some $C^\infty$ metric, and flat perturbations change the continuation. The state then represents only itself | as above | no | 7 (holds only trivially) |
| Whole-universe state (e.g. Cauchy data of one world) | one universe per observer | not local to the observer; conflicts with the ensemble reading | the hidden variable is a global state | no (no whole-world reconstruction, v0.6 §2) | 7 (stronger than required) |
| Records in matter (memory, brain) | the usual picture of representation | needs matter fields and a model of correlation | outcomes are records | no (a cartoon only, v0.6 §6) | 7 |

This is where the infinite-jet and real-analytic reading sits. Real-analyticity makes "fixed by its own state" non-trivial: one jet rigidly determines the unique simply-connected patch. It does not determine a unique whole universe. Many connected analytic worlds contain occurrences of the same lump, related by hole-punching, quotients, and non-unique maximal extensions (v0.6 §2). That matches note 7's open clause exactly.

## 6. Branch and measurement (Problem, note 8)

The weakest reading: a branch point is an observer with more than one allowed next observer, and a measurement is modelled as one.

| Alternative | Buys | Costs | Bell | v0.6 | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Arms at an observer = continue + jumps to targets** ($B_R$, with $\lvert B_R\rvert=1+\lvert\mathrm{succ}_R\rvert$) | an exact arm count | needs $R$; branch points sit on a chosen slice $V^\tau$ (v0.6 §5.1), and there are none on Minkowski, de Sitter or ESU, nor on flat ΛCDM for the example scalars | outcomes are arms | **v0.6 §4.2, §5.1** | 8 |
| Delayed forks among analytic continuations | branching inside geometry alone | ruled out: analytic lifts that share a prefix agree (Prop. 3.2; Thm 7) | none | excluded | 8 |
| A world ending in the ensemble ("dying") | cheap | an ending is not an arm (v0.6 §5.2) | none | excluded | 8 |
| Decoherence branches | standard quantum mechanics | note 8 does not commit to it; it imports the wave function | Everett-type outcome counting | no (arms are not decoherence branches, v0.6 §7) | 8 |
| Continuum of targets (a neighbourhood law) | natural | branching is not countable (Thm 17), so it needs a named cut or exhaustion | counting is undefined | no | 8 |

Where branch points occur, and how many arms they have, stays open, as in note 8.

## 7. Classical probability without additional assumptions (Problem, note 9)

The weakest reading: ordinary probabilities over an observer's futures, obtained from the space and the allowed motions rather than from a separate probability postulate. The method of turning structure into numbers is open, as is whether any choice is needed at all.

| Alternative | Buys | Costs | Bell | v0.6 | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Per-step equal weights (EPP1)**: $1/\lvert A\rvert$ on each arm in force | no free numbers; a product measure on completed histories | named as a postulate; weights co-existing histories unequally | the chance form is a per-observer stochastic process | **v0.6 §7** (working; (M1) in §9) | 9 (tension T2) |
| Counting distinct histories at a named cut | Paper 1's count; carries its uniqueness thesis at the cut | the cut is an extra choice; a prefix's weight depends on the cut; completed histories may be uncountable | resembles outcome counting | (M2), v0.6 §9 (not chosen) | 9 |
| **Coincidence (no choice needed)**: the tree is such that both rules agree | no choice to make | holds only when the degree products are constant on the cut, or balance in the limit | as above | criterion in v0.6 §7 (C13–C15) | 9 |
| Maximal-entropy walk (all paths of a given length equally likely) | also a count form | its stationary density is a squared eigenvector, which is a circularity hazard, not a route | as above | no (a neighbour only, v0.6 §7) | 9, 10 |
| Weight by ensemble size (EPP2) | uses worlds | the ensemble votes; a departure from Paper 1 | none | a foil only, v0.6 §7 | 9 (extra structure) |
| Free kernel, or $\lvert a\rvert^2$ inserted | fits the data | a separate probability postulate | a standard stochastic hidden-variable model | no | **✗9** (and ✗10) |

**Tension T2: per-step weights versus "no additional assumptions".**

EPP1 has no adjustable numbers, since the arm count fixes it. But v0.6 names it a postulate, and it differs from Paper 1's count. For a named finite cut $C$, let $D(v)$ be the product of the out-degrees along the path to $v$. Per-step weighting gives $1/D(v)$, and counting gives $1/\lvert C\rvert$. They agree on $C$ exactly when $D$ is constant on $C$ (v0.6 §7).
- On uneven trees they disagree: $1/6$ versus $1/4$ (v0.6 C12).
- Agreement can fail at one depth and hold at the next (C13).
- It can fail at proper-time cuts even on generation-regular trees (C14).
- Counts converge to per-step weights in the limit iff siblings balance asymptotically, which can happen without constant products at any cut (C15).

EPP1 also cannot be tuned: with $m$ jump targets at each decision, the continue arm has weight $(1+m)^{-n}$ after $n$ decisions (v0.6 §7). Any future $R$ that is to show geodesic motion must therefore make its decisions sparse, or its jumps observationally null.

Which of the rows above satisfies note 9, if any does, is not settled here.

## 8. Aims: Born rule, and recovery in an approximation (Problem, notes 10–11)

These are aims only. No route is claimed, and no derivation or limit is exhibited.

**Note 10: what a derivation would need.** A derivation of the Born rule would need at least four things:
- a uniform rule for $R$ with a motivation that selects it (the public gate of v0.6 §5.4);
- weights fixed by that structure (§7 above);
- a map from arms to measurement outcomes;
- the circularity wall respected. A tree drawn by hand to match $\lvert a\rvert^2$ is not a derivation (v0.6 §8).

Candidate routes, all unexhibited, are typicality over co-existing histories and counting at a cut. The squared-eigenvector hazard of §7 marks a place where circularity could enter unnoticed.

**Note 11: candidate limits.** The candidates include:
- deep-tree limits of the branching structure (§7 above);
- coarse-graining over many branch points;
- regimes in which the jumps are observationally null (condition (J2), v0.6 §7).

Einstein's equations are not assumed: any analytic Lorentzian metric is allowed (v0.6 §2). Recovering them would need a selection principle that is not written anywhere.

## 9. Local hidden variable, and not excluded by Bell (Problem, notes 12–13)

Note 12 says the hidden variable is the observer's own state, with "local" as in note 6. Note 13 says Bell's conclusion, which assumes spacetime locality, does not carry over to observer-space locality. No model is exhibited.

| Mapping of Bell's setup | Buys | Costs | v0.6 | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The hidden variable is the observer's state ($O^u$, or $(O^u,a)$ under (A)); settings and outcomes are features of germs and arms** | the claim of note 12 | the map of settings, outcomes and shared variable is open; so is the meaning of measurement independence when the setting is part of the state | **v0.6 §9** (Obs-local) | 12 |
| Chance reading: one history per observer, following a local stochastic rule | a simple process | this is what Bell's theorem constrains, so it does not escape by itself | v0.6 §7, §9 (defined, not adopted) | 12, 13 (tension) |
| Co-existence reading: all arms co-exist, with weights as a measure over histories | the only route v0.6 leaves open for the Bell Hope | Bell's factorisation premise has no stated analogue yet | **v0.6 §7** (adopted reading) | 13 |
| A shared variable at a spacetime source, with outcomes set locally in spacetime | standard | this is Bell's local hidden-variable model, which Bell excludes | no | **✗12**, ✗13 |

What an exhibiting model would need, from v0.6 §8:
1. two observers as vertices;
2. a way to relate them that does not smuggle in one world;
3. a correlation rule with setting dependence;
4. a named Bell premise shown not to hold in observer space.

None is exhibited.

## 10. Observer space and spacetime (Problem, note 14)

The weakest reading: observer space and spacetime are different sets with different structure. Results do not transfer between them without an argument.

| Relation | Buys | Costs | Bell | v0.6 | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Occurrence relation**: a lump occurs at a point of a world; many-to-many, via the ensembles $E_W$ | no map is assumed; each observer carries many worlds | the relation is not a function | spacetime separation of Bell's wings is not separation in $V$ | **v0.6 §2** | 14 |
| World-dependent projection of curves, $\gamma=\pi_W(\sigma)$ | geodesics become curves in observer space (Thm 3) | lifetimes depend on the world; the curvelet itself does not (Prop. 3.2) | as above | **v0.6 §3** | 14 |
| Observer space as a bundle over one given spacetime | concrete; standard | the structure is spacetime's, so results transfer by construction | Bell applies | no | **✗14** |
| Spacetime reconstructed from observer space | a possible long-run aim | no map is known | open | no | 14 |

The difference is concrete:
- The Fermi-slice topology (Thm 16) lives on observer space, not on any spacetime.
- Two events at spacelike separation in Minkowski space are the same oriented observer: the flat lump modulo isotropy is one point (v0.6 C9, C28).
- Two nearby events at different radii in Schwarzschild are different observers.

So nearness in one space says nothing about nearness in the other without an argument, which is exactly note 14's point. No map between them is claimed.
