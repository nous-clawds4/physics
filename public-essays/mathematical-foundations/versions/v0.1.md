# Mathematical Foundations

David Strayhorn

**Status.** v0.1. Strong opinions, weakly held; expected to change often.

*Statement of the Problem* (v0.1, cited "Problem, note N") states the program's aims and commits each mathematical word only to its weakest reading. This essay lays out, for each deferred word, the mathematical alternatives: what each buys, what it costs, and what it means for Bell. It marks which one the framework paper *Observer space and graph joins* v0.6 prose (`public-papers/observer-space-framework/versions/v0.6-prose.md`, cited "v0.6 §n") uses. Where the two essays conflict, the Problem essay wins. No law of motion, Born derivation, limit, or Bell-evading model is claimed.

Results cited: `papers/geodesic-lifts.md` (Thm 3, Prop. 3.2), `papers/patch-edge.md` (Thm 7), `papers/type-ii-adopted.md` (Prop. 13), `papers/obs-as-a-space.md` (Props. 14–15, Thm 16), `papers/neighborhood-uncountable.md` (Thm 17).

Legend: **✗n** = contradicts the weakest reading of note n; **bold v0.6 entry** = used in v0.6; HV = hidden variable; Cn = v0.6 Appendix A pin.

## 1. The observer and its precise definition (Problem, notes 1–2)

Weakest reading: a physical thing of a kind physics already describes (note 1), with an exact, checkable definition (note 2).

| Alternative | Buys | Costs | Bell | v0.6 | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Oriented metric germ**: infinite jet of a real-analytic Lorentzian metric at $p$ + future unit timelike $u$; equivalently the simply-connected patch $P(O)$ by local continuation, not a completed 4-manifold | exact, rigid (§5) | smooth generality lost; matter open | HV = jet + $u$ | **§2** | 1, 2 |
| Material system (brain, record) | familiar | needs matter and "system" defined | HV = matter state | no (§6) | 1 |
| Event + 4-velocity in a given spacetime | standard | observer space = bundle over one spacetime | HV in spacetime; Bell applies | no | **✗14** (§10) |
| Abstract agent / undefined primitive | none | no physical carrier, or no definition | undefined | no | **✗1**, **✗2** |

Finite jets and $C^\infty$ germs are treated in §5. Gauge beyond germ isotropy is left open (v0.6 §2).

## 3. Motion and the non-deterministic rule (Problem, notes 4–5)

Weakest reading: a history is an ordered succession of observers, with a rule on which are allowed (note 4); from some observer the rule allows more than one next observer, and it forbids others (note 5). Continuity, time parameter, and proper time per step are open.

| Alternative | Buys | Costs | Bell | v0.6 | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Continuous curves only (geodesic lifts, world switching) | analytic (Thm 3) | unique successor (Prop. 3.2, Thm 7) | one deterministic history | type-(i) baseline, **§4.1** | **✗5** if the whole rule |
| Digraph paths | counting | law of $R$ open (Prop. 13) | outcomes countable | part | 4, 5 |
| **Mixed**: proper-time segments joined by jumps that carry no $\tau$ | geodesic motion between decisions | not a $\tau$-curve; needs alternation + no-Zeno (§5.3) | branching at jumps | **§4.2 items 2, 7–8** | 4, 5 |
| Differential inclusion / stochastic equation | calculus | needs a manifold | local stochastic process | no | 4, 5 |
| $R=V\times V$ | trivial | forbids nothing | none | no | **✗5** |

Rule forms: $R\ne\emptyset$ as an existential postulate, not derivable (Prop. 13; v0.6 §4.2); a neighbourhood law, stateable (Thm 16) but continuum-valued (Thm 17); a germ readout such as the Kretschmann rule (v0.6 §5.4), uniform but unmotivated. No law of $R$ is adopted. Type (ii) is abandonable, but abandoning it leaves only the first row, which meets note 5 only if multiple next observers come from elsewhere (v0.6 §10).

## 4. Locality in observer space (Problem, note 6)

Weakest reading: allowed next observers and their weights depend only on the current observer; no nearness, no spacetime locality.

| Alternative | Buys | Costs | v0.6 | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **State-only**: rule is a function of the current state | exactly note 6 | state must hold all the rule reads | **§9** ("Obs-local") | 6 |
| Topological: neighbourhood targets, continuous paths | locality as continuity | needs topology (Thm 16); continua (Thm 17) | type (i) only | 6 + strengthening |
| Metric: bounded jumps | quantitative | no distance given; cf. (J2), §7 | no | 6 + strengthening |
| Differential: the rule reads jets of the path | ODE-like | needs a manifold | segments (geodesic equation) | 6 + strengthening |
| Spacetime locality | Bell's notion | not note 6 | no | not note 6 |

Bell: every notion but the last means "near" in $V$, not spacetime; a per-observer stochastic rule is still Bell-constrained (§9).

**Tension T1: jumps without proper time versus "a local process".**

v0.6's type-(ii) jumps carry no proper time and are discontinuous in oriented observer space (v0.6 §3, §4.2 item 2).
- Note 4: no conflict; proper time per step is open.
- Topological locality: conflict, since no continuous path in $V$ joins a jump's ends. But this is a strengthening of note 6, not its weakest reading.
- State-only locality: turns on what "the current observer" includes. Under arrival rule (A) the arm set depends on $(O^u,a)$, with $a$ recording arrival by segment or jump (v0.6 §4.2 item 7): 1 arm after a jump, at least 2 after a segment (C6).

The options stay open:
- **Enlarge the state.** Count $(O^u,a)$ as the current observer; note 6 holds. But $a$ is history data, not germ data (v0.6 §2), so either the observer exceeds Paper 1's oriented germ or "state" and "observer" come apart.
- **Drop (A).** v0.6's examined alternatives: a bound on consecutive jumps (law-like) or a ban on jumps onto vertices (kills the only worked instance). With no arrival rule, jump chains pile up at one accumulated time (C5).
- **Carry the tension.** Keep (A); note 6 holds only for the enlarged state.

## 5. Internal state as a complete representation (Problem, note 7)

Weakest reading: everything the observer takes to be true of the outside world is fixed by its own state. A unique whole universe and any observer-to-universe map are left open.

| Alternative | Buys | Costs | Bell | v0.6 | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Infinite jet, real-analytic** | fixes the whole patch $P(O)$ | smooth generality lost; one patch, not one universe | knowledge is a function of state | **§2** | 7 |
| Finite jet / $C^\infty$ germ | weaker regularity | surroundings unfixed (Borel's lemma; flat perturbations): represents only itself | same | no | 7, trivially |
| Whole-universe state (Cauchy data) | one universe | not local to the observer | HV global | no (§2) | 7, stronger |
| Records in matter | usual picture | needs matter and a model of correlation | outcomes = records | cartoon (§6) | 7 |

Here sits the infinite-jet, real-analytic reading. Analyticity makes "fixed by its own state" non-trivial: one jet determines the unique simply-connected patch, but not one whole universe, since many analytic worlds contain the same lump (hole-punching, quotients, non-unique maximal extensions; v0.6 §2), as note 7 leaves open.

## 6. Branch and measurement (Problem, note 8)

Weakest reading: a branch point is an observer with more than one allowed next observer, and a measurement is modelled as one.

| Alternative | Buys | Costs | Bell | v0.6 | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Arms = continue + jump targets** ($\lvert B_R\rvert=1+\lvert\mathrm{succ}_R\rvert$), finite | exact count | needs $R$; vertices on a chosen slice $V^\tau$ (§5.1), none on Minkowski/dS/ESU | outcomes = arms | **§4.2, §5.1** | 8 |

## 7. Classical probability without additional assumptions (Problem, note 9)

Weakest reading: ordinary probabilities over an observer's futures, from the space and allowed motions, not a separate probability postulate. The method, and whether any choice is needed, are open.

| Alternative | Buys | Costs | Bell | v0.6 | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Per-step equal weights (EPP1)**: $1/\lvert A\rvert$ per arm | no free numbers; product measure | a named postulate; unequal weights on co-existing histories | chance form: per-observer process | **§7**, (M1) §9 | 9 (T2) |
| Count distinct histories at a named cut | Paper 1's count and uniqueness thesis | cut is extra; weights cut-dependent; histories may be uncountable | outcome counting | (M2) §9, not chosen | 9 |
| **Coincidence**: both rules agree | no choice needed | only if degree products are constant on the cut (or balance in the limit) | same | criterion §7 (C13–C15) | 9 |
| Maximal-entropy walk | a count form | squared-eigenvector density: a circularity hazard | same | neighbour (§7) | 9, 10 |
| Weight by ensemble size (EPP2) | uses worlds | ensemble vote; departs from Paper 1 | none | foil (§7) | 9 (extra structure) |
| Free kernel, or $\lvert a\rvert^2$ inserted | fits data | a separate probability postulate | standard stochastic HV | no | **✗9**, ✗10 |

**Tension T2: per-step weights versus "no additional assumptions".**

EPP1 has no adjustable numbers, but v0.6 names it a postulate, and it differs from Paper 1's count. For a named finite cut $C$, let $D(v)$ be the product of out-degrees along the path to $v$. Per-step weighting gives $1/D(v)$; counting gives $1/\lvert C\rvert$. They agree on $C$ iff $D$ is constant on $C$ (v0.6 §7).
- They disagree on uneven trees: $1/6$ vs $1/4$ (C12).
- Agreement is not monotone in depth (C13).
- They can disagree at proper-time cuts even on generation-regular trees (C14).
- Counts converge to per-step weights in the limit iff siblings balance asymptotically, which does not require constant products at any cut (C15).

EPP1 cannot be tuned: with $m$ jump targets per decision the continue arm has weight $(1+m)^{-n}$ after $n$ decisions, so an $R$ showing geodesic motion needs sparse decisions or observationally null jumps (v0.6 §7). Which row satisfies note 9, if any, is not settled here.

## 8. Aims: Born rule, and recovery in an approximation (Problem, notes 10–11)

Aims only. No route, derivation, or limit is exhibited.

**Note 10.** A Born derivation would need at least a uniform, motivated rule for $R$ (v0.6 §5.4), weights fixed by structure (§7), a map from arms to outcomes, and no hand-drawn $\lvert a\rvert^2$ tree (v0.6 §8). Candidate routes: typicality over co-existing histories, or counting at a cut; the §7 squared-eigenvector hazard marks where circularity could enter.

**Note 11.** Candidate limits: deep-tree limits (§7), coarse-graining over many branch points, observationally null jumps ((J2), v0.6 §7). Einstein's equations are not assumed (any analytic Lorentzian metric is allowed, v0.6 §2); recovering them would need an unwritten selection principle.

## 9. Local hidden variable, and not excluded by Bell (Problem, notes 12–13)

Note 12: the HV is the observer's state, "local" as in note 6. Note 13: Bell's spacetime-locality conclusion does not carry over.

| Mapping of Bell's setup | Buys | Costs | v0.6 | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **HV = observer state** ($O^u$, or $(O^u,a)$ under (A)); settings and outcomes are features of germs and arms | note 12 as stated | settings/outcomes/shared-variable map open; measurement independence unclear when settings are state | **§9** | 12 |
| Chance reading: one history, a local stochastic rule | simple | exactly what Bell constrains | defined, not adopted (§7, §9) | 12, 13 (tension) |
| Co-existence reading: arms co-exist; weights a measure | v0.6's only route for the Bell Hope | no stated analogue of Bell's factorisation | **§7** (adopted) | 13 |
| Shared variable at a spacetime source; outcomes set locally in spacetime | standard | Bell's own model, excluded | no | **✗12**, ✗13 |

An exhibiting model would need at least (v0.6 §8): (1) two observers as vertices; (2) a relation between them that does not smuggle in one world; (3) a setting-dependent correlation rule; (4) a named Bell premise shown to fail in observer space. None is exhibited.

## 10. Observer space and spacetime (Problem, note 14)

Weakest reading: different sets with different structure; results do not transfer without argument.

| Relation | Buys | Costs | Bell | v0.6 | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Occurrence**: a lump occurs at points of many worlds ($E_W$) | no map assumed | many-to-many, not a function | wing separation ≠ separation in $V$ | **§2** | 14 |
| Projection of curves $\gamma=\pi_W(\sigma)$ | geodesics become curves in $V$ (Thm 3) | lifetimes world-dependent, curvelet not (Prop. 3.2) | same | **§3** | 14 |
| Bundle over one given spacetime | concrete | results transfer by construction | Bell applies | no | **✗14** |
| Spacetime reconstructed from observer space | long-run aim | no map known | open | no | 14 |

Concretely: the Fermi-slice topology (Thm 16) lives on observer space, not spacetime. Spacelike-separated events in Minkowski space are one oriented observer (the flat lump modulo isotropy is a point; C9, C28), while nearby events at different Schwarzschild radii are different observers. Nearness does not transfer either way without argument. No map is claimed.
