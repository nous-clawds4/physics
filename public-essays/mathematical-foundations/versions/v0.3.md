# Mathematical Foundations

David Strayhorn

**Status.** v0.3 (proposal; revises v0.2). Strong opinions, weakly held; expected to change often.

The companion essay, *Statement of the Problem*, now titled *The Observer as a Physical System: Intuitions and Ontology*, carries the intuitions; formalisation questions live here. It is cited "Problem, note N", with notes numbered as in its v0.1, and commits each mathematical word only to its weakest reading. One reading has changed: "local" (note 6) is intended as nearness (§4). For each deferred word, this essay lays out the alternatives (buys, costs, Bell), and marks which one the framework paper uses: *Observer space and graph joins*, v0.6 prose (`public-papers/observer-space-framework/versions/v0.6-prose.md`), cited "v0.6 §n". The Problem essay wins any conflict. No law of motion, Born derivation, limit or Bell-evading model is claimed.

Results (`papers/`): `geodesic-lifts.md` (Thm 3, Prop. 3.2), `patch-edge.md` (Thm 7), `type-ii-adopted.md` (Prop. 13), `obs-as-a-space.md` (Prop. 15, Thm 16), `neighborhood-uncountable.md` (Thm 17).

Legend: **✗n** = contradicts note n's weakest reading; **bold** v0.6 entry = used in v0.6 (a fact, not an endorsement); HV = hidden variable; Cn = v0.6 Appendix A pin.

Weakest reading: a physical thing of a kind physics already describes (note 1), with an exact, checkable definition (note 2).

Weakest reading: the set of all possible observers. No topology, distance, manifold structure, dimension or measure is committed to.

| Structure | Buys | Costs | v0.6 | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| Analytic curves (jet inverse limit) | curves (Thm 3) | quotient not known to be Hausdorff (Prop. 15) | **curves** | 3 |
| Fermi-slice topology (a manifold atlas on top: none known) | Hausdorff neighbourhoods (Thm 16) | ball minus curvelet uncountable (Thm 17) | unused | 3 |

Name collision: Gielen–Wise's "observer space" has 4-velocities as points; this program's has germs.

The tables above assume continuum mathematics. One further candidate drops that assumption, in the author's words: "abandon the mathematics of manifolds, continuity, analyticity... use some sort of a lattice mathematical foundation where continuity doesn't exist, or it does but it's completely different than what we think it is".

| Foundation | Buys | Costs | v0.6 | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Continuum**: manifolds, analytic jets, limits | GR's own language; curves (Thm 3), unique continuation (Prop. 3.2, Thm 7), jet rigidity (§5) | continua everywhere (Thm 17): counting at a cut needs added structure | **all** | 1–3 |
| Lattice or discrete: observers and their relations discrete; continuity absent, or present in a different form | countable steps and branches, so note 9's counting has something to count; "near" becomes adjacency, given with the space (§4) | reopens the object itself: §1's observer is an analytic jet, and jets, continuation, Thm 3, Prop. 3.2 and Thm 7 have no direct analogue; a regular lattice picks a preferred frame, breaking Lorentz invariance; random (causal-set) discreteness keeps it (Bombelli–Henson–Sorkin), but then each element of a sprinkled Minkowski space has infinitely many nearest neighbours; the continuum must come back in an approximation (note 11), not yet done for observers | no | 1–3, 5, 6, 9, 11 |

Discrete neighbours: causal sets (Bombelli–Lee–Meyer–Sorkin); finitary substitutes for continuous topology (Sorkin 1991); Regge calculus (Regge); causal dynamical triangulations (Ambjørn–Jurkiewicz–Loll); quantum graphity (Konopka–Markopoulou–Smolin); cellular automata ('t Hooft; Wolfram's hypergraph models). None takes an observer as its point.

## 3. Motion and the non-deterministic rule (Problem, notes 4–5)

Weakest reading: a history is an ordered succession of observers, with a rule on which are allowed (note 4). From a given observer the rule *may* allow more than one next observer, and it forbids others (note 5). The ✗5 flags read the essay's "without being fully deterministic" as "some observer has more than one allowed next observer"; that reading is open. Continuity, time parameter, proper time per step, and whether "next" presupposes discrete steps are open.

| Alternative | Buys | Costs | Bell | v0.6 | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Continuous curves only (geodesic lifts, world switching) | analytic (Thm 3) | unique successor (Prop. 3.2, Thm 7) | one history | type-(i) baseline, **§4.1** | **✗5** if the whole rule |
| **Mixed**: proper-time segments joined by jumps that carry no $\tau$ | geodesic motion between decisions | not a $\tau$-curve; needs alternation + no-Zeno (§5.3) | branching at jumps | **§4.2 items 2, 7–8** | 4, 5 |
| Digraph rule: $R\ne\emptyset$ (existential), neighbourhood law, or germ readout | countable steps | not derivable (Prop. 13); continua (Thm 17); readout unmotivated (§5.4) | countable outcomes | **§4.2** ($R\ne\emptyset$ only) | 4, 5 |
| Differential inclusion, stochastic or non-Lipschitz ODE (Norton's dome) | calculus | needs a manifold; analyticity bars the dome | stochastic process | no | 4, 5 |
| $R=V\times V$ | trivial | forbids nothing | none | no | **✗5** |

No law of $R$ is adopted. Type (ii) is abandonable. If it is abandoned, Paper 1's non-uniqueness is false under the singleton baseline (v0.6 §10).

## 4. Locality in observer space (Problem, note 6)

Weakest reading of note 6 as written: which next observers are allowed, and how weighted, depends only on the current observer; no nearness, nothing about spacetime.

**Intended reading: nearness.** "Local" means the observer does not teleport: it moves only to observers near where it is. Memorylessness (what is allowed depends only on the current observer) is a separate, optional property. The first row below is memorylessness; the next two are forms of nearness.

| Alternative | Buys | Costs | v0.6 | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Observer-only** (memoryless): allowed next observers and weights are a function of the current observer $O^u$ | note 6 as written | the observer must hold everything the rule reads | v0.6's Obs-local (§9) is defined on the state $(O^u,a)$, so it meets this row only under T1's "enlarge the state" option | 6 |
| Topological: neighbourhood targets, continuous paths | continuity | topology (Thm 16); continua (Thm 17) | type (i) only | 6 (nearness) |
| Metric (bounded jumps) or differential (rule reads path jets) | quantitative | no distance or manifold given | segments (differential) | 6 (nearness) |
| Spacetime locality | Bell's notion | not note 6 | no | not note 6 |

**Formalising "near".** Nearness needs note 3 to carry more than a bare set. Four ways to say it, in increasing commitment:

| "Near" as | Note 3 commits to | Costs | v0.6 | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (i) Adjacency: a neighbour relation (a graph on observers) | a set of edges; nothing quantitative | must be given independently of the rule: if "neighbour" means "joined by an allowed move", nearness says nothing | the digraph $(V,R)$ is a rule, not an adjacency | 3, 6 |
| (ii) Closest neighbour | a ranking of other observers from each point (a preorder), or a distance | ties need a rule, and tied closest observers are several allowed next observers; on a continuum with no isolated points a distance has no attained minimum, so "closest" exists only on a discrete space or among a pre-selected candidate set | none | 3, 6 |
| (iii) Topology: stay within a neighbourhood, or move along continuous paths | open sets on observer space | in the Fermi-slice topology every neighbourhood minus the curvelet is uncountable (Thm 16, 17), so "move to a nearby observer" gives no countable choice; open sets alone do not say how near | Fermi-slice unused; type (i) continuous | 3, 6 |
| (iv) Metric | a distance function | gauge beyond germ isotropy is open, so a distance must be built from gauge-invariant data or flagged as gauge-dependent; none is written | none | 3, 6 |

A metric gives a topology and a ranking; on a discrete space (§2) adjacency and ranking come free. Whether steps are continuous or discrete stays open (note 4). How allowed moves are weighted stays a separate choice (T2).

Realisation (a) is not a nearness relation. It admits an edge only if the target lies on a future geodesic curvelet from the source lump (v0.6 §4.2): a reachability condition, which confines edges to the source's analytic-continuation class (an equivalence, i.e. component, condition). Under (a), admissible targets lie at every radius in $(0,10)$ in the pinned instance (C4).

Bell: every notion but the last is about $V$, not spacetime. Under a mapping in which each wing's observer evolves by its own stochastic rule from a shared past state, a per-observer stochastic rule is what Bell constrains (§9).

**Tension T1: jumps without proper time versus "a local process".** v0.6's type-(ii) jumps carry no proper time and are discontinuous in oriented observer space (v0.6 §3, §4.2 item 2). Against note 4: no conflict (proper time per step is open). Against nearness in topological form: conflict, since no continuous path in $V$ joins a jump's ends (T3). Against memorylessness: it turns on what "the current observer" includes. Under arrival rule (A) the arm set depends on $(O^u,a)$, with $a$ recording arrival by segment or jump (v0.6 §4.2 item 7): 1 arm after a jump, at least 2 after a segment (C6).

Options, all open:
- **Enlarge the state.** Count $(O^u,a)$ as the current observer; memorylessness holds. But $a$ is history data, not germ data (v0.6 §2): the observer then exceeds Paper 1's oriented germ, or "state" and "observer" come apart.
- **Drop (A).** v0.6's examined alternatives: a bound on consecutive jumps (law-like), or a ban on jumps onto vertices (kills the worked instance). With no arrival rule, jump chains pile up at one accumulated time (C5).
- **Carry the tension.** Keep (A), and keep the observer as $O^u$. Memorylessness is then openly strained, since the rule also reads $a$.

**Tension T3: nearness versus jumps.** v0.6's histories jump. If every decision has $m$ outgoing edges, under EPP1 the number of jumps in $n$ decisions is Binomial$(n, m/(1+m))$ (v0.6 §7): at $m=2$, two decisions in three are jumps on average. Keeping nearness means one of:
- **Rule jumps out.** Abandon type (ii): the singleton baseline, one arm everywhere (v0.6 §4.1, §10). v0.6 has no other branch point: continuation forks are excluded (Prop. 3.2, Thm 7), and dying is not an arm, so $|B|\ge2$ needs type-(ii) edges (v0.6 §5.2). Note 5's "may allow more than one" then needs a new source.
- **Refine jumps into chains of small moves.** No such structure exists. It would have to say what the small moves are, what proper time they carry (T1), and why the discontinuity does not just reappear inside the chain.
- **Show jumps negligible.** v0.6's hooks: jump vertices sparse (J1), or jumps observationally null in a named topology on curvelets (J2). The worked rule fails both (v0.6 §7); its pure-geodesic weight after 10 decisions is $3^{-10}$ (C17, in the model without (a)). No rule is adopted, so this is open, not refuted.
- **Short jumps.** Under a topology or metric, "near" admits a discontinuous move whose target is close to its source; the worked rule's jump length $\varepsilon=c|K|^{-1/4}$ (v0.6 §5.4) shrinks with $c$. This meets "near" at the vertex but not "never jumps", and nearness at the vertex is not (J2), which asks for it along curvelets.

Keeping jumps as they are means the intuition states an aim the framework does not meet. If steps are discrete (note 4), every step is a move, and the question becomes whether a jump's target is adjacent. Nothing is resolved here.

## 5. Internal state as a complete representation (Problem, note 7)

Weakest reading: everything the observer takes to be true of the outside world is fixed by its own state. Neither a unique universe nor an observer-to-universe map is committed to.

| Alternative | Buys | Costs | v0.6 | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Infinite jet, real-analytic** | fixes the patch $P(O)$ | one patch, not one universe | **§2** | 7 |
| Finite jet / $C^\infty$ germ | weaker regularity | surroundings not fixed (Borel's lemma; flat perturbations) | no | 7, trivially |
| Whole-universe state (Cauchy data) | one universe | not local to the observer | no (§2) | 7, stronger |
| Records in matter (Barbour's "time capsules") | usual picture | needs matter, correlation model | cartoon (§6) | 7 |

This is v0.6's reading: one jet fixes the unique simply-connected patch, but not one universe, since many analytic worlds contain the same lump (v0.6 §2). The map from what the jet fixes to what the observer takes to be true is itself open (note 7).

## 7. Classical probability without additional assumptions (Problem, note 9)

Weakest reading: ordinary probabilities over an observer's futures, from the space and allowed motions, not a separate probability postulate. The method, and whether any choice is needed, are open.

| Alternative | Buys | Costs | Bell | v0.6 | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Per-step equal weights (EPP1): $1/\lvert A\rvert$ per arm | no free numbers | a named postulate; unequal weights on co-existing histories | per-observer process | **§7** (working postulate; (M1)/(M2) unchosen, §9) | 9 (T2) |
| Count at a named cut (variant: maximal-entropy walk) | Paper 1's count | cut is extra; cut-dependent; the walk's squared-eigenvector density is a circularity hazard | outcome counting | (M2) §9, unchosen | 9 (T2) |
| Weights narrowed by symmetry/causality conditions (Rideout–Sorkin) | axioms cut the choice to a family (couplings $t_n$), not one measure | no conditions stated on $(V,R)$ | open | precedent (§7) | 9 |
| Ensemble-size weight (EPP2) | uses worlds | ensemble vote | none | foil | 9 (extra structure) |
| Free kernel, algorithmic prior (Müller), or $\lvert a\rvert^2$ inserted | fits data | a separate probability postulate | standard stochastic HV | no | **✗9**, ✗10 |

A measure still needs a reading: co-existence (typicality), a measure over co-existing histories, adopted in v0.6 and the route v0.6 names for the Bell Hope (v0.6 §9; neighbours Dürr–Goldstein–Zanghì, Sebens–Carroll, Vaidman); or chance, one history followed, defined but not adopted (neighbour: many-minds, Albert–Loewer).

**Tension T2: per-step weights versus "no additional assumptions".**

EPP1 has no adjustable numbers, but v0.6 names it a postulate, which strains note 9. On a named cut $C$, with $D(v)$ the product of out-degrees above $v$, per-step weighting gives $1/D(v)$ and counting gives $1/\lvert C\rvert$. They agree iff $D$ is constant on $C$ (in the limit, iff siblings balance); agreement is not monotone in depth and can fail at proper-time cuts (v0.6 §7; C12–C15). Where they agree, no choice is needed.

The count is not free of additions either. Its named cut is structure beyond the space and the allowed motions (note 9). Its implied next-step weight, $\lvert C\cap\mathrm{desc}(a)\rvert/\lvert C\cap\mathrm{desc}(v)\rvert$, depends on the cut, and so on where the history started, unless the cut is anchored at the current observer. That strains note 6's "how they are weighted … depends only on the current observer", as EPP1's postulate strains note 9. Each option strains a note; neither is chosen. Which note governs weights is open.

EPP1 also cannot be tuned: with $m$ jump targets per decision the continue arm has weight $(1+m)^{-n}$ after $n$ decisions, so geodesic motion needs sparse decisions or observationally null jumps (v0.6 §7).

## 8. Aims: Born rule, and recovery in an approximation (Problem, notes 10–11)

These are aims only. No route, derivation or limit is exhibited.

**Note 10.** A derivation would need at least a uniform, motivated rule for $R$ (v0.6 §5.4), weights fixed by structure (§7), a map from arms to outcomes, and no hand-drawn $\lvert a\rvert^2$ tree (v0.6 §8).

**Note 11.** Candidate limits: deep trees (§7), coarse-graining over branch points, observationally null jumps. Einstein's equations are not assumed (v0.6 §2).

## 9. Local hidden variable, and not excluded by Bell (Problem, notes 12–13)

Note 12: the HV is the observer's own state; "local" as in note 6. Note 13: Bell's theorem assumes locality in spacetime; its conclusion does not carry over to locality in observer space. No model is exhibited.

| Mapping | Buys | Costs | v0.6 | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **HV = observer state $O^u$.** Under (A) the rule also reads the flag $a$; whether $a$ is part of "the observer's own state" is T1 (§4), not settled here | note 12 as stated | settings/outcomes map open | **§9** | 12 |
| Shared variable at a spacetime source, outcomes set locally in spacetime | standard | Bell's own model, excluded | no | **✗12**, ✗13 |

**Which Bell premise fails.**

| Premise declined | Known programme | Fits | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Locality, reread: Bell's premise is stated in spacetime; the model claims only observer-space locality (note 6) | phrase from the Problem essay ('local in observer-space, not in classical spacetime'); whether that selects this row is open | either reading, once a mapping is given (note 12) | if the other premises hold under the mapping, Bell's factorisation fails in spacetime, so the model is Bell-nonlocal in spacetime and local in $V$, which Bell does not exclude; no signalling and no preferred frame are further costs to check (relativistic GRW incurs neither: Tumulka), not a way out of the parameter/outcome row; no mapping given |
| Single outcomes | Everettian locality (Deutsch–Hayden; Brown–Timpson; Wallace) | co-existence reading, if co-existing arms count as outcomes (a mapping choice) | the known Everett move; faces Local Friendliness (Bong et al.) |
| Measurement independence | superdeterminism (Hossenfelder–Palmer) | settings as state | conspiracy objection |
| Parameter or outcome independence (Jarrett's decomposition; his terms "locality" and "completeness") | Bohm; GRW | chance reading: what Bell constrains, under the per-wing mapping of §4 | nonlocal in spacetime in Bell's sense; parameter-independence failure (Bohm) needs a preferred foliation, outcome-independence failure alone need not signal or pick a frame (Tumulka) |
| No retrocausation | Wharton–Argaman | none | a time-symmetric rule |

Which premise fails depends on how Bell's settings, outcomes and shared variable map onto observer space, and note 12 leaves that open. This essay lists the rows and picks none; neither does v0.6.

An exhibiting model would need at least (v0.6 §8): (1) two observers as vertices; (2) an embedding of both in one world with spacelike-separated anchors, which selects an ensemble member and so smuggles in one world, a cost v0.6 names; (3) a setting-dependent correlation rule; (4) a named Bell premise. None is exhibited.

## 10. Observer space and spacetime (Problem, note 14)

Weakest reading: different sets with different structure; results do not transfer without argument. The map is open.

| Relation | Buys | Costs | v0.6 | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Occurrence**: a lump occurs at points of many worlds ($E_W$) | no map assumed | many-to-many | **§2** | 14 |
| Projection $\gamma=\pi_W(\sigma)$ | curves in $V$ (Thm 3) | lifetimes world-dependent (Prop. 3.2) | **§3** | 14 |
| Bundle over one given spacetime | concrete | misses other worlds' observers; the projection supplies the transfer argument, so the Bell burden moves to the mapping (note 12) | no | **✗3**; note 14 allowed |

Bell: spacetime separation of the wings is not separation in $V$. Concretely, the Fermi-slice topology (Thm 16) lives on observer space; spacelike-separated Minkowski events are one oriented observer (C9, C28); nearby events at different Schwarzschild radii are different observers.

## 11. Nearest existing programmes

We know of no programme that combines the Problem essay's aims with v0.6's choices: an analytic germ as observer, a non-deterministic rule on the space of observers, classical probability over their futures, and Bell locality read in that space. Neighbours: next-state chances from the current observer state alone (Müller, algorithmic, no geometry); observer space as a geometry (Gielen–Wise; deterministic, no branching); stochastic observer states (many-minds, Albert–Loewer); a structurally narrowed family of measures on discrete growth (Rideout–Sorkin); counting or typicality over branches (Strayhorn 2008; Sebens–Carroll; Vaidman; Dürr–Goldstein–Zanghì); locality without single outcomes (Deutsch–Hayden; Brown–Timpson). The combination has no established name.
