## Verdict in one breath

v0.4 writes the composition #105 asked for, nearly verbatim: histories alternate curvelet segments and jumps, $R$ contains no type-(i) pairs, $B_R(O^u)=\{[\gamma_{O^u}]\}\cup\mathrm{succ}_R(O^u)$, and $B_R$ is a property of the pair. Given a directed graph on oriented lumps, that is a definite set. The HOLE of #105 is therefore cleared as a definition. As a problem it is still relocated, and this round it has moved into the two devices v0.4 introduced to complete the relocation. The Option F join clause (truncated jet and orientation matched through finite $k$) does no work for the edges that produce $|B|\ge 2$: at $k=1$ it is vacuous between any two lumps, at $k\ge 2$ it forbids every curvature-changing jump including the in-house toy's own edge, and the singleton under CWS never depended on the order of matching in the first place. The $V^*$ rule, applied to the ensemble as §2 defines it, marks every proper time on every curvelet, so hard-gate clause (2) fails identically and the toy's discrete vertex set is a property of a hand-picked two-member ensemble that includes a world the in-house corpus has already rejected. Two further items are new: per-vertex equal weight is not Paper 1's count of distinct evolutions, so "typicality for consistency with Paper 1" names a departure as consistency; and the new public gate ("a stated rule, not a hand list") is passed by an arbitrary but fully lock-side rule, so it is not yet a bar. None of this is fatal. The consistent residue is a discrete graph on oriented lumps with geodesic-labelled edges and out-star arms, with costs named honestly, and nothing claimed downstream depends on the two devices. Not PASS, not FAIL.

## 1. Status of the relocated HOLE

### 1.1 What v0.4 did right

The composition of #105 §2 is written as clauses (1)–(4) of §4.2, with the oriented type-(i)/(ii) split in §3 that fixes the bare-lump misclassification and makes bare orientation jumps type (ii). The hard gate has both clauses (§5.3), with the $\tau$-slice named as a further extra. The exit gate rejects a hand-listed digraph (§5.4, firewall). §4.3 names the singleton-or-continuum dilemma as living in the law of $R$ and softens Prop. 13 to "kinematics does not single out a dynamics," dropping the stale "waits on $\mathrm{Obs}$ as a space." No law of $R$ or $E^*$ is smuggled anywhere; nothing in the draft defines $\mathrm{succ}_R$ for any germ.

### 1.2 Where the problem now lives

- **The law of $R$.** Named honestly (§4.3). Unchanged.
- **The join clause.** Introduced as the mechanism of escape ("Infinite-order / $C^\infty$ matching at joins is rejected: that was the HOLE under CWS"). It is not the mechanism, and it constrains nothing that matters (§2.1 below).
- **The vertex rule.** Introduced to supply the $\tau$-slice. Under §2's own ensemble it supplies a continuum (§2.2). The draft presents this as a contingency ("if the ensemble or the lifetimes are wild, continuum risks return"); it is a two-line theorem.
- **The gate.** Moved from "explicit $R$" to "stated rule." Passed by an arbitrary uniform rule built from the draft's own ingredients (§3.2).

### 1.3 Not fatal

Option E, the residue once the join clause is set aside, is consistent: analytic-geodesic pieces on open edges, discontinuous jumps between lumps, out-star arms, first-edge equivalence. No claimed result uses the join clause or the toy. The Schwarzschild rule of §3.2 shows discrete branching is achievable in form from germ data alone, so the dilemma of §4.3 is a motivation problem, not a theorem against examples. The honest content of the paper is a negative theorem (the CWS singleton, still unproved in the text) plus a schema with named costs. It should be written and titled as that.

## 2. HOLD — the two devices introduced to complete the relocation

### 2.1 The Option F join clause does no work (§4.2; title; §1; §5.4)

The clause: "At a vertex $O^{u*}$, matching requires the truncated metric jet $j^kg$ and the orientation $u$ through a fixed finite order $k\ge 1$, together with incidence in the discrete graph. Infinite-order / $C^\infty$ matching at joins is rejected: that was the HOLE under CWS."

Two cases. *Pieces that share the oriented germ.* The outgoing piece is an analytic-geodesic lift from $O^{u*}$, hence the unique curvelet, for every $k$ (in-house Prop. 3.2). The singleton of #92 never used $C^\infty$: its Step 1 uses only the shared oriented germ and geodesic pieces, and its Step 2 identifies all continuations at $0$ under either equivalence. Finite $k$ reopens nothing. The sentence "that was the HOLE under CWS" misattributes the HOLE to the order of matching. *Pieces at a type-(ii) jump.* The outgoing piece starts at a different lump. Jets modulo pointed diffeomorphism are the lump (§2), and in Riemann normal coordinates adapted to $u$ every Lorentzian germ has the same 1-jet ($\eta$, vanishing first derivatives) with $u=\partial_0$; along a geodesic piece $\nabla_uu=0$, so $u$ has no derivatives to match. At $k=1$ the clause holds between any two lumps: vacuous. At $k\ge 2$ the 2-jet is the Riemann tensor at the point, so the clause requires equal curvature: it forbids the toy's Minkowski-to-FLRW edge and every curvature-changing jump, which are the only jumps a measurement picture could want. There is also no canonical identification of tangent spaces between distinct lumps; the in-house "germ isometry / isotropy" identification exists only for the same lump. The toy itself concedes that matching is "impossible across distinct germs anyway."

So the escape from the CWS singleton is the discontinuous jump plus graph incidence, i.e. Option E, which the in-house menu note called "too thin" and paired with C to avoid. C is inert or prohibitive. "Option F joins" in the title and §1 overclaims. Fix: say the truncated-jet condition is automatic on the continue arm and empty between distinct lumps; state that the HOLE escape is the jump plus out-star equivalence; rename the join reading ("graph joins on analytic-geodesic pieces"); delete "that was the HOLE under CWS." If the clause is kept to exclude bare $u$-jumps at a fixed lump, say so and reconcile with §3, which already makes those type (ii).

### 2.2 $V^*$ by extend-versus-not marks every proper time under §2's ensemble (§5.1; §2; §5.3; §10)

§5.1: "mark candidate vertex times $\tau_*$ at which some members of the hitchhiking ensemble extend analytically through $\tau_*$ and some die." §2: $E_W(O)$ is "the set of pointed worlds compatible with the germ," $M$ "a connected real-analytic Lorentzian 4-manifold," with no inextendibility, completeness or isometry clause. Take any member $(M,p)$ whose geodesic $\sigma$ has lifetime $\tau_M$ and any $\tau_0\in(0,\tau_M)$. $M'=M\setminus\{\sigma(\tau_0)\}$ is connected, analytic, Lorentzian, carries the same germ at $p$, has a lift agreeing with $\gamma$ near $0$, and its geodesic dies at exactly $\tau_0$. Distinct $\tau_0$ give pairwise non-isometric pointed worlds (a pointed isometry fixing $(p,u)$ preserves $\sigma$ and its lifetime), so this survives the in-house quotient too. Hence $T_{\mathrm{die}}\supseteq(0,\tau_M)$ for every germ, $V^*$ is the whole curvelet, clause (2) fails on every history, and the rule contributes no discreteness. Requiring inextendible worlds excludes the toy's cut world but not the continuum: conical flat spacetimes (a cosmic-string deficit angle) are inextendible, contain the flat germ everywhere, and an observer at distance $r_0$ from the axis moving inward dies at $r_0/\sinh\eta$, with $r_0$ an isometry invariant. With $P(O)$ alone, nothing dies and $V^*$ is empty.

Three consequences. "Discreteness of $V^*$ along $\gamma$ inherits discreteness of death times; if the ensemble or the lifetimes are wild, continuum risks return" states as a risk what is a theorem under the paper's own §2. "Better motivated than an arbitrary grain of all of $\mathrm{Obs}$" is not earned: discrete $V^*$ is exactly the choice of a finite or discrete sub-ensemble, a grain on $E_W$ rather than on $\mathrm{Obs}$, and the $\tau$-slice of §5.3 under another name. And §2's clause "not a vote of global topology on when evolutions split" is contradicted by §5.1, which lets which worlds die when determine the proper times at which a split may occur; the in-house standard the draft itself invokes in §8 (Thm 23(2a): using an extra $W$ from $E(O)$ to timestamp a type-(ii) event is E-smuggling) applies to $V^*$ verbatim. Paper 1 §4's identity claim survives; the timing clause does not. Fix: state the theorem in §5.1 and name the sub-ensemble as the extra; import the in-house ensemble definitions into §2 (this is the third round for that item, and it is now load-bearing) and say whether inextendible worlds are required; reword the §2 clause to Paper 1's identity claim and add a §10 row for the timing vote, or move $V^*$ onto lock-side data (the §5.3 candidate) and keep §2 as written.

### 2.3 The toy is hand-built in three places, not one (§5.4; in-house `B-ge2-minimal-toy.md`)

§5.4 says the toy "exhibits $|B(O^{u*})|=2$ under $V^*$ extend-versus-not, Option F, and the $E^*$ lean ... existence-in-form, including a hand-named type-(ii) out." The hand-naming is not only the out. (i) The ensemble $\{W_{\mathrm{M}},W_{\mathrm{cut}}\}$ is a chosen two-member subset; under §2's ensemble the Minkowski death set is the whole half-line (§2.2), so no vertex at $\tau=1$ is singled out, and the toy's "$T_{\mathrm{die}}=\{1\}$, discrete" is false for the Minkowski germ under every reading of the ensemble the draft could adopt. (ii) $W_{\mathrm{cut}}$, "Minkowski cut off so $\sigma$ is future-inextendible at $\tau=1$," is the "restricted Minkowski" that `patch-edge.md` Thm 6 lists under "Not an example" and its report line under "Killed" ("contradicts maximality"); the toy cites Thm 6 in the same sentence. (iii) The flat curvelet is a single point of $\mathrm{Obs}$: every point of Minkowski carries the same germ and $u$ is unique modulo boosts (in-house `obs-as-a-set.md` §4 says so; #105 §3.7 said so). By §5.1's own "the same germ is the same vertex," the toy's vertex $\gamma(1)$ is the root lump. A type-(ii) out at the flat lump is then available at every $\tau\ge 0$, the branch points along the toy history are all of $[0,\infty)$, and clause (2) fails in form. The extend-versus-not mark is a function of history time, not of the vertex, and descends to a graph on $\mathrm{Obs}$ only where $\gamma$ is locally injective. Fix: in §5.4 say the toy is hand-built in ensemble, vertex time and out, and exhibits $|B|=2$ as a digraph fact only; in §5.1 add the local-injectivity presupposition and say that on homogeneous or periodic curvelets vertices must be (lump, history-time) pairs, a history-dependent extra; correct or retire the in-house toy note.

### 2.4 Vertex set, attachment, equivalence (§4.2; §5.1; §5.3)

§4.2 mixes two graphs. Clause (1) makes the vertex set flow-closed, a continuum for any non-homogeneous germ; §5.1 defines a discrete $V^*$; the firewall calls $V^*$ "the vertex set." No sentence says that type-(ii) edges attach only at $V^*$ (there is no "$R\subseteq V^*\times V$"). Without it, $\mathrm{succ}_R(\gamma(\tau))$ may be non-empty on a continuum of $\tau$ even if $V^*$ were discrete, and $V^*$ contributes nothing to clause (2); with it, clause (2) along a segment *is* discreteness of $V^*$ on that segment, so "a further extra ... distinct from" $V^*$ is the same extra. §5.3's critical-point candidate is then a competing definition of $V^*$, and the draft does not say whether vertices are one, the other, or their union. Two numbers disagree: "Arms are the graph out-star: distinct outgoing type-(ii) edges" gives $|\mathrm{succ}_R|$; clause (4) gives $1+|\mathrm{succ}_R|$. "First-edge equivalence" and $\mathrm{succ}_R$ are never defined, and "the type-(i) term is exactly one class by the CWS singleton" is a non sequitur under any first-edge quotient (all histories opening with a segment are one class by fiat; the singleton is needed only for "at most one," and at a vertex where no member extends the term is empty). The toy lists the analytic continuation $e_{\mathrm{ext}}$ as an element of $E^*$, a type-(i) pair in the edge set, which clause (3) forbids. Strict alternation in clause (2) forbids two consecutive jumps, so at a jump target the available set is not the $B_R$ of clause (4). Fix: $V:=$ flow-closure of $V^*\cup\mathrm{tgt}(R)$; $R\subseteq V^*\times V$; at $O^u\notin V^*$, $B_R=\{[\gamma_{O^u}]\}$; define first-edge equivalence in one sentence (both histories open with a segment along $\gamma_{O^u}$, or with the same edge); write "at most one class"; state that $V^*$ is the $\tau$-slice and that the critical-point rule is an alternative definition of it; allow consecutive jumps or say why not.

## 3. HOLD — measure and gate

### 3.1 Per-vertex $1/|B_R|$ is not Paper 1's measure (§7; §10)

§7: "assign equal weight $1/|B_R(O^u)|$ to each discrete arm" at each branch point, and "This draft prefers the typicality reading for consistency with Paper 1." Paper 1's measure is uniform counting over distinct evolutions ("each distinct evolution counts as one"; "the normalized counting measure"). Composed along a history, per-vertex $1/|B_R|$ is the 2005 product rule. The two differ on the smallest uneven tree: root with arms $A,B$; $A$ with three sub-arms, $B$ with one. Paper 1 at depth 2: four evolutions, each $1/4$, so the $A$-prefix has weight $3/4$. Per-vertex: $1/2$ at the root, $1/3$ under $A$, so the $A$-prefix has weight $1/2$. They agree only on generation-regular trees, and never in general with fusion. The author's own notes say so: `fusion-and-path-counting.md` §4 ("on an uneven tree it disagrees with path-count"), `circularity.md` §2(1) ("the rule $1/N$, which is not path-count except on special graphs"). The typicality *reading* (all arms realised) is Paper-1-consistent; the *rule* is a labelled departure, and §7 labels it consistency. The choice matters: the product rule is projectively consistent and extends to completed histories; the count is a slice measure needing a depth cut. Fix: state both rules, give the four-leaf tree, choose one and label it; if the per-vertex rule, add a §10 row "measure: departs from Paper 1's count"; if the count, EPP1 is not a per-vertex weight at all.

### 3.2 The stated-law gate is passed by an arbitrary lock-side rule (§5.4; §5.3; §4.3)

The gate: "An $R$ or $E^*$ given by a stated rule on lumps (a function of $O^u$ and at most named extra structure declared as such), applied to a computable germ, with arm count a consequence of the rule and with branch points discrete along every history. A hand-listed digraph does not qualify." Take a rule built only from ingredients the draft itself names. Vertices: the isolated critical points along $\gamma$ of the Kretschmann scalar $K$ (the §5.3 candidate). Edges at such a vertex: $\langle\nabla K,u\rangle=dK/d\tau=0$ there, so $\nabla K\in u^\perp$; set $\hat n=\nabla K/|\nabla K|$ when non-zero, $\varepsilon=c\,|K|^{-1/4}$ with a named constant $c$, and $\mathrm{succ}_R(O^u)=\{$the lumps at $\exp_p(\pm\varepsilon\hat n)$ in $P(O)$, with $u$ parallel-transported$\}$; empty elsewhere. Apply it to a bound timelike geodesic in Schwarzschild ($M=1$, periastron $10$, apastron $20$): $K=48/r^6$, $dK/d\tau=K'(r)\dot r$ with $K'\ne 0$, so the critical points are exactly the turning points, periodic in $\tau$ and finite on every compact interval; there $\dot r=0$, $\nabla K\propto\partial_r$ is spacelike and in $u^\perp$; $|B_R|=3$ at every turning point. Every clause of the gate is met, every ingredient is a function of $(j_\infty,u)$ plus $c$, and nothing is motivated. So "stated rule" does not discriminate between a hand list and "any rule will do." Two repairs. Add uniformity (one rule in germ invariants applied at every vertex, no case-splitting on named germs; the in-house `uniform-law-of-r.md` names it as a separate extra), since a finite case list is a function of $O^u$ and the toy becomes "stated" by one line. Then say what would count as motivation, with a named menu (a symmetry or naturality principle, a variational rule, an empirical constraint), else the relocation ends at a choice among continuum-many admissible rules, Bertrand's problem one level up. §4.3 already concedes "kinematics does not single out a dynamics"; the gate should say the same. Also say what "weights forced" would mean, given that nothing in the draft could force a weight; Rideout–Sorkin classical sequential growth is the one worked precedent for weights forced by axioms, and its measure lives on completed causets, so it belongs beside the typicality reading too, not only under "chance."

### 3.3 Countably infinite stars; no exhaustion (§5.3 clause (1); §7)

Clause (1) admits "discrete (finite or countable)" stars; §7 then assigns $1/|B_R|$ per arm. For $|B_R|=\aleph_0$ that is weight zero per arm. Paper 1 §6 says as much and handles it by ratios along an exhaustion, which is chosen structure. The only exhaustion language in v0.4 is the product-ansatz sentence about EPP2's ensemble ratios; none is named for EPP1's arms, and none along histories with infinitely many branch points, which #105 §3.1 asked for. Fix: restrict clause (1) to finite stars where EPP1 acts, or state EPP1 as counting measure with ratios on finite sub-collections named by an exhaustion declared as extra, for arms and along histories.

### 3.4 Spacetime-realisation is not a neutral open choice (§4.2; §2; §5.4)

"Either every type-(ii) edge is realised by a timelike geodesic in some world ... or 'across-worlds' is a label without realisation. ... neither is locked here." By §2's own rigidity clause, a connected analytic world containing the Minkowski germ is flat everywhere, so no world realises a jump from the flat germ to the FLRW comoving germ: option (a) confines $R$ to pairs occurring in one analytic continuation of the germ (in-patch support up to monodromy, a continuum by in-house Thm 27) and forbids the only edge the paper cites. The toy has silently taken option (b). §2's compatibility relation is, by the same rigidity, exactly the option-(a) relation, and the paper does not connect the two. Fix: say so in §4.2; either use the compatibility relation as the option-(a) relation or delete it (it is used nowhere else and is still symmetric for want of a time orientation).
