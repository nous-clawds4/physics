## 4. Ruling: (M1)/(M2) symmetry. Balanced; no tilt.

The two §9 lines now read:
- **(M1):** "Per-vertex EPP1, the working postulate here. It needs no cut: its weights are functions of the current state (Problem note 6). Its costs: …" There are four costs.
- **(M2):** "Return to Paper 1's count of distinct evolutions at a named cut. It would carry the uniqueness thesis at that cut. Its costs: …" There are five costs, and the line closes with "(M2) is Paper 1's own escape clause, 'countable at a grain'."

**The test.** Does the (M2) line carry a comparable neutral description, or only costs? It carries both.
- Each line opens with what the option *is*: "Per-vertex EPP1" and "Return to Paper 1's count … at a named cut".
- Each line then gives one feature that a reader may count in its favour: "needs no cut … (note 6)" and "would carry the uniqueness thesis at that cut".
- Each line then lists its costs.

(M2) in fact has a second descriptive clause (the escape clause). The modal difference, "needs" against "would carry", tracks the status of the working postulate against the alternative, which both lines already state. It is not a lean. The calls table mirrors this: "(M1): no cut; weights are functions of the current state" against "(M2): Paper 1's thesis at a cut; equal weight to distinct evolutions". The following sentence, "The countable case sharpens a cost of (M1) and leaves a cost of (M2) in place. Neither line gains from it", is neutral.

**One precision point (optional, N3).** "It needs no cut" sits a few lines above §9's own sentence "EPP1 is the count at the depth-1 cut anchored at the current state". Read literally, the two disagree. The fix is one word, and it changes no balance: "It needs no **named** cut: it is the count at the depth-1 cut anchored at the current state, so its weights are functions of that state (Problem note 6)." The table cell would then read "(M1): no named cut; weights are functions of the current state."

## 5. Ruling: T3 neutrality. Neutral; no tilt.

The §9 paragraph "How the (a) transient bears on T3" gives the following:
- the transient: finitely many jumps, one at $c=0.1$, 13 or 14 at $c=0.01$;
- its bearing as "a data point for the 'show jumps negligible' route through a late-window (J1)";
- three counterweights in the next sentence: each jump is $O(1)$ in the orbit (it fails (J2)), the early-window share is 1/2, and each jump is discontinuous in oriented observer space;
- the summary "the transient reduces how often the observer jumps without making any jump near";
- the decision handed to *Mathematical Foundations* and the open calls;
- the scope "one unadopted example at two values of $c$".

The paragraph does not frame the transient as evidence that jumps are negligible, and it does not frame it as settling T3. The T3 table row carries both the as-stated and the (a) facts. The following paragraph, "Nearness versus jumps, carried as open", records the essay's intuition and jump dominance under the rule as stated, and reconciles neither. The phrases "13 or 14" (a.s.) and "within 14 jumps" (§4.2) are consistent with each other, and the transient numbers match C33 (§2).

Optional N4: the heading's self-label "(stated neutrally, not resolved)" asserts neutrality rather than showing it. "(not resolved)" alone suffices.
