## 1. Summary

**What the paper does.** It fixes the observer as an oriented analytic metric germ $O^u$ and shows that geodesic hitchhiking, even with world switching, gives one continuation (the CWS singleton, §4.1). It then postulates discontinuous jumps, a relation $R$ between oriented germs, as an abandonable "pad" (the Option E residue). It sets out the conditions any such $R$ must meet: a lock-side slice for branch times, strict alternation with an arrival flag, a per-history no-Zeno clause, and a hard gate and a public gate. It puts an equal-split-per-arm measure (EPP1) on the resulting histories as a named working postulate. It compares EPP1 exactly with Paper 1's count, which agree at a cut iff the ancestor-degree product is constant there. It exhibits one stated, uniform but unmotivated rule, the Kretschmann rule on a bound Schwarzschild orbit, and analyses it with and without the realisation condition (a). Everything else is honestly marked open, a Hope, or firewalled.

**What holds up.**
- **The numbers.** Every pinned number I could recompute reproduces from scratch (§2.1). That includes the full C33 census and its exact jump-count law, every Schwarzschild target and orbit, the C8, C15 and C36 values, and the cosmological and plane-wave pins. The sampled figures are statistically consistent with large independent samples.
- **The sources.** Every quotation from Paper 1, the companion essays and the author's manuscripts is verbatim and on the page given. The essays' Status lines are byte-exact. The B.1 pointers resolve.
- **The status discipline.** It is careful and mostly clean. No Hope or Open item is stated as a result, and the firewalls hold.

**Why HOLD.** Eight text errors survive adversarial verification (§2.2), and each is fixable in a few words:
- four mathematical statements are false as worded (R1–R4);
- one pin's description is false (R5);
- one conditional cost in the Bell row reads as a property of the model (R6);
- §4.2 and §4.3 contradict each other about what locked data determine (R7);
- the SHPMP reference misstates what the paper cites it for and omits the referee reports the text relies on (R8).

None of them changes a pin, a number, the (M1)/(M2) balance or any status category. With R1–R8 applied, the verdict is **PASS**.

**For a newcomer** the paper is hard going (§3). It has no abstract and no summary of results. It opens with 606 words before §1: 244 of process material, then a 362-word Firewall that uses terms defined later. Its worked example is used from §1 onwards but defined only in §5.4. Several symbols are overloaded, including $R$ for both the relation and the Ricci scalar. §5 gives a prioritised list for v0.9.

### 2.3 Suggested findings on correctness and precision

**S1. The Kretschmann rule is not defined at every vertex, and the paper does not say so** (§5.4; also the Firewall's "stated and uniform" and §5.4's "one rule … at every vertex"). This is undisclosed, not false.
- **$\nabla K=0$.** At a vertex where $\nabla K=0$, $\hat n=\nabla K/\lvert\nabla K\rvert$ is $0/0$. In every FLRW lump this happens at every $K$-vertex, C8's included. There $K=K(t)$, so $u\cdot\nabla K=K'(t)u^t=0$ forces $\nabla K=0$.
- **The bifurcation 2-sphere.** It also happens at every vertex on the Schwarzschild bifurcation 2-sphere ($\nabla r=0$), which is in the instance's own continuation class.
- **$K=0$.** Where $K=0$, $\varepsilon=c\lvert K\rvert^{-1/4}$ is undefined. Such vertices exist: on Kerr with $a=0.9$, at $r=2.908846$, $\theta=\pi/6$, a static observer has an isolated critical point of $K$ with $K=0$ ($d^2K/d\tau^2=-0.00294$; checked twice independently).
- **Why it is not a false statement.** Where $\hat n$ or $\varepsilon$ does not exist, the construction gives no target, and no instance figure depends on the gap. So the fix is a disclosure that decides nothing and adds no rule.

## 3. Clarity and structure

### 3.1 Can a newcomer follow the argument?

**Partly.** Someone fluent in GR, differential geometry and probability can rebuild the core from a careful second read:
- the observer is an oriented analytic germ;
- hitchhiking cannot branch (§4.1);
- branching needs a postulated relation $R$ of jumps, with branch times on a lock-side slice;
- histories alternate segments and jumps, so the process is Markov only on (germ, arrival flag);
- EPP1 is an equal-split measure that agrees with Paper 1's count at a cut iff $D$ is constant there;
- one unmotivated uniform rule shows the schema has a model, and on the instance its (a)-restriction's branching dies out.

The mathematics is mostly elementary once found. The status discipline is unusually careful. But the paper makes the reader work much harder than it needs to, in five ways.

1. **No front door.** There is no abstract, no statement of the question, no motivation and no summary of results. Paper 1 has an Abstract and an Introduction; this paper has neither. Of the 606 words before §1, the first 244 are process material: PR numbers, "the Lead's rulings", P1–P12, the "relocated HOLE (from #105)" block and file pointers. Then comes a 362-word Firewall that uses about twenty terms defined later or never: HOLE, Option E residue, lock-side, grain, bar in force, motivated gate, the Kretschmann rule, realisation (a), the instance, the schema, C33, EPP1/EPP2, CWS, MERW, (M2), (J1)/(J2), extend-versus-not, named extra.
2. **The worked example is used long before it is defined.** The Kretschmann rule and "the instance" appear in the header, the Firewall, §1 and throughout §4.2 (classes, chains, (a) edges, the (a)-restricted model). They are defined only in §5.4. Their results are spread over §4.2, §5.1, §5.3, §5.4, §7, §9, §10 and more than a dozen Appendix A rows.
3. **Sections run against their dependencies.** §4.2, the core (about 1,500 words), uses the slice $V^\tau$ (§5.1), the hard gate (§5.3), the Kretschmann rule (§5.4), EPP1 (§7) and the readings (§7). §4.3 uses the co-existence reading of §7. §3 cites the chance reading and the product-ansatz Hope (§7).
4. **Symbols are overloaded in ways that cause real misreadings.**
   - $R$ is both the transition relation and the Ricci scalar (§5.1).
   - $D(v)$ (ancestor product) and $D_n(w)$ (descendant count) sit in adjacent sentences of §7.
   - $s$ is both the slice scalar and proper radial distance (§5.4).
   - $c$ is both the rule constant and the speed of light ("$G=c=1$").
   - $W$ is both a world and the Bel–Robinson superenergy.
   - $u$ is both the 4-velocity and the null coordinate of C22, and $k$ there is undefined.
   - $a$, (a), (A) and the essay label "(a) = T3" are four different things.
   - (i)/(ii) label both motion types and Zeno readings.
5. **Labels are used but never introduced.** These include "option (A)", "decision", "vertex" (any element of $V$, or a point of $V^\tau$), "the schema" and "model", "named extra", "lock" and "locked", and "grain". The status vocabulary is also never explained as a system: working Postulate, named postulate, named extra, named constraint (not a law), Open, Hope, foil, firewall, model and witness.

None of this is an error of substance. Together, it means the paper reads as a record of a review process rather than as a paper. §5 below proposes a reorganisation that keeps every claim, pin and firewall.
