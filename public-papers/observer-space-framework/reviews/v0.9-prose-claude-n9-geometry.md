## What C37's thresholds are, and what "hold" means

- **The rule.** The rule (§7) jumps from a turning point at radius $r$ by proper distance $\varepsilon=cK^{-1/4}=c\,r^{3/2}/48^{1/4}$ ($M=1$) along the $t=$const radial direction.
- **What "holds" means.** The inward path meets the bifurcation 2-sphere iff $\varepsilon\ge \ell(r)-\ell(2)$, where
  $$\ell(r)-\ell(2)=\sqrt{r(r-2)}+2\ln\frac{\sqrt r+\sqrt{r-2}}{\sqrt2}=\sqrt{r(r-2)}+2\,\mathrm{arsinh}\sqrt{(r-2)/2}.$$
  This is exactly the branch `d<0` of `kr.target`, which is where the throat convention is used.
- **Exactly two boundary points.** Let $g(r)=\ell(r)-\ell(2)-\varepsilon(r)$. Then $g'(r)=(1-2/r)^{-1/2}-\tfrac32 c\sqrt r/48^{1/4}$ is strictly decreasing, so $g$ is concave on $(2,\infty)$.
  - $g(2^+)=-\varepsilon(2)<0$, $g(10)>0$ (10.63 at $c=0.1$; 11.71 at $c=0.01$), and $g\to-\infty$.
  - So $g$ has exactly two roots $r_-<r_+$. The path meets the sphere iff $2<r\le r_-$ or $r\ge r_+$ (strictly beyond them for a crossing).
  - C37 pins only $r_+$; N9 adds the window $(2,r_-)$.

- **Closed form.** There is none for $r_-$, because the equation is transcendental. Expanding about $r=2$ with $x=r-2$ gives:
  - $\ell(r)-\ell(2)=2\sqrt{2x}\,(1+x/12+O(x^2))$;
  - $\varepsilon=\varepsilon(2)(1+3x/4+O(x^2))$;
  - hence $\sqrt x\,(1-2x/3+O(x^2))=c/48^{1/4}$.

  So
  $$r_--2=x_0+\tfrac43x_0^2+O(x_0^3),\qquad x_0=\frac{c^2}{\sqrt{48}}=\frac{c^2}{4\sqrt3}.$$
  The computed ratios $(r_--2)/x_0-1=1.929\times10^{-3}$ and $1.925\times10^{-5}$ at $c=0.1$ and $0.01$ match $\tfrac43x_0$.
- **Scaling.** The window shrinks as $c^2$, and $r_+$ grows as $\sqrt{48}/c^2$ (to leading order, with a slowly decaying correction: factor 1.0178 at $c=0.1$, 1.00031 at $0.01$).

## A vertex in the window is an apastron

- **Proof.** Write $V(r)=(1-2/r)(1+L^2/r^2)$. Then $V'(r)=\frac{2}{r^4}\big(r^2+L^2(3-r)\big)>0$ on $(2,3)$.
  - At a timelike turning point ($E^2=V$) with $r<3$, $d(\dot r^2)/dr=-V'<0$, so it is an apastron, for every $L$.
  - Its orbit has no turning point in $(2,r)$. A second one would need $V<E^2$ between two roots of $V=E^2$, hence a minimum of $V$ there, but $V'>0$. So the orbit is a plunge, and the segment from it falls inward.
- **Corrected classifier.** `n9b.py` used `classify_robust` from `/workspace/g168/c33r.py` (f9e9c31b; definitions only) and the pinned `kr.py` (0432cc20). Over the following samples there were 0 exceptions to "apastron, kind plunge" and 0 to "inward `d<0`":
  - 20,000 random turning points in each window ($c=0.1$, $0.01$), with $L\in[0,10]$;
  - 100,000 in $(2,3)$, with $L\in[0,50]$.

## Reached only by a jump, and no decision is taken there

- **Not by free motion.** A segment reaches the next turning point moving toward it. Reaching an apastron $r_a<3$ along a segment therefore needs an outward leg from a periastron below $r_a$. Neither exists: there is no periastron in $(2,3)$, and no orbit has two turning points there. The instance starts at $r=10$. So a history reaches such a vertex only as a jump target.
- **This happens in the model without (a).** Inward targets from sources just beyond $r_+$ ($c=0.1$, continue-through) land in the window: 705.16 → 2.00000083 and 705.2 → 2.0000664.
- **No decision is taken there.** At a jump-reached state, §8.2 item 7 gives $A(O^u,\mathrm{jump})=\{[\gamma_{O^u}]\}$, so the only arm is to continue, and the segment that follows plunges. The throat convention is therefore never used at a window vertex.
- **Under (a) the window is not reached at all.** A target $r'<3$ is a plunge apastron with range $(0,r']$, so (a) needs the source at $r<3$. By induction from $r=10$, no (a)-history has a vertex below $r=3$. So C37's "Under the (a)-restriction the thresholds are not reached" stays true, although its parenthetical (maximum apastron) argues only for $r_+$.
