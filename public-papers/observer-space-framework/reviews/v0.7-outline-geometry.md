## Rulings

### F3: throat convention. Confirm the default in substance; reword it (RC1).

**(a) Fit with the locked object.** As written, the default does not fit the locked object. The wording is "$\exp$ taken in the maximal analytic continuation … the target then reflects to a region-I turning point". This names a global object: the Kruskal manifold, with its regions I and III. That presumes a completed 4-manifold $W$, which the lock does not adopt.

The geometry needed is only local. The jump is the spacelike geodesic from $p$ with initial tangent $-\hat n$ and proper length $\varepsilon$. Continuing the jet analytically *along that path* is the same operation §3 already uses for a curvelet's maximal analytic domain, and it never needs a completed $W$.

On the instance, the path is the radial geodesic of the time-symmetric slice. It meets the bifurcation 2-sphere at a regular point of the continued metric, and continues through it. The germ it reaches is isometric to the $M=1$ turning-point germ at the radius $r'$ with $s(r')-s(2)=\varepsilon-(s(r)-s(2))$, where $s$ is proper radial distance on the slice. The static-frame speed is unchanged, and the isometry $X\mapsto-X$ composed with $\theta\mapsto\pi-\theta$ preserves both time and space orientation. Since $V$ consists of germs modulo pointed diffeomorphism, "which exterior" is not a property of the target at all. So "reflects to region I" is at best a description of one embedding, and should go.

On the choice itself, I confirm the default:
- The alternative ("undefined past the throat, $m=1$") adds a case clause keyed to an instance feature, namely meeting a Killing bifurcation surface. That strains the gate's "uniform, no finite case list".
- The default needs no extra clause.

This is a Geometry observation, not a ruling on the example's adoption.

**(b) Reachability under realisation (a).** In spacetime, no future-directed causal curve runs from region I to region III. But (a) is a germ-level test, $O'^{u'}\in F(O)$, and it is blind to "which exterior". The decisive fact is on the instance: the (a)-restricted model never gets near the thresholds. The maximum apastron is 23.24 at $c=0.1$ and 25.37 at $c=0.01$, against thresholds of 705.155 and 69,303.7. **So the convention is exercised only in the model without (a).** The outline should say so.

**(c) The "0.08 pp" figure.** The figures 0.0658 and 0.0666 come from #155's two 200k runs. They share a seed but decouple at the first divergent decision, so their difference, 0.08 pp, is one draw with no error bar. The coupled estimate is **−0.11 ± 0.03 pp**, with the cross convention giving the lower bound fraction. So "about 0.1 pp" is right, and "0.08 pp" should not be stated as exact. The headline "about 6.6%" stands under either convention.

### F2: Zeno-mass convention. Values correct and stated neutrally; two wording defects (RC2).

The three readings in C35 and §5.3, with their values, match my #155 toy exactly:
- state-level gate: $X$ undefined;
- history-level excision with the mass kept: $1/2$;
- mass conditioned away: $1$.

The Zeno mass is $1/2$ in all three. "v0.7 does not pick one, because the choice moves weights" is neutral, and so is F2.

The defects are these:
1. **The range sentence partly decides the Open item.** "Clause (2) ranges over the histories through the state, from the state onward" is my V6 wording. Read literally, it includes the Zeno histories through the root, and that *is* reading (i). Whether the range is taken before or after item 8's excision is exactly the Open convention, so the sentence must say so.
2. **The definition of "branch point" is circular.** It is defined as a state with $|A|\ge2$ "at which the hard gate holds", but gate clause (2) counts branch points (v0.6 §5.3). This is my #155 wording too. The fix is to define branch points without the gate.
