## Findings

### 1. Pedigree: the 2005–06 corpus contains both rules (fix before prose; would HOLD prose)

The outline says EPP1 has "2005 (per-branch) pedigree" (§1, §7) and that the type-(ii) pad is "the author's 2005 / Strayhorn3 transition / random-walk hypothesis" (§3). Against the old manuscripts:

- **MMWI (Jan 2006), stated rule = the count.** "each of these alternative N_O evolutions of the observer is attributed an equal probability 1/N_O" (p. 11). That is Paper 1's count over distinct evolutions.
- **MMWI, applied rule = per-vertex.** In the χ-minimisation argument (the Fig. 1E passage): "each time the diagram 'splits' into two directions, each of the two trajectories is attributed an equal probability measure. Therefore … lower values of χ1s are more probable than higher values (Note the assumption of transitivity …)". That is the per-split product rule, a random walk, used on an uneven tree where it disagrees with the stated count. The manuscript does not notice the difference.
- **`Strayhorn3`, stated rule = path counting.** "the relative probability of evolution to point p_i is proportional to the number of distinct paths linking p to p_i" (p. 4). This is a count of paths to a target, not a product of 1/deg. It is the form of Paper 1's count, and of the maximal-entropy random walk (finding 5), not of per-vertex EPP1. The same draft then writes |c_i|² = ⟨p, p_i⟩ (p. 5), i.e. squares from path counting.
- **AFLB 2008** counts 2^M equally real worlds after M binary measurements. That tree is generation-regular with constant D on the cut, so per-vertex and count agree (C13's criterion). AFLB cannot distinguish them.

So the accurate statement is: per-vertex EPP1 and the random-walk chance reading have pedigree in MMWI's **applied** per-split rule; Paper 1's count has pedigree in MMWI's **stated** rule and in `Strayhorn3`'s path counting; AFLB 2008 is neutral. v0.6's measure revision is therefore a choice between two rules the 2005–06 corpus already used side by side without separating them, and C12–C15 are what separate them. **Fix:** in §3 and §7 (and prose §7/§10), replace "Strayhorn3 transition / random-walk" with "MMWI's per-split rule (random walk)", and add one sentence stating the above. Keep the SHPMP 2008 hidden-variable reading attached to the pad as now.

### 4. Rideout–Sorkin sits beside both readings (nit, not HOLD)

Classical sequential growth is a stochastic sequential process, which is the chance form and the closest structural neighbour to a Markov jump rule on a digraph. Its measure also lives on completed causets (Brightwell, Dowker, García, Henson, Sorkin, "'Observables' in causal set cosmology", Phys. Rev. D 67, 084031 (2003), gr-qc/0210061), which is the co-existence form. §7 says "too", but §10 now lists it on the co-existence side only, and the move is not in the changelog. Put it beside both.

### 5. New neighbour: maximal-entropy random walk, with a Born hazard (nit, not HOLD)

Burda, Duda, Luck, Waclaw, "Localization of the Maximal Entropy Random Walk", Phys. Rev. Lett. 102, 160602 (2009), arXiv:0810.4113. The generic random walk (1/deg at each vertex) is the per-vertex EPP1 form. MERW (all paths of a given length equally likely) is the count-at-a-cut form. The two coincide on regular graphs, exactly the D-constant criterion of C13–C15. The hazard: MERW's stationary density is ψ_i², the square of the Perron eigenvector, and Duda reads this as explaining the Born rule (arXiv:0710.3861, arXiv:1111.2253, arXiv:2304.02368). With `Strayhorn3`'s path counting and its |c_i|² line in the pedigree (finding 1), a referee who knows MERW will file option (C) with Duda. Name MERW in §7/§9 as a neighbour of the per-vertex/count split and firewall it: not adopted, not a Born route here, circularity kept. For the reference pass, name Dürr–Goldstein–Tumulka–Zanghì Bell-type QFT jump processes as the precise chance-reading neighbour behind "Bell-type jumps". Also avoid "GRW" for the generic random walk anywhere; the outline already uses GRW for Ghirardi–Rimini–Weber.
