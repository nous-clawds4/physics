## 1. §9 "Both rules as counts at a cut", T2, and the (M1)/(M2) cost lines

**(a) The false-exclusivity claim: Claude is right.**
- Define a count anchored afresh at the current state with horizon $k$. It gives arm $c$ of state $v$ the weight $N_{k-1}(c)/N_k(v)$, where $N_m$ is the size of the depth-$m$ cut with dead ends kept.
- That weight is determined by $v$ (given $R$) for every $k$, not only for $k=1$.
- On the scan's Kretschmann tree (rule as stated, $c=0.1$, start at the $r_p$ periastron of $[r_p,20]$), the continue-arm share at the start state is:

  | $r_p$ | $k=1$–$3$ | $k=4$ | $k=5$ | $k=6$ |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 9.00 | 1/3 | 19/53 | 49/131 | 131/339 |
  | 10.00 | 1/3 | 19/53 | 49/131 | 131/341 |
  | 11.00 | 1/3 | 19/53 | 7/19 | 131/351 |

  So each horizon gives a function of the state, and different horizons give different functions.
- What is particular to depth 1 is only that it reads nothing of $R$ beyond the current arms and needs no named depth.
- That is exactly my #162 N3 clause ("needs no named cut"). N3 stays correct, but it is not an exclusivity. #162 read the paragraph as balanced and missed both this sentence and (b); I withdraw that part of #162.

**(b) The "unless anchored" claim: Claude is right, with one refinement.** "Anchored" can mean two things.

1. *Re-anchored at each state (rolling horizon).*
   - Weights are state functions, but in general they are not equal at any fixed cut.
   - C13's tree with $k=2$: $A$-leaves $1/10$, $B$-leaves $1/15$, against $1/12$ at the depth-3 cut. This confirms Claude.
   - Kretschmann tree, $r_p=10$, depth-5 cut (131 paths including dead ends): $k=2$ gives 9 distinct path weights, from $1/441$ to $1/21$. The fixed cut gives $1/131$ each.
2. *Anchored once (a fixed cut at depth $n$ from the current state).*
   - Equal weight on that cut is kept.
   - But after $d$ steps the implied weight is $N_{n-d-1}(c)/N_{n-d}(v)$. It depends on the remaining depth, that is, on where the cut sits. At the same state, the continue share is 1/3, 19/53 or 49/131 with 3, 4 or 5 steps left to the cut (table above).
   - So note 6's cost is not escaped either way.

Both properties hold together only where the tree's shape allows it:
- $D$-constancy on the cut (C13);
- a finite tree counted to its completed histories (on C13, $k\ge3$ gives $1/12$ everywhere).

Under the (a)-restriction, completed histories are countably infinite, so the second case does not help there. Claude's "not jointly available in general" is therefore accurate.

**Is Claude's replacement text neutral?** Nearly. Four fixes are needed:
1. "Only a fixed named cut gives … equal weight" is too strong. EPP1 gives equal weight at its own depth-1 cut, and the shape-allowed cases above give both properties.
2. "(Problem note 6)" is attached without noting that a deeper anchor needs a named depth (note 9), just as EPP1 needs its postulate and (M2) its cut.
3. "MERW in the long-path limit" is a finite-graph statement (C36) and should say so.
4. "Anchoring removes that dependence" holds only for re-anchoring at each state.

The replacements below fix these. They make no (M1)/(M2) pick.

## 2. "13 or 14 jumps" and the stuck count at $c=0.01$

**Measure.** EPP1, i.e. (M1)-style equal branching per decision. It is not a count over orbits.
- Under (a), $m\le1$, so $|A|=2$ at every decision.
- A jump-reached state does not branch. The next decision is at the other turning point.
- On an orbit with an admissible edge at both turning points, the first jump is taken at the current point with weight $2/3$ and at the other with weight $1/3$. With one edge, it is taken with weight 1.
- The root is the $r=10$ periastron of $[10,20]$, with $a=\mathrm{seg}$.
- The enumeration is exhaustive and uses exact rationals; there is no sampling.

**Results.** With a robust classifier (`c33r.py`: deflate the cubic by the target's own root in 50-digit arithmetic):
- $P(12)=26/3^{12}\approx4.892\times10^{-5}$
- $P(13)=9338/3^{10}\approx0.158140$
- $P(14)=447373/3^{12}\approx0.841811$
- No other counts occur.
- $E[\text{jumps}]=13.8418$ and $E[\text{decisions}]=27.6835$.
- Census: 29,119 reachable orbits; 14,478 stuck; longest chain 14; no plunge or unbound target.

**Claude's figures are confirmed:** $4.9\times10^{-5}$, $0.15814$, $0.84181$, $27.684$ and 14,478.

The 20,000-history sample (3,169 and 16,831) is consistent with this law.

**Orbit-count readout (not used by the paper).** The orbit graph is a tree (in-degree 1). Its 14,478 stuck orbits sit at chain depths 12, 13 and 14 in the numbers 5, 1,726 and 12,747.

**Why the pin says 14,477.** `kr.classify` mislabels one near-circular bound target as a plunge.
- The target is at $r=17.1479610$, reached from the $r=16.900001$ apastron of $[16.158567, 16.900001]$ by the outward jump.
- In 60-digit arithmetic the target's orbit is bound on $[17.1479374, 17.1479609]$.
- `np.roots` returns the target's own root about $1.4\times10^{-8}$ (relative) too low. That exceeds kr's $10^{-9}$ tolerance, so the root is taken as an inner turning point. $\dot r^2$ at the midpoint then reads $-3\times10^{-16}$, and the label becomes `'plunge'` with range $(0,17.148]$.
- That range contains the source radius 16.900, so the edge passes (a), and the source orbit is no longer counted as stuck. Hence 14,477 and "target kinds plunge: 1".
- Correctly classified, the source radius lies outside the target's range, the edge fails (a), and the orbit is stuck: 14,478.
- This is the only disagreement among the 116,476 targets compared (`c33d.py`).
- With `kr.classify` as pinned, the exact law acquires a spurious 15-jump atom, $256/3^{14}\approx5.4\times10^{-5}$ (a jump into the mislabelled "plunge"), and $P(14)=0.841758$.
- Which near-circular target is mislabelled depends on last-bit float differences: the census and the history tree hit different ones. It is a rounding artefact, not physics.
