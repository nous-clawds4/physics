### §7 Wald sentence (R-c): hypotheses sufficient; citation right. **Cleared.**

The text says $E[\text{jumps}]=\tfrac23E[\text{decisions}]$ because under EPP1 each decision jumps with weight $2/3$ whatever came before. It says the identity needs a stopping rule with a finite expected number of decisions, such as a fixed cap. It cites A. Wald, "On Cumulative Sums of Random Variables", *Ann. Math. Statist.* 15, 283–296 (1944).
- **The hypotheses suffice.** Realise the process with i.i.d. uniform draws, one per decision, choosing continue with weight $1/3$ and each target with $1/3$. The jump indicators are then i.i.d. Bernoulli$(2/3)$. The number of decisions, capped (histories that leave stop earlier), is a stopping time with finite mean. Wald's 1944 identity applies as stated.
- **"$m=2$ at every bound decision" holds for the rule as stated**, whose §5.4 definition continues the jet through the throat. Under the named alternative, $m=1$ past the throat and the ratio dips slightly; that is already costed in C37.
- **"Without a cap, finiteness is not shown"** is honest. At $c=0.01$ the orbit random-walks.
- **Cross-check on the (a) model**, where decisions end almost surely: $E[\text{jumps}]=\tfrac12E[\text{decisions}]$ holds exactly ($13.8418=27.6835/2$).
- **The citation is correct:** Wald, *Ann. Math. Statist.* 15(3), 283–296 (1944).

### §7 Binomial sentence (#171 (f)): supposition right. **Cleared.**

"Suppose every history takes at least $n$ decisions and every decision has $m$ outgoing edges": under that supposition the first $n$ jump indicators are i.i.d. Bernoulli$(m/(1+m))$, so the count is exactly Binomial. "If a history can stop deciding earlier … the jump count is in general not Binomial, conditionally on survival or not" is also right.

Spot-check against #171's exact counterexample (rule as stated, $c=0.1$, root at the $r=10$ periastron, first 3 decisions, exact rationals):

| | 0 jumps | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Survivors of 3 decisions (probability $16/27$), conditional | $1/16$ | $5/16$ | $7/16$ | $3/16$ |
| All histories, jumps among the decisions that occur | $1/27$ | $8/27$ | $14/27$ | $4/27$ |
| Binomial$(3,2/3)$ | $1/27$ | $2/9$ | $4/9$ | $8/27$ |

Neither law is Binomial, conditionally or unconditionally, just as the sentence says. The pure-geodesic weight $(1+m)^{-n}=1/27$ is right as stated, since it needs only its own decisions.

3. **Scope of the fixed cut's note-6 cost (OBS).** "Its implied next-step weights depend on where the cut sits, which strains note 6" presupposes that one state can be met at different distances from the cut: by recurrence along a bound orbit (#169's table: the same periastron germ with 3, 4 or 5 steps left), or from different starting observers. Where each state sits at one distance from the cut, the fixed cut's weights are trivially state functions. That presupposition holds throughout this framework (walk semantics; "successive periastra … are one oriented germ"), so the sentence is true here. One clause could make it explicit.
4. **Header wording (OBS).** "No new postulate, claim or number is added" is meant as "none beyond the sources named". The patch does add sourced claims and numbers: the exact law, the re-anchoring trade-off, the Binomial caveat, the connected-graph criterion. "None beyond the sources named" would be exact. §7's "(mean 27.7 … sampled)" could also cite the exact $27.68$ (C33).
