## (1) The structural sketch

- **Under the current analytic lock, divergence along the path is impossible.** Hosts sharing the germ agree on all of $P(O)$ and on the whole curvelet wherever it is defined (`papers/geodesic-lifts.md` Prop 3.2, Cor 3.1). They can differ only in *where the path ends*: the nested-prefix case, for example the inequivalent analytic extensions of Taub–NUT (Chruściel–Isenberg 1993 (V, #332)). The batch already says this. The sketch only has content once the lock is relaxed to smooth or finite regularity, which David allowed for exploration.
- **The smooth case: agreement spreads to domains of dependence.** Say two hosts agree, up to isometry, on an open set $U\ni p$. For any acausal spacelike slice $S\subset U$, geometric uniqueness for vacuum Einstein gives agreement on $D(S)$ (Choquet-Bruhat–Geroch 1969 (V, #332)). The boundary of $D^+(S)$ is the Cauchy horizon $H^+(S)$, which is generated by null geodesics (Hawking–Ellis 1973, §6.5 (U)).
- **The path does cross the horizon.** A future-inextendible timelike curve either stays in $\mathrm{int}\,D^+(S)$ or leaves it through $H^+(S)$ (Hawking–Ellis §6.5 (U)). Crossing is a codimension-1 event, so when the horizon is non-empty and lies ahead of the path, no aiming is needed. This is unlike #336.
- **Repair: the boundary of the agreement region need not be null.** The maximal agreement region $A$ need not be the union of the $D(S)$, because hosts can agree beyond $H^+(S)$ by coincidence. If $A$ had a timelike boundary piece, points just outside it would be undetermined by any slice in $A$.
  - Ruling that out needs unique continuation across timelike hypersurfaces for vacuum Einstein. That holds for analytic metrics by Holmgren's theorem. For smooth metrics it holds only under extra conditions; Alexakis–Ionescu–Klainerman-type results are (U), and I have not verified a general theorem.
  - So "first divergence only on null boundaries" is **conditional**, as the batch's own parenthesis concedes.
- **Is "the first divergence point" well defined?** As a parameter, yes. $A$ is open, so $\{\tau:\gamma(\tau)\in A\}$ starting at 0 is an interval $[0,\tau_*)$, and $\tau_*$ is well defined. But at $\gamma(\tau_*)$ every derivative of the two metrics agrees, by continuity. So the two hosts give the **same observer state** $(j_\infty,u)$ at $\tau_*$, and they differ only on $(\tau_*,\tau_*+\delta)$ for every $\delta>0$. There is no first point at which the states differ; the fork is at a boundary point of an open set. A further caveat: "agree" means agreement up to an isometry, and that isometry has to be fixed along the path.
- **Data class decides none, countable or continuum. Confirmed.**
  - Analytic: none.
  - Quasianalytic: none (#331 §4).
  - $C^\infty$, Gevrey or finite $C^k$: a continuum, since the characteristic initial value problem takes free data on a null hypersurface (Rendall, *Proc. R. Soc. A* 427, 221 (1990) (U)).
  - Countable: only with a countable admissible class, which is Set 1.

## (2) Set 1: worlds of finite description (L20)

- **Do nontrivial examples exist? Yes.** Analytic pieces glued along null hypersurfaces are standard:
  - Penrose's cut-and-paste plane impulsive waves (Penrose 1972, in *General Relativity*, ed. O'Raifeartaigh (U));
  - the Aichelburg–Sexl solution (*Gen. Rel. Grav.* 2, 303 (1971) (U));
  - colliding plane waves (Khan–Penrose, *Nature* 229, 185 (1971) (U));
  - null shells (Barrabès–Israel, *Phys. Rev. D* 43, 1129 (1991) (U)).
- **Caveat on regularity.** Impulsive waves carry delta-function curvature, so their metrics are $C^0$, not $C^{1,1}$. L20's "$C^{1,1}$" therefore excludes them. Inside $C^{1,1}$, the examples are sandwich or shock waves whose Weyl curvature *jumps* across a null hypersurface, for example plane waves with a piecewise-constant or piecewise-polynomial profile. These are non-trivial and finitely specifiable.
- **Are the seams forced to be null?** In vacuum with a $C^{1,1}$ metric, essentially yes. Jumps in second derivatives, the weak discontinuities, can lie only on characteristic hypersurfaces, which for Einstein are null (Lichnerowicz-type classical result (U)). Across a non-null hypersurface, $C^{1,1}$ vacuum matching of analytic pieces forces the pieces to continue each other (Cauchy–Kovalevskaya and Holmgren uniqueness for the analytic case). Non-null seams require an Israel thin shell, which has a $C^0$ metric and a surface stress-energy (Israel 1966 (U)), and that means matter. So the batch's "null, not chosen" holds for vacuum hosts.
- **Countability.** "Finitely specifiable" with real parameters gives a continuum: profiles and amplitudes are real numbers. Countability comes only by restricting the constants, for example to algebraic numbers or a semialgebraic class. That is a choice, a grain with an LT2/TT2 burden, and the result is **dense**. "Finitely definable" is ill-posed until a logic or field is named.
  - Most radiative solutions are not semialgebraic.
  - Limits of the class leave the class, so the class is not closed under the evolution's own limits. This is unverified as a general claim.
- **Free fall across a null seam.** It is unique in $C^{1,1}$ (Picard–Lindelöf). For impulsive pp-waves, the regularised geodesics have a unique, regularisation-independent limit with a jump and refraction (Steinbauer, *J. Math. Phys.* 39, 2201 (1998) (U); Kunzinger–Steinbauer, *J. Math. Phys.* 40, 1479 (1999) (U)). So motion stays deterministic in each world, as the target requires. The branching is entirely the choice of world, which matches David's 3:19 reframing.
- **Gain.** Set 1 satisfies the target's structure: deterministic per world, divergence at seams that are crossed in the typical case, no rule selecting a future, and no measure. The count is the open point: a continuum unless a class is named, and if one is named, countable but dense.
