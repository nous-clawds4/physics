## R1. Add SHPMP 2008 to the pedigree (required)

The SHPMP submission (31 March 2008) is where the author separated the two rules and chose between them:

- **Definition 2** (p. 15): the probability of an outcome "is proportional to the number of branches associated with that outcome at the end of the experiment". That is the count at a final cut.
- **Definition 3** (p. 16): "Given a node that splits into N branches, each branch receives 1/N of the share of probability measure from its parent branch". Footnote 6 gives the product ∏ 1/N_k along a path and a sum over paths when branches merge. That is per-vertex EPP1 on a DAG. The text then reads the tree as "a giant classical stochastic state machine" with these transition probabilities, which is the chance reading.
- **Fig. 2A** shows the two disagree on one tree (1/3 by Definition 2, 1/4 by Definition 3) and notes that only Definition 3 is transitive.
- The paper **rejects Definition 2** on Wallace's objection that the count at slice τ₁ and at slice τ₂ gives inconsistent answers. That is the cut-dependence cost the prose lists for (M2) ("a prefix's weight depends on the cut", §9), and §7's "the count's value for a fixed prefix changes with the cut; the per-vertex value does not".
- **Definition 5** (p. 28) weights each branch in proportion to the number of worlds in its ensemble. That is the EPP2 form.

So the corrected history is this. In 2005–06 the author used both rules without separating them (MMWI, `Strayhorn3`). In 2008 SHPMP named both, rejected the count on cut dependence, and adopted the per-split rule. Paper 1 went back to the count. v0.6's EPP1 returns to the 2008 choice, and (M2) would revisit the rule SHPMP rejected. AFLB 2008 cannot tell the two apart.

**Proposed text, as a fifth bullet in §3 (the outline bans the 2008 word, so the rules are cited by definition number):**

> - The 2008 SHPMP submission (31 March 2008; rejected 1 July 2008) separates the two rules explicitly. Its Definition 2 is the count ("proportional to the number of branches associated with that outcome at the end of the experiment"); its Definition 3 is the per-split rule ("each branch receives 1/N of the share of probability measure from its parent branch"), read as a classical stochastic state machine. It shows that they disagree on one tree, rejects the count because its answer changes with the slice at which branches are counted, and adopts the per-split rule. Its Definition 5, weight in proportion to the worlds in each branch's ensemble, is the form of EPP2. That submission argued inside the Everett-versus-decision-theory frame; this paper does not, and cites it for the rule only.

Then change "Its pedigree is the per-split rule the author applied in 2006 (§3)" (§1) and "Its pedigree is MMWI's applied per-split rule, the random walk (§3)" (§7) to "the per-split rule, applied in MMWI (2006) and stated as SHPMP 2008's Definition 3 (§3)". Optional: add to (M2)'s costs in §9 "(the objection on which SHPMP 2008 rejected the count)".

The last sentence of the proposed bullet matters for desk-reject risk. Citing SHPMP by its rule, with the frame disowned in the same place, keeps a referee from reading this paper as the 2008 Everett-versus-Deutsch–Wallace argument come back.

## R2. The hidden-variable sentence (required)

Rejection letter, 1 July 2008:
- **Referee 1** says the programme amounts to adding "'hidden variables' with their own dynamics (T)" to Everettian QM and deriving the probability rule there, "a far inferior cousin" to Bohmian typicality (Dürr et al.; Callender; Maudlin).
- **Referee 2** makes a different objection: the question is whether the rule works inside Everett "with no other ontology", so a deeper model T is irrelevant.

So the §7 sentence "The per-branch rule behind it is exactly what the 2008 SHPMP referees read as a hidden-variable program" overstates twice. Only one referee read it that way, and what he read as hidden-variable was the added layer T with its own dynamics, which carries the per-split weights, not the rule taken alone.

**Proposed §7 replacement:**

> The per-split rule is SHPMP 2008's Definition 3 (§3). The first SHPMP referee read that programme, equal-share weights on an added state-transition layer with its own dynamics, as a hidden-variable programme and a cousin of Bohmian typicality; that reading stays attached (§3, §4.3). The second referee's objection, that the question inside Everett allows no added ontology, belongs to the frame this paper does not use.

In §3, change "the hidden-variable reading of the 2008 SHPMP referees" to "the first 2008 SHPMP referee's hidden-variable reading". §4.3 is already right.

## Nits (not HOLD)

1. **Paper 1 sentence not verbatim.** The outline required it "to appear verbatim in prose §1". The prose rewords it ("histories are now discontinuous, and their jumps carry no proper time"; "Paper 1's thesis that countability makes counting the unique measure is not carried"). The reword is plainer and accurate against Paper 1's subtitle, so keep it. But log it as changelog item 7.
2. **"Obs-local" against "local in Obs".** §9 defines "Obs-local" for rules, and §6 still calls "local in Obs" an undefined slogan. A reader will take these for one notion. Either say in §6 that the slogan is not §9's defined term, or delete it now.
3. **Obs-local Bell Hope and the chance reading.** Under the chance reading, an Obs-local rule is a local stochastic process for each observer, which is what Bell's theorem constrains. If the Hope has a route, it runs through the co-existence reading. One sentence in §9 saying so removes the obvious referee objection without claiming anything.
4. **C16 witness is numerical** (200 random ω over 2000 decisions). Uncountability needs every ω ∈ {0,1}^ℕ to stay in the bound region. Either give the one-line invariance argument or call it numerical evidence. This is Geometry's lane; flagged only because §10 cites it.
5. **Rideout–Sorkin reference.** Add the classical sequential growth paper itself in the reference pass (Rideout and Sorkin, Phys. Rev. D 61, 024002 (2000), gr-qc/9904062), beside Brightwell et al.
