By §2 rigidity this confines $R$ to pairs in one analytic continuation of the germ and **forbids curvature-changing jumps**." And "(b) … Curvature-changing digraph edges are allowed." ## 2. Clause (2): discrete branch points along $\tau$

**Rule (#117 §3.2, as used in #128).**

- Vertices are the isolated critical points of $K$ along $\gamma$.
- At a vertex, set $\hat n=\nabla K/|\nabla K|\in u^\perp$ and $\varepsilon=c|K|^{-1/4}$.
- $\mathrm{succ}_R$ is the pair of lumps at $\exp_p(\pm\varepsilon\hat n)$, with $u$ parallel-transported.

**Lemma (Schwarzschild).** $dK/d\tau=K'(r)\,u^r=-288\,u^r/r^7$. So $K$ is critical exactly where $u^r=0$. The critical points are isolated unless the orbit is circular, where $K\circ\gamma$ is constant and none is isolated.

The spacelike geodesic from $p$ with tangent $\propto\partial_r$ keeps $t,\theta,\phi$ fixed, and the static orthonormal frame is parallel along it ($\Gamma^r{}_{rt}=\Gamma^r{}_{r\phi}=0$). So the frame components of $u$ are preserved: $u^{\hat t}=1.083473$, $u^{\hat\phi}=0.417029$, $u^{\hat r}=0$. It follows that every target has $u^r=0$ and is a turning point. The frame speed $v=0.3849$ exceeds $v_{\rm circ}=(r-2)^{-1/2}$ at every target computed, so no target is circular. **Every target is in $V^\tau$.**

**Option (B), consecutive jumps with multiplicity under a bound $N$.** On the instance, $R$-chains are infinite, so $N$ is a number imposed on histories, not read from $R$. It is a law-like extra. It is available, but not recommended while the Kretschmann rule is the draft's consistency witness.

**Decision: (A), with the enlarged state named, plus a named no-Zeno clause.** Wording is in fix 2 below.

## 3. Object: the dropped isotropy clause

**Check.** `v0.5-prose.md` contains neither "isotropy" nor "modulo". §2 reads "$O^u=(O,u)$ for that lump together with a unit timelike vector $u$". The outline's "Isotropy: $u$ modulo germ isotropy (named)" is gone, and the changelog does not log the removal.

**Flat lump.** $\mathrm{Riem}=0$, so every Lorentz transformation of $T_p$ is a local isometry (linear in normal coordinates), and the germ isotropy is $O(1,3)$. $SO^+(1,3)$ acts transitively on the future unit hyperboloid $H^3=SO^+(1,3)/SO(3)$, which has dimension $6-3=3$. Explicit check: for three random $u\in H^3$, the boost $\Lambda(u)$ with $\Lambda_{00}=\gamma$, $\Lambda_{0i}=\Lambda_{i0}=w_i$ and $\Lambda_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+w_iw_j/(1+\gamma)$ satisfies $\Lambda^T\eta\Lambda=\eta$, $\det\Lambda=1$ and $\Lambda e_0=u$.

Without the quotient, "$(O,u)$" needs a tangent space, which a lump lacks. Choosing a presentation supplies one, but presentations related by an isotropy element carry $u$ to $\Lambda u$. So $V$ contains, over the flat lump, a whole $H^3$ of pairs that no invariant separates: every curvature scalar, including the $u$-dependent ones, vanishes, and every such curvelet is constant.

Named instance: a vertex with $[\gamma]$ and targets $(\text{flat},u')$ and $(\text{flat},\Lambda u')$.

- Unquotiented: $|B_R|=3$, and EPP1 gives $1/3$ each.
- Modulo isotropy the two targets are one point: $|B_R|=2$, and EPP1 gives $1/2$ each.

So $\mathrm{succ}_R$, $|B_R|$ and the EPP1 weights are not functions of physical data without the quotient. With it, the flat oriented germ is one point, the flat curvelet is constant, and a bare $u$-jump at the flat lump is the identity, not an edge.

**ESU, $-dt^2+a^2(d\chi^2+\sin^2\chi\,d\Omega^2)$.** Sympy gives:

- $R_{ab}=(2/a^2)(g_{ab}+t_at_b)$;
- $R^a{}_b=\mathrm{diag}(0,2/a^2,2/a^2,2/a^2)$, with eigenvalue $0$ on $\partial_t$ (simple) and $2/a^2$ on the spatial 3-space;
- $R=6/a^2$, $R_{ab}R^{ab}=12/a^4$, $K=12/a^4$.

A germ isometry preserves the simple timelike Ricci eigenline, hence $\partial_t$ (future). So the isotropy is contained in $O(3)$, and it equals $SO(3)$, the point stabiliser in $\mathbb R\times SO(4)$, up to reflections. Oriented germs at a point, modulo isotropy, are labelled by $\gamma_{\rm rel}=-u\cdot\partial_t\in[1,\infty)$. A boost of $u$ is therefore a genuinely different oriented class.

Along a numerically integrated geodesic ($a=1$, initial frame velocity $(0.5,0.3,0.4)$), $\gamma_{\rm rel}=1.4142135624$ at $\tau=0,1.5,3,4.5,6$ (constant to 10 digits, $g(u,u)=-1$). This is expected, since $\partial_t$ is Killing. ESU is homogeneous, so the lump is the same at every event, and the oriented germ is constant along every geodesic.

Consequences:

- A bare boost $\partial_t\to u$ (with $\gamma_{\rm rel}=\sqrt2$) is type (ii) modulo isotropy, while the Obs (lump) path stays constant.
- "Discontinuous in Obs" is false. The discontinuity lives only in the oriented layer.
- ($V^\tau=\emptyset$ in ESU, so such an edge is not in $R$ under $R\subseteq V^\tau\times V$. The error is in §3's *classification* sentence, which v0.6 must still fix.)

**ACCEPT.**

## 4. Measure

**What v0.5 says (§7).** It compares per-vertex $1/|B_R|$ (the product rule) with Paper 1's uniform count over depth-$n$ evolutions, and says "The rules agree only on generation-regular trees."

**Named-cut criterion (proved here).** Let $C$ be a finite cut: a finite set of nodes that every root-to-end path meets exactly once. Depth cuts and proper-time cuts both qualify. Let $D(v)=\prod_{w<v}\deg(w)$ over strict ancestors.

- Per-vertex assigns $1/D(v)$ on $C$, and these sum to 1 (flow conservation).
- The count assigns $1/|C|$.
- **The two agree on $C$ iff $D$ is constant on $C$**, and then $D\equiv|C|$. This is equivalent to #128's "every arm subtends the same number of evolutions at that cut".
- If depth cuts $n$ and $n+1$ are both constant, then every depth-$n$ node has degree $D_{n+1}/D_n$. So the rules **agree at every depth $\le N$ iff the tree is generation-regular to depth $N-1$**. Depth 1 always agrees.

**Instances.**

| Tree / cut | Per-vertex | Count |
| --- | --- | --- |
| #128: root→$A,B$; $A_{1..3}$ each 2 leaves; $B_{1,2}$ each 3 leaves; depth 3 | $1/12$ each | $1/12$ each (agree; not generation-regular) |
| same, depth 2, $A$-prefix | $1/2$ | $3/5$ |
| Geometry: root deg 2; $a$ deg 1 → $a_1$ deg 6; $b$ deg 3 → each child deg 2; depth 1 | $1/2,1/2$ | $1/2,1/2$ |
| same, depth 2, $a$-prefix | $1/2$ | $1/4$ ($D=2$ vs $6$) |
| same, depth 3 | $1/12$ each | $1/12$ each ($D\equiv12$) |
| #128: binary, $A$ branches at $\tau=1$, $B$ at $\tau=5$; proper-time cut $T=3$ | $A$-prefix $1/2$ | $2/3$ ($D=(4,4,2)$ on $C=\{A_1,A_2,B\}$) |

Agreement is not monotone in depth: the Geometry tree agrees at depths 1 and 3 and disagrees at depth 2.

**Dead ends (die ≠ arm, §5.2).** A convention must be named.

- (i) Include truncated paths in $C$, with $D$ taken along their actual length.
- (ii) Count only survivors, and condition per-vertex on survival.

They can disagree. Example: root→$a$ (binary forever) and $b$; $b\to\{b_1$ (binary forever), $b_2$ (dies)$\}$. Under (ii), $D=2^n$ on every survivor, so the rules agree at every depth $\ge2$. Under (i), $b_2$ has $D=4$, so they disagree at every depth $\ge3$.

**Limit.** Per-vertex is projectively consistent, so it defines $\mu$ on the boundary (Ionescu-Tulcea). The depth-$n$ count $\nu_n$ is not consistent. With finite stars, **$\nu_n\to\mu$ on every cylinder iff siblings are asymptotically balanced**: $D_n(w)/D_n(w')\to1$ for all siblings $w,w'$, where $D_n$ counts depth-$n$ descendants. The proof is by induction on depth, using $\nu_n(w)/\nu_n(\text{parent})=D_n(w)/\sum_{\rm sib}D_n$.

Exact constancy of $D$ along a cofinal sequence of cuts is **sufficient but not necessary**. Counterexample, built from three degree schedules by generation $g$:

- type $A$ has degree $g+2$;
- type $E$ has degree $g+3$ at even $g$ and $g+1$ at odd $g$;
- type $O$ is $E$ with the parities swapped;
- an $A$ node's first child is $O$ or $E$, whichever starts a balanced pair at its generation, and its other children are $A$.

Then:

- $D$ is non-constant at **every** depth cut $n\ge2$ (the perturbed child born at generation $n-1$ has degree $n+2$ against its $A$-siblings' $n+1$);
- yet the root's $O$-child count share is 0.5714, 0.4800, 0.5373, 0.4826 at $n=2..5$, and 0.501278, 0.498762 at $n=120,121$, tending to $\mu=1/2$;
- the depth-2 $E$/$A$ sibling ratio is 0.99534 and 1.00484 at $n=120,121$, tending to $1$.

The counterexample uses unbounded (finite) degrees. The bounded-degree case is not settled here.

**Jump domination.** Take recurrent outs, $|\mathrm{succ}_R|=m$ at every branch decision, $B_R=\{[\gamma]\}\cup\mathrm{succ}_R$ and EPP1. Then:

- the pure-geodesic history has weight $(1+m)^{-n}$ after $n$ decisions ($3^{-10}=1.69\times10^{-5}$ for $m=2$; $2^{-10}=9.8\times10^{-4}$ for $m=1$);
- the number of jumps is Binomial$(n,\,m/(1+m))$, so the expected jumping fraction is $2/3$ for $m=2$.

On the Kretschmann instance under (A), every turning point reached along a segment is a decision, giving at least $2\times2/3=4/3$ expected jumps per radial period (425.13).

Jumps that are small at the vertex are not small in the orbit: at $c=0.01$ a 1 % displacement moves apastron from $20$ to $21.332$, and at $c=0.1$ the inward jump turns $[10,20]$ into $[8.93,10.10]$.

EPP1 weights cannot be tuned. So if typical histories are to show GR geodesic motion over an observed proper-time window $[0,T]$, any future $R$ must meet the following. This is a named necessary condition, not a law.

> **No-jump-dominance condition (J).** $J(T)=\sum_{\text{decisions in }[0,T]} m_v/(1+m_v)$ must be small along typical histories. Each decision with $m_v\ge1$ contributes $\ge1/2$. So either **(J1)** vertices with $\mathrm{succ}_R\ne\emptyset$ are sparse (their number over observed windows is $\lesssim$ tolerance, far below the number of $V^\tau$ vertices), or **(J2)** jumps are observationally null: every target lies within a stated tolerance of the continuation in a named topology *on curvelets* (not merely on germs at the vertex).

The Kretschmann rule fails both. That is recorded as a constraint on any future $R$, not as a demand to write one.

**ACCEPT**, sharpened as above.

## 5. $V^\tau$ residuals

**Constant-scalar backgrounds.** Computed with sympy:

| World | Invariants |
| --- | --- |
| Minkowski | $K=R=0$ |
| de Sitter | $R=12/\ell^2$, $R_{ab}R^{ab}=36/\ell^4$, $K=24/\ell^4$ |
| ESU | $R=6/a^2$, $R_{ab}R^{ab}=K=12/a^4$ |

In all three, the *oriented germ* itself is constant along every curvelet: by maximal symmetry for Minkowski and dS, and by $\gamma_{\rm rel}$ conservation for ESU (§3). So **every** lock-side scalar, polynomial or $u$-dependent, is constant along every curvelet, and $V^\tau=\emptyset$ for any choice of scalar. Only $(\mathrm{lump},\tau)$ tags could mark times there, and they are not lock-side (#128 row 2).

**Flat ΛCDM.** Sympy confirms $K=12[(\ddot a/a)^2+H^4]$ and $R=6(\ddot a/a+H^2)$ for flat FLRW. With $x=a^{-3}$, $H^2=H_0^2(\Omega_mx+\Omega_\Lambda)$ and $\ddot a/a=H_0^2(\Omega_\Lambda-\Omega_mx/2)$ (checked against $\ddot a/a=H^2+\tfrac12\,dH^2/d\ln a$):
$$\frac{K}{12H_0^4}=2\Omega_\Lambda^2+\Omega_\Lambda\Omega_mx+\tfrac54\Omega_m^2x^2,\qquad \frac{d}{dx}\frac{K}{12H_0^4}=\Omega_m\big(\Omega_\Lambda+\tfrac52\Omega_mx\big)>0.$$
Also $R/H_0^2=12\Omega_\Lambda+3\Omega_mx$ and $R_{ab}R^{ab}/H_0^4=36\Omega_\Lambda^2+18\Omega_\Lambda\Omega_mx+9\Omega_m^2x^2$, both increasing. At $\Omega_\Lambda=\Omega_m=1$ these reproduce #128's $2+x+\tfrac54x^2$ and $12(3+\tfrac32x+\tfrac34x^2)$.

Along any future timelike geodesic $dt/d\tau=u^t\ge1$ and $dx/dt=-3xH<0$. So $K$, $R$ and $R_{ab}R^{ab}$ are strictly monotone in $\tau$, and $V^\tau=\emptyset$.

By contrast, $(\ddot a/a)^2/H_0^4=\Omega_\Lambda^2-\Omega_\Lambda\Omega_mx+\tfrac14\Omega_m^2x^2$ is non-monotone. It is stationary at $x=2\Omega_\Lambda/\Omega_m$, which is $a=0.598$, $z=0.671$ for $(0.3,0.7)$. So a $u$-dependent slice such as $(R_{ab}u^au^b)^2$ would put a vertex at the onset of acceleration for comoving observers. Which scalar is chosen matters, which is why the choice is a named extra.

**Codimension one.** $u^a\nabla_aK=0$ is one equation on oriented-germ space. On the Schwarzschild exterior, oriented germs modulo isotropy are labelled $(r,v_r,|v_\perp|)$. $V^\tau$ is contained in $\{v_r=0\}$, minus the circular locus $|v_\perp|=(r-2)^{-1/2}$ where $K\circ\gamma$ is constant and nothing is isolated. That is a 2-parameter set, and it is discrete only per curvelet, as the isolated zeros of a non-constant analytic function of $\tau$. The instance orbit has $v=0.3849>v_{\rm circ}(10)=0.3536$.

**Detail correction to #128 ("empty on … radial infall").** This is true only for $E\ge1$ infall. A bound radial geodesic ($E<1$) has exactly one vertex, at its apex. Example: from rest at $r=10$, $\tau=0$ is an isolated minimum of $K\circ\gamma$ ($K=4.8\times10^{-5}$), and there is no other vertex before $r=0$ at $\tau=35.124$.

**VSI / plane wave.** Take $ds^2=2\,du\,dv+e^{-u^2}(x^2-y^2)\,du^2+dx^2+dy^2$. Sympy gives:
