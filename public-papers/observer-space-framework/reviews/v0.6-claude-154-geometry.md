## 1. C16: the invariant window is a proof

**Map.** Take the $[10,20]$ orbit ($E=0.969087$, $L=4.170288$; roots $2.857,10,20$). At a periastron, the static-frame speed is $v=L/r\big/(E/\sqrt{1-2/r})$, and $v^2=4/27$ exactly ($v=0.384900$). A Kretschmann jump runs along the $t=\text{const}$ radial geodesic. The static frame is parallel along that geodesic, so the jump preserves the frame components of $u$. Every target is therefore a turning point with speed $v$. The target is:
- a periastron iff $v>v_{\rm circ}=(r'-2)^{-1/2}$, i.e. $r'>2+27/4=8.75$;
- bound iff $E'=\sqrt{1-2/r'}\,(1-v^2)^{-1/2}<1$, i.e. $r'<27/2=13.5$.

Let $f_{\rm in},f_{\rm out}(r)=s^{-1}(s(r)\mp\varepsilon(r))$, where $s(r)=\sqrt{r(r-2)}+2\ln(\sqrt r+\sqrt{r-2})$ is radial proper distance and $\varepsilon=c\,r^{3/2}/48^{1/4}$. Take $c=0.01$ and the rule "jump toward 10": $f_{\rm in}$ if $r\ge10$, $f_{\rm out}$ if $r<10$.

**Endpoints (reproduced).**
- $a=f_{\rm in}(10)=9.892616$ and $b=f_{\rm out}(10)=10.107529$.
- $f_{\rm out}(a)=9.998274$ and $f_{\rm in}(b)=9.998262$. Both are "9.9983" to four places, as Claude says, but they are not equal.
- A 2001-point grid maps into $[9.892687,10.107493]\subset[a,b]$.

**Proof.** On $[a,b]$, $s'(r)=(1-2/r)^{-1/2}\ge1.118$, while $\varepsilon'(r)=1.5c\,r^{1/2}/48^{1/4}\le0.0181$. So $s\pm\varepsilon$ is strictly increasing, and so are $f_{\rm in}$ and $f_{\rm out}$ (minimum derivative of $s-\varepsilon$: $1.0984$). Then:
- for $r\in[10,b]$: $a=f_{\rm in}(10)\le f_{\rm in}(r)\le f_{\rm in}(b)<b$;
- for $r\in[a,10)$: $a<f_{\rm out}(a)\le f_{\rm out}(r)<f_{\rm out}(10)=b$.

So $f([a,b])\subset[a,b]$. **No numerics are needed beyond the endpoints**, and those sit $1.143$ and $3.392$ inside $(8.75,13.5)$.

**Hypotheses the proof uses** (C16 should list them):
- (H1) the Kretschmann rule as stated, with outward edges (the model without (a));
- (H2) frame components preserved by the jump (exact);
- (H3) option (A) with walk semantics: successive periastra are one oriented germ, and the history decides at each segment-reached periastron and continues at each apastron;
- (H4) monotonicity, as above;
- (H5) $[a,b]\subset(8.75,13.5)$, so every target is a non-circular periastron of a bound orbit;
- (H6) no Zeno: half radial periods on the window lie in $[200.26,225.98]$, so each infinite history has infinite accumulated $\tau$ and meets the hard gate;
- (H7) first-edge equivalence, which makes the map $\omega\mapsto$ history injective.

Then $\{0,1\}^{\mathbb N}$ injects into completed histories. **ACCEPT**, with C16 reworded as "(proof; model without (a))" and its window stated as $[9.8926,10.1075]$.

## 2. Realisation (a): a model, but a transient one

**Targets on $[10,20]$ (all reproduced).**

| $c$ | source | out target (orbit, (a)) | in target (orbit, (a)) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.1 | $r=10$ | $11.0813$, $[11.0813,39.0029]$, fails | $8.9332$, $[8.9332,10.1029]$, **passes** |
| 0.1 | $r=20$ | $23.2368$, $[17.4250,23.2368]$, **passes** | $16.7925$, plunge $(0,16.7925]$, fails |
| 0.01 | $r=10$ | $10.1075$, $[10.1075,21.3322]$, fails | $9.8926$, $[9.8926,18.7504]$, **passes** |
| 0.01 | $r=20$ | $20.3225$, $[10.6517,20.3225]$, **passes** | $19.6778$, $[9.3613,19.6778]$, fails |

The (a) test is the lock-side one: the target's own curvelet must pass through the source lump ($r_s$) in its past. So $r_s$ must lie in the target orbit's $r$-range, which is $(0,r']$ for a plunge from the white-hole region.

**After the first jump at $c=0.1$ (reproduced).**
- On $[8.9332,10.1029]$: targets $9.8329$ (bound), $8.0465$ (plunge), $11.2023$ (bound), $9.0182$ (plunge).
- On $[17.4250,23.2368]$: targets $20.0365$, $14.8390$, $27.3201$, $19.1875$.
- **None passes (a).**

So under (a) the history jumps once and is then geodesic forever. The histories are "jump at decision $k$", with weight $2^{-k}$, plus the pure geodesic. That set is countable. Claude is right here.

**At $c=0.01$ Claude's "keeps branching" does not survive a longer run.** I enumerated every orbit reachable from $[10,20]$ by (a)-admissible jumps; continues return to the same orbit:
- 29,119 reachable orbits;
- no turning point ever carries two admissible edges, so $m\le1$ at every decision;
- no cycle;
- the **longest jump chain is 14**;
- 14,477 orbits are *stuck*: no admissible edge at either turning point.

The mechanism is the circularisation Claude observed. Inward-at-periastron and outward-at-apastron both shrink the orbit. Once its width is below $\varepsilon$, the inward target overshoots to an apastron below the source, and the outward target to a periastron above it, so both fail (a).

The EPP1 chance process under (a) (20,000 histories, run to 10,000 decisions) confirms this:

| $c$ | jumps per history | decisions until none remain | share of decisions that jump |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.1 | exactly 1 (all) | mean 2.0, max 20 | 0.5015 |
| 0.01 | 13 (3,169) or 14 (16,831) | mean 27.7, max 57 | 0.4999 |

Consequences:
- **Histories do jump under (a)**: once a.s. at $c=0.1$, 13–14 times a.s. at $c=0.01$.
- While branching lasts, jumps follow Binomial$(n,1/2)$, the $m=1$ case of v0.6 §7's Binomial$(n,m/(1+m))$, not the 2/3 of the rule as stated.
- Decisions are a.s. finite, so a history's total jump count is bounded, not Binomial over all $n$.
- **Completed histories are countable under (a) at both $c$.** Each is fixed by at most 14 decision indices plus a finite prefix, and continuing forever at a non-stuck orbit has probability 0.
- Claude's first-20-decision figures (share 0.500; "100% bound") reproduce: 96.3% of histories are still branching at decision 20. Survivors' apastra 5/50/95% are $13.52/15.80/18.75$ against Claude's $13.3/15.8/18.7$, and periastra $10.86/12.78/14.95$ against $10.96/12.79/15.04$.

**Verdict: QUALIFY.** The (a)-restriction is a stated, uniform, lock-side model with $|B_R|=2$ at both turning points of $[10,20]$, so "not fatal" does not depend on dropping (a). But its branching is **transient** at both $c$. The v0.7 sentence must say so. Otherwise "(a) has a model" reads as "(a) has an ongoing branching model", which is false on this instance.

**Bearing on (J1)/(J2) and on T3 (not decided here).**
- The (a)-restriction meets a *late-window* (J1): decisions end a.s.
- It fails (J1) over early windows: share 1/2 over the first ~28 decisions at $c=0.01$.
- It fails (J2): one jump turns $[10,20]$ into $[9.8926,18.7504]$ or into $[8.9332,10.1029]$.
- So "(J1) sparse" is window-dependent, and v0.6's "fails both (C17)" is correctly scoped to the rule as stated.
- For Foundations T3, the (a) transient is a data point for the "show jumps negligible" route. Jumps become finitely many per history, but each is $O(1)$ in the orbit. It is not a resolution.

## 3. Jump dominance under the rule as stated

EPP1 chance process from the $r=10$ periastron with $a=\mathrm{seg}$ (option (A), walk semantics), 20 decisions per history. "Left" means the history moved onto a plunging or unbound orbit.

| Case | $N$ | share of decisions that jump | still bound after 20 | mean decisions among leavers | survivors' apastron 5/50/95% |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $c=0.1$, throat crossing | 200,000 | 0.6672 | **0.0658** (SE 0.0006) | 5.90 | 12.2 / 39.6 / 616 |
| $c=0.1$, target undefined past throat | 200,000 | 0.6655 | **0.0666** (SE 0.0006) | 5.90 | 12.3 / 40.1 / 938 |
| $c=0.01$ | 20,000 | 0.6669 | 0.9994 | — | 15.15 / 19.92 / 26.83 |

Findings:
- **2/3 is exact in expectation.** Every bound decision has $m=2$. The jump indicators are i.i.d. Bernoulli(2/3), and the stopping time depends only on the past, so Wald's identity gives $E[\text{jumps}]=\tfrac23E[\text{decisions}]$. The pooled share therefore converges to 2/3. It dips (0.6655) only where the inward target is undefined (next bullet).
- **The 7% is 6.6%.** Claude's 7.2% from 2000 histories has SE $\approx0.57$ pp and lies about 1.1 SE above my estimate, so it is not wrong, just noisy. "Mean 6.8 decisions before leaving" matches the mean of $\min(T,20)$ over **all** histories ($0.934\times5.90+0.066\times20=6.83$). Among histories that do leave, the mean is 5.90. The survivors' 95th-percentile apastron is heavy-tailed and depends on convention and sample (Claude 4022; here 616 or 938).
- **No history avoided jumping** in any run, which fits $3^{-20}$.
- **$c=0.01$ reproduces** Claude's row: 99.9% bound; periastra 7.41/9.95/13.10 against 7.42/10.03/12.95.
- **Ambiguity found (not in v0.6 or #154).** $\exp_p(-\varepsilon\hat n)$ leaves the exterior when $\varepsilon>s(r)-s(2)$, i.e. for $r>705.155$ at $c=0.1$ and $r>69{,}303.7$ at $c=0.01$. The $t=\text{const}$ radial geodesic then passes through the bifurcation sphere into the other exterior (Kruskal region III). There the reflection $X\to-X$ maps the target to a region-I turning point of the same lump class. The rule must say whether $\exp$ is taken in the maximal analytic continuation or the target is undefined (then $m=1$ there). It moves the bound fraction by 0.08 pp and does not touch the $[10,20]$ pins.

So Claude's R9b sentence is right in substance: under the rule as stated, typical histories are jump-dominated. v0.7 should quote 2/3 (exact) and "about 6.6% at $c=0.1$ after 20 decisions (sampled)", not 7.2%.
