## 4. Required items (smallest set to move to PASS)

The instances behind them (C30, C31) are periastron edges at $r=10$. At the apastron, §5.4's own bullet and C33 show the reverse. Five short edits:

1. **§4.2, "The worked example under (a)":**
   - OLD: "The Kretschmann rule’s outward edges fail (a): their target orbits"
   - NEW: "At the $r=10$ periastron, the Kretschmann rule’s outward edges fail (a): their target orbits"
   - OLD: "Its inward edges pass:"
   - NEW: "Its inward edges there pass:"
   - OLD: "imposing (a) would drop its outward edges."
   - NEW: "imposing (a) would drop, on the instance, the outward edge at the periastron and the inward edge at the apastron (§5.4)."
2. **§5.4, third bullet under "The Kretschmann rule":**
   - OLD: "Its outward edges fail (a) and its inward edges pass (§4.2), so as stated it is a (b) model"
   - NEW: "On the instance one edge at each turning point fails (a), the outward edge at the periastron and the inward edge at the apastron (§4.2), so as stated it is a (b) model"

   - OLD: "Under (a), the worked witness loses its outward edges, and its restricted form branches only transiently (C33)."
   - NEW: "Under (a), the worked witness loses one edge at each turning point of the instance (outward at the periastron, inward at the apastron), and its restricted form branches only transiently (C33)."
4. **C30, claim column:**
   - OLD: "the outward Kretschmann edges fail it"
   - NEW: "the outward Kretschmann edges at the $r=10$ periastron fail it"
5. **C31, claim column:**
   - OLD: "The inward Kretschmann edges pass (a) (§4.2)"
   - NEW: "The inward Kretschmann edges at the $r=10$ periastron pass (a) (§4.2)"

## 5. Suggestions (not required)

- **S1. §10, SSA row.**
  - OLD: "Here the only link is the phrase: $E_W$ and $E_M$ are ensembles of worlds that carry a given germ, and the retired toy's edge ran from flat to FLRW."
  - NEW: "Here the only link is resemblance: $E_W$ and $E_M$ are ensembles of worlds that carry a given germ, and the retired toy's edge ran from flat to FLRW."
  - The sentence follows the junction row's pattern ("the only link is the phrase: 'joins'"), but it names no phrase.
- **S2. Stoica traceability.**
  - v0.8 cites arXiv:2209.08621 ("Born rule: quantum probability as classical probability"). That choice was deliberate (F11: one work per attribution; #180 chose it because the sentences use "$|\Psi|$ is in the count").
  - Paper 1 §9, whose Stoica firewall this paper carries, cites arXiv:2209.08623 ("Background freedom leads to many-worlds with local beables and probabilities").
  - A reader who follows the carry from Paper 1 will find a different work. Suggested addition to the §10 Stoica row: "(Paper 1 §9 firewalls his background-freedom paper, arXiv:2209.08623, on the same ground)".
- **S3. EKT row.**
  - "One set of initial data can admit several, for some data infinitely many, self-consistent trajectories, each with a self-collision" is right for EKT's "dangerous" initial data, whose collision-free motion is self-inconsistent.
  - For other data the collision-free trajectory can itself be a solution.
  - Suggested: "…each with a self-collision (for initial data whose collision-free motion would be self-inconsistent)". This was checked against the abstract only.
