**Main question.** On v0.6 I judged the problem "honestly confined": the law of $R$, its motivation, the slice, and the measure choice sat in §9 as Open. v0.7 keeps it there, labels it the same way in the Status line, §5.4 and §9, and adds nothing that disguises it.

**The two v0.6 flags the brief asks about are honestly answered.**
- **(a) undersold (my R2).** v0.7 records the (a)-restricted Kretschmann rule as a stated, uniform, lock-side model with $|B_R|=2$ at both turning points of $[10,20]$. It also records that its branching is **transient** and its completed histories countable, and it firewalls "(a) has a model" against "(a) has an ongoing branching model" (Firewall; §10 "does not claim"). That is more accurate than my own R2, which wrongly said the $c=0.01$ model "keeps branching". My exact enumeration confirms the transient: at most 14 jumps, then no decision (row 1).
- **Jump dominance in words (my R9b).** §7 now says it: "Under the Kretschmann rule as stated, typical histories are jump-dominated." The numbers are 2/3 of decisions, about 6.6% still bound after 20 decisions at $c=0.1$, and a leavers' mean of 5.90, all marked sampled. I reproduce all three at $N=200{,}000$ (row 2).

**Why HOLD, narrowly.**

**(i) The T2 balance paragraph (§9, "Both rules as counts at a cut").**
- The sentence "The depth-1 anchor is the only cut whose weights are functions of the current state" is false.
  - A count anchored at the current state at **any** depth gives weights that are functions of that state (given $R$).
  - The draft's own MERW paragraph and C36 contain the counterexample. The count anchored $k$ steps ahead of the inner vertex of $P_4$ gives first-step shares $0.667, 0.600, 0.615, 0.618$ ($k=2,3,5,10$). Each is a function of the current vertex, and the limit is MERW.
- The same paragraph, the (M2) cost line and the T2 row then say (M2)'s note-6 cost applies only "unless it is anchored".
  - An anchored count is no longer Paper 1's count: it gives up equal weight on co-existing evolutions, which is (M2)'s reason to exist.
  - On C13's 12-leaf tree, the count anchored two steps ahead gives the $A$-leaves $1/10$ and the $B$-leaves $1/15$, where the fixed depth-3 count gives $1/12$ each.
- So the paragraph credits (M1) with an exclusive note-6 property it does not have, and credits (M2) with a note-6 escape that costs it its defining property. The honest trade-off is: state-function weights (note 6) **or** equal weight at a fixed cut, not both in general; each option keeps one.
- This traces to my own #154 T2 remark. It carried the qualifier "without looking ahead in $R$", which v0.7 dropped, and it never noted what anchoring costs the count.

**(ii) "13 or 14".** At $c=0.01$ under (a), the exact law of the jump count is $P(12)=4.9\times10^{-5}$, $P(13)=0.1581$, $P(14)=0.8418$. The body's "a history jumps 13 or 14 times" (§4.2) and "13 or 14 at $c=0.01$" (§9) state a 20,000-sample observation as fact. The pin C33 marks it sampled; the body does not.

**Why not FAIL.** No law of $R$ is smuggled. No Hope or Open item reads as a result. (M1)/(M2), T1–T3 and every open-call row stay undecided, with costs on both sides. The HOLD is about stating one balance accurately, not about taking a side.
