**Against the paper:**

- C33 agrees:
  - "$\lvert B_R\rvert=2$ at both turning points of $[10,20]$ for $c=0.1,0.01$";
  - maximum apastron 23.24 at $c=0.1$ (the outward apastron target);
  - at $c=0.1$ one jump, since no post-jump orbit has an admissible edge (3 reachable orbits, 2 stuck).
- The c = 0.01 census recomputed with the new code gives **29,119 reachable orbits and 14,478 stuck**, as in C33.
- The §4.2 inward chain is admissible for 12 steps and fails at step 13 (target 8.7383287, which turns into an apastron of $[8.66401, 8.73833]$).
- C6's $X'$ ($r=9.8926$) has its outward edge failing (9.99827) and its inward edge passing.

**General or orbit-specific?** Lemma (proved, and checked on 3,059 edges with 0 mispredictions in `lemma.py`):
- A turning point at $r$ with static-frame tangential speed $v$ is a periastron iff $v^2>1/(r-2)$, because $dV/dr>0\iff(L/r)^2(r-3)>1$.
- The rule keeps $v$, and $1/(r-2)$ decreases in $r$. So **the edge into the orbit's radial range (outward at a periastron, inward at an apastron) keeps the source's turning type**. Its orbit then lies wholly beyond the target, and it **fails (a) at every turning point of every orbit, for every $c$**, provided the jump does not reach the throat.
- The other edge (inward at a periastron, outward at an apastron) passes iff its target keeps its type and its orbit reaches back to $r_s$. That holds at both turning points of $[10,20]$ for $c\le0.1$. It fails in 29,120 of the 58,238 such edges in the $c=0.01$ census, and also at step 13 of the inward chain.
- `census.py` tallies these. With (a), $c=0.01$: all 58,238 into-range edges fail. Without (a), depth 7: $c=0.01$, 0 exceptions; $c=0.1$, all 3,008 into-range edges fail. A grid of bound orbits at $c$ from 0.001 to 0.3: all 740 into-range edges fail.

So the reversal is general, not specific to this orbit: what matters is the turning type, not the direction. **The precise correct claim:** at every turning point, the edge into the orbit's radial range fails (a). The other edge passes on the instance $[10,20]$, but not in general. As a corollary, at most one admissible edge exists per turning point for every orbit (C33's $m\le1$ is a theorem, not only a census fact). The paper's sentences state the passing half without scope. Claude's instance-scoped NEW strings are correct and add no claim. The lemma is recorded here, not proposed as text.
