## Verdict in one breath

The public convention of §2, the firewalls, and the EPP1/EPP2 separation are careful and honest, and the v0.2 → v0.2.1 polish is clean. But the central construction does not do what the paper needs it to do: under Continuous World Switching exactly as postulated in §4, the branch set $B(O^u)$ of §5 is a singleton for every oriented observer. World switching is invisible in $\mathrm{Obs}$, no branch point exists, EPP1 has nothing to act on, and the §5 stress test, the §9 open problem and the §11 gate already have forced answers. This follows from the paper's own analyticity lock plus the identity-theorem fact it states in §3, and it is already proved in the in-house notes the paper cites as background. That is a HOLE, and it is the reason for the HOLD. A second tier of HOLD items is definitional (the "unique simply-connected patch," the compatibility relation, the cardinality of $E_W$, the carrier of the "bit," the meaning of "Obs-local" and of EPP1's weight). A third tier concerns presentation: as it stands the file is an internal specification, not a paper, and would be desk-rejected before reaching a referee. None of this reopens Born, Einstein, or Paper 1's elementary object.

## 1. HOLE — under CWS as stated, $|B(O^u)| = 1$ for every $O^u$

### 1.1 Claim

Fix an oriented observer $O^u=(O,u)$. With CWS postulates 1–4 (§4) and the §5 definition of allowed continuation, every allowed continuation at $O^u$ coincides, on a positive initial interval, with a single curve $\gamma_{O^u}$ determined by $O^u$ alone. Hence $B(O^u)=\{[\gamma_{O^u}]\}$ under the default equivalence (same infinite jet of the Obs-path at $0$) and also under the weaker one (agreement on a common initial interval). Branch points, defined in §5 as $|B(O^u)|\ge 2$, do not exist.

### 1.2 Argument

*Step 1 (the first piece is fixed by $O^u$).* An allowed continuation is a germ at $0$ of a path that is "piecewise" a geodesic lift, so it has a first piece $[0,\tau_1)$, $\tau_1>0$, on which it is the lift of a timelike geodesic $\sigma_1$ in some world $W_1$, starting at an occurrence of $O$ with $\dot\sigma_1(0)=u$. By the several-variable identity theorem the paper itself invokes in §2, the germ $O$ fixes the metric on a connected neighbourhood of that occurrence up to pointed isometry, whatever $W_1$ is. The geodesic with initial data $(p,u)$ in that neighbourhood is unique (analytic ODE). The jets of the metric along it are therefore functions of $(O,u)$ alone. So the first piece agrees on some $[0,\tau')$ with one curve $\gamma_{O^u}$, independent of $W_1$, of its topology, of which anchor was used, and of whether $\sigma_1$ is later incomplete. (In-house: `geodesic-lifts.md` Prop. 3.2.)

*Step 2 (equivalence at $0$).* Under the default equivalence, Step 1 already puts every allowed continuation in the class of $\gamma_{O^u}$. Under the weaker equivalence, Step 1 gives agreement on $[0,\tau')$, and proper-time parameterisation leaves no reparameterisation freedom. Either way $|B(O^u)|=1$. Nothing about switches has been used.

*Step 3 (switches never change the path later either).* At a switch time $\tau_s$ the traveler continues as a geodesic lift in $W_2$ from an occurrence of the oriented germ $O^u(\tau_s)$. Step 1 at $\tau_s$ shows the $W_2$-lift coincides near $\tau_s$ with the $W_1$-lift continued. Both lifts are real-analytic in $\tau$ (Fermi coordinates along an analytic geodesic; in-house `geodesic-lifts.md` Thm 3), so the one-variable identity theorem — the very fact §3 states for type (i) — extends the agreement to the whole common domain (in-house Cor. 3.1; `patch-edge.md` Thm 7 covers the edge of the simply-connected patch). So the set of Obs-paths from $O^u$ under CWS is exactly $\{\gamma_{O^u}|_{[0,T)} : 0<T\le T_{\max}\}$. A switch can change how long the path lasts; it cannot change where it goes.

Two consequences for §4 follow at once. The "substance note" has the situation backwards: infinite-order matching at a switch is not a strong constraint that "may force fewer switches than a naive EKT billiard cartoon suggests"; it is satisfied automatically, and switching is unconstrained precisely because it is invisible. And postulate 3 ($C^\infty$) and postulate 4 (orientation does not jump) are implied by postulates 1 and 2, since $u$ is part of the shared oriented germ and the lift on either side of a switch is the same analytic curve.

### 1.3 Escape routes examined

Each of the following was tried as a way to obtain two inequivalent continuations; each fails.

| Route | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| Switch at the edge of the simply-connected patch $P(O)$ | There is no germ at the edge to switch at. If both worlds continue the geodesic analytically through $\tau_\partial$, the continuations coincide (Thm 7(2)); if one does not, there is at most one continuation (Thm 7(3)–(4)). |
| Incomplete geodesic in one world | Shortens the path; does not fork it. Not an element of $B(O^u)$, which is a set of germs at $0$. |
| Sibling anchors in one $M$ | Occurrences of the same germ are locally isometric; same lift. |
| Distinct topologies, quotients, coverings, monodromy | A geodesic in a quotient lifts to the cover; jets are local. Multiplicity of $E_W$ is locally invisible (Cor. 3.1) and constant along the curvelet (`ensemble-labels.md` Thm 4). |
| Null pieces | Null geodesics carry no proper time and no unit timelike $u$; §2's oriented object excludes them (see nit 4.1). |
| Switches accumulating at $\tau=0$ | Each piece still lies on $\gamma_{O^u}$. |
| Echeverria–Klinkhammer–Thorne multiplicity | Lives in impulsive self-collisions of a matter body on a fixed background, which postulates 1 and 4 exclude and which the ontology (metric germ only) has no matter to supply. |

### 1.4 This is already in the author's notes

The result is not new to the project. `geodesic-lifts.md` (Prop. 3.2: from one $O$, a unique curvelet, "not a star of lifts"; Cor. 3.1: "delayed local 'knows which path' is killed; multiplicity of $E(O)$ is locally invisible"), `patch-edge.md` (Thm 7: "the hitchhiker story is dead at the boundary too"), and `phi-straits.md` (Thm 8: under geodesic hitchhiking the set of distinct evolutions from one $O$ "has cardinality one"; Thm 9: any law with real-analytic solution curves cannot delayed-fork, and "concatenating disagreeing analytic pieces at $\tau_\partial$ is not a solution of it") say exactly this. `type-ii-adopted.md` §0 then defines type-(ii) edges as precisely those pairs $(O,O')$ that "do not arise as successive germs along a geodesic lift," and Prop. 13 proves the locked data at $O$ supply no such edge; `tree-as-structure.md` Prop. 12 explains that delayed forks escape Theorem 2 only because graph-paths "are not required to be analytic curves." The public CWS requires every piece to be a geodesic lift. It therefore reverses, silently, the one in-house conclusion that made type (ii) different from type (i). The three v0.2 PASS reviews checked firewall hygiene and definitional form; none asked whether the defined $B(O^u)$ is ever non-trivial.

### 1.5 Consequences through the draft

- **§3.** "Analytic lifts in a single $M$ do not delayed-fork" is scoped too narrowly. The no-delayed-fork result is about real-analytic curves in $\mathrm{Obs}$, whatever worlds realise them, and CWS paths are such curves. "World switching is the main example" of across-worlds motion is false in $\mathrm{Obs}$: CWS is type (i) with an optional change of lifetime.
- **§5.** The hard gate is met vacuously. Every stress-test question has a forced answer: $|B(O^{u*})|=1$; not finite-with-two, not countable, not a continuum; strong matching collapses the cartoon to one continuation for every $O^u$, not only the EKT one; nothing to exhaust.
- **§6.** "The recorded bit enters the local germ while the system may be far" cannot happen along any CWS path: the germ at $\tau$ is a function of $O^u$ alone, and the hitchhiking ensemble is constant along the curvelet (Thm 4(1)). The ensemble does not go from mixed to purified; members can only drop out when their geodesic ends. See also 2.5.
- **§7.** EPP1 has empty domain.
- **§8.** No tree with two arms is available inside the framework, so "non-50-50 trees can be drawn on $\mathrm{Obs}$" currently has no witness.
- **§9, §11.** The open problem is not "what makes $B(O^u)$ discrete under CWS." It is: which change of postulate makes $|B(O^u)|\ge 2$ at all, and then countable without a hand-put grain. The §11 gate ("until $B(O^u)$ is discrete in a controlled example") is met trivially by the Minkowski germ, where $B$ is a singleton and $\tau_\partial=\infty$ (in-house Thm 6), which is not what the gate intends.

### 1.6 What would repair it, and what each repair costs

To fork in $\mathrm{Obs}$ one of the three CWS commitments has to go. Each route is a conscious extra of the kind §9's last bullet promises to flag, and each runs into a second problem that §5's two equivalences create between them.

- **(a) Drop geodesic pieces.** Allow $C^\infty$ non-geodesic motion, i.e. smooth Direction Switching (§4's "alternative"). Delayed forks then exist. But the default equivalence (same infinite jet at the fork germ) identifies any two $C^\infty$ continuations that fork after a shared prefix, since their left-jets agree and $C^\infty$ makes the right-jets agree; it is structurally blind to smooth forks. The weaker equivalence sees them, but then counts every flat-function perturbation as a distinct arm, and $B$ is a continuum (in-house Thms 17–18: neighbourhoods and continuous invariants give one class or a continuum).
- **(b) Drop orientation continuity.** A finite kink in $u$ is a jump; the arm set is then the unit hyperboloid modulo the isotropy of the germ, a continuum, unless a grain on the hyperboloid is put in by hand.
- **(c) Drop continuity of the Obs-path or analyticity of worlds.** A jump in $\mathrm{Obs}$ is exactly an in-house $R$-edge (`tree-as-structure.md` Def. 7); countable out-degree is then a restriction on $R$, not a theorem (`countable-outdegree.md` Thm 31). A $C^k$-glued world is not in the locked category (`patch-edge.md` Prop. 7.3).

The honest options for v0.3 are therefore: (i) keep CWS and state the result as a proposition — "under CWS 1–4, $B(O^u)=\{[\gamma_{O^u}]\}$; world switching changes lifetime, not path" — repositioning CWS as a baseline / null result rather than "a working type-(ii) motion law" (§1); or (ii) revise the postulates along (a), (b) or (c), name the extra, and then confront the singleton-or-continuum dilemma of the two equivalences explicitly. Either way §5 should say what would count as success: a motion rule plus an equivalence for which, at some $O^u$, the class set is provably countable with at least two elements and no hand-put grain. And §5 should add one worked example — the Minkowski germ is fully computable and currently returns a singleton.

None of this touches Paper 1's elementary object, the analyticity lock, the relative-ensemble reading, or the Born / Einstein firewalls. It is a statement about the motion postulate only.

## 2. HOLD — definitional and mathematical

### 2.1 "Jet ≡ the unique simply-connected real-analytic patch" (§1, §2, §9)

The sentence "Equivalently, $O$ is the unique simply-connected real-analytic patch determined by that jet (unique analytic continuation)" has two readings and holds under neither in the form written. At the germ level it is true (in-house Thm A) but "simply-connected" and "the" add nothing: every small ball is simply connected and uniqueness is uniqueness of the germ. At the maximal level uniqueness fails. Example: present the flat germ in Rindler coordinates, $g=\mathrm{diag}(-\rho^2,1,1,1)$ at $\rho=1$, $u=\partial_\eta$. The components are polynomials; continuing them gives the wedge $\rho>0$, simply connected, on which that geodesic exits in proper time $1$. Present the same germ in inertial coordinates and it continues to all of Minkowski, where the same geodesic is complete. Both are simply-connected real-analytic patches realising the germ, and Minkowski does not immerse isometrically into the wedge while fixing the germ (any such map is the identity by the identity theorem). So "the unique patch" is not well-defined at the maximal level; the in-house construction (`analytic-patch-from-jet.md` Thm B) continues chart components and inherits the chart, and $\tau_\partial$ inherits the ambiguity ($1$ versus $\infty$ for the same germ). The classical, chart-free version of the same fact is the non-uniqueness of maximal analytic extensions (Taub–NUT; Chruściel and Isenberg, Phys. Rev. D 48, 1616 (1993)). A GR reader will raise this on sight. Fix: write "the germ, equivalently any simply-connected analytic patch realising it (unique as a germ)"; drop "the unique … patch (unique analytic continuation)" and the definite article; say in §5 what "where a shared tube ends" refers to once no canonical maximal patch is claimed.

### 2.2 Compatibility (§2)

"$O_i\Leftrightarrow O_j$ when each is an allowable future of the other … and the reverse. … Its transitive closure is reachability if world switching is allowed." Three defects. (i) $M$ is not required to be time-oriented and the germs are bare, so "future" has no meaning and "and the reverse" is vacuous: reversing the connecting geodesic, or the time orientation of $M$, witnesses the converse. (ii) The relation is symmetric by construction, so its transitive closure is symmetric; reachability under forward, orientation-preserving motion is a preorder that is not symmetric (a comoving germ in a crunching FLRW world reaches later comoving germs and is never reached back). (iii) "Reachability" is never defined, and under CWS as stated it is just the set of germs on $\gamma_{O^u}$ (§1). Fix: define a one-step relation $O_i\to O_j$ (future-directed timelike geodesic from an occurrence of $O_i$ to one of $O_j$ in some time-oriented $M$), let reachability be its transitive closure, and let $\Leftrightarrow$ be its symmetric part; preferably define both on oriented germs.

### 2.3 $E_W$, $E_M$ and EPP2 (§2, §7, §9)

As defined in §2 ("the set of pointed worlds compatible with the germ," with "several distinct anchors" counted separately and $M$ merely "a connected real-analytic Lorentzian 4-manifold"), $E_W(O)$ carries no isometry quotient, no Hausdorff or second-countability condition, and no maximality condition. Without a quotient it is a proper class. With the in-house quotient (`ensemble-labels.md` Defs 1–2: pointed analytic isometry, second-countable $W$) it still has continuum cardinality for every germ, by deleting closed sets away from the anchor. For the Minkowski germ it is a continuum even among complete worlds: the flat cylinders $\mathbb{R}^{1,3}/L\mathbb{Z}$ are pairwise non-isometric for distinct $L$. So "weight proportional to $|E_W|$" (EPP2a/b) is a ratio of continua, and Paper 1's own Bertrand argument (§5 there) applies to it: any ratio can be produced by a choice of parameterisation, and a "matching density" (§7) is a measure, i.e. exactly the extra structure Paper 1 says cannot be read off the ontology. The §9 phrase "countable $|E|$" is false as written. In-house Prop. 5.1 lists countability of $E(O)$ as open; the closed-set-deletion argument closes it in the negative unless inextendibility is imposed, and the cylinder family shows inextendibility is not enough. Separately, the public anchor-counting convention differs from the in-house occurrence-modulo-isometry convention, and the two give different $|E_W|$; the difference is not flagged. Fix: adopt the in-house definitions in §2 (connected, Hausdorff, second-countable, time-orientable; occurrences modulo pointed isometry), state whether inextendible $M$ is required, state that $E_W$ is a continuum in the simplest example, and either withdraw EPP2 as a count or restate it as a hope that presupposes a measure on ensembles.

### 2.4 "Each arm's post-branch world ensemble" is undefined (§7)

$E_W$ is defined on germs; an arm is a class of path germs; no map from arms to ensembles is given. The two natural candidates disagree: the fiber $E_W(\gamma(\tau))$ at some $\tau>0$ along a representative depends on $\tau$ and can jump (in-house Thm 4(3)); the subset of the pre-branch $E_W(O^u)$ whose in-world lift lies in the arm is what the product ansatz $\{\mathrm{A},\mathrm{B}\}\times\mathcal{R}$ presupposes, but is ill-posed under CWS because an allowed path is not tied to one world. Until one is chosen, EPP2 has no domain and the "hoped hinge" compares an undefined object with $B(O^u)$. Fix: define $E_W(b)$ for $b\in B(O^u)$ before EPP2a and say which reading the ansatz uses.

### 2.5 The "bit" has no carrier; analyticity's cost and the absence of matter are undisclosed (§2, §5, §6, §7)

The ontology contains a metric germ and nothing else. "Same measured bit (for example spin up versus spin down)" appears in the definitional §5 as an "allowed further quotient," "the measured bit locks other topology" in §7, and "the recorded bit enters the local germ" in §6, yet no object of §2 carries a bit, no discrete lock-side invariant of the germ is named that could induce the quotient, and Paper 1 §10's sentence "Matter fields are open … They are absences" was dropped. Under §1 the §6 sentence is not merely "not ontology"; it is inconsistent with §§2–4 (nothing enters a germ along an analytic geodesic lift, and a topological label never appears in a germ that is by construction the simply-connected patch). Relatedly, §2 justifies real-analyticity only by "jet determines lump" and never states its cost: on a connected analytic $M$ the infinite jet at one point fixes the metric on all of $M$, so a world is globally rigid once $(M,p)$ is chosen, there are no localized geometric degrees of freedom, generic smooth GR solutions are excluded, and every ensemble member is a continuation or covering of the same local geometry (in-house `ensemble-labels.md` §2 says this; the public draft does not). Fix: a short "assumptions and their cost" paragraph in §2; move all bit language into §6 in the conditional and mark it as requiring a mechanism CWS does not provide; replace the §5 clause by "a further quotient by a named discrete invariant, if one is adopted (none is here)."

### 2.6 EPP1's "weight" is never interpreted (§5, §7, §8, §10)

The draft never says whether all arms of $B(O^u)$ are realised (Paper 1 §§3–6: multiplicity is ontological and counting is a typicality measure over co-existing evolutions) or one arm is followed (`mathematical-framework.md`: "branch points … are followed with equal probability"). The two readings differ on the Bell remark (a multi-outcome reading exits Bell's single-outcome premise trivially and is not specific to $\mathrm{Obs}$; a single-outcome reading does not) and on §10's Carroll–Sebens disclaimer (under the typicality reading EPP1 *is* a self-locating branch-counting rule, on a different branch structure and without a quantum state; that, not the form of the rule, is the difference). Fix: one sentence in §5 or §7 fixing the reading, then adjust §8 and the §10 bullet.

### 2.7 "Obs-local" is never defined (§1, §6, §8, §10)

It is the paper's one positive program claim and it appears four times without a definition. Since §9 defaults to option (1), no topology or tangent space on $\mathrm{Obs}$, "local in $\mathrm{Obs}$" cannot mean "depends only on a neighbourhood." The only available reading is "determined by $O^u$ alone," under which every object in the paper ($B(O^u)$, $E_W(O^u)$, both EPPs) is Obs-local by construction and "nonlocal in spacetime" is automatic because $O^u$ fixes the geometry of the whole patch (2.5). A definition is not a program claim. Fix: define it (e.g. "a branching rule is Obs-local if $B(O^u)$ and its weights depend on $O^u$ and not on the hidden ensemble label $W$"), say plainly that this is not a spacetime-locality notion, and name the contrast class; or delete the term.

### 2.8 Oriented observer modulo isotropy; transport of $u$ across a switch (§2, §4, §5)

"Nothing in the bare metric jet prefers a particular $u$ among the unit hyperboloid at $p$" is true, but a lump has no canonical $T_p$: once presentations are identified by pointed analytic diffeomorphisms, $u$ is defined only modulo the isotropy group of the germ (in-house $\sim_L$ carries $u$ along; the public "pointed" does not say so). For the Minkowski germ every unit timelike $u$ is boost-related to every other, so there is exactly one oriented observer, not a hyperboloid; for an FLRW germ a single rapidity. This does not create a fork of $B$, but it changes any $|E_W(O^u)|$ count, and it means "orientation does not jump" needs a transport rule for $u$ across a switch (the identification of tangent spaces between worlds is fixed only up to that isotropy). Fix: define $O^u$ as the class of $(j,u)$ under pointed diffeomorphisms carrying $u$, add "modulo the isotropy of the germ," and state that $u$ is transported across a switch by the germ isometry.

### 2.9 The pointer to `mathematical-framework.md` (§2)

"Precise working definitions live in `mathematical-framework.md`" points the reader to a file that is less precise than §2/§5 and inconsistent with them: it writes $B(O)$ throughout (which §5 forbids), reads $V$ as a tangent vector in $T_O\mathrm{Obs}$ (the option §2 refuses to lock), keeps the promissory "Equal probability needs …" opening that v0.2.1 removed, states the compatibility clause backwards, and has no lump quotient. Fix: either "informal living notes are kept in …; the definitions of record are §2 and §5 here," or synchronise the file.

## 3. HOLD — presentation, before any external circulation

These do not block the next internal version, but they would be the first thing an editor sees.

2. **Process text in the body.** The Status and Changelog blocks; §2 "clears the Ontology review of the v0.1 outline," "Do not smuggle a locked chart"; §3 "In-house work explored type-(ii) leftovers on a relation $R$ … That inventory is not restarted here"; §5 "Do not write ambiguous $B(O)$ for the EPP story"; §7 "Do not reuse the 2008 word 'egalitarianism' (desk-reject risk …)"; §8 "Place this section so reviewers cannot confuse the no-no-go with the prize"; §10 "In-house theorem numbers from `papers/` are background"; all of §11. Keep version history in the repository, not the paper.
3. **Undefined insider vocabulary.** "Paper 1" (cited nowhere), "lock/locked," "firewall," "in-house," "no-no-go," "leftover inventory," "relation $R$," "hitchhiking," "lump," "Obs-lift," "Fermi-slice," "shared tube," bare surnames ("Hadley-adjacent," "Not Stoica"). 4. **No reference list.** Roughly a dozen external works are named (Gielen–Wise, Larsson, Stoica, EKT, Hadley, Wallace, Deutsch–Wallace, Carroll–Sebens, Vaidman, Bell, Everett, Strayhorn 2008) with no bibliography and no citation markers, so none of the "not X" disclaimers can be checked. 5. **§10's "nearby, not us" omits EPP1's actual neighbours.** EPP1 is a branch-counting rule; the list names only the anti-counting camp. An outside referee will expect Saunders, Proc. R. Soc. A 477, 20210600 (2021) (equi-amplitude carving); Khawaja, Brit. J. Phil. Sci. 77 (2026) 313–344 (equal-weight indexed branch-counting as rational credence, diverging from Born — exactly the EPP1-versus-Born tension); Hanson 2003 and Weidner, arXiv:2504.06495 (2025), which files Strayhorn 2008 under world counting and which Paper 1 §9 already disowns; Adlam and Barandes, "Against Many Worlds," arXiv:2607.17086 (2026), a general obstruction to axiomatic, deductive and inductive routes to Born inside many worlds (say why it does not apply to arms on $\mathrm{Obs}$, or concede that it does apply to the Born hope); and Stoica, arXiv:2209.08623, which counts 3-geometries with a $|\Psi|$ weight and is the closest cousin of EPP2b. Zurek's envariance belongs beside Carroll–Sebens.
6. **§6 and §8 promise objects they do not deliver.** §6 is titled "measurement cartoon" and contains no cartoon; §8 refers to "the toy" and contains no toy, no tree, no two-observer configuration, and no statement of which Bell premise is denied. There is no figure, no worked example, and no equation of motion in the paper. Given §1 above, the honest version of §8 today is one paragraph: an explicit model is not yet exhibited.
7. **Disclaimer density.** Roughly 44% of sentences are negations; §10 is entirely disclaimer, and the same eight disclaimers recur three or four times each. One firewall paragraph at the end of the introduction and one "what this paper does not claim" section modelled on Paper 1 §§9–10 would do.

## 4. NITS

1. **"Causal geodesic"** (§2 compatibility, §3, §4(1), §5(2)) versus "unit timelike $u$" and proper time $\tau$: null geodesics have neither. Write "timelike" throughout, or say null observers are excluded.
2. **§4 substance note** attributes rigidity to $C^\infty$ matching; see 1.2. Rewrite once §1 is resolved.
3. **§1 "a working type-(ii) motion law"** versus §4 "CWS constrains which paths are allowed. It is not yet an ODE." An admissibility class is not a law of motion; say "admissibility condition."
4. **"The 2008 word 'egalitarianism'."** The published 2008 AFLB paper uses "outcome counting" and mentions "egalitarianism" once, attributed to Greaves; the manuscript that used it as its term (title and throughout) is the rejected 2008 SHPMP submission. If the sentence survives, say so; better, drop it (3.2) and just use EPP1/EPP2.
5. **§10 "Vaidman typicality talk."** Vaidman's position is the measure-of-existence rule ($|\alpha|^2$ as the weight, counting rejected); Paper 1 §9 has this right. Typicality-in-Everett is Everett / Barrett / Lazarovici.
6. **§7/§10 "Hilbert-space ontic counting"** for Stoica: his ontic states are continuous-basis (classical-field) configurations with the $|\psi|^2$ density supplied by the state; a Stoica-sympathetic reader will object to "Hilbert-space ontic." The $|\psi|^2$-in-the-density point is the real disanalogy; keep that, drop the other.
7. **EKT is matter non-uniqueness on a fixed background** (nonrelativistic billiard ball, no self-gravity). The draft borrows the shape (several continuations from one datum) without saying that the object that is non-unique is different. One clause in §4.
8. **Hard gate wording.** $B(O^u)$ has no topology, so "discrete" is undefined; write "at most countable." For countably infinite $B$ no uniform probability exists; an exhaustion is chosen structure (different exhaustions give different limits), and the draft never says what one would be.
9. **Rational weights.** EPP1 on finitely many arms with the same-bit quotient gives only rational weights; a generic $|a|^2$ needs countably many arms plus an exhaustion or a frequency limit. §8's "hand-built trees that match $|a|^2$" overstates, and hand-chosen arm counts per bit are Paper 1 §8's "multitude of copies" circle in operational form.
10. **Switch semantics** (§4, §5): define "piece" (finitely many on $[0,\varepsilon)$), say whether pieces are closed segments, and that a switch germ must be realised at a point of each world.
11. **§3 last sentence** ("It is not the claim that 'germs still agree' at a purported fork instant — that would already be no split") reads, cold, as denying that a fork has agreeing germs at the fork instant.
