### B. The reconciled §11 Kretschmann sentence

New text: "a through-throat path gives it only at turning points with $\varepsilon=2\Delta\ell$ (the Proposition of §8.2), periastra as well as apastra; under (A) a decision there is reached along a segment (§8.2 items 2 and 7), so it sits on a bound orbit and the germ is recurrent, and the near-throat self-target, a plunging orbit's apastron, is no counterexample, since it, like an unbound orbit's periastron, is reached only by the jump that lands on it and so carries no decision."

- **Self-targets:** right. $r'=r_0$ iff $\varepsilon=2\Delta\ell$ (#290 (T3)). This depends only on $r_0$, so it holds at any turning-point type. That is the point of my #306 R4.
- **Unbound periastra: handled correctly.** The sentence no longer claims recurrence there. It says such a point carries no decision. That holds under (A): §8.2 item 7 gives one arm on arrival by jump, so there is no decision there.
  - On the Kretschmann rule, vertices are turning points (C38). Jump targets are turning points (#290 (T1)). So an unbound orbit, which has only one turning point, is entered only at its periastron, by a jump, and the next segment runs outward and never returns to a vertex.
  - A plunging orbit is the same: a turning point at $r\le3$ is an apastron (#290 §1), reached only by the jump that lands on it.
  - So a decision reached along a segment needs a second turning point on the same orbit, which means a bound, and so recurrent, orbit (case 2 of #292: the self-pair is type (i) and §8.2 item 3 removes it).
  - The sentence is consistent with #292's original reasoning, with #290, and with my #306 R4.
- **One residual gap: the root.** The root's arrival flag is $\mathrm{seg}$ "by convention" (§8.2 item 7). So the root has a decision without being "reached along a segment".
  - For the instance this is harmless: the root is the $r=10$ periastron of $[10,20]$, which is bound and not a self-target.
  - But "a decision there is reached along a segment" is false for a root that is itself a self-target, or that lies on the inbound leg of an unbound or plunging orbit.
  - "On the Kretschmann rule" reads as the instance. Recommended string **K1** scopes the sentence to non-root decisions. #310 has the same gap (**K2**).
- **The pointer.** "(the Proposition of §8.2)" points to a statement that covers only into-range edges; the $r'=r_0$ landing for away edges is #290 (T3). This is acceptable as a pointer, and I propose no string.
