## 2. Main question: cleared, honestly confined, or not addressed

In v0.5 the problem was "still relocated, not fatal". The six devices that leaked were:
- the (a) cost row;
- per-history discreteness;
- the isotropy clause;
- the Paper 1 statement;
- the advertised no-no-go sketch;
- the dropped disclosures.

v0.6 closes all six (§3, response audit).

What remains is the singleton-or-continuum problem in the target direction. A finite $\mathrm{succ}_R$ needs a law, and a law needs a motivation. The draft states this where it belongs:
- **§9 Open:** "The law of $R$: continuum candidates, and motivation among admissible (slice, rule) pairs"; "The slice: which scalar …"; "The measure: undecided"; "(a) versus (b)"; "Whether any future $R$ can meet (J1) or (J2)".
- **§5.4:** "The relocated problem ends at Bertrand's problem over admissible (slice, rule) pairs".
- **Status line:** "still relocated as a problem, with a final address".

That is **honestly confined**: the remaining problem is an Open item, labelled as such, in every place it matters.

It is **not fatal**. The Option E residue has a model: the Kretschmann rule on a bound Schwarzschild orbit has finite stars ($\le3$) and per-history no Zeno (C7). Row 2 adds that its (a)-restriction is a model too, so "not fatal" does not depend on giving up (a).

## 3. The cover's re-check table, row by row

### Row 1: (a) cost row, one analytic-continuation class, lock-side. **Answered; one wording residual.**

C1, C2 and C3 recompute exactly (§1). The pair "periastron → radial germ at $r=8$" is type (ii): the orbit's $K$ stays in $[7.5\times10^{-7},4.8\times10^{-5}]$ and never reaches $1.83\times10^{-4}$. It is (a)-admissible because the target's own past curvelet passes through the $r=10$ lump.

The **lock-side form** is correct. Once the arrival tangent $u'$ is fixed, the realising geodesic is the target's own past curvelet (Prop. 3.2 uniqueness). So admissibility is a property of the pair of oriented germs, and "$O'^{u'}\in F(O)\setminus\gamma_{O^u}$" is equivalent to (a) as defined.

One bookkeeping point, consistent with the draft: $F(O)$ must use the past curvelet's full maximal analytic domain. For a plunging target that domain runs back through the white-hole region. The $c=0.1$ apastron target $16.7925$ emerged from there, covering $r\in(0,16.79]$, so it misses $r=20$ and fails (a), as §5.4 says.

"Analytic-continuation class, up to monodromy" rather than "maximal world" is the right object.

*Residual (NIT R1).* The row's last sentence reads: "The cost of (a) is real, but it is not “no curvature change”: what (a) forbids are exactly the cross-continuation jumps a measurement picture would want."
- Read as a set equality, "exactly" is false. It contradicts the draft's own $F(O)$ form and its (b) gloss ("not-(a) is not the same as cross-continuation"). The Kretschmann outward edges stay in one class, change curvature, and fail (a).
- Suggested text: "(a) forbids every edge whose target lies off $F(O)$. That includes all cross-continuation jumps, the ones a measurement picture would want, and also same-class jumps whose target's past curvelet misses $O$."
- Optionally, give the reason a measurement picture wants cross-continuation jumps. By analytic rigidity, outcome geometries that differ locally lie in different continuation classes.

### Row 2: Kretschmann example models the schema without (a). **Answered; undersold.**

C30, C31 and both apastron targets reproduce exactly (§1). "At the periastron the outward edge fails, at the apastron the inward edge fails" holds for $c=0.1$ and $c=0.01$.

*Residual (NIT R2).* The draft says: "The Kretschmann rule meets it under (b) only" (§5.4). Status: "a model of it without realisation (a)". Both are true of the rule **as stated**. But the same rule **restricted to its (a)-admissible edges** is also stated, uniform and lock-side, since the filter is the lock-side test $F(O)$. It gives $\lvert B_R\rvert=2$ at both turning points of $[10,20]$, for both $c$. So the schema has a model under (a) as well, and "not fatal" does not depend on dropping (a).

The (a)-model is thin at $c=0.1$. After one jump, neither new orbit has an (a)-admissible edge (§1). So almost every history jumps exactly once and is geodesic afterwards. The pure-geodesic history, of weight $2^{-n}$ after $n$ decisions, is the only one that never jumps. At $c=0.01$ the (a)-model keeps branching (row 12). One sentence in §4.2 or §5.4 would record both facts.

This corrects my own #128 §1 table, which called the $c=0.01$ outward edge (a)-realisable by static recurrence. That is true of lumps, false of oriented germs with the arrival convention (C30).

### Row 6: Isotropy, $u$ modulo germ isotropy. **Answered.**

- **C9, C10 and C19 check** (§1).
- **On a generic Schwarzschild exterior germ** the point stabiliser is the rotations about the radial axis, plus reflections. Oriented germs modulo isotropy are then labelled by $(r,v_r,\lvert v_\perp\rvert)$, as C21 uses.
- **The Kretschmann rule is well defined on classes.** At a critical point $u^a\nabla_aK=0$, so $\nabla K\in u^\perp$ automatically, and $\hat n$ is isotropy-invariant.
- **§2's definition is well-posed and equivalent to "$u$ modulo germ isotropy".** It is the class of (presentation, $p$, $u$) under pointed analytic diffeomorphisms carrying $u$.
- **"Discontinuous in oriented observer space"** is now used in §3.
- **"Such a pair is never an edge"** (ESU) is right, since the pair's source is an ESU germ and $V^\tau=\emptyset$ there.

*Paper 1's "time direction" read as $u$.* Paper 1 §2 says the time direction is what gives "later" and "proper time along which the object can move". A curve with proper time needs a tangent, so reading "direction" as a unit vector fits better than a bare time orientation. The draft calls its reading "a reading, stated as such", which is honest. (On the bare-orientation reading, $V$ would add three parameters over a generic lump. The draft's sentence already covers that possibility.)

*What I got beyond the draft (NIT R9a).*
