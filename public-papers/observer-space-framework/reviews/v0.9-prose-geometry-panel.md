## 2. §9 short proofs

- **Cut criterion.** Correct and complete. Weights over a finite cut sum to 1 because the cut meets every maximal path exactly once, so $D\equiv|C|$ follows.
  - Consistent with R4: per-vertex weights against the depth cut give D = 2 against 6 on the unbalanced tree.
- **Limit iff.** Correct and complete. Telescoping the sibling shares gives the forward direction on finite stars. The converse uses the positive limits, and dead ends are handled because they stay in the cut.
  - Consistent with R3: on the 3,2/2,3 alternating tree the depth-n share is 1/2 at odd depths and 3/5 at even, so there is no limit. R3 is a non-example and the proof does not claim otherwise.
- **MERW criterion.** Correct and complete.
  - Equating $P_{ij}=A_{ij}\psi_j/(\lambda\psi_i)$ with $A_{ij}/d_i$ forces $d_id_j=\lambda^2$ on every edge, so a connected graph is regular or bipartite biregular.
  - The converse was checked with $\psi\equiv1$ ($\lambda=d$) and with $\psi=(1,\sqrt{q_2/q_1})$ ($\lambda=\sqrt{q_1q_2}$).
  - Consistent with R2. The 5-path is neither regular nor biregular, so the transition criterion fails there, yet the walk-level match is exact for every length $L\ge2$. §9's walk-level sentence ("on some graphs, regular ones among them, it matches at every length") claims no iff, so this is no counterexample. The 4-path Fibonacci limit is not contradicted either.
  - **Nit (optional, no string).** The walk-tree clause, "D-constancy at every depth … equivalent to asymptotic sibling balance", follows from Limit + C36 + the criterion but is not itself proved in §9. It is not an overclaim, since it is stated as a consequence.
- **Burda et al. 2009.** PRL 102, 160602 (arXiv:0810.4113) supports the citation as made.
  - Its Eq. (7) is the MERW transition $P_{ij}=(A_{ij}/\lambda)\psi_j/\psi_i$ on a finite connected undirected graph. GRW is $A_{ij}/k_i$.
  - The paper says the two coincide on $k$-regular graphs. §9's bipartite-biregular half is proved in §9 and not attributed to Burda, which is correct.
