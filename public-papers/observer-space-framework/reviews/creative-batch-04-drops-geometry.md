## D7: transmit or reflect at a wall with a metric jump

**Verdict: the drop stands, with its reason made sharper.** No discrete two-option fork survives for typical data under any smoothing. The fork that appears for exceptional data, or across a change of smoothing, is a choice of added data, i.e. a law of $R$ in disguise.

1. **The jump itself is ill-posed.** If $g$ jumps across a hypersurface $W$, the Christoffel symbols contain a delta function multiplied by a jump, so the geodesic equation has no meaning as a distributional equation. Such metrics are also outside the Geroch–Traschen class (Geroch–Traschen 1987 (V), #332 R3), which requires a continuous metric. A thin shell in Israel form keeps $g$ continuous and lets only its normal derivative jump (Israel, *Nuovo Cim. B* 44, 1 (1966) (U)). Then the Christoffel symbols are bounded, and the batch's own "p continuous, no reflection" applies.
2. **The second Weierstrass–Erdmann root is not a free critical point.** For the length functional with a jump, matching the momentum along the wall and the normalisation gives $p_n=\pm\sqrt{\cdot}$ on the far side. The $+$ root is transmission with refraction. The $-$ root (reflection) is a corner lying wholly in one region. Moving that corner off $W$ into its own region changes the length at first order, because the first variation is $(\hat t_1-\hat t_2)\cdot\delta$, which is normal to $W$ and nonzero. So reflection is stationary only among curves *constrained* to touch $W$, as in Fermat optics. That gives one critical point, not two, whenever a transmitted root exists. When none exists (beyond the critical angle), the only option is reflection. The batch's "two roots" overstates the fork. (Geometry's argument; not checked against a source.)
3. **Smoothing, wall by wall.** Each mollified metric $g_\epsilon$ is smooth, so its geodesics are unique. The question is the limit as $\epsilon\to0$.
   - *Timelike wall, monotone profile.* Subcritical rays refract through; supercritical rays reach a turning point inside the layer and reflect. The limit is unique, the same for every monotone mollifier, and the outcome is fixed by the angle of incidence. There is no fork.
   - *Timelike wall, non-monotone profile (overshoot).* For incidence in a band near the critical angle, the outcome depends on the profile's shape, but it is still unique for each profile. The $\epsilon\to0$ limit is **not mollifier-independent** there. The "two options" are then two smoothings, i.e. two hosts. That is ensemble structure, or a rule that picks the mollifier, which is a law of $R$ in disguise.
   - *Exactly critical or grazing incidence.* The limit can be non-unique: sliding along $W$ versus leaving it, as in Filippov-type solutions of ODEs with a discontinuous right-hand side (Filippov, *Differential Equations with Discontinuous Righthand Sides*, Kluwer 1988 (U)). That data set has codimension 1 and is meagre (#336 (a)), and the choice there is a rule.
   - *Spacelike wall.* The normal is timelike, and the two roots are future- and past-directed. Only one is future-directed, so every future timelike geodesic crosses once and uniquely. There is no reflection for any smoothing.
   - *Null wall (impulsive wave).* The regularised geodesics have a unique limit that does not depend on the regularisation (Kunzinger–Steinbauer, *J. Math. Phys.* 40, 1479 (1999) (U)). There is no fork.
   - *The sign of the jump* only decides which side has a critical angle, so it moves the measure-zero band in the cases above. It does not create a fork for typical data.
4. **Low-regularity classes.** For $C^{1,1}$ metrics, geodesics are unique. Below that, non-uniqueness occurs: Hartman–Wintner, *Amer. J. Math.* 73, 132 (1951) (V per #342) for $C^{1,\alpha}$; Lipschitz ($C^{0,1}$) and $C^0$ settings in Chruściel–Grant, *Class. Quantum Grav.* 29, 145001 (2012) (U) and Sämann–Steinbauer, *J. Phys. Conf. Ser.* 968, 012010 (2018) (U). There the non-uniqueness is a continuum (Peano-type), not two options. This is D1's failure again, not D7's.

**What would fix a two-option choice:** only an added rule, either "which mollifier" or "at grazing, slide or leave". The added rule is the law of $R$. The batch's three conditions for what would pass (`batch-04.md`) are confirmed: D7 meets conditions (1) and (2) only on the exceptional set, and fails (3).

## D8: topology change through a trousers crotch

**Verdict: the drop stands, with corrections.** Free-fall continuation does not fork off the crotch. A discrete two-leg fork exists only for geodesics that hit the crotch point. Their continuation is undefined there, so choosing a leg is a rule.

1. **The crotch is singular by necessity.** A compact topology-changing Lorentzian cobordism with a nondegenerate metric has closed timelike curves (Geroch, *J. Math. Phys.* 8, 782 (1967) (U)). The usual alternative is Morse metrics, which degenerate at the critical points of a Morse function (Sorkin; Louko–Sorkin, *Class. Quantum Grav.* 14, 179 (1997) (U)). The crotch is such a point, and quantum fields there have a singular energy burst (Anderson–DeWitt, *Found. Phys.* 16, 91 (1986) (U)). At the crotch the geodesic equation is undefined, so "continuation through the crotch" is an ill-posed term (well-posedness principle).
2. **Off the crotch nothing forks.** Away from the critical point the metric is nondegenerate and smooth, and Prop 3.2 applies there (`papers/geodesic-lifts.md`). A geodesic from the waist that misses the crotch is unique, and it enters exactly one leg, determined by its data. The legs split the data by a separatrix, and the outcome is determined on either side.
3. **The hitting set is rare.** The crotch is a point. In $n$ dimensions, the data whose geodesic reaches it form a set of dimension $0+(n-1)+1=n$ inside a data space of dimension $2n-1$. Its codimension is therefore $n-1$: codimension 1 in 1+1 and codimension 3 in 3+1. The batch's "codim ≥2" is right for 3+1 but not for 1+1. The set is meagre either way (#336 (a)); "almost every" would smuggle in a measure.
4. **At the crotch the fork is binary in the leg, with a continuum inside each leg.** At an index-1 Lorentzian crotch, the future of the critical point has one future sector per leg (the double light cone of the Morse picture; Geometry's reading, (U)). Choosing a leg is two options, a number fixed by topology. But the direction inside the chosen leg's sector is a continuum, unless a matching rule (for example, conserved rapidity) is added. Both the leg choice and the matching are rules, which is a law of $R$ in disguise.
5. **The ensemble reading.** Two trousers hosts that differ only near the crotch are different hosts, so how the leg is chosen across hosts is a fact about $E(O)$, not branching of motion. This agrees with #336 and #338.
