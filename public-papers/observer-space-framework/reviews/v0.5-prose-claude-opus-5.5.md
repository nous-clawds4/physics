## 2. Main question: cleared, still relocated, or fatal

The cover's "What to re-check" table is answered in §3. This section gives the one-paragraph answer.

#117 said the #105 HOLE was *cleared as a definition* ($B_R$ given $R$) but *still relocated as a problem*. That problem sat in the law of $R$ and in three devices that claimed work they did not do: the finite-$k$ join clause, ensemble-timed $V^*$, and a "stated rule" gate. v0.5 removes or re-costs all three.

- The join clause is demoted, and its three finite-$k$ facts check out (row 1).
- $V^*$ is replaced by a lock-side slice. Membership $u^a\nabla_aK=0$ is a property of the oriented germ, not an ensemble vote (row 2).
- The gate now requires uniformity and motivation, with a Bertrand fallback (row 6).

So no device is now doing hidden work. The residue of the HOLE is the singleton-or-continuum problem *in the target direction*: a finite $\mathrm{succ}_R$ needs a law. It now sits at its final, declared address: the law of $R$, its motivation, and the choice of scalar and level for $V^\tau$ (§9).

That is **still relocated**: honestly now, with nothing further owed. It is **not fatal**: the Option E residue is a consistent schema, and the Kretschmann instance of §1 is a model of it.

Three leaks remain at the new joints, and closing them completes the relocation:

- the (a) cost row (row 7);
- the per-history reading of clause (2) (row 2);
- a motivation clause with no test (row 6).

The draft's own Status line ("still relocated as a problem (law of $R$, costs); not fatal") is the right label. I would add "(final address: law of $R$, its motivation, choice of $\tau$-slice)".

## 3. The cover's re-check table, row by row

### Row 1: Option F inert/prohibitive; escape = E residue. **Answered.**

The demotion is honest and complete. In $u$-adapted Riemann normal coordinates every germ has 1-jet $(\eta,0)$, so $k=1$ matching between distinct lumps is vacuous. The 2-jet is the Riemann tensor in the $u$-frame, so $k\ge2$ forbids curvature-changing jumps. On the continue arm, matching is automatic. The matching-order story is gone, and "graph joins" in the title is accurate. §4.1 places the singleton correctly: shared oriented germ, uniqueness of the geodesic piece, and equivalence at the start.

*Residual (NIT).* The Firewall says finite-$k$ matching "constrains no branching type-(ii) edge", and §1 says it "does no work for branching outs". Both contradict §4.2's "prohibitive at $k\ge2$". The Firewall line is my #117 §8 wording. The accurate statement: finite-$k$ matching *cannot create* a branching edge. At $k=1$ it constrains nothing, and at $k\ge2$ it only forbids.

### Row 2: $V^*$ continuum under §2; $V^\tau$ lock-side. **Answered, with one HOLD.**

The continuum theorem is stated and true (§1 table), and ensemble-timed extend-vs-not is demoted. Membership in $V^\tau$ is lock-side: $\frac{d}{d\tau}K(\gamma_{O^u}(\tau))|_0=u^a\nabla_aK$ is jet data, and isolatedness is fixed by the curvelet, which is fixed by $O^u$. §2's identity-versus-timing paragraph now matches.

**HOLD: clause (2) holds per segment, not per history (§5.3; §4.2 items 2 and 6).** §5.3 says branch points "along any history are discrete in $\tau$, enforced by $V^\tau$". With $R\subseteq V^\tau\times V$, $V^\tau$ gives local finiteness along each type-(i) segment, in its open lifetime. It says nothing about histories, because jumps carry no proper time.

The draft never answers #117 §2.4's last request, "allow consecutive jumps or say why not". On the only worked lock-side rule, the question is live: every Kretschmann target has $u^r=0$ and is itself a vertex (§1 table). Two readings are possible, and both fail:

- If consecutive jumps are allowed, a history can take infinitely many branch points at one value of $\tau$. With $c=0.01$: $10\to10.108\to10.217\to10.328\to\cdots$, each target again a vertex.
- If strict alternation holds, as item 2's "alternates" suggests, then $B_R$ at a jump target counts two arms the history cannot take. EPP1 would then assign 1/3 to each of them, and $B_R$ would depend on how the vertex was reached, contradicting "$B_R$ marks a property of the pair $(O^u,R)$".

Separately, a rule whose targets land ever closer to the next critical point produces accumulation across segments even with alternation. And one segment can carry infinitely many vertices in finite proper time at a singular endpoint (the FLRW instance).

The outline had the right, weaker wording: "finite on every compact proper-time interval of each type-(i) segment". v0.4 had "finite alternations". The prose dropped both and widened the claim. Fix in §5, change 2.

*Residuals (NIT).*

- **$V^\tau$ is not a discrete set of oriented germs.** Across all curvelets it is the codimension-one zero set of $u\cdot\nabla K$. On the Schwarzschild exterior, oriented germs modulo isotropy are labelled $(r,v_r,|v_\perp|)$, and $V^\tau$ is the two-parameter set $\{v_r=0\}$. Only its trace on each curvelet is discrete. So "discrete vertex set" (§4.2) and "discrete set of oriented germs" (§5.1) overstate the result, and "non-constant scalar" should read "$K\circ\gamma$ non-constant".
- **The example engine is empty on the standard backgrounds.** This is true of Minkowski, de Sitter, ESU, ΛCDM, radial infall and circular orbits, and of every vacuum curvelet if the scalar is $R$. In Schwarzschild the vertices sit at orbital turning points. Under the public engine the flat laboratory never branches. That is not an overclaim, since the draft claims no flat-space branching. But it is a cost worth one sentence in §5.1 or §9, and it is what the Minkowski example #117 asked for would have shown.
- **The "$(\text{lump},\tau)$ tags" fallback is not lock-side.** $\tau$ needs an origin, which is history data. The tags change the vertex type, and they can separate recurrences but cannot select times where the slice selects none.
- **"Level" is undefined.**
- **Periodic curvelets.** The Schwarzschild loop makes flow plus any $R$ cyclic. So "a direction hypothesis on $R$" (§4.2 Direction) cannot make histories acyclic, and per-visit application is forced there (#117 nit 10, still open).

### Row 7: realisation (a) forbids curvature-changing edges. **HOLD: the cost row is false.**

§4.2 says: "(a) Every type-(ii) edge is realised by a timelike geodesic in some analytic world. By §2 rigidity this confines $R$ to pairs in one analytic continuation of the germ and **forbids curvature-changing jumps**." The first sentence is right. The second is false.

Rigidity fixes which lumps co-occur in one connected analytic world. It does not make curvature constant across that world. The §1 table has two (a)-realised type-(ii) pairs in Schwarzschild $M=1$ across which $K$ changes:

- the radial geodesic from $r=10$ to $r=8$;
- the static recurrence of the $r=10.108$ target.

What (a) forbids is jumps *between analytic continuations*: flat to FLRW (the retired toy's edge, which the prose no longer names), and Schwarzschild $M=1$ to $M=2$. Those are exactly the across-worlds jumps a measurement picture wants, so the true cost of (a) is at least as heavy as the stated one. It is just a different cost.

Two further points the row should carry:

- **(a) never says which endpoints must match.** If the realising geodesic must leave along $u$, it is $\gamma_{O^u}$, the pair is type (i), and $R=\emptyset$.
- **Every lump-level (a)-edge factors** as an orientation jump at $O$ followed by type-(i) transport. The realising geodesic carries positive proper time, which item 2 says jumps do not carry. So say that it labels admissibility and is not the motion.

The admissible targets under (a) form an in-patch continuum (soft Prop. 13). The outline said so, and the prose dropped it.

My #117 §8 compressed it to "exclude curvature-changing jumps", apparently importing the $k\ge2$ jet result, and the outline, cover and prose carried the compression forward. #118 tested only the jet claim. ## 4. New issues not on the cover's table

### 4.1 Paper 1: object blurred, and the revision of motion and measure not said plainly (HOLD)

The brief asks whether the draft says plainly that Paper 1's object is unchanged while its motion and measure are revised. It does not. The separation appears only in the §10 table header, and §1 says only "It is not Paper 1's path-count".

**Object.** Four problems.

- **Obs lost the time direction.** Paper 1: "A point of observer space is one elementary observer at one instant: one infinite analytic jet ... and one future time direction." v0.5 §2 says "$\mathrm{Obs}$ is the space of lumps". v0.4's "(oriented or not as marked)" was dropped. §1 says the direction is present "when branching is discussed", which makes it optional, while §10 row 1 says it survives.
- **The isotropy clause was dropped.** The outline locked "Isotropy: $u$ modulo germ isotropy (named)", and v0.4 had the sentence. The prose has neither, and the changelog does not log it. A lump has no canonical tangent space, so "$O^u=(O,u)$ ... together with a unit timelike vector $u$" rests on an unstated equivalence. The vertex set $V$, and with it $\mathrm{succ}_R$, $B_R$ and the EPP1 weights, depends on that equivalence. At the flat lump the quotient gives one oriented observer; without it there is a hyperboloid of vertices. The Ontology PASS scored outline fidelity "Holds" and missed the loss.
- **"Discontinuous in Obs" is false for bare $u$-jumps.** Under §2's Obs, a bare $u$-jump leaves the lump path constant. §3 says every type-(ii) edge, "including a bare jump of orientation at fixed lump", makes "the path in $\mathrm{Obs}$ discontinuous". ESU instance: $(L,\partial_t)\to(L,\text{boosted }u)$ is type (ii) even modulo isotropy, since boosts are not ESU isometries, yet the lump path is constant.
- **The vertices are lump plus 4-velocity.** Paper 1 distinguishes its observer from Gielen–Wise's "4-velocity on a given metric". v0.5's vertices are (jet, unit timelike $u$), and the "not Gielen–Wise" disclaimer is attached to Obs, not to the vertex space. That is fine if Paper 1's "time direction" is read as $u$, but the draft should say it is a reading.

**Measure.** The departure is labelled, but its scope is understated in three ways.

- **Paper 1's thesis lapses silently.** Paper 1's headline is "countability as the unique measure": if distinct evolutions are countable, counting is the unique reasonable measure, and "a measure that assigned 0.9 to one evolution and 0.1 to another ... would be a different theory" (Paper 1 §6). Per-vertex EPP1 is such a measure. On the draft's own tree the co-existing evolutions $A_1$ and $B_1$ get 1/6 and 1/2. With recurring branch points the completed evolutions are uncountable (Schwarzschild instance: $|B_R|=3$ at every turning point), so Paper 1's antecedent fails too. EPP1 is then one chosen product measure on a continuum, the situation Paper 1 §5 warns about. The §10 table lists the uniqueness thesis under neither "Survives" nor "Revised".
- **The reading of the weight was deleted.** v0.4 §7's "Reading of the weight" paragraph answered #105 §3.6 at HOLD. v0.5 deleted it without a log entry, so §10's "under a *chance* reading" now points at nothing. The two readings differ in category. Under typicality, EPP1 is a measure on co-existing histories. Under chance, it is a Markov jump rule, the random-walk pedigree §3 names.
- **"Can still match Paper 1's spirit" blurs the departure.** §7 says "The typicality *reading* (arms co-exist) can still match Paper 1's spirit." The co-existence *ontology* matches Paper 1. The *weighting* does not. I reopen my #117 §3.1, which called the typicality reading "Paper-1-consistent": that was right for the ontology and wrong for the weighting.

Also, EPP2 lost its outline label "departure from Paper 1 if promoted", and with it the reason it is a foil ("each distinct evolution counts as one").

**Motion.** The §10 table is right that non-uniqueness now rests on an abandonable pad. Two points need one clause each:

- a history is no longer Paper 1's $\tau$-parametrised curve, since jumps carry no $\tau$;
- the pad as written admits $R=\emptyset$, which is CWS, so Paper 1's non-uniqueness premise has no Postulate/Hope/Open/Firewall category. File "$R\ne\emptyset$" as a working Postulate or as Open.

Fix in §5, changes 3 and 4.
