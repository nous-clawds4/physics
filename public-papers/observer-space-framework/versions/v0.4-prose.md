## 1. What this paper is

An *observer*, in this programme, is a physical object: the infinite jet of a real-analytic spacetime metric at a point, equivalently a local simply-connected analytic patch, together with a future time direction when branching is discussed. Observer space is the space of such objects. Distinct evolutions are individuated by disagreement of that local data along proper time, before any amplitude is assigned. Relative ensembles of worlds compatible with a germ are multiplicity of *occurrence*, not a second elementary observer and not a reconstruction of a whole spacetime from the germ.

Motion has two layers. **Type (i)** is geodesic hitchhiking in a fixed world. Under Continuous World Switching — piecewise geodesic lifts, $C^\infty$ matching in observer space, no orientation jump — the branch set is a singleton. That null result is accepted. **Type (ii)** is the working branching pad: Option F joins on a discrete graph of oriented observers, with histories that alternate type-(i) curvelet segments and type-(ii) jumps. The branch set is written
$$
B_R(O^u)=\{[\gamma_{O^u}]\}\cup\mathrm{succ}_R(O^u)
$$
under first-edge equivalence. Vertices along a hitchhiking curvelet may be selected by an extend-versus-not rule ($V^*$); outgoing edges lean on analytic continuation plus true type-(ii) outs ($E^*$). An in-house hand-built toy exhibits $|B|=2$ *in form*; the public gate for a controlled example is a **stated law** of $R$ or $E^*$, not a listed pair of edges.

**EPP1** is a named postulate: equal weight $1/|B_R|$ on discrete arms under a hard gate (countable stars **and** discrete branch points along proper time). Its domain is empty until a law-given controlled example exists. It is not forced by ontology, not tuned to $|a|^2$, and not an Everett-versus-Deutsch–Wallace argument. **EPP2** (weight proportional to post-branch ensemble size) is labeled against Paper 1’s “ensemble does not vote.” A no-no-go sketch appears only with type (ii) on the table: illustration, not Born, not a Bell refutation.

Born and Einstein remain later goals. They are not theorems of v0.4.

## 2. Ontology

Precise working definitions for review are the ones in this section. Informal living notes sit in `mathematical-framework.md`; where that file disagrees, this section wins.

**Metric germ.** Write $O=j_p^\infty$ for the infinite jet of a real-analytic Lorentzian metric $g$ at a point $p$. Equivalently, $O$ may be presented as a simply-connected real-analytic patch obtained by analytic continuation of that jet — a *local* identification of presentations of the same germ, not a claim that a global manifold $M$ is uniquely determined, and not a claim about maximal analytic extensions. Smooth is not enough: in the smooth category a jet need not pick a unique local lump. Analyticity is required.

**Gauge / lump identity.** Two presentations that differ only by a pointed analytic diffeomorphism renaming the same local patch are the same lump. The public object is that lump, not a raw coordinate jet with gauge deferred forever.

**Oriented observer.** Write $O^u=(O,u)$ for that lump together with a unit timelike vector $u$ (null observers excluded here). Nothing in the bare metric jet prefers a particular $u$ among the unit hyperboloid at $p$, up to the isotropy of the germ. Branching and EPP1 are about oriented observers. Graph vertices in the type-(ii) package are oriented observers $O^u$. Bare $O$ is used when orientation is not yet chosen.

**Observer space.** $\mathrm{Obs}$ is the space of lumps (oriented or not as marked). It is not Gielen and Wise’s 7-manifold of future-directed unit 4-velocities. It is not Larsson’s Quantum Jet Theory ($p$-jets of fields about an expansion point; no counting measure of the kind discussed here).

**Spacetime and analyticity cost.** An $M$ is a connected real-analytic Lorentzian 4-manifold. Einstein’s equation is not assumed in v0.4. Analyticity has a cost that belongs in the ontology, not only in motion: on a connected analytic $M$, one infinite jet rigidly determines $g$ on all of $M$. There are no localised geometric degrees of freedom of the smooth kind; generic smooth GR solutions and matter fields are open or excluded. That rigidity is why type-(i) hitchhiking yields a singleton branch set and why type (ii) must be extra structure if branching is wanted.

**World and relative ensembles.** A world is $W=(M,p)$: a spacetime with an anchor. $E_W(O)$ (or $E_W(O^u)$ when orientation matters) is the set of pointed worlds compatible with the germ. The same $M$ may appear more than once under distinct anchors. This is a **relative ensemble**: not a second elementary observer, not a vote of global topology on when evolutions split, and **not** a reconstruction of a whole $W$ from $O$. Ensemble members are occurrences of the *same* lump, not extra vertices of $\mathrm{Obs}$. $E_M(O)$ is the corresponding multiset of underlying manifolds. Sibling points (other anchors in the same $M$ with the same germ) are multiplicity in the ensemble, not a fork of the elementary object. Ensemble cardinality is underdetermined without further structure (Bertrand); do not treat raw $|E|$ as a finished measure.

**Compatibility (sketch).** Say $O_j$ is an allowable future of $O_i$ if there exist a spacetime $M$, occurrences of the two germs, and a finite **timelike** geodesic joining them. Write $O_i\Leftrightarrow O_j$ when each is an allowable future of the other. Compatibility is not automatically transitive. Refinements remain open (§9).

## 3. Type (i) versus type (ii)

**Type (i) — in-world hitchhiking.** The observer-space curve is the germ along a timelike geodesic in a fixed world: one oriented *curvelet* $\gamma_{O^u}$ (the lift of that geodesic into $\mathrm{Obs}$). Analytic lifts in a single $M$ do not delayed-fork: the identity theorem forbids a split that appears only after a finite proper-time delay while the germs still agree.

**Type (ii) — across-worlds jumps.** Edges that are **not** successive oriented germs along $\gamma_{O^u}$. At such an edge the path in $\mathrm{Obs}$ is discontinuous. Histories are finite alternations of type-(i) curvelet segments and type-(ii) jumps (Option F below), not pure analytic curves in $\mathrm{Obs}$.

**Oriented split.** A pair $(O^u,O'^{u'})$ is type (i) if and only if $O'^{u'}=\gamma_{O^u}(\tau)$ as an oriented germ for some proper time $\tau$ in the lifetime of the curvelet. Every other edge is type (ii), including a bare jump of orientation at fixed lump. This replaces the v0.3 wording that sorted edges by bare lumps alone.

**Not restored.** The 2007 construction that ran a geodesic in each world compatible with $O$ and superimposed the lifts as a tree is not a theorem of hitchhiking.

**Not restarted.** The leftover inventory of formal properties of $R$ (acyclicity clauses, descendant finiteness programmes, and so on) is not imported into this public paper.

**Pad pedigree.** The type-(ii) pad is the author’s own 2005 / Strayhorn3 transition-matrix / random-walk hypothesis under a new name. Naming the pedigree makes the hidden-variable / typicality desk-reject smell concrete rather than allusive (§4.3, §10).

## 4. Motion: CWS baseline; Option F as public join reading

### 4.1 Type (i) baseline — CWS singleton

CWS remains a coherent type-(i) package: allowed paths in $\mathrm{Obs}$ are piecewise geodesic lifts; the traveler may switch worlds at a shared germ; the Obs path is $C^\infty$; orientation does not jump.

**Proposition (CWS singleton).** Under CWS and either named equivalence on path germs (infinite-order path-jet at the start, or agreement on a common initial interval), $|B_{\mathrm{CWS}}(O^u)|=1$: the class of the unique geodesic curvelet through $O^u$. World switching can change lifetime; it does not change the Obs-path. EPP1 has empty domain under CWS alone.

CWS is therefore **not** the public branching law. It is the honest null baseline for type (i). Direction Switching and orientation jumps taken alone are rejected as primary branching laws: each tends toward a continuum of arms unless a grain is invented by hand.

### 4.2 Option F — public join and branching pad

**Option F** (working, abandonable) is the public join reading. On each open graph edge the motion is an analytic-geodesic lift in some analytic world (type (i) on the open interval). At a vertex $O^{u*}$, matching requires the truncated metric jet $j^kg$ and the orientation $u$ through a **fixed finite** order $k\ge 1$, together with incidence in the discrete graph. Infinite-order / $C^\infty$ matching at joins is rejected: that was the HOLE under CWS. Glueing a $C^k$ spacetime metric in a single world (Option D) is rejected as primary: it clashes with locked analytic worlds or collapses into Direction Switching.

**Arms** are the graph **out-star**: distinct outgoing type-(ii) edges. They are not infinite-order jets of Obs-paths.

**Composition.** Write $R$ (or $E^*$ when the edge set is selected by the lean below) for the set of type-(ii) edges.

1. The vertex set is closed under type-(i) flow along curvelets that meet vertices.
2. A **history** alternates type-(i) curvelet segments and type-(ii) jumps. The path in $\mathrm{Obs}$ is discontinuous at each jump. Proper time is carried only along type-(i) segments.
3. $R$ contains **no** type-(i) pairs. Out-degree means type-(ii) out-degree.
4. Under **first-edge** equivalence,
   $$
   B_R(O^u):=\{[\gamma_{O^u}]\}\cup\mathrm{succ}_R(O^u).
   $$
   The type-(i) term is exactly one class by the CWS singleton. The notation $B_R$ marks that the branch set is a property of the pair $(O^u,R)$, not of the observer alone.

Dropping Option F leaves layer (i) unchanged.

**Direction (open).** Either impose a direction hypothesis on $R$ (for example irreflexive and acyclic on the reachable set), or treat histories as walks and apply EPP1 per visit. One of these must be chosen before examples speak of “post-branch” prefixes; neither is locked here.

**Spacetime-realisation (open).** Either every type-(ii) edge is realised by a timelike geodesic in some world (a constraint on $R$, not a revival of the 2007 tree-as-theorem), or “across-worlds” is a label without realisation. One choice must be named in examples; neither is locked here.

### 4.3 Cost of $R$ — hidden-variable smell kept loud

**Honest report (soft Prop. 13).** No law of $R$ or $E^*$ is written. Lock-side candidates examined in-house — Fermi-slice neighbourhoods, continuous invariants, in-patch support, curvature and energy conditions, and kin — give trivial maps or a **continuum** of successors. A law with countable stars is a **new physical choice**, not a readout of the germ. It is wrong to claim that locked data can never determine any off-curvelet edge; the point is that kinematics does not single out a dynamics.

A neighbourhood constraint on stars is stateable once $\mathrm{Obs}$ carries the Fermi-slice topology already available in-house. The obstruction is continuum successors in balls, not the absence of any topology. The older phrase that a neighbourhood-law “waits on $\mathrm{Obs}$ as a space” is dropped.

**Singleton-or-continuum dilemma.** Under every lock-side candidate examined so far, $R$ either collapses or yields continuum out-degree. $R$ escapes the continuum only by remaining unspecified or by adding selection extras. That — not a theorem forbidding examples — is why this public paper claims no *law-given* controlled example. Equal weight $1/|B_R|$ is **not** implied by the mere existence of $R$; weights are a further postulate (EPP1) or a further hope.

## 5. Vertices, edges, hard gate, and the toy

### 5.1 $V^*$ by extend-versus-not

Along a hitchhiking curvelet $\gamma_{O^u}$, mark candidate vertex times $\tau_*$ at which some members of the hitchhiking ensemble extend analytically through $\tau_*$ and some die (finite lifetime). Those are the loci of the in-house Theorem 7(3). The vertex is the oriented germ $\gamma(\tau_*)$; the same germ is the same vertex.

**Caveats.** Such vertices mark **ensemble events on one history**. They are **not**, by themselves, forks of the elementary observer: extend-versus-not does not produce two disagreeing next germs. Discreteness of $V^*$ along $\gamma$ inherits discreteness of death times $T_{\mathrm{die}}$; if the ensemble or the lifetimes are wild, continuum risks return. Choosing the extend-versus-not rule is still extra structure (same pattern as soft Prop. 13) — better motivated than an arbitrary grain of all of $\mathrm{Obs}$, not a theorem that $\mathrm{Obs}$ is discrete. The ensemble marks a vertex; it does not vote on the identity of the elementary object and does not create a second elementary observer.

### 5.2 $E^*$ lean

**Die** is ensemble dropout, not a second Obs arm, unless an explicit terminal sink vertex is added and named. Multiple analytic extenders forced by Theorem 7 to the same next germ count as **one** arm. Therefore $|B|\ge 2$ needs disagreement among outgoing pieces: at least one **true type-(ii)** edge off the unique Theorem 7 continuation — not extend-versus-not alone.

### 5.3 Hard gate

EPP1 applies only when both hold:

1. $B_R(O^u)$ is **discrete** (finite or countable) at the vertex — countable stars; **and**
2. the set of branch points along any history is **discrete in proper time**: finite on every compact proper-time interval of each type-(i) segment.

Clause (2) is a further extra (a $\tau$-slice), distinct from countable out-degree and from a grain of all of $\mathrm{Obs}$. Without it, equal weight at continuum-many times along a segment has no well-defined action. Lock-side *candidates* for such a slice (for example critical points of a non-constant analytic curvature scalar along $\gamma$) exist; the choice of scalar and level is still extra.

### 5.4 Existence-in-form versus the public gate

An in-house minimal toy (`papers/B-ge2-minimal-toy.md`) exhibits $|B(O^{u*})|=2$ under $V^*$ extend-versus-not, Option F, and the $E^*$ lean, with outs equal to the Theorem 7 Minkowski continuation plus a **named** type-(ii) edge to an FLRW comoving germ. That is **existence-in-form**, including a hand-named type-(ii) out. It does **not** clear the public gate.

**Public controlled-example gate.** An $R$ or $E^*$ given by a **stated rule** on lumps (a function of $O^u$ and at most named extra structure declared as such), applied to a computable germ, with arm count a consequence of the rule and with branch points discrete along every history. A hand-listed digraph does not qualify. “Weights forced, not assumed” is a later bar — it needs a law, a typicality rule, and work on circularity — and is not claimed here.

## 7. EPP1, EPP2, and the product ansatz

Prefer the labels **EPP1** and **EPP2**. Do not reuse the 2008 word “egalitarianism.”

**EPP1 (named postulate).** At a branch point where the hard gate of §5.3 holds, assign equal weight $1/|B_R(O^u)|$ to each discrete arm of $B_R(O^u)$. This **is** the per-branch equal-weight rule (the author’s 2005 pedigree) under a new label. It is adopted as a **named postulate**, not as a theorem of germs, not as a consequence of the mere existence of $R$, and not as a choice of arm multiplicities tuned to reproduce $|a|^2$. Until a law-given controlled example exists, its domain is **empty**. This paper is not an Everett-versus-Deutsch–Wallace argument about decision weights on decoherence branches; arms here are Option F out-stars under extra structure $R$.

**Reading of the weight.** Two readings are available: typicality over co-existing arms (Paper 1’s spirit — multiplicity is ontological; counting is typicality), or single-outcome chance $1/|B_R|$ along one followed history. This draft prefers the **typicality** reading for consistency with Paper 1. The chance reading would place $R$ plus EPP1 nearer stochastic jump dynamics; that neighbour is named in §10, not adopted.

**EPP2.** Weight an arm in proportion to post-branch $|E_W|$ or $|E_M|$. That lets the ensemble vote. Paper 1’s measure excludes that vote (“each distinct evolution counts as one”; extra weighting is new theory). In this public paper EPP2 is a **foil** against which EPP1 is stated, not a co-equal silent hope. Promoting it would be a labeled departure from Paper 1.

**Product ansatz (hope, not theorem).** If post-branch ensembles factor with a shared reservoir, EPP2 might collapse toward EPP1. The hope fails if the measured distinction locks other topology, or if the arms admit different external structure. Even with a shared reservoir, $\infty/\infty$ needs a named exhaustion. Not Stoica: the count here is not Hilbert-space ontic counting with $|\Psi|$ in the measure.

## 8. No-no-go sketch (not a Born derivation)

Only with type (ii) on the table does a correlation sketch even get a graph to draw on. A digraph fact is not a Bell theorem. This paper does not refute Bell, does not derive Born, and does not claim a local hidden-variable dynamics that beats Bell. Circularity remains: EPP plus an engineered tree is not a proof (Paper 1’s circularity wall).

What a real sketch would need — and what is **not** exhibited here — includes at least: two observers as vertices; an embedding of both in one world with spacelike anchors (which selects an ensemble member and is E-smuggling in the in-house sense); a correlation rule with setting dependence; and a named Bell premise. An $|a|^2$-matching tree drawn by hand on $(V,R)$ is Paper 1’s trivial scheme, not a derivation.
