The existing §10 table has two cells per row: **Neighbour** and **Relation**. Each new row follows it, stating what the paper uses (usually a picture or nothing) and what it does not use. The wording below is Literature #180's (R3a–R3c), verbatim. The source of each clause is tagged in brackets, and the tags are removed in prose.

| Neighbour | Relation (draft) |
|---|---|
| Echeverria–Klinkhammer–Thorne (EKT 1991) | EKT study a classical billiard ball in a fixed wormhole spacetime with closed timelike curves and find that one set of initial data can admit several, for some data infinitely many, self-consistent trajectories, each with a self-collision [EKT 1991]. The paper borrows only the picture, from Paper 1, of a lump hitchhiking a trajectory [Paper 1 §2, §3]; EKT is not the mechanism and not the ontology [Paper 1 §2, §3]. EKT's multiplicity is of a matter body's motion on a fixed geometry [Claude v0.2.1 review; EKT 1991], and matter fields are open here [v0.7.1 §2]. Nothing here uses closed timelike curves or wormholes, and non-uniqueness here does not rest on them: it rests on the abandonable $R\ne\emptyset$ [v0.7.1 §4.2, §10 Paper 1 table and Hadley row; Paper 1 §3]. |
| Junction conditions (Israel) | Israel's junction conditions glue two spacetime metrics across a hypersurface: the induced metric agrees on both sides, and a jump in extrinsic curvature is fixed by the stress–energy of a thin shell on the surface [Israel 1966]. Here the only link is the phrase: "joins" and matching across a join [Literature v0.4-outline row]. A join here is a type-(ii) jump plus incidence in a graph between oriented germs, not a spacetime metric glued across a surface [v0.7.1 §4.2; Literature v0.4-outline row]. The worked rule continues a jet along a path and uses no completed 4-manifold [v0.7.1 §5.4]. Matching jets to a finite order at a join cannot create a branching edge [v0.7.1 §4.2, C27]. Not used. |
| Self-sampling assumption (SSA; Bostrom) | SSA is a rule for self-locating credence: other things equal, reason as if one were randomly selected from all actually existing observers in one's reference class [Bostrom 2002]. Here the only link is the phrase: $E_W$ and $E_M$ are ensembles of worlds that carry a given germ [v0.7.1 §2; Literature v0.1-outline row], and the retired toy's edge ran from flat to FLRW [v0.7.1 §4.2; Literature v0.4-outline row]. It is a different measure problem [Literature v0.1-outline row]: nothing here assigns credence over which observer one is, and the ensemble does not vote on identity or on timing [v0.7.1 §2]. Weighting an arm by its ensemble size is a separate rule, EPP2, kept as a foil [v0.7.1 §7]. Not used. |
