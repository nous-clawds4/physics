## 1. What this paper is

An *observer*, in this programme, is a physical object: the infinite jet of a real-analytic spacetime metric at a point, equivalently a local simply-connected analytic patch, together with a future time direction when branching is discussed. Observer space is the space of such objects. Distinct evolutions are individuated by disagreement of that local data — under the type-(ii) package, stepwise at vertices and first edges (§10) — before any amplitude is assigned. Relative ensembles of worlds compatible with a germ are multiplicity of *occurrence*, not a second elementary observer and not a reconstruction of a whole spacetime from the germ.

Motion has two layers. **Type (i)** is geodesic hitchhiking in a fixed world. Under Continuous World Switching the branch set is a singleton. That null result is accepted. **Type (ii)** is the working branching pad: **Option E residue** — discontinuous jumps on a discrete graph of oriented observers, with analytic-geodesic pieces on open edges and arms equal to the out-star. Truncated-jet / Option F matching is demoted: it does no work for branching outs. The branch set is
$$
B_R(O^u)=\{[\gamma_{O^u}]\}\cup\mathrm{succ}_R(O^u)
$$
under first-edge equivalence. Public discreteness along proper time is a **lock-side $\tau$-slice** (for example curvature critical points), not extend-vs-not under the raw §2 ensemble. An in-house digraph can exhibit $|B_R|=2$ *in form*; the public gate requires a stated rule that is also **uniform** and **motivated**.

**EPP1** is a named postulate: equal weight $1/|B_R|$ on finite discrete arms under a hard gate. Its domain is empty until a gate-passing controlled example exists. It is the 2005 per-branch rule under a new label. It is **not** Paper 1’s path-count (§7). **EPP2** is a foil. A no-no-go sketch appears only with type (ii) on the table.

Born and Einstein remain later goals. They are not theorems of v0.5.

## 2. Ontology

Precise working definitions for review are the ones in this section. Informal living notes sit in `mathematical-framework.md`; where that file disagrees, this section wins.

**Metric germ.** Write $O=j_p^\infty$ for the infinite jet of a real-analytic Lorentzian metric $g$ at a point $p$. Equivalently, $O$ may be presented as a simply-connected real-analytic patch obtained by analytic continuation of that jet — a *local* identification of presentations of the same germ, not a claim that a global manifold $M$ is uniquely determined. Smooth is not enough. Analyticity is required.

**Gauge / lump identity.** Two presentations that differ only by a pointed analytic diffeomorphism renaming the same local patch are the same lump.

**Oriented observer.** Write $O^u=(O,u)$ for that lump together with a unit timelike vector $u$ (null observers excluded here). Branching and EPP1 are about oriented observers. Graph vertices are oriented observers $O^u$.

**Observer space.** $\mathrm{Obs}$ is the space of lumps. It is not Gielen and Wise’s 7-manifold of future-directed unit 4-velocities. It is not Larsson’s Quantum Jet Theory.

**Spacetime and analyticity cost.** An $M$ is a connected real-analytic Lorentzian 4-manifold. Einstein’s equation is not assumed. On a connected analytic $M$, one infinite jet rigidly determines $g$ on all of $M$. That rigidity is why type-(i) hitchhiking yields a singleton branch set and why type (ii) must be extra structure if branching is wanted.

**World and relative ensembles.** A world is $W=(M,p)$. $E_W(O)$ is the set of pointed worlds compatible with the germ: a **relative ensemble**, not a second elementary observer, not a reconstruction of a whole $W$ from $O$. Ensemble members are occurrences of the *same* lump, not extra vertices of $\mathrm{Obs}$. Sibling points are multiplicity, not a fork. Ensemble cardinality is underdetermined without further structure (Bertrand).

**Identity versus timing.** The ensemble does **not** vote on the *identity* of the elementary object: evolutions individuate by disagreement of local germ data, not by global topology labels. That Paper 1 claim stays. What v0.5 does **not** do is let which worlds die when select the proper times at which branching may occur (ensemble-timed extend-vs-not as the public $\tau$-engine). That would be a timing vote / E-smuggling. Public discreteness along $\tau$ is lock-side (§5.1).

**Ensemble hygiene (open).** Whether $E_W$ is restricted to inextendible or maximal members is open. Inextendibility alone does not restore discrete death times in general (cosmic-string families).

**Compatibility (sketch).** $O_j$ is an allowable future of $O_i$ if there exist a spacetime and a finite **timelike** geodesic joining occurrences. Mutual compatibility is not automatically transitive. If kept, this relation tracks realisation option (a) in §4.2.

## 3. Type (i) versus type (ii)

**Type (i).** The observer-space curve is the germ along a timelike geodesic in a fixed world: one oriented curvelet $\gamma_{O^u}$. Analytic lifts in a single $M$ do not delayed-fork.

**Type (ii).** Edges that are **not** successive oriented germs along $\gamma_{O^u}$. At such an edge the path in $\mathrm{Obs}$ is discontinuous.

**Oriented split.** A pair $(O^u,O'^{u'})$ is type (i) if and only if $O'^{u'}=\gamma_{O^u}(\tau)$ as an oriented germ for some proper time $\tau$ in the lifetime. Every other edge is type (ii), including a bare jump of orientation at fixed lump.

**Not restored.** The 2007 tree-of-lifts is not a theorem of hitchhiking. **Not restarted.** The leftover inventory of formal properties of $R$ is not imported here.

**Pad pedigree.** The type-(ii) pad is the author’s 2005 / Strayhorn3 transition-matrix / random-walk hypothesis under a new name.

## 4. Motion: CWS baseline; Option E residue

### 4.1 Type (i) baseline — CWS singleton

CWS remains a coherent type-(i) package: piecewise geodesic lifts; switches at shared germs; Obs path $C^\infty$; orientation does not jump.

**Proposition (CWS singleton).** Under CWS and either named equivalence on path germs, $|B_{\mathrm{CWS}}(O^u)|=1$. World switching can change lifetime; it does not change the Obs-path. EPP1 has empty domain under CWS alone.

CWS is not the public branching law. The escape from the singleton for branching is **not** “reject infinite-order matching.” Among geodesic lifts the singleton is shared oriented germ, uniqueness of the geodesic piece, and equivalence at the start — not matching order as the engine.

### 4.2 Option E residue — public join and branching pad

**Option E residue** (working, abandonable) is the public join reading. On each open graph edge the motion is an analytic-geodesic lift in some analytic world. Joins are discontinuous type-(ii) jumps plus **graph incidence**. Arms are the graph **out-star**.

**Not Option F as a branching mechanism.** Truncated-jet matching through finite $k$ (Option C) is automatic on the continue arm (same oriented germ), **vacuous** at $k=1$ between distinct lumps, and **prohibitive** at $k\ge 2$ for curvature-changing jumps. Pairing C with E does not buy branching power. Optional housekeeping: if bare $u$-jumps at a fixed lump are excluded from $R$, say so as a selection rule on $R$, reconciling with §3 (those jumps are already type (ii) by the oriented split). That is not the HOLE escape.

**Composition.** Write $R$ for the set of type-(ii) edges, and $V^\tau$ for a discrete vertex set selected by the public $\tau$-slice of §5.1 when hard-gate clause (2) is in force.

1. Require $R\subseteq V^\tau\times V$ when $V^\tau$ enforces clause (2); otherwise every point of a flow-closed curvelet is a potential attachment point (a continuum).
2. A **history** alternates type-(i) curvelet segments and type-(ii) jumps. The path in $\mathrm{Obs}$ is discontinuous at each jump. Proper time is carried only along type-(i) segments.
3. $R$ contains **no** type-(i) pairs. Out-degree means type-(ii) out-degree.
4. $\mathrm{succ}_R(O^u):=\{O'^{u'}:(O^u,O'^{u'})\in R\}$.
5. **First-edge equivalence:** two allowed continuations from $O^u$ are equivalent iff they share the same first type-(ii) edge, or both take the continue / type-(i) class with no first type-(ii) edge.
6. $B_R(O^u):=\{[\gamma_{O^u}]\}\cup\mathrm{succ}_R(O^u)$. When a continue segment exists, $|B_R|=1+|\mathrm{succ}_R|$. The continue / Theorem 7 arm is the $[\gamma]$ term — **not** a type-(ii) edge in $R$.

Dropping Option E residue leaves layer (i) unchanged. Notation $B_R$ marks a property of the pair $(O^u,R)$.

**Direction (open).** Either a direction hypothesis on $R$, or walk semantics with EPP1 applied per visit.

**Spacetime-realisation (honesty, not a neutral open).**

- **(a)** Every type-(ii) edge is realised by a timelike geodesic in some analytic world. By §2 rigidity this confines $R$ to pairs in one analytic continuation of the germ and **forbids curvature-changing jumps**.
- **(b)** “Across-worlds” is a label without realisation. Curvature-changing digraph edges are allowed; any such toy edge has silently taken (b).

These are not interchangeable. Compatibility, if kept, tracks (a).

### 4.3 Cost of $R$ — hidden-variable smell kept loud

**Honest report (soft Prop. 13).** No law of $R$ is written. Lock-side candidates examined in-house give trivial maps or a continuum of successors. A law with countable (or finite) stars is a **new physical choice**, not a readout of the germ.

**Singleton-or-continuum dilemma.** $R$ escapes continuum arms only by remaining unspecified or by adding selection extras. That is a motivation problem, not a theorem against all examples: arbitrary lock-side rules can pass a bare “stated rule” bar (§5.4). Equal weight $1/|B_R|$ is not implied by the mere existence of $R$.

## 5. Vertices, hard gate, toy, and the gate

### 5.1 Public $\tau$-slice (lock-side)

**Continuum theorem.** Under §2’s ensemble as written (no inextendibility), naive extend-vs-not marks a continuum of proper times: for each $\tau_0$ one can punch a hole so a compatible world dies there. Hard-gate clause (2) fails identically under that reading. Inextendibility alone does not restore discreteness in general.

**Public engine.** Prefer a **lock-side $\tau$-slice**: for example the isolated critical points of a non-constant analytic curvature scalar along $\gamma$. One-variable analyticity makes those times discrete when the scalar is non-constant. Write $V^\tau$ for the resulting discrete set of oriented germs (or $(\mathrm{lump},\tau)$ tags on homogeneous curvelets — named extra if needed). **Choice of scalar and level is named extra** — an honest grain-like choice on the curvelet, not a theorem that $\mathrm{Obs}$ is discrete, and not a vote of $E_W$.

**Ensemble-timed extend-vs-not** is demoted to an in-house schematic, or else requires an explicit chosen discrete sub-ensemble (grain on $E_W$). It is not the public clause-(2) engine.

### 5.2 Residual $E^*$ honesty

Die is ensemble dropout, not a second Obs arm, unless a named sink is added. Multiple analytic extenders forced to the same next germ count as the single $[\gamma]$ arm. $|B|\ge 2$ needs true type-(ii) outs in $R$.

### 5.3 Hard gate

EPP1 applies only when both hold:

1. $B_R(O^u)$ is **finite** at the vertex where EPP1 acts (or countable with a **named exhaustion** declared as extra); **and**
2. branch points along any history are **discrete in $\tau$**, enforced by $V^\tau$ (or another named extra), not by raw extend-vs-not under §2’s ensemble.

### 5.4 Digraph fact versus the public gate

The in-house note `papers/B-ge2-minimal-toy.md` is **retired as a $V^*$ demonstration**. At most it is a **digraph fact**: some abstract $(V,R)$ has $|B_R|=2$ under composition. If cited, it is hand-built in ensemble, vertex, and out; it does not clear the public gate.

**Public controlled-example gate.** An $R$ given by a **stated** rule on lumps that is **uniform** (one rule in germ invariants at every vertex; no finite case-list of named germs) **and** carries a **named motivation** (symmetry, naturality, variational, or empirical — a menu, not a blank cheque). Else admit the bar is only “not a hand list,” and the relocation ends at Bertrand’s problem among admissible rules. Arm count must be a consequence of the rule; branch points must be discrete along every history. Hand-listed digraphs do not qualify. “Weights forced, not assumed” remains a later bar.

## 7. EPP1, EPP2, and the product ansatz

Prefer **EPP1** and **EPP2**. Do not reuse the 2008 word “egalitarianism.”

**EPP1 (named postulate).** At a branch point where the hard gate holds, assign equal weight $1/|B_R(O^u)|$ to each discrete arm. This is the per-branch equal-weight rule (2005 pedigree), adopted as a **named postulate** with empty domain until a gate-passing controlled example exists. It is not implied by bare $R$, not tuned to $|a|^2$, and not an Everett-versus-Deutsch–Wallace argument.

**Label versus Paper 1.** Per-vertex $1/|B_R|$ **departs** from Paper 1’s uniform count over distinct evolutions. On the smallest uneven tree — root arms $A,B$; $A$ with three sub-arms, $B$ with one — Paper 1 at depth 2 gives four evolutions of weight $1/4$, so the $A$-prefix has weight $3/4$; per-vertex gives weight $1/2$ to that prefix. The rules agree only on generation-regular trees. The typicality *reading* (arms co-exist) can still match Paper 1’s spirit; the *rule* is not Paper 1’s measure (§10).

**EPP2.** Weight proportional to post-branch ensemble size — an ensemble vote, kept as a **foil**.

**Product ansatz.** Hope, not theorem. Bertrand / exhaustion still needed for ensemble ratios. Not Stoica.
