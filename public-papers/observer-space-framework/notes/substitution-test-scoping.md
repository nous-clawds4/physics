## The test as recorded, and an operational form

The test (`notes/born-litmus-test.md`): swap a rule $W'$ into a derivation of $W$; if it slides in with nothing else changing, the rule was put in by hand (failure); a genuine derivation breaks an input, lemma or symmetry upstream. Failure means circular injection in whole or in part; no technicality yields a pass. David's conjecture (DW fails, this framework passes) is quoted there and not analysed here.

**One operational form (option T-a).** For a derivation $D$ of $W$ and a substitute $W'$, record:
- **(S1)** the break list: the premises, lemmas or symmetries of $D$ that are inconsistent with $W'$, each traced back to the minimal sets of premises of $D$ that are jointly inconsistent with $W'$ (a set may have two or more members, as in the combination the table below records for branch counting), and any object of $D$ on which $W'$ is not defined;
- **(S2)** the minimal edits to $D$ that yield $W'$ (in general several: where a break set has two members, weakening either may suffice; each is recorded);
- **(S3)** for each broken item or set, two separate judgements: whether it is independently motivated, and whether it restates $W$ (or a special case of $W$). Four cells result, including "both" (independently motivated, yet restating $W$ on a sub-case, as the equal-amplitude step is charged with doing: the step critic question 2 asks about) and "neither" (not independently motivated, and not a restatement of $W$; the Kretschmann rule, stated and uniform but unmotivated (C26), is an example outside any derivation).

"Slides in" means that S1 is empty, or holds only items judged under S3 to restate $W$. "In part" means that some item restates $W$ on a sub-case (for example, equal weights for equal amplitudes, if S3 judges that a restatement), or is a parameter tuned to $W$. "Restates" is narrower than "entails": the premises of any valid derivation jointly entail $W$ on every sub-case, so entailment alone would make every derivation fail "in part". Whether an item in S3's "both" cell (independently motivated, yet restating $W$ on a sub-case) triggers either definition is left open **[PJ]**: it is critic question 2, and the same judgement would reach any equal-weight item in this framework, EPP1 included, if amplitudes were ever introduced.

**Cost of T-a:** where $W'$ conflicts with $W$ on the domain $D$ covers, every valid derivation has a non-empty S1 by logic, so the content lies in where S1 falls, in S2 and in S3, an LT2-type judgement. **Option T-b** keeps David's informal wording ("slides in / does not"). **Cost of T-b:** "nothing else changing" is not defined. Paper 1's trivial scheme (Paper 1 §8: copies "proportional to $|a_n|^2$") shows the gap. If the copy-number rule counts as something else, swapping $|a|^2$ for $|a|^p$ changes it, so the scheme does not slide in, and ruling it a failure needs the judgement that the changed item restates $W$, which is S3 left implicit (David's "cannot be passed on a technicality" may be read as supplying it). If it counts as $W$ itself, the scheme slides in.

## (i) Deutsch–Wallace: structure, described only

**Framing caution.** The companion paper would use Deutsch–Wallace (DW) as a worked case for the test, not as a rival frame. Paper 1 says these objects "are not Everett worlds" and that it "does not argue against Deutsch and Wallace in order to occupy their problem" (§9); v0.9 §12 keeps DW as a firewall row ("Not this frame"). No side-by-side may suggest an Everettian alternative. Within that limit, one option is a panel rather than DW alone, in one record format, with Paper 1's trivial scheme as a known-failure control (its failure itself needs the S3 judgement; see the cost of T-b) [RECORD STRING PENDING — Literature: the panel's cases and citations (#263 X14 proposes Zurek, Saunders 2021, Sebens and Carroll 2018, Bohmian equivariance, Gleason 1957, frequency-operator arguments and MERW)]. Cost: more Literature work, an S3 judgement for every case, and a case whose pass the parties agree on, which may not exist; gain: calibration (critic question 1) and no two-party comparison (critic question 5).

**Origin.** Deutsch (1999), cited in v0.9: the values a player assigns to quantum games, with additive payoff utilities and a zero-sum rule, give an equal-amplitude superposition its mean value; unequal amplitudes are then reduced to equal ones by splitting with an auxiliary system, which yields $|a|^2$ weights. Barnum et al. (2000; readiness refs) charge a hidden probabilistic assumption; [RECORD STRING PENDING — Literature: the reply to that charge and its reception (#263 X7: Wallace 2003; Greaves 2004)].

**Wallace (2012)**, ch. 5, "Symmetry, Rationality, and the Born Rule", cited in v0.9 and readiness §1.1; the axiom statements below follow the preprint version (Wallace 2010, arXiv:0906.2718, §§4–7) [RECORD STRING PENDING — Literature: the preprint, chapter and book labels, and what is unchecked against the book (#263 X17)]. The premises:
- rationality axioms, including ordering and **diachronic consistency**;
- **richness** axioms (reward availability, branching availability, erasure and problem continuity);
- **state supervenience** (preferences between acts depend only on the physical state each act leaves the agent's branch in);
- **branching indifference** (indifference to branching that leaves payoffs unchanged);
- **macrostate indifference** and **solution continuity** (the other two axioms specific to the Everettian setting).

A **representation theorem** (the "Born rule theorem" of Wallace 2010) then gives a utility function, unique up to affine transformations, such that acts are ranked by expected utility computed with the quantum-mechanical weights $|a|^2$. Readiness §1.1 records the two conditions as they bear on a branch measure: (a) invariance under symmetries swapping equal-amplitude branches, and (b) fine-graining invariance.

The test can only be run pre-emptively, asking for each component where a target weight could enter. **[PJ]**: no row is a pass or a fail. ## Relation to LT1–LT4

The options are:
- **(R-a)** a refinement of **LT2**. LT2 checks where the inputs come from, and S3 asks the same of each broken item;
- **(R-b)** a separate test (an LT5). It checks whether the inputs fix the output (whether a different output is consistent with them, and what must change if not), which LT2 does not;
- **(R-c)** a procedure for running LT1–LT4:
  LT1's amplitude work elsewhere adds uses that break under $p\ne2$ (unitary evolution conserves $\sum|a|^2$ but in general not $\sum|a|^p$, and for $p\ne2$ no nontrivial linear map preserves the $p$-norm [RECORD STRING PENDING — Literature: the result that for $p\ne2$ no nontrivial linear map preserves the $p$-norm (#263 X28: Aaronson 2004)]); LT3's "irrelevant further splitting" parallels DW's fine-graining premise; LT4's second setup adds independent breaks;
- **(R-d)** the operational form of the litmus test's single question ("was the Born rule put in by hand?", "Scope and outcomes"): a detector for the failure outcome, with LT1–LT4 kept as the conditions for genuine emergence ("YES on all four").

**Adds** under any option: a concrete counterfactual, named substitutes $W'$ and a record format (S1–S3). **Costs:** (R-a): LT2 as worded names the law of $R$, the slice and the measure, so applying S3 to other items (symmetries, rationality axioms, the outcome labelling) widens LT2, and S1–S2, which record what must change rather than where inputs come from, have no slot in it. (R-b): it needs an S3 criterion distinct from LT2, or it reduces to (R-a). (R-c): it can run only where LT1, LT3 and LT4 can be stated, which for this framework waits on readiness gaps 6 (LT1), 4 (LT3) and 5 (LT3's "irrelevant", and LT4), and on a law of $R$ (gap 1). (R-d), if recorded: two criteria then bear on one test, and which governs when they disagree must be said.

## Questions for an outside critic

1. Since every valid derivation breaks something under a swap, does the test have content beyond S3, and is S3 just LT2 under another name?
2. In DW, is the equal-amplitude symmetry an independent input, or the Born rule restricted to a sub-case (the "in part" form)? Is this already settled in the literature **[L]**?
3. Is a declared postulate such as EPP1 "put in by hand" in the test's sense, or outside its scope because it claims nothing about Born?
4. Does the $p=2$ in MERW's density (a left-times-right Perron product) count as breaking something upstream, or as the definition restating the target?
5. Can a companion paper run the test on DW without staging the framing v0.9 §2 excludes?
