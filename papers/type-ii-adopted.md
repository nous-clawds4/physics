## 0. What is adopted, and what is not

**Adopted (working, abandonable).** Existence of type-(ii) edges: pairs $(O,O')\in R$ that do not arise as successive germs along a geodesic lift of $O$ in a real-analytic $W$. This is 2005 / Strayhorn3 (links on lumps), not OutcomeCounting’s tree generated by geodesics across $M_O$.

**Not adopted.** $1/N$. A grain. A measure on $\mathrm{Obs}$. A Lagrangian. Born. 2005 $3$-manifold observers as the object. The 2007 geodesic-generated tree as remaining motion (Theorem 11 still killed as a theorem of layer (i)). $\Phi$ itself.

**Not a theorem of hitchhiking.** Hitchhiking still gives one $\gamma_O$ (Prop. 3.2) and empty typicality of branches from one $O$ (Theorem 8). Type (ii) is extra structure.

## 1. Two layers, as now used

**In-world.** Geodesic hitchhiking. One $\gamma_O$ per $O$. Theorems 2–8 still apply to those curves: no analytic delayed fork, no $\sim$-split at $\partial P$ among $E_\gamma$ members, $E_\gamma$ shares the curvelet. Analytic $W$+geodesics remains a law whose solutions are analytic maps $I\to\mathrm{Obs}$ (Theorem 9).

**Across-worlds.** Extra edges of a transition structure $R\subseteq V\times V$, $V\subseteq\mathrm{Obs}=\mathcal{P}/{\sim_L}$ (Definition 7). Paths in this graph are candidate histories. Two paths may delayed-fork (Definition 8): common prefix, then distinct vertices. Those paths are not required to be analytic curves, so Theorem 2 does not forbid them (Proposition 12).

The 2007 picture that ran a geodesic in each $W\in M_O$ and superimposed the lifts as the tree is *not* this layer. Theorem 11 still kills that as a theorem of hitchhiking. Type (ii) does not bring it back.

## 2. What can determine $R$, without $1/N$ and without a locked grain

The locked data at $O$ are the lump, the patch $P(O)$, the curvelet $\gamma_O$, and the ensemble $E(O)$.

**Proposition 13.** Those data determine at most type-(i) successive pairs (germs along $\gamma_O$, and no $\sim$-split at $\partial P$ among $E_\gamma$ members). They do not determine a type-(ii) edge $(O,O')$ with $O'$ not on $\gamma_O$.

**Proof.** Prop. 3.2 and Theorems 3–4, 7–8: from $O$, hitchhiking produces one analytic curvelet; $E_\gamma$ labels share it; they are not extra points of $\mathrm{Obs}$. A pair $(O,O')$ with $O'\ne\gamma_O(\tau)$ for all $\tau$ in the lifetime is not among those successive pairs. Specifying it is a further rule. $\square$

Candidate further rules, and why they are not written here:

- *Neighborhood law.* “$O'$ lies in a neighborhood of $O$ in $\mathrm{Obs}$.” That needs $\mathrm{Obs}$ as a *space* (a topology, or a uniformity, or an atlas). Theorem 10 identifies $\mathrm{Obs}$ as a set of lumps. Gauge as a space is open. A neighborhood-law for $R$ is blocked until that is closed. This is an obstruction, not a vibe.
- *Grain law.* “$O'$ is a successor in a discrete grain of $\mathrm{Obs}$.” Grain is not locked. $d$-decimal is combinatorial, not put in by hand. Engulfing-in-$P(O)$ coarsens $\gamma_O$ and does not pick $O'$ off the curvelet.
- *Ensemble law.* “$O'$ is read from some $[W,\iota]\in E(O)$.” $E(O)$ members are occurrences of the *same* lump, not extra vertices. Using them as targets would collapse $\mathrm{Obs}$ into $E$ or fold $E$ into $\sim$. Both remain forbidden.
- *Named list.* A specific $R$ written as a set of pairs. That would be the law. It is not written. Do not invent one to look busy.

No $1/N$ is placed on edges. Weights are a further hypothesis, not implied by existence of $R$.

## 3. Honest remainder

Adopted: existence of type-(ii) edges. Not written: the law for which edges. Neighborhood-laws wait on gauge as a space. Grain-laws wait on a grain. Stop.

Typicality of branches from one $O$ is no longer *empty as a matter of structure* (graph-paths can delayed-fork), but it is still empty as a *theorem*: without a written $R$ and without a grain, there is no countable set of distinct evolutions to typicalize. Unique-measure stays conditional. $1/N$ is not put in to fill the gap.

Abandonable: if this working hypothesis is a blind alley, drop type (ii) and the in-world layer is unchanged.
