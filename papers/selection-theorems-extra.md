# Named selection-theorem hypotheses are extra-on-extra

David Strayhorn

Live hole is the law of $R$, not a grain. A measurable selection of successors is already named leftover (`papers/measurable-selection.md`, on main via #63): a function, not the graph, extra-on-extra, not a law of $R$. Closed-graph plus compact-valued plus leftover (1) as a bundle for upper hemicontinuity is already named extra-on-extra. The classical selection theorems that would *produce* a selection from those extras are a further extra-on-extra. Name them. Do not adopt them as a law of $R$. Do not invent Polish structure. Do not invent a grain. Not $\Phi$. No $|a|^2$. No $L$. No $1/N$. Paper 1 not rewritten.

**Status.** Named leftover. Kuratowski--Ryll-Nardzewski, Jankov--von Neumann, and Michael are extra-on-extra relative to a measurable selection and to the Fermi-slice topology. None is lock-side (Proposition 13, Theorem 19). None grains $\mathrm{Obs}$. Not adopted as a law of $R$.

## 1. What would have to be assumed

Theorem 16 (`papers/obs-as-a-space.md`): Fermi-slice topology exists and is Hausdorff. Borel $\sigma$-algebra of that topology is stateable. That does not make $\mathrm{Obs}$ Polish, standard Borel, paracompact in a useful way, or a Banach/Fréchet space. Those are not locked. Do not invent them.

**Kuratowski--Ryll-Nardzewski.** A weakly measurable closed-valued map from a measurable space into a Polish space admits a measurable selection. Extra-on-extra: it needs a Polish (or complete separable metric) structure on $\mathrm{Obs}$, which is not locked.

**Jankov--von Neumann.** Analytic uniformization. Extra-on-extra: it needs a standard Borel (or similar) structure, which is not locked.

**Michael.** A lower hemicontinuous closed *convex*-valued map from a paracompact Hausdorff space into a Banach (or Fréchet) space admits a continuous selection. Extra-on-extra: it needs paracompactness and a linear structure on $\mathrm{Obs}$, which are not locked, *and* convex-valued stars, which is a further extra-on-extra (convex subsets of a linear structure that is itself not locked). Closed-valued into Banach does not suffice. Do not write Michael as if closed stars in a Banach $\mathrm{Obs}$ give a continuous selection. A continuous selection would typically give a continuum of lumps (Theorems 17--18). That is not a grain and not the 2008 job.

Closed-graph plus compact-valued plus leftover (1) as a bundle for upper hemicontinuity is already named. It is not a selection theorem and is not a law of $R$.

---

## 2. Not a law of $R$, not a grain

Proposition 13, Theorem 19: locked data at $O$ do not determine type-(ii) edges. A selection theorem is a rule that *outputs* a function $s$ from extra hypotheses on stars. That is extra-on-extra relative to the law of $R$, not the law itself. Do not write KRN, JvN, or Michael as a law of $R$.

None of them grains $\mathrm{Obs}$. A measurable or continuous selection can have a continuum image (Theorems 17--18). They do not finite-support $R$. They do not make $\mathrm{Path}(O_0)$ countable (Theorem 31 is countable out-degree).

**Not claimed.** No such theorem applies. Empty $R$ has no nonempty-star selection. A constant map on a singleton star is a selection and a map, not the 2008 job. Do not claim no selection exists.
