# The transition matrix as a structure on lumps

David Strayhorn

What the 2005 / Strayhorn3 transition matrix would even be if restated on $\mathrm{Obs}=\mathcal{P}/{\sim_L}$. Still not adopted. Not a law of $R$ (Theorem 19). Do not put $1/N$. Do not lock a grain. Do not restore 2005 $3$-manifold observers. Do not restore the 2007 geodesic-generated tree. Do not write $\Phi$.

Companion: `papers/law-of-r.md`.

**Status.** On lumps, “transition matrix” means a directed graph $R$ on a vertex-set $V\subseteq\mathrm{Obs}$ (Definition 7). Without a grain, $V$ may be uncountable and $R$ is a relation, not a matrix in the usual sense. With a grain, $R$ becomes a $0$–$1$ matrix indexed by $V$. Strayhorn3’s $d$ and 2005’s discrete $N$-way branches are how the corpus got a matrix; both are extra. Merging is allowed in the corpus (Strayhorn3 fn. 4), so the structure is not necessarily a tree. Named, not adopted.

---

## 1. What the corpus wrote

**Strayhorn3.** Truncate each jet coefficient to $d$ decimals. From each remaining point $p$, draw directed links to other points. Paths from $p$ form a branching diagram, called the *transition matrix*. Evolution is read off the local diagram in an “infinitesimal ball” about $p$. Merging as well as branching. Probability by counting paths (outcome counting). The $d\to\infty$ geodesic slogan is not in-world hitchhiking.

**2005 RHF.** Observer $=$ bounded $3$-manifold. Unique tree diagram in $O$-state space. Random walk along that diagram. $1/N$ at a discrete $N$-way branch; $N\to\infty$ as a current. That *object* is not restored. The *combinatorial shape* — vertices, directed edges, paths, optional fusion — is what can be copied onto lumps.

**2007 OutcomeCounting.** Tree generated by geodesics across $M_O$. Not this structure (Theorem 11).

## 2. Restated on lumps, without adopting it

Let $\mathrm{Obs}$ be the set of lumps (Theorem 10), with Fermi-slice topology (Theorem 16) available but not required.

**Definition 9 (transition structure on lumps, not adopted).** A pair $(V,R)$ with $V\subseteq\mathrm{Obs}$ and $R\subseteq V\times V$. Same as Definition 7. Call it a *transition matrix* only when $V$ is at most countable, so $R$ is a $0$–$1$ matrix in some enumeration of $V$. Weights are not part of the definition; $1/N$ is not written.

**What this is not.**

- Not a $3$-manifold observer. Vertices are $\sim_L$-classes of analytic germs, not bounded $3$-geometries.
- Not type (i). Edges in $R$ that lie along $\gamma_O$ are hitchhiking redrawn; the across-worlds content is type (ii), off $\gamma_O$.
- Not the 2007 geodesic-generated tree.
- Not a neighborhood-law: “links into an infinitesimal ball” names an uncountable set of candidates (Theorem 17), not a row of a matrix.

**Discrete vs not.** If $V=\mathrm{Obs}$, Definition 9 is a directed graph on a (likely uncountable) set. That is a relation, not a matrix. Strayhorn3 obtained a matrix by $T_d$. 2005 obtained discrete $N$ by assumption on the tree diagram. Both cuts are extra structure (Theorems 18–19; grain not locked).

**Not necessarily a tree.** Unique path from the root to each vertex is an extra property. The corpus allows fusion. A DAG, or a general directed graph, is the honest copy of the corpus shape. “Unique Everettian tree rooted at $O$” is 2007 language, and for geodesic-generated trees it is already killed as a theorem of layer (i).

## 3. What would still be needed to have a matrix

To have a transition *matrix* on lumps, in the corpus’s sense, one would still need:

1. A countable (or finite, locally finite) $V$ — a grain, or another discrete cut, not adopted.
2. A rule for which pairs from $V\times V$ lie in $R$ — the law of $R$, extra structure (Theorem 19).
3. Optionally, uniqueness of paths (a tree) or permission to merge — a further graph-theoretic hypothesis.
4. Optionally, $1/N$ or path-counting — a probability rule, not adopted, not Born, not $|a|^2$.

None of (1)–(4) is supplied by the jet/patch, hitchhiking, $E(O)$, or the Fermi-slice topology.

---

## 4. Report line

- **Named, not adopted.** Definition 9: on lumps, the corpus “transition matrix” is Definition 7, and is a matrix only once $V$ is discrete. Merging allowed; not necessarily a tree. $1/N$ not included.
- **Killed.** Treating an uncountable neighborhood as a matrix row. Restoring $3$-manifold vertices. Identifying this with the 2007 geodesic-generated tree.
- **Open.** Grain; the law of $R$; tree vs DAG vs general $R$.
