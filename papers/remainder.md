## Locked

- Elementary observer $O=(j_\infty,u)$, equivalently the unique simply-connected real-analytic patch $P(O)$. Local continuation only, not a completed $4$-manifold $W$.
- Extra topologies are the ensemble $E(O)$, not extra observers. $E$ is not folded into $\sim$.
- $\mathrm{Obs}=\mathcal{P}/{\sim_L}$ as a *set* of lumps (Theorem 10). Fermi-slice $/O(3)$ is a Hausdorff topology on that set (Theorem 16); gauge as a *named* space (Gielen–Wise, a jet bundle of a $W$) stays open.
- Motion stand-in: geodesic hitchhiking. One curvelet $\gamma_O$ per $O$ (Prop. 3.2). $E_\gamma$ shares it (Theorem 4). No $\sim$-split at $\partial P$ (Theorem 7). Lifetime $\tau_\partial$ may be finite or infinite (Theorem 6).
- Analytic geodesic lifts cannot delayed-fork (Theorems 2–3, 9). Typicality of branches from one $O$ is empty (Theorem 8). The 2007 across-worlds tree is not a theorem of that layer (Theorem 11).
- Hitchhiking is a restriction of 2007, not a faithful reading.

Paper 1’s ontology cut is unchanged.

## Killed (on this object, under hitchhiking)

- Reconstructing a unique cosmos $W$ from $O$.
- Finite $k$-jets or $C^\infty$ as the elementary object (Borel).
- Delayed forks of analytic geodesic lifts, including at $\partial P$.
- Restoring “agree then diverge” as a theorem of leaving the patch.
- Typicality over $\sim$-branches from one $O$ without a new hypothesis.
- Reading hitchhiking as 2007. Reading the 2007 geodesic-generated tree as a theorem of the geodesic-analytic layer.
- Writing a geodesic $\Phi$ to save 2008 (analytic-curve $\Phi$ cannot delayed-fork).
- Raw coordinate jets, or $\mathrm{Met}(M)/\mathrm{Diff}(M)$, as $\mathrm{Obs}$. Collapsing $\mathrm{Obs}$ into $E(O)$.
- Union-of-ensembles as Hilbert space. $|a|^2$ as $M$. $\chi$ / $4$-geon / spin-foam as this program.
- Restricted Minkowski as a finite-lifetime example.
- Engulfing-in-$P(O)$ as the leftover motion (PR #15): it coarsens $\gamma_O$, it does not supply type-(ii) edges.
- Locking a grain, $1/N$, a measure on $\mathrm{Obs}$, or $\Phi$, in the notes that named them.
- Neighborhoods as a countable $N$ (Theorem 17): a Fermi-slice open ball minus $\gamma_O$ is uncountable.
- A countable grain read off finitely many continuous invariants (Theorem 18, connected opens). Rounding is a mesh, the same kind of choice as $d$. Grain is extra structure, not a theorem of the jet/patch, and is not adopted.
- Named lock-side functors as a law of $R$ (Theorem 19): listed data at $O$, open neighborhoods, continuous $f:\mathrm{Obs}\to\mathbb{R}^n$ do not supply a nonempty countable typicality set of delayed forks. Not a blanket “no map” (empty and $\{O_{\mathrm{Mink}}\}$ are maps). Not a ban on nonempty proper subsets of a neighborhood (Ricci-flat / Weyl $=0$ / Petrov walls). Discrete algebraic invariants (Petrov type, $\dim$ Killing) fail to grain; not a fourth functor in Theorem 19.
- Walk-count as a finite relative probability on a graph with a pumpable cycle (Theorem 20). Strayhorn3 both-ways, as walks, is ill-defined (Corollary 20.1). Grain or $|a|^2$ as a lock-side fix of the count.
- Named candidates as a lock-side slice (Theorem 22): hitchhiker $\tau$, Fermi balls, continuous $f$, grain. Not a closed “nothing supplies a finite slice.” Locally finite one-step out-neighborhoods are finite slices and come from a law of $R$ (extra). Unique-tree as a theorem of Definition 7. $1/N$ flow as path-count (they disagree except on special graphs).
- Extra $W$ from $E(O)$ as a lock-side clock (Theorem 23(2a)): $E$-smuggling. Hitchhiker $\tau$ as a type-(ii) timestamp (Theorem 23(1)). In-patch Lorentzian distance as a *general* type-(ii) clock, or as a unique function of $(O,O^\prime)$ when $O^\prime$ occurs more than once (Theorem 23(2b): unique given a fixed $q$). Infimum among causal curves is typically $0$, not a clock.
- Fermi-slice distance as a lock-side slice or generation (Theorem 24): a continuous scale; rounding is a grain. Not a claim that no metric exists. Theorem 18 controls *values* of $d(O,\cdot)$, not fibers; metric spheres inherit Theorem 17’s continuum except at isolated extrema.
- Continuous $y$ as a lock-side finite-support of $R$ (Theorem 25). Integer $y\in[0,Y]$ is a grain. $|a|^2$ as $y$. $L$ is not written.
- Combinatorial $y$ (edge count, Euler, out-degree) as lock-side finite-support of $R$ (Theorem 26): it presupposes a finite diagram (a grain, or a locally finite law of $R$ plus a slice). Compact Lorentzian $\chi=0$ (Poincaré–Hopf) does not select type-(ii) edges; that is not Theorem 5 (Markov no-census).
- $S(O)$ as a finite typicality set, or $E$-smuggling as an enlargement of in-patch support (Theorem 27). The set is lock-side; nonconstant curvature $\Rightarrow$ continuum of lumps; local homogeneity $\Rightarrow$ Occ may collapse and $S$ may be empty. The restriction of $R$ to $S(O)$ is extra as a choice and does not grain.
- Named lock-side subsets of $S(O)$ as a countable typicality set without a grain (Theorem 28): Fermi spatial slice at one $\tau$, a Cauchy surface in $P(O)$, integer curvature bins. One class or a continuum, or a mesh. Not a closed “no countable subset” ($\mathbb{Q}$-meshes extra; Minkowski $S=\emptyset$).
- Morse critical points, conjugate locus, or cut locus of $p$ as a lock-side countable $N$ (Theorem 29). Morse is extra. Cut is where a geodesic ceases to maximize $\tau(p,q)$ (sup), typically a spine/hypersurface; first-cut in each spatial direction is a $2$-parameter continuum.
- Chronological/causal future in $P(O)$, Einstein/vacuum, or energy conditions as a lock-side finite support of $R$ (Theorem 30). Named remainder: the well of lock-side support constraints without a grain is dry as of that inventory. Not a closed “no constraint exists.”
- Reading a grain of $\mathrm{Obs}$ as required for countable evolutions from one $O$ (Theorem 31): countable out-degree of $R$ already makes $\mathrm{Path}(O_0)$ countable; $V$ may remain a continuum. Countable out-degree is extra, not lock-side, and is not adopted. Uniformity of $R$ does not replace it (`papers/uniform-law-of-r.md`). Reading type (ii) as if extra edges already supplied delayed forks (Theorem 32): unique-successor $R$ has none.
- Reading Theorem 16 as a measurable, closed-graph, compact-valued, or proper law of $R$. Those extras are named (`papers/measurable-r.md`, `papers/closed-graph-r.md`, `papers/compact-stars.md`, `papers/proper-r.md`).

## What the 2008 typicality job still needs

2008 wanted a unique measure from typicality of a countable set of distinct evolutions. On the locked object, hitchhiking does not supply those evolutions from one $O$.

Type-(ii) links are working leftover motion, abandonable (not taken: `papers/abandon-type-ii.md`). Lock-side support constraints without a grain are dry as inventoried (Theorem 30). Companion: `papers/extra-structure.md`.

To do the 2008 job *here*, one would still have to assume extra structure, at least:

1. **Grain of $\mathrm{Obs}$** (or some other discrete cut of lumps). Extra. Neighborhoods do not count (Theorem 17). Continuous invariants do not grain (Theorem 18). Named subsets of $S(O)$, Morse/conjugate/cut, $I^+$, vacuum, energy conditions do not (Theorems 28–30). $d$-decimal is combinatorial, not put in by hand. Not required for countable *paths* from one $O$ (Theorem 31).
2. **A law of $R$.** Which type-(ii) pairs are edged. Extra relative to named lock-side functors (Theorem 19). Uniformity extra (`papers/uniform-law-of-r.md`). Countable out-degree extra (Theorem 31): $\mathrm{Path}(O_0)$ is finite walks and is countable without graining $\mathrm{Obs}$; infinite rays of a countably branching tree may be a continuum; 2008 counts finite walks. Branching extra (Theorem 32): delayed forks need some reachable out-degree $\ge 2$. Locality extra (`papers/local-law-of-r.md`). No dead ends extra (`papers/dead-ends-and-rays.md`). Countable in-degree extra (Theorem 33). Acyclicity extra for finite $N$, not for a countable walk-set (`papers/acyclicity-of-r.md`). Finite $\mathrm{Anc}(q)$ extra for $N\in\mathbb{N}$ (Theorem 21 example; `papers/finite-ancestors.md`). Markov (one-step) is Definition 9; history-dependent is extra-on-extra (`papers/markov-law-of-r.md`). Measurability extra, toward a measure, not a grain (`papers/measurable-r.md`). Closed-graph extra, distinct from locality and Borel (`papers/closed-graph-r.md`). Compact-valued stars extra, distinct from closed-graph (`papers/compact-stars.md`). Leftover (1) extra (`papers/proper-r.md`, on main via #58). Open-graph extra, dual of closed-graph (`papers/open-graph-r.md`, on main via #59). None is lock-side. Empty and $\{O_{\mathrm{Mink}}\}$ are laws; they are not the 2008 job.
3. **A slice / clock**, if path-count is to be a probability of type-(ii) targets. Named non-slices fail (Theorem 22). Hitchhiker $\tau$ is type (i) only (Theorem 23(1)). Fermi-slice distance is a continuous scale (Theorem 24). A locally finite one-step out-neighborhood is a finite slice *from* a law of $R$ (extra). Rounding is a grain.
4. **Tree or DAG**, if walk-count is the typicality rule. Unique-tree extra. On a DAG, $N(p,q)$ is well-defined (Theorem 21). On a pumpable cycle it is infinite (Theorem 20). $1/N$ flow is a different extra rule.
5. **A measure on $\mathrm{Obs}$**, if typicality of many $O$ is wanted instead of (or after) delayed forks from one $O$. Fermi-slice topology exists (Theorem 16) and does not count (Theorem 17). A measure is not written.

Those five are extra. Unique-measure stays conditional on them (`papers/unique-measure-conditions.md`). Circularity of “count first, then typicality, then the measure that was used to count” is a named remaining lock (`papers/circularity.md`). No resolver adopted.
