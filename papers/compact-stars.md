## 1. What is now stateable

Theorem 16: the Fermi-slice $/O(3)$ quotient is a Hausdorff topology on $\mathrm{Obs}$. Out-stars $\mathrm{succ}(O)$ are then subsets of a topological space. Relative compactness (or compactness) of each such subset is a well-defined extra rule. It is not written. Do not claim the Fermi-slice topology is locally compact or Polish. That is not locked. Name the extra without inventing that structure.

Locality (`papers/local-law-of-r.md`, on main) is a neighborhood-constraint $\mathrm{succ}(O)\subseteq U$ for some Fermi-slice open $U\ni O$. Extra and different. Already named. Not reopened here.

Measurability (`papers/measurable-r.md`, on main) is Borel (etc.) graph of $R$ in the product. Extra and different. Already named. Not reopened here.

Closed-graph $R$ (`papers/closed-graph-r.md`, on main via #55) is closed graph in the product, or closed out-stars. Extra and different. Compact-valued is extra even without closed graph.

Closed plus compact-valued would be a further extra (the pair often used for upper hemicontinuity). Extra-on-extra. Not adopted. Do not write a selection theorem as a law of $R$.

## 2. Compact-valued stars are not lock-side and are not a grain

Proposition 13: locked data at $O$ do not determine type-(ii) edges. Theorem 19: named functors do not supply $R$. Compact-valued stars are therefore extra relative to the lock, same as the law of $R$ itself.

A compact set in a Fermi-slice chart is typically a continuum (Theorem 17). Compact-valued stars do not grain $\mathrm{Obs}$. They do not finite-support $R$. They do not make $\mathrm{Path}(O_0)$ countable. Theorem 31 (countable out-degree) is a different extra. Compact does not imply countable or finite.

**Not claimed.** No compact-valued $R$ exists. Empty star is compact. A singleton star is compact. They are maps and not the 2008 job. A finite set of lumps is compact in any topology (and, under Theorem 16, the Fermi-slice quotient is Hausdorff, so finite stars are compact there). Finite is compact; compact is not finite.
