## 2. König, and cardinality of rays

Let $(V,R)$ be a directed graph, $O_0\in V$. Write $\mathrm{Ray}(O_0)$ for the set of infinite walks from $O_0$. $\mathrm{Path}(O_0)$ remains finite walks (Theorem 31).

**Lemma (König, named not numbered).** If $|\mathrm{succ}(v)|$ is finite and nonempty at every $v$ reachable from $O_0$, and the reachable set is infinite, then $\mathrm{Ray}(O_0)$ is nonempty.

**Proof.** Standard: the reachable subgraph is an infinite locally finite tree (or fold to the unfolding). Some vertex has infinitely many descendants; pick a successor that still has infinitely many, and iterate. $\square$

**Lemma (cardinality).** There exist locally finite graphs with countable $\mathrm{Path}(O_0)$ and $|\mathrm{Ray}(O_0)|=2^{\aleph_0}$. The infinite rooted binary tree is one.

**Proof.** Finite words on $\{0,1\}$ are countable (Theorem 31 with $|\mathrm{succ}|=2$). Infinite words are $2^{\aleph_0}$. $\square$

Typicality of *completed* evolutions would need a further cut of $\mathrm{Ray}(O_0)$ (a grain of the boundary, or a finite depth). Extra. 2008 as written counts finite walks, not rays.

**Not claimed.** König writes a law of $R$. Finite out-degree is extra (Theorems 22, 31). No dead ends is extra. Infinite reachable set is extra.

**Not claimed.** A closed “no infinite-ray typicality.” Empty $R$ has no rays. A ray-graph has one.
