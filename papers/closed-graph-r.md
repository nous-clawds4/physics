## 1. What is now stateable

Theorem 16: the Fermi-slice $/O(3)$ quotient is a Hausdorff topology on $\mathrm{Obs}$. Product topology on $\mathrm{Obs}\times\mathrm{Obs}$ exists. $R\subset\mathrm{Obs}\times\mathrm{Obs}$ is then a subset of a topological space. Closedness of that subset in the product, or closedness of each out-star $\mathrm{succ}(O)$ in the Fermi-slice topology, is a well-defined extra rule. It is not written. Do not claim the Fermi-slice topology is Polish or standard Borel. That is not locked. Closed graph is extra even without it.

Locality (`papers/local-law-of-r.md`, on main) is a neighborhood-constraint $\mathrm{succ}(O)\subseteq U$ for some Fermi-slice open $U\ni O$. Extra and different. Already named. Not reopened here.

Measurability (`papers/measurable-r.md`, on main via #54) is Borel (etc.) graph of $R$ in the product. Extra and different. Already named. Not reopened here as if new.

Closed graph is distinct from a neighborhood-law, from a mere set of pairs, and from Borel measurability.

## 2. Closed-graph $R$ is not lock-side and is not a grain

Proposition 13: locked data at $O$ do not determine type-(ii) edges. Theorem 19: named functors do not supply $R$. A closed graph is therefore extra relative to the lock, same as the law of $R$ itself.

A nonempty closed set in a Fermi-slice neighborhood is typically a continuum (Theorem 17). Closed graph does not grain $\mathrm{Obs}$. It does not finite-support $R$. It does not make $\mathrm{Path}(O_0)$ countable. Theorem 31 (countable out-degree) is a different extra.

**Not claimed.** No closed-graph $R$ exists. A continuous selection of successors exists. Continuous selection would typically give a continuum of lumps (Theorems 17–18). Empty $R$ is closed. A singleton pair is closed. They are maps and not the 2008 job.
