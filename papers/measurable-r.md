## 1. What is now stateable

Theorem 16: the Fermi-slice $/O(3)$ quotient is a Hausdorff topology on $\mathrm{Obs}$. Product topology on $\mathrm{Obs}\times\mathrm{Obs}$ exists. $R\subset\mathrm{Obs}\times\mathrm{Obs}$ is then a subset of a topological space. Borel / universally measurable / Baire-property of that subset is a well-defined extra rule. It is not written.

Locality (`papers/local-law-of-r.md`, on main) is a neighborhood-constraint $\mathrm{succ}(O)\subseteq U$ for some Fermi-slice open $U\ni O$. Extra and different. Already named. Not reopened here.

---

## 2. Measurability is not lock-side and is not a grain

Proposition 13: locked data at $O$ do not determine type-(ii) edges. Theorem 19: named functors do not supply $R$. A Borel graph is therefore extra relative to the lock, same as the law of $R$ itself.

A Borel set can be a continuum. Measurability does not grain $\mathrm{Obs}$. It does not finite-support $R$. It does not make $\mathrm{Path}(O_0)$ countable. Theorem 31 (countable out-degree) is a different extra. Unique-measure conditions (`papers/unique-measure-conditions.md`) stay named, not adopted. A Borel graph is extra *toward* a measure, not a measure, not $\Phi$.

**Not claimed.** No measurable $R$ exists. A continuous selection of successors exists. Continuous selection would typically give a continuum of lumps (Theorems 17–18). Empty $R$ is Borel. $\{O_{\mathrm{Mink}}\}\times\{O_{\mathrm{Mink}}\}$ is Borel. They are maps and not the 2008 job.
