# Unique-measure as named conditions

David Strayhorn

What would have to be true for a unique measure to go through on this object. Named, not adopted. Not a theorem of the lock. Not $\Phi$. No $|a|^2$. No $L$. No $1/N$ put in by hand. Paper 1 not rewritten.

Companions: `papers/typicality-under-the-lock.md`, `papers/extra-structure.md` (PR #42), `papers/remainder.md`.

**Status.** Unique-measure stays conditional. Hitchhiking does not supply a countable typicality set from one $O$ (Theorem 8). Named lock-side functors do not (Theorem 19). Named support constraints do not finite-support $R$ without a grain (Theorem 30). The conditions below are extra structure. None is written. Circularity of “count first, then typicality, then the measure that was used to count” is not resolved.

---

## 1. Two jobs, two condition-lists

2008 wanted one reasonable measure so that GR and QM look valid to the vast majority of observers. Under the lock that splits:

**From one $O$ (delayed forks).** Count $\sim$-distinct evolutions that agree on a prefix and then disagree. Empty under hitchhiking (Theorem 8). Type (ii) is the working leftover motion that could refill it (`papers/type-ii-adopted.md`). Not abandoned (`papers/abandon-type-ii.md`, PR #42).

**Of many $O$.** A measure on $\mathrm{Obs}$ as a set of lumps. Fermi-slice topology exists (Theorem 16) and does not count (Theorem 17). Continuous invariants do not grain (Theorem 18).

Either job, if it is to yield a *unique* measure, needs the conditions in §2. They are extra. Not adopted.

---

## 2. Named conditions (all extra)

For unique-measure from delayed forks at one $O$ to go through, all of the following would have to hold:

1. **Leftover motion.** Type-(ii) edges exist (or some other non-geodesic law that supplies delayed forks). Working, abandonable. Not a theorem of layer (i) (Theorem 11).
2. **A countable typicality set.** A grain, or some other discrete cut, so the set of distinct evolutions from that $O$ is nonempty and countable. Extra (Theorems 17–19, 28–30).
3. **A law of $R$.** Which pairs are edged. Extra relative to named lock-side functors (Theorem 19). The lock-side support well is dry (Theorem 30).
4. **A well-defined count**, if the typicality rule is path-count. Unique-tree extra. DAG: $N(p,q)$ well-defined (Theorem 21). Cycle: infinite (Theorem 20). A slice so $N(p,\cdot)$ is a probability (Theorem 22).
5. **A typicality rule that actually determines a unique measure.** Path-count and $1/N$ flow disagree except on special graphs. $1/N$ is extra, not put in. Uniqueness of the *rule* is extra, not a theorem of Definition 7.
6. **Circularity resolved.** The measure used to call a set “typical” is not the measure being derived from that typicality. Named leftover, not resolved.

For unique-measure from many $O$: a measure (or a grain that makes a counting measure) on $\mathrm{Obs}$. Extra. Fermi-slice neighborhoods do not supply it.

---

## 3. What is not claimed

Not claimed: these conditions hold. Not claimed: unique-measure is impossible. Empty and singleton typicality sets give a unique (trivial) measure; they are not the 2008 job. $\{O_{\mathrm{Mink}}\}$ is a law of $R$, not that job.

Not $\Phi$. Not Born. Not union-of-ensembles as Hilbert space.

---

## 4. Named leftover, not adopted

Unique-measure as the 2008 output, conditional on §2. Grain, law of $R$, slice, tree-or-DAG, typicality rule, measure on $\mathrm{Obs}$, circularity-resolution: extra. Type (ii) working, not abandoned.
