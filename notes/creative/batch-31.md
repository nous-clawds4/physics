## Plain summary for David

Your picture: a topological geon whose shadow traces a loop, like a virtual pair that appears and later recombines. If something winds around the handle in the not-shadow space, its total winding must come in whole multiples of 2π, and two geons can annihilate only if their phases line up exactly (for opposite directions, perhaps 180° apart). Annihilated geons never reach the detector, and that would be a classical foot in the door for negative interference.

What we propose:
- **A name for the not-shadow space:** *object spacetime* (the thing that casts the shadow), or *host spacetime*. This is your choice.
- **There is no "change" in one world** (closest prior art: Stueckelberg 1941 and Feynman 1949 read a pair as one time-reversing worldline; Hawking's virtual black-hole pairs are quantum and are not imported). Seen from above, one 4-manifold is fixed. A "virtual pair" is a fixed 4D handle structure (like a cobordism) whose shadow is a closed loop, or a path from the source to the detector.
- **There are more closures than pairs:** triples, n-tuples, nested splittings (1→3→1 inside a pair), and in general a graph of geon worldlines, which looks like a Feynman diagram. Each one shadows to one path in "all possible paths".
- **Where the phase would come from:** a twist or holonomy carried around the 4D structure. The step from a whole-number winding to an angle must be fixed by the geometry, not chosen by us.
- **Your clock-hand refinement (1:54 AM):** one small correction. +π and −π are the same clock position (both −1), so on their own they add up rather than cancel. More generally, your closure condition (the hand lines up at the end, so the loop winds a whole number of turns) makes the two arrows point the same way whatever each path winds, so two closed paths never cancel. Cancelling needs the hands to end half a turn apart, which breaks the alignment you asked for; our earlier fix (cancel at φ = π/2) has exactly that problem. Your own example does cancel in one reading (a Creative Lead / reviewer reading, Claude 021, not your words): spin-½ (MTW's 4π), where the arrow turns half as far as the hand. Then π and −π give arrows a quarter turn either way, which point opposite ways and cancel, while the hand itself has turned one full 2π. So spin-½ is not a follow-up: it is the reading in which your picture cancels at all. Two things stay open: what geometry sets how far each path winds, and how arrows add.
- **What "annihilate" would mean:** the handles must be capped off by a compact 4D cobordism (A2). Trivial holonomy alone (A1) does not remove a handle, and "180° out of phase" (A3) is a picture of A1, not the condition. In our G1 toy the ℤ₂ is a reflection, not a 180° rotation. Capping off, though, is always possible as far as topology goes. What limits it is the metric, causality and spin structure (with a spin structure, wormholes can only be made or removed in pairs: Gibbons–Hawking 1992), and none of these looks at the phase. So "they annihilate only if their phases line up" still needs a geometric source. In a toy with 2D space, handles cannot appear or disappear between connected slices under a smooth, non-degenerate Lorentzian metric, so this needs 3D space.
- **CTCs and the lab:** the CTCs that Geroch's theorem requires can, by our argument (not a proved theorem; unchecked), be confined to where the space topology changes, at the closure's vertices (where geons appear, split, join or disappear), not along the paths between them. Our flat map simply collapses small CTCs (#410 §1). So each vertex region must be micro or lie outside the lab region, while the members can separate macroscopically between vertices. They must, for a loop or path shadow, since a closure that fits in a single ℓ-ball shadows to a point. CTCs are now adopted, per your 2:27 AM words (relayed via Physics Lead; hold level unspecified). The 1:56 AM "allowed tentatively" ruling is kept as history, superseded.
- **Negative interference without negative numbers:** a closure that annihilates never reaches the detector, so the geometry excludes those worlds. No weight is negative. This is the "exclusion" exit from Lemma A.
- **The goal is Feynman's path integral.** We must *derive* the rule that adds phases. The known rule (Schulman 1968; Laidlaw–DeWitt 1971) is quantum, so importing it is not allowed.

Nothing is computed, and the dark-fringe test is set aside for now, as you asked.

Their arrows e^{iφ₁} and e^{iφ₂} then coincide and reinforce, whatever each path winds. Cancellation needs φ₁ − φ₂ = π (mod 2π), a loop winding of an odd multiple of π, which breaks alignment for a clock hand. ## 3. Bird's-eye reading (Creative Lead proposal)

- In one world there is one fixed 4-manifold, so nothing "changes" (David 1:36 AM). Topology change is a statement about spatial slices: the topology differs between two spacelike hypersurfaces that bound the region (with CTCs there is no global time function, so no family Σ_t is assumed).
- A "virtual pair" is a 4D handle structure. "Virtual" means only that its members' worldlines begin and end inside the world (created and capped off). Each member is an ordinary classical geon; nothing is off-shell, and no quantum sense is meant. Between vertices the members are carried handles in batch 30's sense (fixed spatial topology, B5). Between two slices, the spatial topology gains a handle and then loses it. The region between them is cobordism-like.
- **Shadow:** the image under the LEA chart f of the pair's world-tubes (each geon micro, mouths in a common ℓ-ball), determined to within εR + ℓ. It is a closed loop (vacuum-like closure) or, when tied to the source and detector, a path from source to detector. A closure contained in one ℓ-ball shadows to a point, so a loop or path shadow needs the members to separate macroscopically. (C3) passes closed causal curves whose images have time-extent ≲ εL, so micro CTCs collapse, and fails macroscopic ones (#410 §1 and §4 condition 3).
- **Costs** (CTCs adopted, David 2:27 AM, hold level unspecified): Geroch 1967 (compact interpolating region) says Lorentzian topology change on a compact region needs CTCs or loses time-orientability. The alternative is degenerate metrics (Horowitz 1991) or Morse-type points (Dowker et al.). With CTCs adopted, the Geroch route can be examined: a time-oriented compact Lorentzian cobordism between non-diffeomorphic slices contains CTCs, and this is consistent with the charter addendum. If the field equations and the weak energy condition hold, topology change cannot occur on Geroch's compact interpolating spacetime, and within a finite region it must come with singularities (Tipler, Ann. Phys. 108, 1 (1977); abstract read). Borde (gr-qc/9406053; abstract read) extends Geroch's causality violation to "causally compact" interpolations, a wide class, and shows that in dimensions ≥ 3 these cannot satisfy Einstein's equation with a reasonable source, even at the price of singularities. So Borde widens the cost rather than relaxing it; only interpolations that are not causally compact escape. Geroch's theorem gives a CTC somewhere in any time-oriented compact cobordism with spacelike boundary. Applied to thin slabs, and since product regions need none, this suggests (an argument, not the theorem; spatial localisation needs a causally compact version, Borde, read in abstract only) that CTCs can be confined to the region where the slice topology changes: for a closure, neighbourhoods of its vertices (creation, splitting, joining, annihilation). Between vertices the slices keep one topology and the geons just move, and a product region with a timelike field transverse to the slices needs no CTCs. #410's ill-posedness clause (§4 condition 3) concerns closed causal curves whose images have time-extent > εL. So each vertex region must be micro, which collapses its CTCs under (C3), or lie outside U, and the members may separate macroscopically between vertices, as a loop or path shadow requires. (Requiring the whole closure to be micro or outside U would leave no closure in U with a loop or path shadow, contrary to §4 and §8.) The analytic lock's status for such manifolds is not checked.

## 4. The family of closures (Creative Lead proposal)

- **Pair:** two geons appear and later recombine. The shadow is one loop, or two arcs.
- **n-tuple:** n geons appear and annihilate all together.
- **Nested:** a pair appears, one member splits 1→3→1, and then the pair recombines.
- **General:** a finite graph of geon worldlines in object spacetime. Vertices are creation, splitting, joining or annihilation events, and the edges are geon worldlines. This is Feynman-diagram-like 4D topology. Each closure shadows to one path (or path set) in the sum over paths.
- **Bookkeeping:** a closure is labelled by its 4D topology plus its gluing data (Γ and the extension), so it is world data (#399 §3). Different closures are different worlds, or different structures within one world. Which one holds is open (§8: across worlds it would be a complex-valued weight on W).

## 5. The phase (Creative Lead proposal)

- **Geometric source:** the twist or holonomy carried along the 4D structure. That is the holonomy of the closed loop the members' worldlines form (only loop holonomies are gauge-invariant, §9): the linear part of its deck element, together with the enclosed curvature (#418 B1–B3: fixed by the germ, Γ and the route, and loop-dependent).
- **From integer winding to phase:** David's "integer multiple of 2π" needs a map ℤ → U(1), n ↦ e^{inα}. For T2 cleanliness, α must be fixed by the geometry, for example the holonomy angle of a one-parameter subgroup selected by the structure's own symmetry. It must not be chosen by hand. Topology alone does not fix it: Laidlaw–DeWitt find "as many distinct propagators as there are scalar representations" of π₁, so there the character is a free label. A geometry-fixed α must come from metric data (the holonomy) together with how the carried object turns under it: e^{ikθ}, with k = 1 for a carried vector and k = ½ for the spin-½ candidate (§1b). k is a property of the particle, still to be derived. In a non-orientable (G1) world the half-angle needs a Pin structure rather than a spin structure (unchecked for the toy). A natural candidate is the rotation angle θ of an orientable (mirror) gluing, which is circle-valued. A non-orientable G1 piece has reflection holonomy (det −1), which gives ℤ₂ = ±1 only through the chosen character det ↦ ±1. It is not rotation by 180° (det +1), which is a different gluing, the corrected point inversion of #408. Under the lock a flat 2D cover has no rotating deck element (#413 R2), so a circle-valued θ is a seam-toy property or needs curvature or 3D.
- **T1:** values on the unit circle (a continuum, or a sign ±1) are necessary, not sufficient (#418). They label routes. They do not add.
- **T2:** clean only if α comes from Γ and the germ.

## 6. The annihilation condition (Creative Lead proposal)

Candidate topological statements:
- **(A1) Charge triviality:** the product of the geons' holonomies, or their topological charges, around the closure is the identity in the relevant group (for instance, the mapping-class or deck charges cancel).
- **(A2) Capping off:** the slice carrying the geons and a later slice without them are joined, inside object spacetime, by a compact 4D cobordism that caps off the geons' summands, so no handle survives to the detector slice. (It is the 3D slice that is capped off; the 4D structure does not itself "bound".)
- **(A3) Phase matching:** relative holonomy π between the two members (David's "180° out of phase" for opposite orientations). For a clock hand this breaks alignment (§1b). In the spin-½ reading it is an aligned relative turn of 2π, which puts the half-angle arrows π apart.

Reading: (A3) is probably a *picture* of (A1) for a pair. Reversing orientation inverts the holonomy, so "coincide" for opposite directions means the product is trivial. For a ℤ₂ piece the matching is a sign (det ↦ ±1), not an angle (§5). Whether (A1) and (A2) are equivalent for our gluings is not checked. (A1) alone does not remove a handle: trivial holonomy does not mean the handle is absent (a flat wrap has trivial holonomy and non-trivial π₁). Only (A2), a compact cobordism capping the handles off, is topological removal. But none of the three selects which closures annihilate. By this Reading's own step (reversing orientation inverts the holonomy), (A1) holds for every geon and its orientation-reversed partner. Every closed 3-manifold bounds a compact 4-manifold (Rokhlin; Thom), so topologically any set of geons can be capped off. What restricts capping is extra structure: a Lorentzian metric (an Euler-characteristic condition: Reinhart 1963; Sorkin 1986), CTCs or loss of time-orientability (Geroch 1967), a spin structure (Gibbons–Hawking 1992: no single wormhole, but pairs are possible), and field equations with an energy condition (Tipler 1977; Borde 1994). None of these involves the holonomy of the loop the members' routes form, which is what (A3) and §7 need. Keep two things apart: a geon's own charge (its handle's deck element, which cancels automatically in a pair) and the holonomy carried along its route (route-dependent, which interference needs).

## 8. Path-integral goal (Creative Lead proposal)

What would need to be derived, not imported, to reach Feynman's rule:
1. The set of closures that shadow to source→detector paths: the "all possible paths" of David's 1:36 AM message.
2. A geometry-fixed phase per closure (§5).
3. **The phase-addition rule:** why contributions combine as Σ e^{iφ} and not some other way. The known rule (Schulman 1968; Laidlaw–DeWitt 1971: weight each homotopy class by a unitary character of π₁ and sum) is the quantum path integral. Importing it imports QM, so it must come out of §6–§7. First say where the sum is taken. Across worlds, a sum of e^{iφ} is a complex-valued weight on W: a generalisation of the signed weights Set R rejects, and a quantum phase rule put in by hand. Within one world, every summed closure must be realised in that world, and a geometric process must do the combining; §6–§7 offer only capping off, which is all-or-nothing per closure, not addition. So the coherent target is a within-world exclusion or re-routing rule whose consequences match the observed pattern, not the form Σ e^{iφ}, which would be aiming at the answer (LT2).
4. Only after that, the step to frequencies. The Born rule itself is out of scope, and so are μ and |a|².
