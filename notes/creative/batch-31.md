## Plain summary for David

Your picture: a topological geon whose shadow traces a loop, like a virtual pair that appears and later recombines. If something winds around the handle in the not-shadow space, its total winding must come in whole multiples of 2π, and two geons can annihilate only if their phases line up exactly (for opposite directions, perhaps 180° apart). Annihilated geons never reach the detector, and that would be a classical foot in the door for negative interference.

What we propose:
- **A name for the not-shadow space:** *object spacetime* (the thing that casts the shadow), or *host spacetime*. This is your choice.
- **There is no "change" in one world** (closest prior art: Stueckelberg 1941 and Feynman 1949 read a pair as one time-reversing worldline; Hawking's virtual black-hole pairs are quantum and are not imported). Seen from above, one 4-manifold is fixed. A "virtual pair" is a fixed 4D handle structure (like a cobordism) whose shadow is a closed loop, or a path from the source to the detector.
- **There are more closures than pairs:** triples, n-tuples, nested splittings (1→3→1 inside a pair), and in general a graph of geon worldlines, which looks like a Feynman diagram. Each one shadows to one path in "all possible paths".
- **Where the phase would come from:** a twist or holonomy carried around the 4D structure. The step from a whole-number winding to an angle must be fixed by the geometry, not chosen by us.
- **Your clock-hand refinement (1:54 AM):** one small correction. +π and −π are the same clock position (both −1), so on their own they add up rather than cancel. With closure, the two halves wind φ and −φ, and they cancel when φ = π/2, because the hands then point opposite ways. So the real question is your own: what geometry sets how far each half winds? Spin-½ (MTW's 4π trick) is a follow-up, because it changes which windings count as closed.
- **What "annihilate" would mean:** the handles must be capped off by a compact 4D cobordism (A2). Trivial holonomy alone (A1) does not remove a handle, and "180° out of phase" (A3) is a picture of A1, not the condition. In our G1 toy the ℤ₂ is a reflection, not a 180° rotation.
- **CTCs and the lab:** the CTCs sit inside the closure, so a closure must be micro or lie outside the lab region; one that fits in a single ℓ-ball shadows to a point, so a loop or path shadow needs the members to separate macroscopically. CTCs are now adopted, per your 2:27 AM words (relayed via Physics Lead; hold level unspecified). The 1:56 AM "allowed tentatively" ruling is kept as history, superseded.
- **Negative interference without negative numbers:** a closure that annihilates never reaches the detector, so the geometry excludes those worlds. No weight is negative. This is the "exclusion" exit from Lemma A.
- **The goal is Feynman's path integral.** We must *derive* the rule that adds phases. The known rule (Schulman 1968; Laidlaw–DeWitt 1971) is quantum, so importing it is not allowed.

Nothing is computed, and the dark-fringe test is set aside for now, as you asked.

**Creative Lead note** (on the refinement):
- e^{iπ} = e^{−iπ} = −1, so phases +π and −π are the *same* clock-hand position. Added as arrows, they reinforce (−1 + −1 = −2) rather than cancel.
- Under closure the two halves wind φ and −φ (together 0, or a multiple of 2π once the loop is closed). Their arrows e^{iφ} and e^{−iφ} differ by 2φ and sum to 2cos φ, so they cancel exactly when φ = π/2 (relative angle π), not when φ = π.
- The open question is therefore: **what geometry sets each half's winding φ?** This is David's "geometric source for the thing that's spinning", and §5 (holonomy along the structure, a ℤ→U(1) map fixed by geometry) is where it must come from. How arrows add is still the open step (§8), and it is not imported.

Here a 2π turn gives −1 and only 4π returns to the start. That changes which windings count as "closed", so it changes the cancellation condition above, and it connects to bosons versus fermions (and to Friedman–Sorkin spin ½ from gravity).

## 3. Bird's-eye reading (Creative Lead proposal)

- In one world there is one fixed 4-manifold, so nothing "changes" (David 1:36 AM). Topology change is a statement about spatial slices: the topology of Σ_t differs between slices.
- A "virtual pair" is a 4D handle structure. Between two slices, the spatial topology gains a handle and then loses it. The region between them is cobordism-like.
- **Shadow:** the image under the LEA chart f of the pair's world-tubes (each geon micro, mouths in a common ℓ-ball), determined to within εR + ℓ. It is a closed loop (vacuum-like closure) or, when tied to the source and detector, a path from source to detector. A closure contained in one ℓ-ball shadows to a point, so a loop or path shadow needs the members to separate macroscopically. (C3) is one-way and says nothing about CTCs inside a closure.
- **Costs** (CTCs adopted, David 2:27 AM, hold level unspecified): Geroch 1967 (compact interpolating region) says Lorentzian topology change on a compact region needs CTCs or loses time-orientability. The alternative is degenerate metrics (Horowitz 1991) or Morse-type points (Dowker et al.). CTCs adopted (David, 2:27 AM, hold level unspecified), so the Geroch route can be examined: a time-oriented compact Lorentzian cobordism between non-diffeomorphic slices contains CTCs, and this is consistent with the charter addendum. With the field equations and an energy condition, compact topology change also forces singularities (Tipler, Ann. Phys. 108, 1 (1977)); Borde (gr-qc/9406053, unchecked) relaxes compactness; non-compact interpolations escape Geroch. The CTCs lie inside the cobordism, so if it is macroscopic and lies in U it triggers #410's ill-posedness clause (macroscopic chronology violation in U). Closures must therefore be micro or lie outside U. The analytic lock's status for such manifolds is not checked.

## 4. The family of closures (Creative Lead proposal)

- **Pair:** two geons appear and later recombine. The shadow is one loop, or two arcs.
- **n-tuple:** n geons appear and annihilate all together.
- **Nested:** a pair appears, one member splits 1→3→1, and then the pair recombines.
- **General:** a finite graph of geon worldlines in object spacetime. Vertices are creation, splitting, joining or annihilation events, and the edges are geon worldlines. This is Feynman-diagram-like 4D topology. Each closure shadows to one path (or path set) in the sum over paths.
- **Bookkeeping:** a closure is labelled by its 4D topology plus its gluing data (Γ and the extension), so it is world data (#399 §3). Different closures are different worlds, or different structures within one world. Which one holds is open (see §9).

## 5. The phase (Creative Lead proposal)

- **Geometric source:** the twist or holonomy carried along the 4D structure. That is the linear part of the deck element along each geon worldline, together with the enclosed curvature (#418 B1–B3: fixed by the germ, Γ and the route, and loop-dependent).
- **From integer winding to phase:** David's "integer multiple of 2π" needs a map ℤ → U(1), n ↦ e^{inα}. For T2 cleanliness, α must be fixed by the geometry, for example the holonomy angle of a one-parameter subgroup selected by the structure's own symmetry. It must not be chosen by hand. A natural candidate is the rotation angle θ of an orientable (mirror) gluing, which is circle-valued. A non-orientable G1 piece has reflection holonomy (det −1), which gives ℤ₂ = ±1 only through the chosen character det ↦ ±1. It is not rotation by 180° (det +1), which is a different gluing, the corrected point inversion of #408. Under the lock a flat 2D cover has no rotating deck element (#413 R2), so a circle-valued θ is a seam-toy property or needs curvature or 3D.
- **T1:** circle values are necessary, not sufficient (#418). They label routes. They do not add.
- **T2:** clean only if α comes from Γ and the germ.

## 6. The annihilation condition (Creative Lead proposal)

Candidate topological statements:
- **(A1) Charge triviality:** the product of the geons' holonomies, or their topological charges, around the closure is the identity in the relevant group (for instance, the mapping-class or deck charges cancel).
- **(A2) Bounding:** the closed 4D handle structure bounds, i.e. it caps off in object spacetime, so no handle survives to the detector slice.
- **(A3) Phase matching:** relative holonomy π between the two members (David's "180° out of phase" for opposite orientations).

Reading: (A3) is probably a *picture* of (A1) for a pair. Reversing orientation inverts the holonomy, so "coincide" for opposite directions means the product is trivial. A relative angle of π is one way to describe that for a ℤ₂ piece. Whether (A1) and (A2) are equivalent for our gluings is not checked. (A1) alone does not remove a handle: trivial holonomy does not mean the handle is absent (a flat wrap has trivial holonomy and non-trivial π₁). Only (A2), a compact cobordism capping the handles off, is topological removal.

## 8. Path-integral goal (Creative Lead proposal)

What would need to be derived, not imported, to reach Feynman's rule:
1. The set of closures that shadow to source→detector paths: the "all possible paths" of David's 1:36 AM message.
2. A geometry-fixed phase per closure (§5).
3. **The phase-addition rule:** why contributions combine as Σ e^{iφ} and not some other way. The known rule (Schulman 1968; Laidlaw–DeWitt 1971: weight each homotopy class by a unitary character of π₁ and sum) is the quantum path integral. Importing it imports QM, so it must come out of §6–§7.
4. Only after that, the step to frequencies. The Born rule itself is out of scope, and so are μ and |a|².

## 9. Ill-posed questions to avoid

- "When does the geon pop into existence?" in one world without a foliation. Topology change is slice-relative; the 4-manifold itself does not change.
- "The phase of one geon": only closure (loop) holonomies are gauge-invariant.
- "Do the two geons annihilate?" before (A1)/(A2) is fixed.
- "Which closure happened?" when nothing records it (#401 R2).
- "The amplitude of a closure": we have phases as labels, not amplitudes.
- "The probability of d_i": μ stays open.
