## Plain summary (for David)

In #431 the detector's loop could carry only a plus-or-minus sign, which is too little to be a record. Your idea: the loop may carry more geometric detail, such as handles inside the cube, knotted or not, and that detail could be what makes a fermion an electron rather than some other fermion. If so, your proposal is that exact closure and consistency would force the detector's loop to carry the same detail, and the particle's presence on the second arc would itself be the record we have been looking for; whether anything forces the match, and whether it lasts, are the open tests (T-a, T-b). We have written this up as your proposal and listed four questions for Geometry: what forces the match, whether the detail lasts, whether it follows the route the particle took, and whether it fits with spin-½. Nothing here is a result yet. **Update (§3a, Geometry 12:13 PM, reviewer reading):** exact closure does not force the match, and a species-conservation rule would; a record that lasts needs the particle's topology to leave along a new edge, and then the detector's loop is no longer a virtual pair, so its −1 is open again; literal handles cannot make a particle spinorial.

## 2. Creative Lead framing (CL proposal; paraphrase of David's idea)

- Particle-specific internal topology (handles, knots inside the cube) would give the loop richer data to match.
- Exact closure plus consistency would then force the detector loop to carry the same topology.
- The particle's presence on arc b then becomes the record.

## 3a. Geometry's reading (12:13 PM, reviewer reading, not a ruling)

- **(T-a) What forces the match.** Not exact closure: closure is a holonomy condition on cycles and carries no topological label. Pair creation and annihilation of a prime summand P with its orientation reverse P̄ is natural (P#P̄ bounds the punctured P times an interval), but it is not forced. Every closed oriented 3-manifold bounds, with or without spin structure (Ω₃^SO = Ω₃^Spin = 0), so with topology change (CTCs adopted; Geroch) a single P can be created or destroyed alone, subject to Lorentzian conditions (Gibbons–Hawking, CMP 148, 345 (1992): wormholes are created in pairs under a spin-Lorentz structure; the parity condition for a general P is unchecked). [Claude 026: the abstract gives the invariant as the mod 2 Kervaire semi-characteristic, u(Σ) = dim H₀(Σ; ℤ₂) + dim H₁(Σ; ℤ₂) mod 2, with Σ the region's whole spacelike boundary. Adding one prime summand P changes u by dim H₁(P; ℤ₂), so in a compact region with a Lorentzian metric and a spinor structure (needed for the adopted spin-½) a single P can be created or destroyed only if dim H₁(P; ℤ₂) is even. A P with odd dim H₁(P; ℤ₂), such as a handle (its own reverse, so its chain is ℤ₂-valued), is then conserved mod 2 by the geometry, with no postulate; a P with even dim H₁(P; ℤ₂), such as the Poincaré homology sphere, is not; and no P gets a ℤ-valued law that tells P from P̄. The ℤ₂ coefficients are my reading: RP³ bounds the spin disk bundle of Euler number 2 over S², so after a connected sum with S¹×S³ it is the spacelike boundary of a compact spin-Lorentz region, which fits u(RP³) = 0 with ℤ₂ coefficients and not with real ones. The paper itself is not read.] See #434 (notes/creative/spinorial-parity.md) for Geometry's follow-up on mod-2 conservation; Claude's #432 S1 bears on it and is under check. So the match needs a species-conservation rule, which is a postulate (T2 at risk) unless the geometry supplies it.
- **(T-b) Persistence.** Under that rule the species is a 1-chain on edges, conserved at vertices; it is not a cycle datum. On the pure theta graph every conserved chain is a cycle, so the chain itself does not persist, and the particle on arc A2 has both ends at the junctions, so it is not a lasting record (#428 X1). A record carried by the chain itself needs a fourth, outgoing edge carrying P into the future. That edge lies on no cycle, so exactness is untouched, and it persists because P cannot vanish without P̄: a relocation, not net topology change. (This corrects Geometry's earlier "a record needs an uncapped edge, i.e. net topology change", which assumed topologically trivial edges, #428 §6a(c)3.) A closed chain can still leave a lasting field: if P carries charge and its cycle winds through #431's handle, the flux through the throat changes by the charge times the winding, which is #388's own write (Claude 026 on #431 §3(iv)); that records the winding, not the species. The −1 is a cycle datum and the record is a chain datum, so #428 R4's conflict is separated in kind, but not removed: it moves onto L's virtual status. L's P̄ member is annihilated at the attachment, so L is no longer a virtual pair under the 5:14 PM definition. The 10:48 AM ruling still requires L to be exact, but L's expected value of −1 rested on L being a virtual pair (#431 §3b), so it is open again. With at most one particle per edge this cost does not need the outgoing edge: on #431's closed graph, conservation at the degree-3 vertices already puts the routes' line on one arc of L and nothing on the other (Claude 026 on #431). Open: what L adds beyond P simply arriving along a route and staying.
- **(T-c) Which arc.** With one outgoing edge, conservation gives f₁ + f₂ = 1 on the two routes, so with at most one particle per route one route carries P and the other carries nothing. A P̄ on one route (f = −1) would need two units on the other, so the Feynman–Stueckelberg reading (#428 §4 item 4) belongs to the closed graph, where f₁ + f₂ = 0, not to the graph with an outgoing edge (Claude 026). The empty route then enters C1 and C2 as a path with no carrier; whether it still counts as a member of the detection pair (#428 §2), and what frame is carried along it, should be said. The split over A1, A2 (f_A1 + f_A2 = 1) is free up to L's circulation, so the arc label does not by itself track the route. The chain is discrete world data, so it escapes the analytic lock, and it is independent of the spin-structure sign. Caveat: a nontrivial summand changes H₁(M), and with it the spin-structure classes of nearby loops. The outgoing edge names the detector, not the route, which is consistent with no which-way (#428 §2 item 4).
- **(T-d) Spin.** Whether a prime is spinorial (a 2π rotation not isotopic to the identity) is classical topology. Friedman–Sorkin's half-integral angular momentum is a statement about quantum states (PRL 44, 1100 (1980); erratum PRL 45, 148). The only non-spinorial primes are the lens spaces and S¹×S² (Giulini, arXiv gr-qc/9301020, Theorem 2, after Hendriks 1977), so literal handles give tensorial (non-spinorial) species; a species whose topology alone is to make it spinorial needs another prime. [Claude 026: "boson-like" and "fermion-like" are quantization labels (Friedman–Sorkin; Finkelstein–Rubinstein for kinks, J. Math. Phys. 9, 1762 (1968), doi:10.1063/1.1664510, text not read), which this classical model does not supply. The spin-½ adopted in #431 §3c is the spin lift of the carried frame, which exists whatever the species' topology: an orientable 3-manifold is parallelizable, so ℝ × Σ has a spin structure (Geroch, J. Math. Phys. 9, 1739 (1968), doi:10.1063/1.1664507, text not read). So a handle species does not conflict with spin-½; a handle just cannot be the topological source of it. Knotted handles (12:02 PM) need a definition: a handle summand has no knot type, while surgery on a knot gives other primes, some spinorial, such as the Poincaré homology sphere from surgery on a trefoil.] Spin-statistics for geons is not automatic: Dowker and Sorkin show it for certain geons in a sum-over-histories setting with topology change (CQG 15, 1153–1167 (1998), doi:10.1088/0264-9381/15/5/007). The geon mechanism differs from the spin-structure lift adopted in #431 §3c; whether the two agree is unchecked. Hint for arbitrary spin: spinorial or not is a ℤ₂, so topology suggests the statistics class, not the spin magnitude. More would need, for example, how the mapping class group acts (a hope, not a result).
- **Well-posedness.** With CTCs there are no global slices (#426 P2), so a tube's species must be defined by a local cross-section. If P ≅ P̄ (P amphichiral), the chain is ℤ₂-valued and P is its own antiparticle. Where the outgoing edge leaves the graph (at a, at b, or elsewhere on L) is also to be said: if the record's place depends on it, the record does not forget the attachment (#428 S2) (Claude 026).
