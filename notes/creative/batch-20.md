## For David (plain summary)

- **The toy.** A small classical particle goes from a source to a screen in a world that wraps around once, like a cylinder. It can reach a screen point "directly" or "by going round" the wrap one or more times. Those are the two (or more) routes. In one world with one launch only one of them is taken; reaching the same screen point by a different route needs a different launch angle. Nothing records which route it took: the observer keeps only where it hit (and, in our example, the launch angle), not the route, its direction of arrival or its flight time.
- **"Which route?" without a record.** In any one world, the route is fixed: the launch and the wrap size settle it. So it isn't a fork, and it isn't undefined; it is a fact about the world. But a route number only means something relative to a declared reference route. Asked of the observer's records alone, "which route" is usually not a well-posed question (your well-posedness principle, about 10:33 AM ET 2026-10-11, recorded in born-litmus-test.md only as Physics Lead's paraphrase; relayed via Physics Lead, not signed): the hit point usually fits worlds with different routes. There are two exceptions. A hit at the straight-through point fits only worlds where the particle came the direct way. A hit between a third and a half of the way from the foot point to the straight-through point fits only one wrap.
- **What differs at the screen.** Across compatible worlds, which differ in how wide the wrap is, the same launch lands at different screen points. Conversely, one observed hit fits a short **list** of wrap sizes (finite, except at the straight-through point), one per route: the wrap could be 5.0 (one wind) or 2.5 (two winds) in our example. That list is the hit's record class. It is finite for a hit off the straight-through point; a hit exactly at the straight-through point fits every wide-enough wrap (a continuum). So a hit narrows the worlds to one record class, which is one branch. Inside it the worlds are separate, and they are labelled by how many times the particle went round, counted from the declared reference. Because nothing records the route, those labels are not branches; a which-route record (step 2) would make them branches.
- **A pattern?** Yes, a structural one. Hit position against wrap size is piecewise linear with jumps (teeth crowding toward small wraps), and the compatible wrap sizes for a hit form a harmonic list (Δ, Δ/2, Δ/3, …), stopping before the wrap gets smaller than the hit's distance from the foot point, so it is finite (Δ is the hit's distance from the straight-through point). The exception is a hit at the straight-through point, which fits every wide-enough wrap. But **no fringes**: in each world the particle takes one route, and the hits from different routes simply add up (see the obstacle). Turning "many worlds per hit" into brighter and darker bands would need a way to weight worlds, which we have deliberately not chosen.
- **The two-slit case (fixed wrap).** With the wrap known and the launch angle not recorded, a hit fits exactly two launches, one going each way round (11.32° and −2.85° in our example). That is the honest two-slit configuration: same geometry, two routes, no record of which.
- **Step 2's baseline is fixed now (Lead ruling).** Adding a passive which-route counter leaves every hit where it was; only the grouping of worlds splits by route. So any change in the pattern would have to come from a weighting of worlds chosen in advance.
- **The obstacle.** Each particle takes one route. Point hits are not the problem, since in real single-particle experiments the bands also build up from point hits. The problem is that, with any weighting of worlds that ignores the routes not taken, the hits from the different routes simply add up, so opening a second route can never make any part of the screen darker. Dark bands need waves added together (excluded), a weighting that depends on the whole set of routes and is fixed in advance (parked), or a particle that feels its own field coming round the other way (not in this toy). What we do get is the discrete skeleton a pattern would sit on.

## Set 1: is "which route" well-posed without a record?

**Plain summary.** The route is fixed in each world, but it is a world fact, not a record fact. It needs a declared reference to be a number at all.

- **Setting.** Cover: the flat plane $(x,y)$, with time suppressed (static). World $W_L$: $x\sim x+L$ (one handle direction, $\Gamma=L\mathbb Z$). Source at $(0,0)$, screen line $y=D$. A test particle is launched with speed $v$ at angle θ from the screen normal (the $y$ axis; θ>0 towards $+x$), which is test matter in the world (#399 R2). There is no diary on any route. **Record.** For each run, the observer's entry is the hit's screen coordinate $x$ and, in Set 2, the launch θ, logged by the source. This is coarser than #372's reply entry (#399 §5 (ii)). It holds no deck element, so the route is not recorded by definition. It also holds no arrival direction or flight time, either of which would tell apart launches that reach one screen point by different routes. The record classes below also assume that only $x$ reaches the observer: light from the hit comes round several routes as several images, whose delays fix $L$ (the ghost images of #399 §3).
- **Route = deck class rel reference.** A path from the source to a screen point has no absolute winding: an open segment is cut-dependent (#381, #387). The route number $n$ is defined only relative to a declared reference path, here "the straight segment inside the fundamental strip $[0,L)$". With that declared, the cover lift of the hit is $X=x+nL$ with $x\in[0,L)$ the arc length along the oriented screen from the foot point $(0,D)$, so $n=\lfloor D\tan\theta/L\rfloor\in\mathbb Z$ (negative for $\theta<0$) [A, N]. The cut sits at the source, so absolute $n$ shifts with the cut, and the reference is a rule-defined, different segment in each $W_L$. Only differences $n-n'$ between routes to the same point in one world are cut- and basepoint-free (#381, #387); across worlds, see below.
- **Is it fixed by the world?** Yes, because the launch is part of the world (#399 R2). Given $(L,\theta)$, the geodesic is unique, so $n$ is unique: no fork within a world (#399 §3). For a fixed screen point instead, each $n$ has its own geodesic, launched at $\theta_n=\arctan((x+nL)/D)$, so $n$ labels a route; two-slit-style "two routes to one point" means two launches in one world, not one launch across worlds.
- **Is it the same as which world?** Not quite. For a fixed launch, $n$ is a function of the world $L$. On a hit's record class with $x\neq X$ it is one-to-one, so there "which route" and "which wrap" are the same question. On the straight-through class ($x=X$), $n=0$ for every fitting $L$, so the record fixes the route but not the world. For an observed hit $x$ without the launch recorded, many $(L,\theta,n)$ fit. "Which route" is a **coarse function of the world**, not a datum of the reception history.
- **Well-posedness principle (Lead reading).** Asked of the observer's record alone, "which route" is ill-posed unless $n$ is constant on the record class. In the example below it is not: one hit is compatible with $n=1$ and with $n=2$. It is constant on the straight-through class ($n=0$) and for $X/3\le x<X/2$ (one world, $n=1$). Because $n$ is compared across worlds, each with its own reference, the verdict is cut-free only for cuts whose ends stay within the class's smallest wrap of the source and of the hit. Those cuts shift $n$ by the same integer in every world of the class, so in the example none of them makes $n$ constant. Farther cuts can change the verdict: some make $n$ constant on the example's class, and on the straight-through class a vertical cut at $c<0$ gives $n=1$ for $X<L\le X-c$ and $n=0$ beyond.
- **Status: live** (L75).

## Set 2: what differs at the screen; record classes of hits

**Plain summary.** One hit point is consistent with a discrete set of wrap sizes, one for each route.

- **Forward [N]** (θ = 35°, D = 10, so the cover hit is $X=7.0021$): screen point $x(L)=X \bmod L$, class $n(L)$. For example:
  - L = 2.5 gives x = 2.0021, n = 2;
  - L = 4 gives x = 3.0021, n = 1;
  - L = 7.5 gives x = 7.0021, n = 0.

  As a function of L this is piecewise linear, $x=X-nL$ with slope $-n$ on $(X/(n+1),X/n]$, jumping at $L=X/n$, with teeth crowding toward $L\to0$. It is a periodic sawtooth only in $X$ at fixed $L$.
- **Inverse: the record class of a hit [A, N].** With the launch known (so the cover hit $X=D\tan\theta$ is known), a hit at $x$ is compatible with $X-x=nL$. If $x\neq X$ (and $X>x$, the θ>0 convention): $L_n=(X-x)/n$, $n=1,2,\dots$, subject to $L_n>x$ (and $L_n>2\rho$ for the tube, #399), so $n<(X-x)/x$: a **finite** list for $x>0$, empty for $X/2\le x<X$ (no world puts that launch there), a single world ($n=1$) for $X/3\le x<X/2$, infinite (accumulating at 0, cut by the tube) only for $x=0$. If $x=X$: $n=0$ and every $L>\max(X,2\rho)$ fits, a **continuum**, so the record class is not always discrete. For the hit $x=2.0021$: $L\in\{5.0\ (n=1),\ 2.5\ (n=2)\}$. **A discrete record class, indexed by route.** At a finite resolution δ in $x$, each $L_n$ widens to an interval of width $2\delta/n$ (for δ = 0.01, [4.99, 5.01] and [2.495, 2.505]), while the straight-through class is unchanged. So "discrete" means separated intervals, one per route, as long as $(2n+1)\delta<X-x$.
- **Timing carries no information.** The arrival time $D/(v\cos\theta)$ is the same in every $W_L$, so only position separates the worlds.
- **Fixed wrap, launch not recorded [A, N].** This is Set 1's two-routes-to-one-point case. With $L$ known and θ not recorded, a hit at $x$ fits one launch per route, $\theta_n=\arctan((x+nL)/D)$ for $n\in\mathbb Z$ (finitely many for a bounded aperture). If the source's aperture is $|D\tan\theta|<L$, a hit off the foot point fits exactly two: $n=0$ and $n=-1$, one each way round, whose difference, 1, is cut-free. For $L=2.5$ and $x=2.0021$ these are θ = 11.32° and −2.85°. Their arrival directions and flight times differ, so the record must hold neither (Setting). Here the record class is indexed by route while the geometry is fixed, which is the two-slit question.
- **Branching.** The fork at the hit is over the hit value $x$, with one arm per possible $x$; once the tube is imposed, each arm holds finitely many worlds, except the continuum at $x=X$. Routes label worlds inside an arm, not arms, because no route is recorded. So a hit narrows the prior class (all L) to one arm, its record class $\{L_n\}$. Further hits (other launches) refine it further, nested (#372 §2.6). Fork = discovery of Γ.
- **Status: live** (L76).

## Set 3: does anything pattern-like arise from grouping worlds?

**Plain summary.** There is a definite skeleton: hit position against wrap size is piecewise linear with jumps, and a finite harmonic list of compatible wrap sizes per hit. But there is no intensity.

- **Structure that exists without a measure:**
  - (i) the piecewise-linear, jumping $x(L)$ (a periodic sawtooth only in $X$ at fixed $L$). Each jump, from $x=0$ to $x\to L$ at $L=X/n$, is where the hit passes the foot point and the coordinate wraps. On the screen circle the hit moves continuously, once round per tooth. So the jumps, like absolute $n$, belong to the coordinate, which starts at the foot point; the wraps where they sit, $L=X/n$, are those that put the hit on the foot point;
  - (ii) the harmonic family $L_n=\Delta/n$ for a hit at offset $\Delta=X-x$ from the straight-through point;
  - (iii) periodicity of hits in $x$ with period $L$ inside one world, across launches;
  - (iv) for a discrete Γ-family (Γ ⊂ a fixed lattice, #359), a finite set of hit points.
- **What does not exist:** a density of hits, fringes, or brighter and darker bands. Any of these needs counting or weighting worlds or launches, and that is parked: (M1) is untouched and there is no measure (#383 guardrails, #399).
- **Caustics.** A flat cover has no focusing, so none. A curved handle (batch 15/16) would give caustics, but those are geometric-optics features of a single world, not of grouping.
- **Status: live (skeleton only)** (L77).

## Set 4: the strongest obstacle

- **One route per hit, no fringes (strongest).** A classical test particle makes one hit per world, by one route. Point hits as such are not the obstacle: in single-electron interference experiments each electron also lands at one point, and the bands build up over many (Tonomura et al. 1989). Interference-like bands require waves added by hand (excluded), a weighting over worlds (parked; (M1) untouched), or a particle that feels its own field coming round the other routes (not in this toy; see below). The model supplies the **discrete index set** (routes = deck classes) on which such a weighting would act, and nothing more. Counting worlds by route would be the labelled counting conjecture (#399), separate from (M1), which stays unchanged. A weighting chosen because it yields fringes is an LT2 risk (David's T2, written LT2 in born-litmus-test.md) and must be fixed in advance. A weighting of one hit's record class weights finitely many worlds, but bands belong to many hits, and any weighting of launches or wraps spreads hits over the screen. The obstruction is additivity. Each hit takes one route, so under any non-negative weighting that does not depend on which other routes exist, the pattern is the sum of each route's own pattern, and no part of the screen gets fewer hits than one route alone gives it. A weighting can make dark bands only if it depends on the whole set of routes. A self-field escapes differently: the particle still takes one route, but where it lands depends on the other routes, as with the droplets' guiding wave. Under the lock that field must be a test field, such as charge, not the particle's gravity (#399 §4 item 2).
- **Status: open** (L78). **Toward step 2 (framed only).** Use the fixed-wrap case (Set 2), and add a passive which-route record, e.g. a test-body counter of signed crossings of a line from source to screen. That makes the declared reference physical (#381); #390's flux slot is the handle analogue. In every world the hit is then unchanged, and only the record class splits by route. So any change in the hit pattern when the record is added can come only from a weighting of worlds, and that weighting must be fixed before step 2 (LT2).
