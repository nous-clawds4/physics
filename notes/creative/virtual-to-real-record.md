## Plain summary (for David)

Claude's review of the smallest detector (#431, R2) found a cost: once the incoming routes wire into the detector's virtual loop L and they carry a conserved charge, L no longer appears and disappears as a pair, so it stops being a virtual pair, and the −1 we expected it to pick up is open again. You asked whether that "cost" is actually what we want: if the interaction turns the detector's virtual loop into a real particle, couldn't that real particle be the record we have been looking for? This note writes the idea down and links it to two things already on the table: Geometry's point (#432) that a lasting record carried by the particle's chain itself needs an outgoing edge carrying the particle into the future, and Claude's point (#431 R3, accepted by Geometry in its 3:33 PM ET 2026-10-15 pass, R3 only) that a charged loop through the detector's handle writes a flux record by Gauss's law. It asks Geometry four questions (V-a to V-d): what L's loop is expected to pick up once it is no longer virtual; where the real particle comes from and whether the charge books balance; whether it persists and is fixed by the routes without revealing which way; and whether "virtual becomes real" is even defined in our terms. Nothing here is a ruling, and nothing about probabilities is claimed. **Update (§4a, Geometry 3:51 PM, reviewer reading):** "becomes real" is best read as a pair created at a point c on the detector loop whose antiparticle member annihilates the incoming particle while the other member carries on. That is one zigzag path of the same particle, and the charge books balance. The record is the particle itself, moved onto an outgoing edge (a record carried by the particle's chain, as in #432 §3a (T-b)). It lasts only if species are conserved (a postulate), and it avoids recording which way the particle went only if it leaves from the same detector-fixed point whichever route the particle took. Once the loop is not a virtual pair, its −1 is no longer expected, and if only loops a particle actually goes round count, no detector loop is left at all. So the record is gained by giving up the −1, not by getting both (Claude 028). (Claude 028:) If every loop counts, the loops through the detector must still close exactly, and nothing now supplies the extra turn your 11:48 PM ET 2026-10-14 statement (a problem statement and question, not a ruling; #428 §6(a)) asked of the detector. If only carried loops count, the detector asks nothing of the routes, and a detection has one carried route rather than a pair.
