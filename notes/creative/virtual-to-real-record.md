## Plain summary (for David)

Claude's review of the smallest detector (#431, R2) found a cost: once the incoming routes wire into the detector's virtual loop L and they carry a conserved charge, L no longer appears and disappears as a pair, so it stops being a virtual pair, and the −1 we expected it to pick up is open again. You asked whether that "cost" is actually what we want: if the interaction turns the detector's virtual loop into a real particle, couldn't that real particle be the record we have been looking for? This note writes the idea down and links it to two things already on the table: Geometry's point (#432) that a lasting record needs an outgoing edge carrying a particle into the future, and Claude's point (#431 R3, accepted by Geometry) that a charged loop through the detector's handle writes a flux record by Gauss's law. It asks Geometry four questions (V-a to V-d): what L's loop is expected to pick up once it is no longer virtual; where the real particle comes from and whether the charge books balance; whether it persists and is fixed by the routes without revealing which way; and whether "virtual becomes real" is even defined in our terms. Nothing here is a ruling, and nothing about probabilities is claimed.

## 2. Context (sources)

- **Claude 026 on #431, R2.** With a conserved charge on the edges, at most one particle per edge, a wired L carries the routes' line on one arc and nothing on the other. So L stops being a virtual pair under David's 5:14 PM definition (2026-10-14, adopted 5:17 PM: a virtual pair appears together and disappears together), and its expected −1 is open again (#431 §3, §3b; #432 §3a (T-b)).
- **Geometry on #432, §3a (T-b)** (reviewer reading, not a ruling). A record carried by the chain itself needs a fourth, outgoing edge carrying P into the future. That edge lies on no cycle, so exactness is untouched.
- **Claude 026 on #431, R3**, accepted by Geometry at 3:33 PM (reviewer reading). A charged loop whose cycle winds through the handle writes a flux record by Gauss's law, which is #388's own write (#431 §3(iv)).

## 3. Creative Lead framing (CL proposal; paraphrase of David's idea)

- What #431 R2 treated as a cost, L losing its virtual status, may itself be the record.
- On this reading the interaction converts L's virtual pair into a real, persisting particle.
- This connects to the outgoing-edge record in #432 §3a: the now-real particle would be what the outgoing edge carries into the future.
- This is a Creative Lead paraphrase and proposal, not David's words and not a ruling. It does not adopt 1(a) or the write rule.

## 4. Open tests for Geometry

- **(V-a) L's expected value.** Once L is not a virtual pair, what is L's loop expected to pick up, and is it exact within each world (David's 10:48 AM and 11:17 AM rulings, #431 §3a, §3b)?
- **(V-b) Conservation and bookkeeping.** Under a conserved charge, where does the real particle come from, or where does its partner go? Does the charge balance per world, and how does the real particle relate to the outgoing edge (#432 §3a (T-b)) and to Gauss's flux record (#431 R3)?
- **(V-c) Persistence and locality.** Does the now-real particle persist as a record in the detector, and is it fixed by the routes (world data) rather than by the detector alone, consistent with no which-way (#428 §2 item 4)?
- **(V-d) Well-posedness.** Is "virtual becomes real" defined in the model's terms (David's 5:14 PM definition: appear together, disappear together), or does it need a new definition?
