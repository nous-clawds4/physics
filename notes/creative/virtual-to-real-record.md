## Plain summary (for David)

Claude's review of the smallest detector (#431, R2) found a cost: once the incoming routes wire into the detector's virtual loop L and they carry a conserved charge, L no longer appears and disappears as a pair, so it stops being a virtual pair, and the −1 we expected it to pick up is open again. You asked whether that "cost" is actually what we want: if the interaction turns the detector's virtual loop into a real particle, couldn't that real particle be the record we have been looking for? This note writes the idea down and links it to two things already on the table: Geometry's point (#432) that a lasting record carried by the particle's chain itself needs an outgoing edge carrying the particle into the future, and Claude's point (#431 R3, accepted by Geometry in its 3:33 PM ET 2026-10-15 pass, R3 only) that a charged loop through the detector's handle writes a flux record by Gauss's law. It asks Geometry four questions (V-a to V-d): what L's loop is expected to pick up once it is no longer virtual; where the real particle comes from and whether the charge books balance; whether it persists and is fixed by the routes without revealing which way; and whether "virtual becomes real" is even defined in our terms. Nothing here is a ruling, and nothing about probabilities is claimed. **Update (§4a, Geometry 3:51 PM, reviewer reading):** "becomes real" is best read as a pair created at a point c on the detector loop whose antiparticle member annihilates the incoming particle while the other member carries on. That is one zigzag path of the same particle, and the books balance. The record is the particle itself, moved onto an outgoing edge (a record carried by the particle's chain, as in #432 §3a (T-b)). It lasts only if species are conserved (a postulate), and it avoids recording which way the particle went only if it leaves from the same detector-fixed point whichever route the particle took. Once the loop is not a virtual pair, its −1 is no longer expected, and if only loops a particle actually goes round count, no detector loop is left at all.

## 4a. Geometry's reading (3:51 PM, reviewer reading, not a ruling)

**Graph.** This is #431's theta graph. E0 (route 1 reversed, then route 2) runs from a to b, so route 1 arrives at a and route 2 arrives at b; L = A1·A2⁻¹. To state the proposal, add a vertex c on L, where L's pair is created, and an outgoing edge X at c. Say c lies on A2, splitting it into A2′ (a to c) and A2″ (c to b); if c lies on A1, swap the labels. The cycle rank is unchanged (5 edges and 4 vertices, counting X's far end: 5 − 4 + 1 = 2). So the cycles are still C1, C2 and L, and X lies on none of them.

- **(V-d) Well-posedness.** Under the 5:14 PM definition (virtual particles "appear together and then disappear together", #427 §3a), being a virtual pair is a property of a whole history within one world, not a state that changes in time. So "L becomes real" is ill-posed as a change within a world: in a given world, L's members either appear and disappear together or they do not. It is well-posed as a comparison between worlds (the unwired world, where L is a virtual pair, and the wired world, where it is not), but that is not a process. Well-posed restatement: L's two members are created together at c but do not annihilate each other. P̄ annihilates the incoming P at its arrival point, and P persists along X. That is ordinary pair creation plus annihilation, and it needs no definition beyond "pair-created, not mutually annihilated". This is proposed wording for David to confirm, not adopted.
- **(V-b) Bookkeeping.** Take the world where P arrives on route 1 (f₁ = 1, f₂ = 0). The unit flow runs: route 1 into a; then a to c along L, which is the P̄ running from c to a forward in time; then c out along X. Every other edge carries 0. The flow balances at a and at c and gives f₁ + f₂ = 1. Read as a chain, route 1 → a → c → X is one worldline of P that turns between future and past at a and again at c: a Feynman–Stueckelberg zigzag. The surviving member is the outgoing edge of #432 §3a (T-b), now with a physical identity, and the record is the incoming particle itself, displaced onto X. This needs a time-reversed segment, not CTCs. The segment's end c lies earlier than a, so the creation precedes the arrival: the world is fixed as a whole (per-world consistency), not by a cause running forward in time. The flow's path from the arrival point to c (directly along A2′, or round through A1, b and A2″) is world data, free as in #432 §3a (T-c). The zigzag runs on L's arcs, not on the routes, so it is consistent with Claude 026's #432 R1. If P is charged, #388's flux record (Claude 026 #431 R3; Geometry's 3:33 PM ET 2026-10-15 acceptance covers R3 only) counts the signed passages of this carried chain through the throat. The carried chain is open here, so the record counts the path's own signed crossings, not a cycle's winding class: in this world no carried part is closed. The particle record is a chain datum and the flux record is a field datum, and the two can coexist.
- **(V-a) Which cycles remain.** The outgoing edge X removes no cycle. Which loops count is a reading the note should state:
  - **(F) Frame graph.** Every edge is a transport path, whether or not it carries a particle. Then C1, C2 and L all remain, and the 10:48 AM ruling requires each to be exact per world. L's expected −1 rested on L being a virtual pair (#431 §3b), so it is gone. By 11:17 AM, exact means equal to the expected value, so L needs an expected value from somewhere else, and nothing supplies one yet. Under 1(a) (not adopted) with spin-½, the only constraint is sign(C1)·sign(C2)·sign(L) = +1, and the values are world data, not detector data.
  - **(W) Carried loops only.** A loop exists in a world only if a particle runs round it. With at most one particle per edge (a modelling assumption, Geometry N1 on #431, not a finding of Claude 026 R2), the carried subgraph is the path route → arrival point → c → X. Adding a circulation round L would put 0 or 2 on the path's arcs, so the carried subgraph has no cycle. Then C1 and C2 lose their carrier along with L (one route is empty, #432 §3a (T-c)), the 10:48 AM ruling is vacuous on the detector, and no value is expected anywhere. Here the record changes which loops the world has. That is the form #431's open items (Claude 026 S2) give for a which-route record lifting batch 23's exclusion. Whether a no-which-way record may do the same is a question for David.
  - In both readings, the two turns that gave the virtual L its −1 are still on the zigzag, but on an open path they set no closure condition.
- **(V-c) Persistence and which-way.** P persists along X only under the species-conservation postulate (#432 §3a (T-a); #434: the geometry does not supply it). For electric charge, conservation follows from Maxwell's equations once matter is coupled, but "a unit charge, at most one particle per edge" remains a model assumption. Which route carries P is world data. In this graph the arrival point is route-specific by construction: route 1 arrives at a and route 2 at b, through E0. If the survivor leaves from the arrival point, or from any point that depends on it, X's location encodes the route. That is which-way information and conflicts with #428 §2 item 4. If it leaves from the same c in both route worlds, the location is route-independent. Moving the exit from a route-specific point to a detector-fixed one is exactly what L adds beyond "P arrives and stays" (#432 §3a (T-b), open item). Conditions for no which-way:
  1. c and X are the same in both route worlds and are fixed by the detector (the handle and L) before phases are compared. a and b are solved per world (#428 §6a(c)2), so this is the same condition as for #431 §4's wiring class.
  2. The record reads only the presence (and species) of P on X. It must not read the arrival time, which arc the flow took, or P's frame or spin sign against a detector-fixed reference. Transport along route 1 → a → c and along route 2 → b → c differs by C2's holonomy (for c on A2), and under 1(a) the detector cycles carry opposite signs.
  3. Any flux record has the same signed throat crossing in both route worlds.

  The note's graph does not yet place c or X, so this stays open (as with #432 §3a, Well-posedness; Claude 026 S3).
