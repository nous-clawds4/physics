## For David (one minute)

Two questions.

**1. Can a body really wind around a handle?** Yes, if both mouths of the wormhole are in the *same* space. Then "how many times it went through" is a true whole number. Measured against a declared reference history, it is a clean record. A kick can change it by sending the body through once more. Between kicks it stays fixed **as long as the body is parked away from the mouths**. The catch: an orbit that keeps threading the handle by itself is **unstable**. The two mouths act like convex mirrors and spray nearby paths apart (computed and derived for a flat handle; with gravity at the mouths it is open). So the record has to be "went through *n* times, then parked", not "is going round".

**2. Does any record survive radiation?** Orbits decay, so orbit-based records don't. But **electric charge** does. If a charged body passes through the handle, the electric flux threading the handle changes by exactly its charge (counted with a sign: through A is +, through B is −), and nothing classical can undo that: radiation carries no net charge (Gauss's law). This is Wheeler's "charge without charge". So the handle itself remembers how much charge went through, net. Every charged body counts, not only the carrier, and you have to read it around **one** mouth (around both you see zero). It even turns the winding of question 1 into a radiation-proof record.

**The honest catch on "whole number":** classically, charge comes in any amount. The record is a whole number only as a change from the reference, and only if every carrier carries the same charge, i.e. one particle species. Misner and Wheeler themselves call the flux "unquantized". That is an assumption about matter, not something derived. Otherwise it is an exact real number (still permanent).

Two mouths in one space are also how wormhole time machines are built (offset the mouths' clocks and you get time loops; Morris–Thorne–Yurtsever). That links this back to the geon time-loop picture, but only if the mouths' clocks are offset, which nearby masses tend to cause. In Hadley-type handles the flux also mixes in charge the topology carries by itself.

## Set 1: one-universe handle and the integer winding

**Plain summary.** With both mouths in our space, going through the handle is a loop that can't be shrunk away, so counting passages is a genuine whole-number record. It is clean relative to a reference, kick-writable, and fixed while the body is parked. It does not keep a body threading by itself.

- **Host.** A Visser-type thin-shell handle on $\mathbb R^3$ [M]. Remove two balls of radius $a$ whose centres are a distance $D$ apart, and identify their boundary spheres (with a reflection, so that entry at one mouth is exit at the other; the reflection is what keeps the handle orientable, while a pure translation would give a non-orientable handle, Sorkin's setting, #386). Flat outside; exotic matter on the shell (flagged cost; Visser, PRD 39 (1989) 3182 and NPB 328 (1989) 203, V-text abstracts per #386; the one-universe version stays [M]). Spatial $\pi_1=\mathbb Z$, generated by a loop through the handle [A].
- **The record (#381 convention) [A].**
  - Take the cut Σ to be mouth A's sphere. The signed number of traversals of a worldline segment is its intersection number with Σ.
  - Relative to a declared reference segment with the same endpoints, the integer $w(c\cdot c_{\rm ref}^{-1})\in\mathbb Z$ is cut- and basepoint-independent: signed intersection with the throat sphere is the homomorphism $\pi_1=\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z$, so any homologous cut gives the same value on the closed loop $c\cdot c_{\rm ref}^{-1}$. For an open segment alone the raw count is cut-dependent. Signs: entry through A is +1, entry through B is −1, and a graze is 0 (a body cannot enter A and leave by A).
  - **Unlike batch 15**, this is a $\pi_1$ class, i.e. truly topological.
- **Writes [A].** A carrier kick that sends the body through a mouth changes $w$ by ±1, and one that doesn't leaves it unchanged. The threshold is geometric: whether the post-kick ray meets a mouth.
- **Stable between kicks?**
  - For a body parked on a trajectory that misses the mouths (e.g. bound to some other mass), $w$ is **constant**: no crossings, so the raw count does not advance [A].
  - For a body on the threading orbit, the raw count advances every pass (all passes enter through A, so `threading.py`'s unsigned count equals the signed count, #387), and that orbit is unstable in the flat model (next bullet).
- **The closed threading geodesic is unstable [N].**
  - Equatorial 2D model. Convention (Lead ruling): trace 2(1+L/a) per passage, 4(1+L/a)²−2 over one full period of two passages. The per-passage map is M = [[1,L],[2/a,1+2L/a]], with tr M = 2(1+L/a) > 2 (derived, Geometry #387, §2). The script reports M², the full period of two passages.
  - Trace = 14, 62 and 322 for gaps $L=D-2a=1,3,8$ (with $a=1$), equal to the full-period (two-passage) trace $\operatorname{tr}M^2=4(1+L/a)^2-2$ [A, derived]. Eigenvalues are real, with one $>1$: hyperbolic.
  - Each traversal acts like reflection off a convex mirror (power $2/a$), i.e. defocusing in this flat thin-shell model (the classic two-disc dispersing billiard; mirror equivalence is [A], per #386 and #387).
  - A $10^{-4}$ offset leaves the threading orbit after 5 traversals [N].
  - **With mouth mass: open.** A Schwarzschild exterior (Visser–Poisson-type shell) adds focusing in the gap, but the throat still defocuses (NEC violation at the throat); which wins is not computed (#387). Batch 15's Simpson–Visser throat class shows threading orbits *can* be stable with mouth gravity (#386).
- **Status: live** (topological integer record of the parked kind; L54). The threading orbit is **dead in the flat model; open with mouth mass** (L55).

## Set 2: two mouths in one space ⇒ CTCs (Morris–Thorne–Yurtsever)

**Plain summary.** If one mouth's clock runs behind the other's (because it was moved, or sits deeper in gravity), a trip through the handle and back outside can return before it left. That is a time machine. It is the same kind of time loop as in the geon picture, but only when there is an offset.

- Morris, Thorne & Yurtsever, PRL 61 (1988) 1446 (V-text abstract per #386; the threshold is V-meta): give the mouths a time shift $T$ (by relative motion, the twin effect, or by gravitational redshift; Frolov & Novikov, PRD 42 (1990) 1057, V-text abstract per #386). Once $T$ exceeds the outside light-travel time $D$, CTCs form, through the handle and back outside. A chronology horizon forms first (Hawking 1992's setting).
- **[A]** The static, symmetric thin-shell host of Set 1 has no time offset. With static $t$ a global time function, it has **no CTCs**. So Set 1 itself is CTC-free, but only until other masses are nearby: per Frolov–Novikov, interaction with surrounding matter and external fields "almost inevitably" produces the offset, so CTC-freedom is non-generic once the host contains other masses, including Set 1's "bound to some other mass" parking (#386).
- **Tie-back.** With an offset, the handle is a CTC host with a compactly generated chronology horizon. Batch 13/#364's timing story then applies: replies beyond the horizon, and extension-dependence. That is how the one-universe handle reconnects to the round-trip measurement. **Exploration only** (4:16 PM).
- **Status: noted, consistent** (L56).

## Set 3: records that radiation can't erase

**Plain summary.** Anything stored in an orbit leaks away as the body radiates. What can't leak is a conserved charge. So the best record is "how much electric charge has gone through the handle". It is permanent and readable from outside one mouth.

| Candidate | Survives radiation? | Writable? | Discrete? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Orbit classes (batch 15) | **no**: decay to the throat (Dent et al., 2007.09135, per #383) | yes | in the test limit only | parked |
| Parked winding $w$ (Set 1) | the *integer* survives, but the parked orbit decays, and a decaying body may fall into a mouth and change $w$ | yes | yes | conditional on safe parking |
| Deck-class / $\pi_1$ labels | yes (topological) | **no** | yes | label, not storage |
| Topological charges of the host | yes | no (needs topology change; batch 14 L45) | yes | not writable |
| **Electric flux through the handle** ("charge without charge"; Misner & Wheeler, Ann. Phys. 2 (1957) 525, V-text per #386; Wheeler, Ann. Phys. 2 (1957) 604, V-meta) | **yes**: $d\!\star\!F=J$; the flux through a mouth sphere changes only when charge crosses it, and radiation carries no net charge [A] | **yes**: a charged carrier passing through changes it by $q$ | only if all carriers share one charge $q_0$ (see below) | **live** |
| Gravitational-wave memory (Christodoulou 1991, V-meta per #380) | yes (it *is* a radiation effect) | yes | **no**: continuum | live as an exact-real record |
| Mouth masses (Frolov–Novikov-type mass transfer [M]) | **no**: GW carry energy away; mass is not separately conserved per mouth | yes | no | parked |

**Flux record in detail [A].**
- Let $\Phi_A=\oint_{S_A}\star F$ over mouth A's sphere. In a source-free region, $\Phi_A$ is the same on every sphere homologous to $S_A$: a homology (H₂) class, hence "topologically conserved". Misner–Wheeler (p. 534, V-text per #386): "the wormhole flux remains invariant. This constant of the motion represents the charge. The charge or wormhole flux is unquantized." It changes by exactly $\pm q$ per signed passage of any charge $q$ (stray matter included), and by nothing else, because radiation fields are sourceless. Dissipation-proof.
- **Coupling to Set 1.** If the carrier is a charged body, $\Delta\Phi_A=q\,\Delta w_{\rm carrier}$. The carrier's winding is transcribed into the handle's flux, so the record outlives the carrier's orbit and even its capture. But every charged crossing counts, so $\Delta\Phi_A=q\,\Delta w$ holds only if the carrier is the only charge that crossed; the record is net charge transported (#387).
- **Readable by the observer.** A Gauss surface around mouth A near the ledger tube measures $\Phi_A$ locally (L47/L52: the ledger's Gauss surface must enclose exactly one mouth, since a surface around both reads $\Phi_A+\Phi_B=0$ from the handle; read only when no charge sits between the surface and the throat).
- **What makes it discrete?**
  - Classically, $q$ is a real number. Dirac quantisation is quantum and is excluded by the 5:00 PM constraint.
  - The flux difference from the reference is integer-valued, $\Phi_A-\Phi_{\rm ref}=n\,q_0$ (with $\Phi_0$ an arbitrary real), only if every carrier is of one species with charge $q_0$. That is a classical assumption about the matter content, not a derivation. Picking the species to get integers would be close to a law of R by fiat. Flagged. Misner–Wheeler's "unquantized" (V-text) supports this: geometry alone does not give discreteness. No prior art is known for flux as a written memory, or for discreteness from identical carriers (#386).
  - Without it, the record is an **exact real** that is permanent and monotone in net passages. It is still a clean record (#355 exact-real records).
- **Status: live (flux, exact-real; integer conditional on one carrier species)** (L57, L58). Gravitational-wave memory is live as a continuum supplement (L59).

## Weak points

1. **Exotic matter.** Thin-shell handles need NEC violation on the shell (flagged cost). Their stability as spacetimes is unchecked; related wormholes are unstable (per #383).
2. **Threading orbit unstable [N].** So the winding record works only as "passages then parked". Parking orbits decay under radiation, and a falling body could add a stray passage.
3. **Integer flux needs identical carriers.** Classical charge is continuous, so discreteness is an assumption about matter, not derived.
4. **Charged carriers radiate EM too.** That doesn't change $\Phi$, but it changes the carrier's orbit and so the kick thresholds of Set 1.
5. **CTC tie-back (Set 2)** rests on V-text abstracts (MTY 1988, Frolov–Novikov 1990; #386); the threshold is V-meta. CTC-freedom of the host is non-generic with other masses nearby. It also reintroduces the chronology horizon, with its extension-dependence (#367, #375).
6. **2D equatorial ray model** with a flat exterior and no mouth gravity. With mouth mass, the gap focuses but the throat still defocuses; which wins is open (#387).
7. **Not a Hadley geon.** These handles are time-orientable; the Set 1 reflection gluing preserves both orientations, so nothing flips there. What flips depends on the loop: if it reverses time orientation but preserves total orientation (Hadley-type), the flux on a fixed mouth sphere stays well defined but a carrier's charge reads as −q after the loop; if it reverses total orientation, $\star F$ is twisted and the flux is defined only up to sign. Type F loops cannot write anyway (#381). Sketch (Geometry #387). Moreover, non-orientable and non-time-orientable (non-co-orientable) handles can carry net charge of their own from the source-free Maxwell equations (Sorkin, J. Phys. A 10 (1977) 717; Diemer & Hadley, CQG 16 (1999) 3567, gr-qc/9905069; V-text abstracts per Literature #386). So in Hadley-type hosts the flux mixes the host's intrinsic topological charge with written passages and is not a pure passage count.
