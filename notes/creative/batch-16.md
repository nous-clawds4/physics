## For David (one minute)

Two questions.

**1. Can a body really wind around a handle?** Yes, if both mouths of the wormhole are in the *same* space. Then "how many times it went through" is a true whole number. Measured against a declared reference history, it is a clean record. A kick can change it by sending the body through once more. Between kicks it stays fixed **as long as the body is parked away from the mouths**. The catch: an orbit that keeps threading the handle by itself is **unstable**. The two mouths act like convex mirrors and spray nearby paths apart (computed). So the record has to be "went through *n* times, then parked", not "is going round".

**2. Does any record survive radiation?** Orbits decay, so orbit-based records don't. But **electric charge** does. If a charged body passes through the handle, the electric flux threading the handle changes by exactly its charge, and nothing classical can undo that: radiation carries no net charge (Gauss's law). This is Wheeler's "charge without charge". So the handle itself remembers how many charged carriers went through, net. It even turns the winding of question 1 into a radiation-proof record.

**The honest catch on "whole number":** classically, charge comes in any amount. The record is a whole number only if every carrier carries the same charge, i.e. one particle species. That is an assumption about matter, not something derived. Otherwise it is an exact real number (still permanent).

Two mouths in one space are also how wormhole time machines are built (offset the mouths' clocks and you get time loops; Morris–Thorne–Yurtsever). That links this back to the geon time-loop picture, but only if the mouths' clocks are offset.

## Set 1: one-universe handle and the integer winding

**Plain summary.** With both mouths in our space, going through the handle is a loop that can't be shrunk away, so counting passages is a genuine whole-number record. It is clean relative to a reference, kick-writable, and fixed while the body is parked. It does not keep a body threading by itself.

- **Host.** A Visser-type thin-shell handle on $\mathbb R^3$ [M]. Remove two balls of radius $a$ whose centres are a distance $D$ apart, and identify their boundary spheres (with a reflection, so that entry at one mouth is exit at the other). Flat outside; exotic matter on the shell (flagged cost; Visser 1989 [M]). Spatial $\pi_1=\mathbb Z$, generated by a loop through the handle [A].
- **The record (#381 convention) [A].**
  - Take the cut Σ to be mouth A's sphere. The signed number of traversals of a worldline segment is its intersection number with Σ.
  - Relative to a declared reference segment with the same endpoints, the integer $w(c\cdot c_{\rm ref}^{-1})\in\mathbb Z$ is cut-, basepoint- and framing-independent, because $\pi_1=\mathbb Z$ is abelian.
  - **Unlike batch 15**, this is a $\pi_1$ class, i.e. truly topological.
- **Writes [A].** A carrier kick that sends the body through a mouth changes $w$ by ±1, and one that doesn't leaves it unchanged. The threshold is geometric: whether the post-kick ray meets a mouth.
- **Stable between kicks?**
  - For a body parked on a trajectory that misses the mouths (e.g. bound to some other mass), $w$ is **constant**: no crossings, so the raw count does not advance [A].
  - For a body on the threading orbit, the raw count advances every pass, and that orbit is unstable (next bullet).
- **The closed threading geodesic is unstable [N].**
  - Equatorial 2D model, linearised return map over one full period (two traversals).
  - Trace = 14, 62 and 322 for gaps $L=D-2a=1,3,8$ (with $a=1$), matching $4(1+L/a)^2-2$ [A fit]. Eigenvalues are real, with one $>1$: hyperbolic.
  - Each traversal acts like reflection off a convex mirror, i.e. defocusing, as for any NEC-violating throat.
  - A $10^{-4}$ offset leaves the threading orbit after 5 traversals [N].
  - Adding a Newtonian-like pull at each mouth could focus it, but is not computed.
- **Status: live** (topological integer record of the parked kind; L54). The threading orbit is **dead** as a record carrier (L55).

## Set 2: two mouths in one space ⇒ CTCs (Morris–Thorne–Yurtsever)

**Plain summary.** If one mouth's clock runs behind the other's (because it was moved, or sits deeper in gravity), a trip through the handle and back outside can return before it left. That is a time machine. It is the same kind of time loop as in the geon picture, but only when there is an offset.

- **[M]** Morris, Thorne & Yurtsever 1988: give the mouths a time shift $T$ (by relative motion, the twin effect, or by gravitational redshift; Frolov–Novikov 1990). Once $T$ exceeds the outside light-travel time $D$, CTCs form, through the handle and back outside. A chronology horizon forms first (Hawking 1992's setting).
- **[A]** The static, symmetric thin-shell host of Set 1 has no time offset. With static $t$ a global time function, it has **no CTCs**. So Set 1 itself is CTC-free.
- **Tie-back.** With an offset, the handle is a CTC host with a compactly generated chronology horizon. Batch 13/#364's timing story then applies: replies beyond the horizon, and extension-dependence. That is how the one-universe handle reconnects to the round-trip measurement. **Exploration only** (4:16 PM).
- **Status: noted, consistent** (L56).

## Set 3: records that radiation can't erase

**Plain summary.** Anything stored in an orbit leaks away as the body radiates. What can't leak is a conserved charge. So the best record is "how much electric charge has gone through the handle". It is permanent and readable from outside one mouth.

| Candidate | Survives radiation? | Writable? | Discrete? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Orbit classes (batch 15) | **no**: decay to the throat (Dent et al., 2007.09135, per #383) | yes | in the test limit only | parked |
| Parked winding $w$ (Set 1) | the *integer* survives, but the parked orbit decays, and a decaying body may fall into a mouth and change $w$ | yes | yes | conditional on safe parking |
| Deck-class / $\pi_1$ labels | yes (topological) | **no** | yes | label, not storage |
| Topological charges of the host | yes | no (needs topology change; batch 14 L45) | yes | not writable |
| **Electric flux through the handle** ("charge without charge", Wheeler 1957, Misner–Wheeler 1957 [M]) | **yes**: $d\!\star\!F=J$; the flux through a mouth sphere changes only when charge crosses it, and radiation carries no net charge [A] | **yes**: a charged carrier passing through changes it by $q$ | only if all carriers share one charge $q_0$ (see below) | **live** |
| Gravitational-wave memory (Christodoulou 1991, V-meta per #380) | yes (it *is* a radiation effect) | yes | **no**: continuum | live as an exact-real record |
| Mouth masses (Frolov–Novikov-type mass transfer [M]) | **no**: GW carry energy away; mass is not separately conserved per mouth | yes | no | parked |

**Flux record in detail [A].**
- Let $\Phi_A=\oint_{S_A}\star F$ over mouth A's sphere. In a source-free region, $\Phi_A$ is the same on every sphere homologous to $S_A$: a homology (H₂) class, hence "topologically conserved". It changes by exactly $q$ when a charge $q$ passes through the mouth, and by nothing else, because radiation fields are sourceless. Dissipation-proof.
- **Coupling to Set 1.** If the carrier is a charged body, $\Delta\Phi_A=q\,\Delta w_{\rm carrier}$. The carrier's winding is transcribed into the handle's flux, so the record outlives the carrier's orbit and even its capture.
- **Readable by the observer.** A Gauss surface around mouth A near the ledger tube measures $\Phi_A$ locally (L47/L52: the ledger tube must enclose the mouth).
- **What makes it discrete?**
  - Classically, $q$ is a real number. Dirac quantisation is quantum and is excluded by the 5:00 PM constraint.
  - The flux is integer-valued, $\Phi_A=\Phi_0+n\,q_0$, only if every carrier is of one species with charge $q_0$. That is a classical assumption about the matter content, not a derivation. Picking the species to get integers would be close to a law of R by fiat. Flagged.
  - Without it, the record is an **exact real** that is permanent and monotone in net passages. It is still a clean record (#355 exact-real records).
- **Status: live (flux, exact-real; integer conditional on one carrier species)** (L57, L58). Gravitational-wave memory is live as a continuum supplement (L59).

## Weak points

1. **Exotic matter.** Thin-shell handles need NEC violation on the shell (flagged cost). Their stability as spacetimes is unchecked; related wormholes are unstable (per #383).
2. **Threading orbit unstable [N].** So the winding record works only as "passages then parked". Parking orbits decay under radiation, and a falling body could add a stray passage.
3. **Integer flux needs identical carriers.** Classical charge is continuous, so discreteness is an assumption about matter, not derived.
4. **Charged carriers radiate EM too.** That doesn't change $\Phi$, but it changes the carrier's orbit and so the kick thresholds of Set 1.
5. **CTC tie-back (Set 2)** is from memory (MTY 1988, Frolov–Novikov 1990, unchecked). It also reintroduces the chronology horizon, with its extension-dependence (#367, #375).
6. **2D equatorial ray model** with a flat exterior and no mouth gravity. A focusing mass at the mouths might stabilise threading; not computed.
7. **Not a Hadley geon.** These handles are time-orientable. A non-time-orientable handle flips charge sign along the loop (Type F, #364), which would make the flux record orientation-dependent. Unchecked.
