## For David (one minute)

Yes, and it can be done without creating new handles in spacetime. Three pieces:

- **The signal** should be a small physical body (a "pulse" of matter), not a light ray. A light ray comes back the same way every time. A body that can collide with itself inside the time loop can come back in several self-consistent ways. That is where the countable menu of replies comes from (Set 1).
- **The diary** is your idea: a little flock of geons travelling with the observer. They are made at the start and never created or destroyed (Set 2).
- **Writing** to the diary is the hard part. Making a new handle is close to forbidden in classical gravity: you get time loops or a singularity. So we don't. A returning signal **nudges** the flock instead, and that is the part a review has weakened. It changes how many times a diary body has gone round one of the diary's handles, compared with a declared reference history (a whole number; a body circling another body has no such number) or where they sit relative to one another (Set 3). A whole-number record works only if a kick moves a body into a different stable trapped orbit (e.g. around a different geon); a plain winding count keeps ticking on its own.

The catch: the diary is now part of the world, so its different entries are different worlds. That is exactly your "branch A = world A". It also forces the observer to be a small tube rather than a point, which is a known cost (#336).

## Set 1: the carrier (closes round-trip open item (a), conditionally)

**Plain summary.** A light flash returns in only one way per route, so it can label *which route* but cannot offer a menu. A small body that can hit its own earlier self inside the time loop has several consistent histories (the classic "billiard ball" puzzle). That is the source of the countable multiplicity. So the carrier should be a body that interacts.

| Carrier | Replies per deck class | Can it write? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Null geodesic | one (ODE uniqueness; Prop 3.2-type) | no: no energy or momentum transfer in the test limit | label-only; **insufficient** |
| Linear test field (EM, linearised GW) | one: existence and uniqueness among solutions that die off in time (Friedman–Morris 1991, 1997, V-meta per #377) | no backreaction at test order | **insufficient** for multiplicity |
| Gravitational wave beyond linear order | possibly several; no theorem known to us, and no verified source on non-linear waves through wormholes (#380) | yes, in principle | open; too hard to check now |
| **Matter pulse / interacting test body** | countable families of self-consistent histories (Echeverria–Klinkhammer–Thorne 1991; batch 12 L40) | yes: momentum transfer by collision | **live (preferred)** |
| Small geon as carrier | inherits multiplicity if it interacts | yes | emitting it is topology change (Set 2 obstacle); **parked** |

- **Why "interacting" is needed.** A linear carrier gives the round trip a unique reply per class (Friedman–Morris). Only interaction lets the CTC consistency condition have several solutions (EKT). This is the item-(a) answer: an interacting beacon.
- **Cost.** The multiplicity is a property of the matter law plus the CTC. We must not pick the matter law to get a particular count, which would be a law of R by fiat. Billiard-ball elastic collisions are the standard example, and we take them as given.
- **Topological censorship (Friedman–Schleich–Witt 1993, V-text abstract per #380).** In a globally hyperbolic, asymptotically flat spacetime satisfying the null energy condition, every causal curve from $\mathscr I^-$ to $\mathscr I^+$ is homotopic to a trivial one. The round trip escapes it because geon hosts are not globally hyperbolic (CTCs; eternal geons have no Cauchy surface, #371), and/or because the energy condition fails. Which one applies depends on the host and is not decided here.
- **Status: live** (survives #380). It closes (a) as "interacting test body", conditional on Set 3 using the same body to write.

## Set 2: the physical record (co-moving ledger geons)

**Plain summary.** The diary is a small group of geons that travel next to the observer. They exist from the start, and nothing ever creates or destroys a handle. The diary is "spacetime itself", as you asked, but it never changes its topology.

**The obstacle (topology change under classical evolution; all unchecked):**
- Geroch 1967: a compact, time-orientable spacetime interpolating between spatial slices of different topology contains closed timelike curves.
- Tipler 1977 (Ann. Phys. 108, 1; PRL 37 (1976) 879): with Einstein's equation, energy and generic conditions, topology change in a compact region is forbidden outright, "even at the price of singularities" (as characterised in Borde 1994's abstract, V-text; Tipler V-meta).
- Borde 1994 (gr-qc/9406053, V-text abstract): for "causally compact" interpolating spacetimes (wider than compact), topology change brings causality violation, and in dimension ≥ 3 cannot satisfy Einstein's equation with a reasonable source; it is kinematically possible without a field equation.
- Gibbons–Hawking 1992: spin structure constrains which changes are allowed (a mod-2 condition on created handles).

**Loopholes and their cost.**
- **(2A) Topology-changing write through CTCs.** Geroch's theorem permits topology change *with* CTCs, and CTCs are allowed in geon hosts (4:16 PM, exploration only). Cost: the CTCs must now sit in the ledger region, next to the observer, not just inside the measured geon. The observer's own neighbourhood loses chronology. Also, every write needs a fresh CTC region, so the record pays a causality cost per bit. **Parked.**
- **(2B) Non-Hausdorff branching as the write.** A trousers-type non-Hausdorff spacetime "writes" by splitting. Cost: that is the branching we are trying to *derive*. Using it as the write mechanism assumes the result (circular). Under the lock, non-Hausdorff unions also do not fork a single geodesic (#367). **Parked.**
- **(2C) No topology change: the ledger's topology is fixed at the start.** The geons are eternal or formed before the first measurement. Writes change a non-topological datum (Set 3). **Live.**

- **2A after #380.** It stays parked. Geroch does not close it (CTCs and non-time-orientability are allowed in geon hosts, exploration only), but Tipler and Borde close it for reasonable sources, and Gibbons–Hawking forbids single-handle writes when there is a spin structure. It reopens only with exotic sources (energy conditions violated) or outside causal compactness (#380).

**Status: 2C live; 2A and 2B parked.**

## Set 3: the write mechanism (no topology change)

**Plain summary.** The returning body bumps the diary geons. The bump changes something that can change without new handles: how many times a diary body has gone round a handle of the diary, compared with a declared reference history (a whole number), or possibly a twist (holonomy) carried round a handle, or their relative positions and phases (ordinary numbers). After review (#380) this is **conditional**: a plain winding count keeps ticking on its own, so the whole-number version works only if a kick knocks a body into a different stable trapped orbit (around geon A rather than geon B, say). Whether such orbits are stable is unchecked. Only replies that come back "the right way round" (Type O) can write, the write happens beyond the observer's Cauchy horizon, and a geon whose only loop flips time (π₁ = ℤ/2) cannot write at all (#381).

| Candidate record | Changeable by a classical write? | Discrete? | Stable? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Deck-class labels of ledger loops | **no**: fixed by topology | yes | yes | a *label of a reply*, not a writable state; **feeds Set 1 only** |
| Holonomy / Wilson-loop-like data of ledger geons | yes in principle: flat evolution moves it only continuously, but a curvature transient (flux or matter through the loop) can leave it at a different point or component (#381) | yes if the representation is rigid | yes under flat deformations | stable under flat deformations, writable by a curvature transient (flux or matter through the loop), like winding; **parked for lack of a mechanism** (Levi-Civita near a geon is not flat) |
| Relative positions / phases among ledger geons | yes: a collision transfers momentum | **no** (continuum) | marginal (neutral directions drift) | live as the **exact-real** record (#355); prior art: gravitational-wave memory, a continuum record (Zel'dovich–Polnarev 1974, Braginsky–Thorne 1987, Christodoulou 1991; V-meta per #380); discreteness comes only from which deck class replied |
| Type O/F flag | per-return property of the loop | yes | n/a | a label, not storage; **recorded as part of each entry, not a mechanism** |
| **Winding count** of a ledger body around a ledger handle (relative to a declared reference history; body-around-body winding is not topological in 3+1) | a raw count advances under free motion; a kick changes the orbit continuously | only via **capture** into one of several stable bound orbit classes | only if those classes are dynamically stable (unchecked) | **conditional** (#380) |

**Winding-count write (Set 3 main line; conditional after #380).**
- A ledger body orbits, or is trapped near, a ledger geon's handle. Its record is the class of its worldline segment rel endpoints, relative to a declared reference segment, mapped to ℤ by the handle's homomorphism. Only this difference is cut-, basepoint- and framing-independent; an open segment has no absolute integer winding.
- An incoming carrier (Set 1) collides with it. Whether the winding then advances by 0, 1, … follows from the collision dynamics. **It is not chosen.**
- The winding is an integer, but free orbital motion crosses the cut once per orbit, so the raw count advances by itself (every orbit) and a kick changes the orbit's phase and period continuously. Stability holds only for the deviation from the reference history, read at matched return events: between kicks it is constant when the actual and reference orbits share a period, and otherwise it drifts (#381). A discrete, stable record needs a capture-type invariant instead: which closed homotopy class the body ends up bound in (e.g. bound to geon A vs geon B vs unbound), and only if those classes are dynamically stable (unchecked).
- **No topology change.** The handles are fixed (2C), and only the worldlines move.

**Prior art (#380).** No GR prior art is known for an integer winding-number memory. Gravitational-wave memory (Christodoulou 1991, V-meta) is prior art for the continuous record only.

**Can a write be a change in a non-topological invariant?** Yes, but only for data made discrete by the fixed topology and changed by matter (after #380, only as capture into stable bound classes, not a raw winding count; after #381, the winding is a joint invariant, a worldline class in the fixed π₁, and holonomy moves to the writable side). Topological invariants of the ledger (deck labels, π₁) are fixed. Flat holonomy is stable but writable only by a curvature transient. Writes live in data made discrete by the fixed topology and changed by matter: the classes of ledger worldlines in π₁, and possibly holonomy.

## Traps checked

- **Nested-prefix trap (#367 (1); §2.4 of #372).** Under the lock, the observer's germ does not fork across worlds; the record does. A physical ledger fits this: the ledger *is* the record, so what branches is the ledger state, not the observer's motion. **Passes**, as long as the ledger is not used to make the observer's geodesic fork. #372 R2 concerns the observer's worldline germ; the observer-plus-ledger tube germ after a write does differ across worlds, by construction.
- **Identical-state trap.** If two worlds differ only in the ledger's state, and the ledger is outside the observer state, they look identical to the observer, and nothing has branched for it. So **the ledger must be part of the observer state.** That is L29's germ+record, now physically realised: $\hat O=(O,\text{germ of the ledger tube})$.
- **Cost of including the ledger.** The state becomes the germ of a finite tube containing observer + ledger. That brings back #336's finite-size-observer cost: an undefined tube scale and a changed state definition. It is cheaper here, because the scale is set by the ledger's physical extent, not chosen. **Open.**
- **Law of R by fiat.** It is avoided only if the write map (carrier collision → winding change) follows from the matter law plus the geometry. Choosing the threshold or the cut by hand would be fiat. The cut is a gauge choice: the *difference* of windings from a declared reference history is cut-independent (#381). **Passes, conditionally.**
- **Locality vs memory (#352).** A physical ledger makes the record local again, near the observer. That partly answers OP1 ("where the memory is stored", David 3:53).
- **Classical only (5:00 PM).** No quantum structure is used. Multiplicity comes from classical CTC consistency (EKT), not amplitudes.

## Weak points

1. **EKT multiplicity** is shown for billiard balls in simple wormhole spacetimes, not Hadley geons. No known result transfers it (#380); the transfer is an assumption.
2. **Ledger stability**: no stable bound configuration of several geons is known (Wheeler 1955, Brill–Hartle 1964, Anderson–Brill 1997, V-meta per #380).
3. **Tube scale** (#336) returns in milder form.
4. **The cut** for winding counts has no canonical choice; the record is stated as differences from a declared reference history (fixed).
5. **Countability** relies on batch 12's (L40) generic countability of EKT families; non-generic continua are possible.
6. **Topology-change citations:** answered by #380. Geroch is V-meta (it also allows a non-time-orientable escape); Tipler is V-meta; Borde and Gibbons–Hawking (CMP) are V-text abstracts. 2A is closed for reasonable sources.
8. **Winding write (#380, #381):** a raw winding count is not a stored datum; Set 3 survives only as capture into stable bound orbit classes, whose stability is unchecked. The winding is topological only around a handle, and gauge-free only as a difference from a declared reference.
9. **Holonomy (#381):** writable in principle by a curvature transient, but no mechanism is known; Levi-Civita near a vacuum geon is not flat, so it would need an extra gauge field or a flat region.
10. **Write constraints (#381):** Type O only; the write lies beyond H⁺(S), so the ledger tube must cross the Cauchy horizon and its survival is extension-dependent (#367, #375); ℤ/2 geons cannot write.
7. **Collision writes need the carrier to reach the ledger** after the round trip, so the reply must return to the ledger, not just to γ. That adds a geometric condition to the round-trip definition: the return class must be Type O (Type F arrives past-pointing at the ledger, with negative energy relative to the tube), the write event lies beyond H⁺(S) (#364 (d)), so the ledger tube must cross the Cauchy horizon, and a geon with π₁ ≅ ℤ/2 cannot write.
