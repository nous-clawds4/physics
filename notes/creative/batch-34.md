## Plain summary for David

You described a circle of dots, one per path. Dots that "line up" pair off as virtual particles and vanish; leftover dots must be absorbed by the detector. Evenly spread dots mostly pair, while a clump leaves many unpaired, and that would make the pattern. We checked three things.

1. **What "line up" means.** For two paths to cancel, their dots must sit *opposite* each other on the circle, half a turn apart. Equal dots reinforce. For a clock hand, "opposite" breaks the alignment you asked for (#419). With spin-½, a relative full turn shows up as a sign flip, so aligned paths can still cancel. That sign depends on the spin structure, and on a loop that is non-zero in H₁(M; ℤ₂) (the π₁ = ℤ cap, or handles between the routes) it is a two-way choice (#423). So the clean version of your rule is: **two paths cancel when the spinor sign round the loop they form is −1, under the spin-½ reading. That sign is the product of the spin-structure value on the loop's class (a two-way choice only if the loop's class is non-zero in H₁(M; ℤ₂)) and the sign from its relative full turns; only the product is defined, so the same loop cancels in one spin structure and reinforces in the other.**
2. **Exact or approximate.** If the dots move continuously, exactly opposite pairs are rare (measure zero; Geometry's #421 dilemma). A tolerance would be a number we put in by hand. Discrete phases (signs set by which handles a loop goes round) avoid this. Among the options we listed, they are the only one that can be fixed without tuning (other discrete data, such as non-abelian handle classes, finite holonomy groups, or an isometry exchanging routes, would also qualify), and that again points to handles between the routes. **Update (your 3:33/3:40 PM ruling):** matching is exact inside each world, and worlds with inexact pairs are simply not allowed (self-consistency). At 4:05 PM you made this explicit: an inexact pair rules the world out entirely, like a spacetime that only almost obeys Einstein's equation, and exact alignment must *not* be required in the shadow spacetime, where it is expected to be inexact. Any apparent tolerance comes only from projecting many worlds onto the shadow spacetime. So discrete phases are no longer needed, and the tolerance objection is superseded (§4a). Open: why unpaired paths must end at a detector; how rare exact closures are within a world (non-generic, §4a). Point 3's floor survives (§4a (c)). No claim about the Born rule is made.
3. **How many are left over.** With "remove opposite pairs" as the rule, the leftover count is at least the **length** of the arrow sum |Σ e^{iφ}| (for exactly opposite pairs; approximately, with a tolerance), so near dark fringes it cannot fall below that length. This holds for this pairing rule as posed, not for every rule. The triangle inequality gives a floor: at least that many dots are left. In our toy (greedy matching within a tolerance, N = 400; that reading is superseded, §4a) the log-log slopes are 0.5–0.9. With exact matching, as you ruled, continuous phases almost never give an exactly opposite pair, so nothing is removed and the count does not depend on the arrow sum. If every dot is exactly ±1 and all opposite pairs close, the count equals the arrow sum. So the arrow sum is a floor, not a scaling law, and an exact match allows two paths to cancel without requiring it. We do not adjust the rule.

**Bearing on the topology choice** (we withdraw the case we gave earlier; any preference between the caps is your call): only the cap that leaves π₁ = ℤ, with the closed route loop crossing the 1-handle summand A's S² an odd number of times (in 3D space a loop has no "sides"), gives that loop a sign that differs between the two spin structures, so each exactly aligned pair of that kind cancels in one of them. The cancelling cap leaves no free sign: the frame's turn alone sets it, −1 after an odd number of relative full turns (#423 F1), so point 1 can be met with either cap. Point 2's case for discreteness is superseded (§4a). The ℤ₂ does not make exact matching common either: the frames must still line up exactly, and under the lock that happens only on a nowhere-dense set of routes, with either cap (#421 §8). Nor do the routes yet fix whether the loop crosses A's S²: A is one of the pair's own members, so the crossing depends on the curve taken inside A's world-tube (#421 §3; #423 §4 item 2). We recommend the π₁ = ℤ cap, keeping the remaining ℤ₂ spin choice as world data (both spin structures kept as separate worlds, each with its own fixed sign, neither picked; comparing them would need the measure μ, which stays open). It is your call. Point 3's floor holds whichever cap you pick.

## 2. Point (1): the pairing rule [CL]

- **Equal phase:** arrows e^{iφ} coincide and reinforce. Equal phase cannot be the cancelling rule.
- **Relative π (clock hand):** cancels under arrow addition, but breaks David's 1:54 AM alignment condition, since the loop then winds an odd multiple of π (#419 R1, Geometry pass).
- **Relative 2π with the spin-½ sign −1:** aligned *and* cancelling (#419 R1/A1). It needs (i) a spin-½ particle (L161, not adopted; MTW §41.9, unchecked) and (ii) a spin structure s.
  - On a loop γ that vanishes in H₁(M; ℤ₂), the sign is fixed by the frame's turn alone: −1 for an odd number of 2π turns (#423 H6). That turn is continuous data, so this falls into #421's second horn.
  - On a class [γ] ≠ 0, the sign also depends on s, a ℤ₂ choice that no topological fixer settles (#423, L169: reading pending a Lead ruling).
- **Reading:** David's "line up perfectly" is best read as "relative 2π turn with the spin-½ sign −1". The discrete part of that sign lives only on loops whose class is non-zero in H₁(M; ℤ₂).
- **Note on David's "if (and only if)":** a sign −1 makes a pair cancel. "Only if" also excludes all other pairings, so the rule is exclusive. Whether unpaired dots can also partially cancel (three at 120°) is not covered by pairwise annihilation; triples (David 1:36 AM) would be a separate rule.

## 4. Point (3): the leftover count [CL]

- **Bound (paper).** Let N dots have resultant R = |Σ e^{iφ_k}|. Here φ_k is the phase that the spin-½ holonomy round path k, followed by a reference path reversed, gives a fixed reference spinor. A cancelling pair (frames aligned, sign −1, §2) is then exactly opposite. This needs the loops' holonomies to be rotations about one axis; otherwise a path's dot is an SL(2,ℂ) element, not a point on the circle. Only differences φ_j − φ_k are defined (§6), and R does not depend on the reference. Remove pairs that are exactly opposite (with a tolerance δ each removed pair leaves a residual of up to 2 sin(δ/2), so the bound is then approximate; it held in the toy, min(leftover − R) ≥ 0.2): each removed pair contributes 0 to the sum, so the remaining dots alone have resultant R. They are unit vectors, so their count is at least R. **Leftover ≥ R**, with equality when the leftovers all point the same way (a clump). With a tolerance δ each removed pair contributes up to 2 sin(δ/2), so the bound becomes leftover ≥ R − P·2 sin(δ/2) for P removed pairs. The floor is the decisive point: no rule of this kind can give a count that falls below R (up to the tolerance term) near dark fringes, so minima cannot be darker than linear in R.
- **Toy cases.** N = 2 opposite: leftover 0 = R. N = 3 at 120°: R = 0, but no pair is opposite, so leftover is 3 under pairwise rules. N dots clumped at one phase: leftover N = R.
- **Numeric check** (`batch-34/pairing.py`; greedy antipodal matching within tolerance δ; von Mises phases, N = 400, 20 reps × 8 concentrations):

| δ | log-log slope of leftover vs R | median leftover/R |
|---|---|---|
| 0.05 | 0.49 | 1.68 |
| 0.2 | 0.77 | 1.31 |
| 0.5 | 0.93 | 1.16 |

  For small δ there is a noise floor of unmatched dots; as δ grows, leftover → R (slope → 1). Toy conditions: greedy antipodal matching in random order, von Mises phases, N = 400, 20 reps × 8 concentrations, fit over R > 5; reproduced by Literature (#424). The bound does not cap the leftover from above (three dots at 120°: R = 0, leftover 3). The "slope" is one log-log fit pooled across concentrations at fixed N = 400 (R > 5 only; leftover ≤ N), so it describes this family, not a scaling law; the bound above is the general statement.
- **Plain statement:** under pairwise annihilation of exactly opposite dots the leftover at d_i is at least |Σ e^{iφ}| (proved above), and in the toy (tolerance δ, the superseded reading) it grows at most about linearly with it. With exact matching and continuous phases there are almost surely no exactly opposite pairs, so the leftover is the full count N whatever R is (Claude 023's rerun of this procedure: slopes 0.22, 0.03 and 0.00 at δ = 0.01, 0.001 and 0; median leftover 200, 360 and 400 of 400). If every dot is exactly ±1 and all opposite pairs are removed, leftover = R. An exact match permits pairing but does not force it (1:58 PM: "the option of cancelling"), so a world's leftover lies anywhere from R_w to N_w. R is a floor, not a scaling law. This is a statement about this rule as posed, not about every pairing rule. Comparing it with observed frequencies would need R to stand for a lab amplitude at d_i (unit arrows counted over a defined path set). That is the path-sum rule §7 does not import, over a path set §6 leaves undefined, so this note states only the scaling. We do not adjust the rule: a rule designed to reproduce observed frequencies would be aiming at the answer (LT2). Any other rule must be motivated independently and fixed before frequencies are compared.
