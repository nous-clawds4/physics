## Plain summary (for David)

You said it isn't clear how to tell the path from the particle, and you leaned towards this: in a World with no actors, the particle *is* a topological object, that object is an FS geon, and it may have further topological features. Different fermions would have different features. Whether an "empty" FS geon, one with no extra features, makes sense is open; if it does, it is still a particle, but you don't know which one. This note records that inclination (not a ruling) and Creative Lead's reading of it. It reads your inclination as #437's R-geon reading (Creative Lead's reading; the choice is yours), keeps "empty path" (no geon) separate from "empty FS geon" (a bare geon), names no particular empty FS geon, and suggests one construction for Geometry to check: a species is a spinorial prime joined to extra topology (a connected sum). It also flags, as Geometry corrected it, that a path with no geon still has the World's transport but no object to carry a sign, which bears on the detector loops in #431 and #436. **Update (Geometry and Literature reviews):** features are best read as tensorial prime summands, so a fermion is one spinorial core plus any number of tensorial features; a second spinorial summand would be a second fermion. (Claude 028: that count is a definition taken from the quantum picture, where each spinorial piece's turn counts −1. In classical topology two spinorial pieces are as spinorial as one, and the adopted spin-½ is the frame's sign, which every path has.) The parity rule does not conserve fermion number. An empty FS geon is well-posed but not unique, and no choice of one is principled without an energy; Literature found no published precedent. Your 6:40 PM speculation (very weakly held) that the empty FS geon might be the neutrino is recorded next to that discussion, and at 6:43 PM you asked us to say "empty" rather than "ground state".

This fits Geometry's #432 §3a (T-d) point that spinorial or not is a ℤ₂, so topology suggests the statistics class, not the spin magnitude.

- **(Q-a)** Is "fermion = FS geon + features" well-posed?
- **(Q-b)** Is a connected sum with a spinorial summand spinorial, and is that the right notion of "features"?
- **(Q-c)** What does the #434 parity rule (#437 §4, fit with #434) say for such composites?
- **(Q-d)** What does "no frame on empty edges" do to #431's cycles C1, C2 and L, and to the (F)/(W) question (#436 §4a)?

## 3a. Geometry's readings of (Q-a)–(Q-e) (review of 7408df7, reviewer reading, not a ruling)

- **Verdict.** The proposal is well-posed once "features" is pinned down ((Q-a)/(Q-b)). CL point 2 needed rewording (Q-d, applied in §2). "Empty FS geon" is well-posed, but no choice of one is principled (Q-e).
- **(Q-a) Well-posed?** Yes, given three things: read the particle's region as a compact orientable 3-manifold (the worldtube cross-section, capped off, as in #437); it has a unique prime decomposition (Kneser–Milnor); define the **core** as the spinorial prime summand and the **features** as the remaining prime summands. Without the prime decomposition, "features" is undefined.
- **(Q-b) Connected sums.** Yes: a closed orientable 3-manifold is non-spinorial iff every prime summand is non-spinorial (Geometry's source: Hendriks 1977, as used by Friedman–Witt; see §2 point 5 for the citations). So Poincaré # handle and Poincaré # L(p,q) are spinorial. It is the right notion of "features" only if the features are **tensorial** primes (lens spaces, S¹×S²): a tensorial summand adds no rotation sign, so spin comes from the core alone and the shadow-space Dirac box copies one sign (#437). A second spinorial summand is a different object: two fermions, not one fermion with a feature (P#P is still "spinorial" as a manifold, but in the representation where each spinorial summand's rotation is −1, the total 2π rotation is (−1)(−1) = +1; the geon spin–statistics point; Geometry's clause restored from comment 6203728299). So "spinorial manifold" ≠ "spin-½ particle": topology fixes only that a −1 is possible. [Claude 028, replacing "choosing it is David's 11:24 AM spin-½ adoption (#431 §3c)": that adoption is the spin lift of the carried frame (L161, L163; #432 §3a (T-d)), which every tube has whatever its topology, so it chooses no sign for a core's rotation class. Classically a second spinorial summand does not undo spinoriality either: "a rotation of Σ is isotopic to rotating all P_i's", and Σ is spinorial iff any of its primes is (Giulini, Int. J. Theor. Phys. 33, 913 (1994), Section 1), so P#P is spinorial, like P. "(−1)(−1) = +1" gives each summand's rotation the value −1, which picks a one-dimensional representation of the mapping class group: Friedman–Sorkin's quantum step, not classical topology. So "exactly one spinorial core" below is a definition of a fermion, not a consequence of the topology or of the adoption, and adopting it is David's call.] Geometry's suggested wording: **fermion = exactly one spinorial core plus any number of tensorial features**; different cores also give different species.
- **(Q-c) Parity rule.** dim H₁(ℤ₂) is additive over connected sum, so the #434 conserved quantity is the total count, mod 2, of odd-dim summands across the slice. That count mixes cores and features, so the parity rule does not conserve fermion number, even mod 2. Examples: Poincaré # handle, 0 + 1 = 1, constrained; Poincaré # L(3,1), 0, unconstrained; S³/Q₈ alone, dim 2, unconstrained. Species conservation still needs a postulate. [Claude 028 sketch, not checked against a source, for Geometry: when a core P is created alone from a geon-free slice (S³ → P, allowed, #434 §4), its 2π rotation relative to the lab extends over the creation region. Take a thin tube B³ × I round an arc from the past slice to P and rotate by 2π parallel to its boundary spheres. On P this is P's rotation; on S³ it is isotopic to the identity because S³ is not spinorial, so a collar isotopy makes it the identity on the past slice. The twisted and untwisted continuations are then one World, up to a diffeomorphism that fixes the past slice and a frame along the arc, so a core created alone carries no spinorial world data. This is the classical extension step of Dowker and Sorkin's U-tube argument (CQG 15, 1153 (1998)); only that step is used here, not their wave functions. For a core created with its partner they extend an internal diffeomorphism of one prime only together with the mirror one of the other, so the pair case stays open here.]
- **(Q-d) Empty edges.** A World's metric and spin structure exist everywhere, so parallel transport, and the holonomy of C1, C2 and L, is defined on every loop whether or not anything travels it. What an empty edge lacks is a carried object whose sign could register. On (F)/(W) this decides nothing but sharpens the choice: under (F), exactness is a property of the World's transport on all graph loops, with no carrier needed; under (W), exactness binds only loops a geon actually traces. R-geon makes (W) more natural ("no actors" means nothing tracks the sign on untraced loops) but does not force it. (Claude 028: that holds only if R-geon's sign is the core's rotation class; if it is the frame lift, untraced loops carry it too, as the first sentence of (Q-d) says.) Under (W), as in #436 §4a, the detector cycles drop out unless a geon traces them.
- **(Q-e) Empty FS geon.** Well-posed: a single spinorial prime with no other summands. Not unique, because there are many spinorial primes, so "empty" does not name one particle. Geometry's only natural candidate under an ordering is S³/Q₈ (smallest |π₁| among spinorial spherical forms, 8 vs the Poincaré sphere's 120; Matveev complexity 2; Geometry's recollection, checked by Literature in §3b). Even so it is not principled: ordering by group order or complexity is a choice, not dynamics; a principled choice needs an energy (an action and a scale), and none is adopted. Picking the ordering to get an answer would be a T2 concern. S³/Q₈ has dim H₁(ℤ₂) = 2, so the parity rule does not stop it appearing singly, another reason a species postulate is needed.

## 3b. Literature lookups (not candidate proposals)

- **S³/Q₈** (Literature lookups, answering Geometry's requests): Matveev complexity c = 2 (B. Martelli, "Complexity of 3-manifolds", in *Spaces of Kleinian Groups*, LMS Lecture Note Ser. 329, CUP 2006, doi:10.1017/CBO9781139106993.006, §3.4.1: the first non-lens elliptic manifold, c = 2, smallest non-cyclic π₁, order 8; Martelli names it by its Seifert invariants (S², (2,1), (2,1), (2,1), −1), and the only non-cyclic group of order 8 that acts freely on S³ is Q₈, Claude 028); spinorial (Giulini 1994, Table, S = +); H₁ = ℤ₂ × ℤ₂, so dim H₁(ℤ₂) = 2, even, and it is not in the Gibbons–Hawking parity (CMP 148, 345 (1992); #434 §3(c)).
- **"Simplest" is ambiguous.** By Matveev complexity or by |π₁|, S³/Q₈ comes first among spinorial primes; other measures (Heegaard genus, volume) are unchecked. Any ordering is a choice, not dynamics.
- **Precedent search.** No published work found that names an empty or simplest spinorial geon as a particle (search not exhaustive). Near misses: A. P. Balachandran and S. Vaidya, IJMPA 12 (1997), hep-th/9612053 (recounts Friedman–Sorkin's prime-as-excitation suggestion; names none); L. Smolin, "Fermions and topology", gr-qc/9404010 (fermionic statistics imposed on minimal wormholes, not a spinorial prime; names none).
