## Plain summary for David

Batch 31's weak point (Claude 021): nothing in the geometry yet makes two geons annihilate *only when their phases line up*. Topology lets any pair be capped off, and the known rules (metric, causality, spin structure, energy conditions) never look at the phase. This batch sets up the smallest concrete case where that could change:
- **The closure:** in 3D space, two wormhole handles (each an S¹×S² summand) appear together and later close off together. With a spin structure, and a compact topology-changing region with a Lorentzian metric and spacelike boundary, wormholes can only be made or removed in pairs (Gibbons–Hawking 1992: a single wormhole is impossible, pairs are kinematically possible; with "kinks" in the boundary the count can change), so a pair is the smallest allowed case. 2D space can't host this with a non-degenerate Lorentzian metric (Sorkin 1986; §1).
- **Where the CTCs sit:** by an argument (not a theorem), Geroch's CTCs can be confined to the moments where the topology changes. We put them at the capping vertex, where the pair closes off.
- **The question (reframed per Geometry):** With CTCs, a classical billiard ball's motion is required to be self-consistent (a principle stated by Friedman et al. 1990; EKT worked out what it implies for billiard balls). A little frame (a clock hand) carried round a CTC must likewise come back matching itself. The geometry already fixes how far the hand turns round any loop, so self-consistency cannot *force* that turn. What it can do is restrict the frame's own data, or rule out whole worlds in which no self-matching frame exists. The question is whether the worlds it rules out are exactly the ones where the pair's phases fail to line up, and whether that depends on the routes. Three cautions: a passive frame does not push back on anything, so unlike EKT's balls it gives no extra solutions unless it couples to something; a passive frame carried by the pair is not constrained at all, because a carrier that starts and ends at the vertices never meets itself in a way that constrains it, so the question is open only for a coupled frame; and the constraint reaches the closure loop only if that loop is itself a CTC, or is joined to one by a region whose geometry we know. A time direction defined everywhere does not forbid CTCs (Gödel's and Misner's spacetimes have one), but the closure loop is not one of them: it runs forward in time along one member and backward along the other, so it is not a causal curve at all. If yes, "they annihilate only if their phases line up" (batch 31's plain-summary wording, a Creative Lead paraphrase, not David's verbatim words) would come out of structure we already have plus the spin-½ candidate (on a closed worldline an ordinary vector hand can always match itself, §3), with no ħ and no rule for adding arrows. If no, the exclusion route is parked, like batch 23's Set 1.

Nothing is computed. Below are the setup, what has to be specified, and what would count as yes or no.

## 1. Setup [CL]

- **Object spacetime M:** a 3+1 Lorentzian manifold. Slices: Σ₀ (before) ≅ Σ₂ (after) ≅ some fixed 3-manifold (ℝ³ near the lab); Σ₁ (between) ≅ Σ₀ # (S¹×S²) # (S¹×S²).
- **Closure:** a compact cobordism K₀₁ from Σ₀ to Σ₁ (creation vertex v_c), a product region Σ₁ × [t₁, t₂] (members carried: batch 30 §5, as added by Geometry #418 B5), and K₁₂ from Σ₁ to Σ₂ (capping vertex v_a).
- **Why pairs:** in a compact region with a Lorentzian metric, spacelike boundary and a spin structure, single wormhole creation is impossible but pairs are possible; the selection rule is a mod-2 invariant of the closed boundary 3-manifold (the Kervaire semi-characteristic), so for our asymptotically ℝ³ slices it is applied to the one-point compactification, which is legitimate for a change confined to a compact region (Dowker, gr-qc/0206020 §2, text read by Claude 022: "a topology change from ℝ³ to ℝ³ with a handle – an S² × S¹ – attached can be reduced to the compact case because infinity in both cases is topologically the same"); wormholes plus kinks are conserved mod 2 (Gibbons–Hawking, CMP 148, 345 (1992); PRL 69, 1719 (1992); abstracts read by Claude 021).
- **Why 3+1:** in 2+1 a compact non-degenerate Lorentzian cobordism needs equal Euler characteristics at its two ends (Sorkin, PRD 33, 978 (1986); abstract read by Claude 021), and a handle changes a surface's by −2, so no handle can appear between connected slices. In 3+1 the condition is that the cobordism's own Euler characteristic vanishes, for spacelike ends (Reinhart, Topology 2, 173 (1963); text unchecked); Gibbons–Hawking's spin condition comes on top of this.
- **Shadow:** each vertex region is micro (inside an ℓ-ball) or outside U; between vertices the members separate macroscopically, one through each slit (batch 31 §7, R4), so the shadow is a loop or a source→detector path.

## 2. Where the CTCs sit (argument, not theorem) [Geo, per #419 A4]

- Geroch 1967: a time-oriented compact Lorentzian cobordism with spacelike boundary between non-diffeomorphic slices contains a CTC *somewhere*.
- Applied to thin slabs around each vertex, and since a product region with a timelike field transverse to the slices needs no CTCs, this **suggests** that the CTCs can be confined to the vertex slabs. That is an argument, not the theorem. The slabs are still spatially global; spatial localisation needs a causally compact version (Borde, gr-qc/9406053, abstract read only), or a localisation assumption. A sketch (Claude 022, unchecked): suppose that outside a compact set D the slab has a time function whose level sets are the slices. Then Geroch, applied to the slab compactified as in §1, gives a CTC; every CTC meets D, since outside D the time function increases along it; and a CTC can leave D only on an excursion that rises in time by less than D's own extent in time, so with D in an ℓ-ball every CTC stays within about ℓ of it. The same reasoning confines CTCs to the vertex slabs when the product region between them has a time function.
- **Placement for S7 [CL]:** take the CTCs at v_a (and v_c). Call the chronology-violating set C_a ⊂ K₁₂.
- **Costs:** with the field equations and the WEC, Tipler 1977 says this cannot occur on Geroch's compact spacetime, and within a finite region it must be singular; Borde (dimensions ≥ 3, causally compact) says it cannot satisfy Einstein's equation with a reasonable source even at the price of singularities. So the matter model, or the absence of one (test-field geometry only), must be stated. The analytic lock's status for K₁₂ is unchecked.

## 3. The carried frame and the loop holonomy [CL]

- Each member carries a frame e (a clock hand): a unit vector, or a spinor in the spin-½ candidate (L161; MTW, Gravitation, §41.9, p. 1157, ToC checked; figure number unchecked), parallel-transported along its worldline. Spin-½ needs a spin structure as world data (A1: the choices form a torsor over H¹(M; ℤ₂)).
- **Closure loop γ:** creation at v_c → member 1's route → v_a → member 2's route reversed → v_c. Its holonomy Hol(γ) is gauge-invariant (batch 31 N5) once the loop is fixed, and two choices fix it: the arcs that close γ inside the vertex regions K₀₁ and K₁₂, where the topology changes and the curvature is not small, so they can contribute at order one, and the curve taken in each member's world-tube (threading the member's own handle changes Hol(γ) by the holonomy round that handle). Between the vertex regions the routes run through the lab. Where the lab is flat and simply connected (to the note's accuracy only: under the lock a metric flat on an open set is flat everywhere, by the identity theorem (#410), which the curved vertex regions exclude; so the lab is only approximately flat, and Hol(γ) also depends on the routes, continuously and weakly, through the curvature they enclose (see (Y)); handles between the routes would add discrete dependence), parallel transport there does not depend on the path. So Hol(γ) is set by those micro data and by where, and in which directions, the members meet the vertex regions, not by the region between the routes. This is batch 23 Set 1's flat-cylinder objection (no x-dependent holonomy) again.
- What matters is the hand's turn round γ: mod 2π for a vector, mod 4π for spin-½. A generic element of SO⁺(1,3) is a boost in one timelike 2-plane combined with a rotation by an angle θ in the orthogonal spacelike 2-plane. So θ is defined, but it is the hand's turn only for a hand lying in that plane, which the holonomy picks; a pure boost or a null rotation has no turn. Along a closed worldline whose hand is kept orthogonal to it (Fermi–Walker transport, which is parallel transport on a geodesic), the holonomy fixes the 4-velocity and is a pure rotation. A vector hand then always has a self-consistent direction (the rotation axis, or any direction if θ ∈ 2πℤ), while a spinor has one only if θ ∈ 4πℤ (its lift has eigenvalues e^{±iθ/2}).

## 4. The question: does EKT-type self-consistency constrain Hol(γ)? [C021 S7, set up by CL]

- **EKT** (Echeverria–Klinkhammer–Thorne, Phys. Rev. D 44, 1077 (1991); abstract read by Literature, #421): a classical billiard ball entering a CTC region must have self-consistent motion; all "dangerous" initial trajectories in a wide class have infinitely many consistent solutions ("far too many solutions"), and none was found to have zero (EKT's abstract, re-read via INSPIRE by Claude 022: "A search is made for initial trajectories with zero multiplicity, and none are found").
- **Analogue:** a frame carried into C_a that meets its own future (via a CTC) must match itself: the transported frame e must equal its earlier self at the meeting point. That is a **fixed-point condition**: e must be fixed by Hol(c) for the CTC c through C_a (a vector needs eigenvalue 1 on a unit spacelike vector; a spinor needs eigenvalue 1 of the SL(2,ℂ) lift, which a lifted 2π rotation, −1, never has). Hol(c) is already fixed by the metric (#418 B3), so the condition constrains the frame's data, or excludes worlds whose Hol(c) has no admissible fixed point; it does not force Hol. For a passive parallel-transported frame there is no back-reaction, unlike EKT's colliding balls, so no multiplicity arises unless the frame couples to something. It also needs a member's worldline to traverse c, and for a passive frame carried by the members that cannot happen. A worldline that comes back to the same event with the same 4-velocity is, for deterministic motion, a closed orbit with no start or end, so it is not a member created at v_c and capped at v_a. A worldline that crosses its own past with a different 4-velocity imposes nothing on a passive frame. Inside K₁₂, where the summands are capped, a member's worldline is not defined. So for a passive frame (N1) holds by construction. This fits Friedman et al. (1990; abstract read by Claude 022), who "suspect" that weak constraints of this kind are "typical for noninteracting (linearly superposing), classical fields", while "interacting classical systems can behave quite differently". A constraint needs the frame to act on the motion or the geometry, and the documented outcome of such coupling (EKT) is multiplicity: Lemma A′'s multiplicity exit, which needs a counting rule (#399), not exclusion. It is not yet a condition on γ.
- **The link to γ:** homotopy is not enough. Under the lock M is curved, so freely homotopic loops generally have different holonomies (they differ by the curvature swept by the homotopy), and a shared deck element fixes only the linear (deck) part. The fixed-point condition restricts Hol(γ) only if the CTC is γ itself (up to reparametrisation), or if the holonomy of the region connecting them is known. (Two loops in a 4-manifold generically do not link, so "linking" is not available either.) In a time-oriented M the first case is empty: γ runs future-directed along member 1 and past-directed along member 2, so it is not a causal curve, and no CTC is γ. (γ can be timelike all the way round only if the time orientation reverses inside both vertex regions. That is the other alternative in Geroch's theorem, "CTC's or be time non-orientable" in Dowker's summary, gr-qc/0206020 §2; §5 item 1 excludes it, and an SL(2,ℂ) spin structure, which Gibbons–Hawking assume, needs a time orientation.) The second case moves the question to Hol(c) for loops c in C_a. These lie in the vertex region (§1 Shadow: micro or outside U), so their holonomy is set by the vertex geometry, and a condition on them is (N2) unless that geometry depends on how the members arrive. Find out, for the S¹×S² pair, whether it does.
- **Possible outcomes:**
  - (Y) Worlds whose Hol(γ) lies outside a subset (e.g. a spinor's turn trivial mod 4π; on a closed worldline a vector hand always has its rotation axis, §3) admit no self-consistent frame and so cannot cap off, and the subset depends on the members' routes. (Hol(γ) is world data, so this is exclusion of worlds, not dynamics forcing Hol.) Then "annihilate only if the phases line up" is derived. The subset must also have interior. If Hol(γ) varies continuously with the routes, an exact condition such as "turn ≡ 0 mod 4π" holds only on a set of routes of codimension at least one (isolated points or lines on the screen, not bands), and widening it to a tolerance would put a scale in by hand (LT2). Interior needs Hol(γ) to stay in the subset on an open set of routes, for example by being locally constant in them, as when it is set by discrete data such as which micro-handles each route threads. Under the lock Hol(γ) depends real-analytically on continuous route data, and a fixed-point condition is an algebraic subset of the group, so on a connected family of routes it holds either everywhere or only on a nowhere-dense set. The only escape is discrete data: the class of γ in H₁(M; ℤ) (which handles the routes enclose, carrying the deck part) and, for a spinor, the spin-structure sign on that class, an element of ℤ₂ fixed by the H¹(M; ℤ₂) choice. With §5 item 1's cancelling cap the closure is simply connected and these data are trivial; only the cap leaving π₁ = ℤ, or handles between the routes, leaves room. **Check against LT2:** the condition must be fixed before looking at fringes (batch 23 kill rule).
  - (N1) Self-consistency always has a solution, whatever Hol(γ) is (for balls, EKT found no trajectory with zero solutions; abstract quoted in the EKT bullet above). Then there is no constraint, and §7 is parked with Set 1. For a passive frame carried by the members, (N1) already holds (Analogue above).
  - (N2) The constraint exists but does not depend on the route (only on the vertex geometry). Then it is a filter, not a pattern, and is again parked.
- **Which-path:** a record on one member's route must lift the constraint (batch 23 kill rule). A passive record changes no geometry, so state what a record does here.
- **What is detected:** which object reaches d_i, and where each member's charge ends (#419 R4).

## 5. What must be specified before the question is well-posed [CL]

1. K₁₂ explicitly: a handle decomposition capping two S¹×S² summands, with a Lorentzian metric (or line field) and time orientation, so that Geroch applies. Topologically (Claude 022's derivation by standard handle calculus, unchecked against a source; slices compactified as in §1): the Euler-characteristic condition needs as many odd-index as even-index handles, so the smallest creation is one 1-handle and one 2-handle on an unknot. The spin structure needs that 2-handle's framing to be even (framing 0 gives the pair), while framing ±1 gives a single S¹×S² that is Lorentzian but not spin: Gibbons–Hawking's rule in handle language. The cap is then a 2-handle and a 3-handle, and it is not unique. A cap whose 2-handle cancels the 1-handle and whose 3-handle cancels the 2-handle makes the whole closure a product S³ × I, which is simply connected, so every loop, a CTC or γ, is null-homotopic; the reverse of the creation instead leaves π₁ = ℤ.
2. C_a: the CTC set inside K₁₂, and its homotopy classes.
3. The spin structure (or vector frame) on M and its restriction to γ.
4. The matter model (test frame only, or one obeying an energy condition).
5. The routes: one member through each slit, and the slit geometry; the arcs closing γ inside K₀₁ and K₁₂ and the curve in each member's tube (§3); and what curvature or topology lies between the two routes. Without the last, Hol(γ) sees the routes only through where and how they meet the vertex regions, so any dependence on the slits or on x must be shown to come through that, or the outcome is (N2).

## 6. Ill-posed questions to avoid

- "When does the pair annihilate?" without a time function (with CTCs there is none globally).
- "The phase of one member": only Hol(γ) is gauge-invariant, and only once γ's closing arcs and tube curves are fixed (§3).
- "Does the frame match itself?" before C_a and its loops are specified.
- "The probability of capping": μ stays open, and no weights are used.

## 8. Outcome and next step [CL; outcome per Physics Lead ruling, L167, not David's]

**Outcome (Physics Lead ruling, L167; not David's).** For S7 as posed, adopted structure does not yield a route-dependent "phases line up" capping condition. Under the conditions stated, the outcome is (N1)/(N2):
- **γ is not a CTC** (time-oriented M, both members future-directed between v_c and v_a). This is *not* because a time orientation forbids CTCs; it does not (Gödel's and Misner's spacetimes are time-oriented). It is because γ runs backward along member 2, so its tangent switches from the future cone to the past cone and γ is not a causal curve at all (Geometry, #421 X1). Only Geroch's non-orientable alternative could make γ timelike all the way round.
- **A passive frame gives (N1)** (deterministic carrier; a frame that does not act on the motion or the geometry; Claude 022 R2). With coupling, the documented lever is multiplicity, not exclusion.
- **The lab is only approximately flat.** Under the lock it cannot be exactly flat on an open set (identity theorem, #410), so Hol(γ) depends on the routes weakly and continuously, through the enclosed curvature (Geometry X2). In an exactly flat, simply connected lab it would be route-independent.
- **Dilemma** (Claude 022 S4; Geometry X3). Hol(γ) depends real-analytically on continuous route data, and a fixed-point condition is an algebraic subset of the group. So on a connected family of routes the condition holds everywhere or only on a nowhere-dense set, never in bands. Only discrete data threaded by the routes escapes: the class of γ in H₁(M; ℤ) and, for a spinor, the spin-structure sign on that class. Both are trivial with the cancelling cap (§5 item 1, a simply connected product); they need the cap that leaves π₁ = ℤ, or handles between the routes.
- **Still needed, and open:** the spin-½ candidate (L161, not adopted), since a vector hand on a closed worldline always matches itself (R4); the analytic lock's status for K₁₂; and the conditions in (Y) and §5 item 5.

**Open items, held for David via Physics Lead:** (1) what, if anything, couples the frame at v_a; (2) what curvature or topology lies between the routes. Whether to look at Geroch's non-orientable alternative is also a scope choice for them.
