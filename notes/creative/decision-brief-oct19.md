## 1. Should species be conserved by postulate?

**Plain explanation.** The question, a follow-up to #432 §3a (T-a) written up by Geometry (L182; you approved recording the note at 3:09 PM ET 2026-10-15, relayed via Geometry, not signed, paraphrase, approval of recording, not a ruling on content), is whether the geometry itself keeps count of fermion-like geons. Geometry's answer (#434) is no. The only rule the geometry supplies (Gibbons–Hawking) conserves a different number: how many prime pieces of space have an odd number of independent mod-2 loops (odd dim H₁(P; ℤ₂)), counted mod 2 (for a connected slice; with several separate pieces of space, their number also counts, #434 §3(c)). That number is unrelated to whether a piece is spinorial. A single spinorial piece (the Poincaré sphere P) can appear from an ordinary 3-sphere, P can vanish alone, and one P can split into two, all without kinks (#434 §4, §6). So if each species is to be conserved, that has to be added as a rule.

**Options and what they imply.**
- *(a) Add a species-conservation postulate* (each species conserved, P̄ counting −1; #432 §3a (T-b) already treats this as a postulate, not a result). Consequence: the spinorial count is then conserved mod 2 (#434 §6). Cost, recorded for you (#434 §7, Claude 027 S5, not adopted): a geon-free slice could only produce an even number of non-trivial primes, so the odd n-tuples in your 1:36 AM ET 2026-10-14 closure family (Creative Lead paraphrase, L153), such as a triple appearing together, would be excluded; nested splittings (1 → 3 → 1) would not. It is an added rule, so it carries a T2 risk (#434 §6).
- *(a′) Postulate only fermion parity:* the number of spinorial primes is conserved mod 2, but species are not. This is the other postulate #434 names ("species or fermion-parity conservation remains a postulate", L182), and it is the count #434 §1 asks about. Consequences: a geon-free slice can produce only an even number of spinorial primes, so an odd n-tuple of the 1:36 AM family is excluded only if an odd number of its members are spinorial; tensorial primes stay governed by the Gibbons–Hawking rule alone. A spinorial prime can still turn into another spinorial prime with the same parity of dim H₁(ℤ₂) (the Poincaré sphere into S³/Q₈, for example), and P can vanish together with such a prime instead of P̄. So it does not supply #432 §3a (T-a)'s match, which needs the species itself, or (T-b)'s "P cannot vanish without P̄", and #436's "P on X" keeps its fermion parity but not its species. It is also an added rule, with the same T2 risk (Claude 029's derivation; an option for David, not adopted).
- *(b) No postulate.* The geometry then conserves only the odd-dim H₁(ℤ₂) total. #432 §3a (T-b)'s "P cannot vanish without P̄" would not hold (#434 §6), and any record or bookkeeping that relies on a particle persisting (for example #436's "P on X" record, which lasts only under the postulate, #436 §4a (V-c)) loses that support. (A Gauss-flux record written on a detector-fixed exit through the throat persists by charge conservation alone, not by species conservation: Geometry workstream (A), #438 comment 6272583462.)
- *(c) Look for a physical restriction that yields it* (for example only pair-creation cobordisms, or a link between the frame's spin structure and geon spinoriality). A restriction is an added rule too, with the same T2 risk, unless it is independently motivated (#434 §6), and a spin-structure link could at most give fermion parity, not species. This is #434 §7's open item; nothing has been found. Kinks only relax the rule, and Dowker–Sorkin's result is quantum and gives a spin–statistics correlation, not a conservation law (#434 §6, Claude 027 R2).

**Source.** #434 §6 (Verdict, "The program's class"), §7 (open items, incl. "Cost of a species postulate"); Claude 027 R2, S5; #432 §3a (T-b). **No joint recommendation.**

## 2. Which ℤ₂ is the fermion's −1?

**Plain explanation.** There are two different yes/no signs in play. One is the frame's spin lift: the World's spin structure gives every loop a ±1, whatever the loop carries; this is the sign exact closure uses (#431 §3a–§3c). The other is a spinorial core's own class: whether rotating the core by 2π can be undone, a property of the slice (its mapping class group), not of loops. Under the reading where the fermion is an FS geon (R-geon, #437 §4), one must say which of these is "the fermion's −1". How the two relate is an open question (#434 §7). Geometry adds (Geo-G3, #437 §4) that the Dirac box in shadow space has the frame lift's −1 by construction, so "the Dirac box copies the geon's sign" holds only if, far from the geon, the shadow map sends the core's class to the frame lift.

Your naming, as recorded (5:37 PM ET 2026-10-15, direct in Creative Lead chat, not signed; verbatim as reported by Creative Lead; unverified by Literature, no original seen; no hold level stated): "Yes, that is what I meant. FS geon names the world object, and Dirac box names its shadow-space stand-in." (#437 §1.) The naming does not by itself choose between the options below. (#437 §2's CL observation says this of R-geon versus R-frame; it does not address the choice below.)

**Options and what they imply.**
- *(a) The frame lift.* Fits what exact closure already uses and the Dirac box's sign. But the core then supplies a label, not the sign (#437 §4, Claude 028 R1), and every tube has this lift whatever its topology, so the adopted spin-½ does not single out cores (#438 §3a (Q-b)). Under this choice untraced loops carry the sign too, which bears on (F) vs (W) (#438, Claude 028 S2; call 5).
- *(b) The core's 2π class.* Makes the fermion's sign topological, as R-geon intends, and is classical (Giulini, Quantum Gravity (Birkhäuser), §3, as cited in #437 §4). Cost: the World's spin structure still adds a ±1 on every loop, so there are two signs to reconcile, and the Dirac box copies the geon's sign only if the far-field shadow map sends the core's class to the frame lift, which is #434 §7's open item (Geo-G3). Exact closure is a holonomy condition on loops, so it does not see the core's class (#432 §3a (T-a)); the closure rule and the content of the 11:24 AM adoption would then need restating, which is your call (#437 §4, Claude 028 R1). And a core created alone from a geon-free slice carries no spinorial world data, because its 2π rotation extends over its creation region (#438 §3a (Q-c): Claude 028's sketch as corrected by Geometry, for Geometry to check; the pair case is open). Single creation is allowed unless a postulate forbids it (call 1 (a) or (a′); #434 §4), so in those worlds this option has no world-data sign to use.
- *(c) Defer:* keep both as candidates until #434 §7's relation is worked out. (The frame lift's value on a loop that is non-zero in H₁(M; ℤ₂) is world data, #423. Whether the core's class is world data is open, and for a core created alone it is not, by #438 §3a (Q-c)'s sketch, which Geometry has yet to check.) R-geon and R-frame stay "two readings, not picked" (#437 §4, L184).

**Source.** #437 §4 (R-geon) with Claude 028 R1 and Geo-G3 (Geometry comment https://github.com/nous-clawds4/physics/pull/438#issuecomment-6206123511); #434 §7, last item; L184 (OPEN for David). **No joint recommendation.** (Geometry states this item stays open and makes no ruling, comment 6206123511.)

## 3. Is "exactly one spinorial core" the definition of a fermion?

**Plain explanation.** Following your 6:32 PM ET 2026-10-15 inclination that the particle *is* the FS geon (#438 §1; direct in Creative Lead chat, not signed; paraphrased here, verbatim in #438 §1 as reported by Creative Lead; unverified by Literature, no original seen; an inclination, not a ruling; no hold level stated), Geometry suggested the wording "fermion = exactly one spinorial core plus any number of tensorial features" (#438 §3a (Q-b)). Classically, though, a piece of space with two spinorial cores (P#P) is still spinorial, just as P is (Giulini, IJTP 33, 913 (1994), §1, cited in #438 §3a). The statement "(−1)(−1) = +1", which would make two cores behave as a non-fermion, assigns each core's rotation the value −1. That is a quantization (sector) choice of the kind Friedman–Sorkin opened, not classical topology (#438 §3a (Q-b), Claude 028 R1, Literature J2). So "exactly one core" would be a definition, not a consequence of the topology or of the spin-½ adoption.

**Options and what they imply.**
- *(a) Adopt it as a definition.* Gives a species picture: one spinorial core decides "fermion", tensorial features (lens spaces, S¹×S²) distinguish species, and two cores count as two fermions (#438 §3a (Q-b)). Cost: it imports a sector choice from the quantum side into a classical model, so it should be labelled as a choice; and it interacts with call 2 (the spin-½ adoption is the frame lift and does not supply it, Claude 028 R1).
- *(b) Do not adopt; keep the classical statement only* (a spinorial slice can carry a −1; how many cores make a fermion is left open). Nothing further is fixed, and #438's species construction stays a CL proposal.
- *(c) Decide after call 2:* if the fermion's −1 is the frame lift (call 2(a)), the core count fixes only a label, not the sign; if it is the core's class (call 2(b)), this definition becomes central.

**Source.** #438 §3a (Q-b), Claude 028 R1, Lit-J2; L185 (OPEN for David). **No joint recommendation.** (Geometry, comment 6206123511: "exactly one core" is a definition, "That's all fine"; this agrees on the description, not a recommendation to adopt.)

## 5. Under (W), where does batch 23's exclusion live?

**Plain explanation, with the (F) vs (W) choice as context.** Once the detector loop is no longer a virtual pair, there are two readings of which loops count (#436 §4a (V-a)). Under **(F)**, every edge is a transport path whether or not anything travels it, so all three detector cycles remain and must close exactly; but nothing now supplies the extra turn (the −1) that L used to give. Under **(W)**, a loop exists only if a particle runs round it, so the detector has no cycles left in a detection world and asks nothing of the routes. Claude 028's consequence for (W): since a no-which-way record then removes the detector cycles as fully as a which-route record would, the exclusion behind any dark spot cannot sit in them; batch 23's kill rule (batch 23's term) would have to be met by virtual pairs away from the detector (#428 §5, last item). Claude marks these "questions for David, not results".

**Options and what they imply.**
- *(F)* Keeps the detector's closure demands, but leaves none of the three detector cycles with an expected value: L's −1 is gone, and C1's and C2's followed from it (#436 §4a (F), Claude 028 R1; David's 11:17 AM ET 2026-10-15 ruling: a loop expected to pick up −1 must get exactly −1 within a world; "exact means equal to each loop's expected value" is Creative Lead's reading, #431 §3b). So the dark-spot exclusion can sit in the detector cycles only once something supplies those values. The routes' mismatch still has to be compensated by mechanisms not yet shown (#428 §6a(c)2). The empty route's curve must also be specified (#432 §3a (T-c)).
- *(F) with a funnel exit* (Geometry workstream (A), #438 comment 6272583462, proposal, not adopted): the route chain bypasses L through a detector-fixed exit, so L stays a virtual pair and keeps its expected −1, and the record is a flux write on the exit. Cost: a new cycle C3 with no expected value; the model must say that L and the route chain sharing the vertices a and b is not an interaction (Geometry's well-posedness item); the funnel is detector design, not a law; #427's weight gap is untouched. Because L stays virtual, L "becoming real" is not the record here, so under this option call 4 no longer bears on the record. It is now a Geometry draft note, #444 (detector-funnel-record.md, not adopted), with a Literature pass at https://github.com/nous-clawds4/physics/pull/444#issuecomment-6272868316. Geometry also flags that a virtual pair threading the throat leaves flux, so #428 §5's "a virtual pair writes no entry anywhere" (#444 paraphrases it as "writes none") needs a qualifier (proposed text in #445, not applied).
- *(W), with the exclusion in virtual pairs away from the detector.* Removes the detector's demands, but a detection world then has one carried route, not a pair, while the adopted proposal makes every detection a pairing of two routes (#428 §2 item 1), a tension to resolve. It also requires a model of where those distant virtual pairs are and why they produce the dark spots.
- *(W), but a no-which-way record may not remove loops the way a which-route record does* (#436 §4a, "a question for David"). This needs a rule saying why, which is not yet written.
- Related: call 2(a) (the frame lift as the fermion's −1) puts a sign on untraced loops too, which weakens #438 (Q-d)'s lean towards (W) (Claude 028 S2 on #438).

**Plain explanation.** v_a is the vertex where a closed pair's two members meet and the pair is capped (#421 §1, batch 32). **Options and what they imply** (#427 §2 and §4).
- *(a) Pursue S4.* If a coupling at v_a made exact alignment hold on an open set of routes, closure worlds would meet no extra conditions, and ambient genericity would no longer give them zero weight (exact alignment on an open set of routes, not on all; Geometry B3, https://github.com/nous-clawds4/physics/pull/442#issuecomment-6274284788). A coupling at v_a is new dynamics not in the lock: it must be motivated independently and not chosen to force alignment, or it fails T2 (Geometry); it would also be a route to deriving the exclusion rule from the geometry (#427 open items; Claude 023 R5). #428's detector gluing raises the same coupling question in the detector's matter (#428, Claude 024). Recorded caution (#421 §4): "the documented outcome of such coupling (EKT) is multiplicity: Lemma A′'s multiplicity exit, which needs a counting rule (#399), not exclusion."
- *(b) Do not pursue it now.* #427's answer ("exact closure is a law, not a coincidence") stands without it for the "invalid" half; per Claude 023, record that the pairing picture shows cancellation only for a μ that weights a codimension ≥ 1 set of worlds. μ stays open.

## 7. λ_E and Rec₀

**Found in the repo.** Both come from the low-energy approximation (LEA) work: batch 26 (#410), the LEA consolidation (#417), batches 28–30 (#413, #415, #418).

**λ_E (explicitly your call).** λ_E is the particle's dynamical resolution scale in the LEA's energy window ℓ ≲ λ_E ≪ L (ℓ the micro-handle scale, L the lab scale): "low" energy lets the coarse flat description apply to the apparatus; "not too low" keeps the particle coupled to the microstructure (#410 batch-26.md, condition 7). It is a placeholder: a free test particle follows the same geodesic at every energy, so λ_E needs a length from the classical particle model (its size, or the range of a self-field), and reading it as a de Broglie wavelength would put ħ in by hand (#410; #417 §4, Claude 018 R5). All runs so far use a point particle, which is the λ_E ≲ ℓ case (#417; #415). Batch 30 (#418) lists candidate particles with their natural λ_E: a point test body (none), an extended lump or geon (its size), a handle (its mouth size), a record-defined particle (the record's resolution), and a class of world-tubes (the tube width set by τ and ε); its next step 1 was "Ask David to choose λ_E and the particle candidate (suggest (c) with (e) for identity)", a CL suggestion, not a joint recommendation and not adopted ((c) is the carried handle or topological defect, (e) the world-tube class used for identity across worlds). Choosing λ_E means choosing a particle model; a particle with a length must re-derive the point-particle results (#417 §9 item 2). Claude 028's bracket on the clock-rate item in #437 §5 says the same ("choosing one, like λ_E, is David's call"; Claude's reading).

**Rec₀ (open; not marked as your call in the repo).** Rec₀ is "the observer's prior record": the worlds that count are those "compatible with Rec₀" (#410, renamed from 𝓡₀). It "must be defined; at least, the lab bodies' images match the reference configuration within τ/2 and the diaries hold Rec₀", and the definition must say which data may differ between worlds and which Rec₀ fixes (#410). In the toys it is modelled only as "direction to τ/R, offset free below ℓ" (#413 batch 28, open item 3), and it sets the upper edge of batch 29's ballistic window (#415; #417 §9 item 1, "Rec₀. Not defined."). If Rec₀, or the particle model (λ_E), fixed the launch below ℓ, batch 28's finding would change (#413).

**Options and what they imply.** For λ_E: choose a particle model from #418's list (which fixes λ_E and the action it carries), or take a classical scale that brings in no ħ (for example the mass's gravitational length Gm/c², or a geon's own size; Geometry B4, https://github.com/nous-clawds4/physics/pull/442#issuecomment-6274284788, adapting Claude 028's rate scales in #437 §5), which must not be tuned to the de Broglie or Compton value (for an electron's mass Gm/c² is about 7 × 10⁻⁵⁸ m, so it would put elementary particles in the λ_E ≲ ℓ case unless ℓ is smaller still; Claude 029); or keep the point particle (λ_E ≲ ℓ). Keeping it means the particle model stays outside the LEA: λ_E ≲ ℓ is the first failure case of condition 7 (#410 batch-26.md, ill-posed list item 7; #417 §5: "The geometry is within the LEA; the particle model is not."). A particle with a length has two further costs: #415's two-sided window "also needs a particle that resolves the throat (λ_E ≲ ℓ)" (Claude 020 S6, #417 §9 item 7), and for a lump with λ_E ≫ ℓ, tidal splitting or smoothing may remove the O(1) deflection (paraphrase of #418 batch-30.md §3 (b), whose wording uses a term the standing rule keeps out of new text). For Rec₀: the repo records it as an open definition, not as a choice put to you; defining it fixes which launch data are free across worlds and so moves the window's upper edge; fixing the offset below the sliver widths would close the window (#415 batch-29.md item 7).

**Source.** #410 batch-26.md (plain summary, condition 7, §W definition); #417 lea-consolidated.md (§4, §9 items 1–2); #413 batch-28.md open item 3; #415 batch-29.md; #418 batch-30.md (candidates table, §7 step 1). **No joint recommendation.**
