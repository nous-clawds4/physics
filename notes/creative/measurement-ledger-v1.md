## 1. For David (one page)

**The picture.** Inside any one world, free fall is deterministic and never forks. The observer can't tell at first which world it's in, because many worlds look exactly alike near it. It sends out a signal (the beacon). The signal goes round a nearby handle or geon and comes back. What comes back depends on the world, so each reply rules some worlds out. **That narrowing is the fork**, and a fork is the discovery of which world you're in (your 3:19 PM and 11:42 PM points, in paraphrase).

**Where the difference sits.** Under the "analytic world" rule that we keep, everything near the observer, and everything you reach by following space outward, is the same in every compatible world, including the far side of a nearby handle. The only thing a reply can tell apart is **how the handle is glued back on itself**: with a twist, a time shift, or a boost. (Past a time-loop horizon the world can also go on in more than one way, but that shows only in whether the observer gets through, not in any reply.) That gluing is the ledger state. It was there from the start; nothing has to write it.

**Reading it.** The cleanest example is Misner space, a standard flat toy model of a time-loop horizon, which we use as a model of the time-loop region around a geon. It is flat space glued with a boost. A light signal sent round it comes back blueshifted by an amount that tells you the boost. That works until shortly before the time-loop horizon, while the wrap is still wider than the observer. This reading uses no time loop: the signal goes round the handle, not through the particle's time loop of your 4:17 PM picture.

**Persistence.** The gluing never changes, so a new signal can read it again at any time while reading is possible. Separately, a diary of stored replies can keep entries well or badly. Better diaries keep more entries; losing some is not fatal (your 10:37 PM point).

**What's open.** A generic handle allows only isolated gluings, often just one. The exact examples we have are symmetric, and those allow a continuum, so there is no counting there yet. Everything here treats the observer and its signals as test bodies, whose own gravity is ignored; with their gravity included, worlds that look alike near the observer could not send back replies that differ in timing, direction or frequency. Writing to matter next to the handles (#388) is exact only in the same limit. Probabilities are deliberately not touched.

## 3. The main line

**Terms.** τ is the observer's proper time and γ its worldline. The germ is the observer's oriented metric germ at τ=0, modulo isotropy (#324). The tube is the observer's world tube of radius ρ (with a diary, the observer-plus-diary tube, L47; this ρ is not #372's record ρ(τ)); it injects if every non-trivial deck element moves each point of the worldline by more than 2ρ (#394). "Nearby" means only that a round trip fits inside the read-out window (#398). A loop is Type O if carrying a time orientation once round it brings it back unchanged, and Type F if it comes back flipped (#372 §2.3). S is a Cauchy slice before the geon forms, D(S) its domain of dependence and $H^+(S)$ its future Cauchy horizon (#372 §2.4). The ledger state is the gluing, a world datum that is never written; the diary is the append-only store of replies (#388). The test-field limit neglects the stress-energy of the named bodies in Einstein's equations.

- **Analytic lock** (kept; Lead ruling after David's 9:28 PM answer, #388). The germ fixes the simply connected analytic development along every path (#393 L65). Compatible worlds differ only by the deck group Γ and the extension past Cauchy horizons (#370). Here a **world** is a spacetime $W=\tilde M_{\rm ext}/\Gamma$, with $\tilde M_{\rm ext}$ an extension of that development, together with the observer's own test bodies (observer, beacon, diary) and no other test matter, and it is **compatible** if it contains the observer's germ at τ=0 with the tube injecting. Test bodies are not analytic data, so the germ does not fix them; if worlds could also differ in test matter outside the tube, an outside emitter could mimic any reply, and no reply would rule a gluing out.
- **Host.** Geon/handle hosts (David 4:06 PM on geons; 4:16 PM allows CTCs and non-time-orientability there as exploration only; "main line stays geon/handle hosts" is a Lead ruling, #393). World-dependence sits in the **gluing isometry of a nearby handle**: the deck element closing it, an isometry of the fixed cover acting freely and properly discontinuously, with the tube injecting (#396 L70, #398 F1).
- **Measurement = beacon round trip** (David 4:17, 4:28, 4:37 PM; #372 §2.2). As used here, a round trip is a signal from $\gamma(t_e)$ back to $\gamma(s)$, with γ the observer's worldline by proper time, whose loop with γ goes round the handle (deck element $g\ne1$) and is Type O. Unlike #372 §2.2, it need not pass through a CTC region, and the read-outs below use none (#393 Set 3; #396 Set 2). **Lead ruling (Physics Lead):** the exact read-outs use no CTC, so they are a *handle round trip* that generalizes #372's CTC round trip, not an instance of it. David's 4:17 PM CTC round trip stays the main-line target; the gap is open problem 6 (§4). The replies then differ because the worlds differ, not through EKT multiplicity, so a light signal suffices. #388's interacting pulse is needed only for round trips through a CTC region; where the geon forms after a slice S, those replies still arrive only beyond $H^+(S)$ (#372 §2.4). Only Type O returns read or write (#364, #381). A time-reversing gluing gives Type F, a correlation only (#396).
- **Read-out.** Misner: in the Milne wedge $\{T<-|X|\}/\langle B_\beta\rangle$ ($B_\beta$ a boost of rapidity β), with Milne time $t=\sqrt{T^2-X^2}$ falling to 0 at the horizon, a co-moving reader's signal sent at $t_1$ makes its $n$-th return at $t_1e^{-n\beta}$, blueshifted by $e^{n\beta}$; so the first return of each image is blueshifted by $e^{\beta}$. The world data is the boost with its centre and axis; Doppler is the clean read-out. Valid only strictly before the horizon, while the tube injects ($t\sinh(\beta/2)>\rho$ for a co-moving reader; $\beta t>2\rho$ is the slightly stronger arc-length form); the delay needs the boost centre; the Doppler read-out does not need co-moving readers: a ray that returns via $B_\beta^{m}$ comes back with frequency ratio $e^{\pm m\beta}$ whatever the reader's velocity in the boost plane (null directions are eigenvectors of the boost), and in the contracting wedge only the blueshift occurs, so readers that later cross into an extension read β too; co-moving readers follow the fixed-point/incomplete geodesics of #375, which are incomplete in every union, as are Taub–NUT's p∥=0 geodesics (Hájíček 1971's reported complete extension: unchecked and unreconciled, #372 §2.5), so non-Hausdorff extensions stay parked (#396 L71; #397 M3/M4; #398 F3/F4).
- **Branching.** The record is #372's reception history (per reply: arrival time, emission stamp, direction, deck element), which only grows (#372 §2.1; David's 3:53 PM memory working assumption, ledger.md "Working assumptions for exploration": "Memory: yes, let’s run with that."). Its physical store is the diary, exact only in the test-field limit and only as far as its entries persist (§4, items 2 and 4). A branch is the record class among worlds reaching τ: many worlds per class, refining as replies arrive, nested and never crossing (#372 §2.6; #393 L67). Nesting holds for the reception history, not for the diary: if a later charge reverses a written slot, the slot reads as unwritten, and two classes read off the diary merge. Under the lock the observer's worldline germ is shared, and the fork is in the record (#372 §2.6, from job 013's R2). **Fork = discovery** of Γ. The extension past a Cauchy horizon is not found by any reply: it shows only in whether the observer survives, a nested prefix, and dropout is not an arm (#372 §2.5–2.6; #396 Set 3).
- **Before the horizon.** Γ is present on S, so replies that fit in D(S) read it before $H^+(S)$ (#398 F9); if the handle cannot be traversed inside D(S), replies arrive only beyond $H^+(S)$ (#364 (d)).
- **Exact proof of concept.** Flat lattice quotients give one observer state with many exact ledger states, no ledger matter, and no CTCs (#393 L66). The observer and flashes are test bodies (otherwise their own gravity tells lattices apart), and flashes are isotropic. Compatible worlds include moving lattices (9 parameters) and the other flat quotients; rotated or mirrored returns occur only in non-torus quotients. The read-out is the ghost-image signature of cosmic topology (Lachièze-Rey–Luminet 1995; Cornish–Spergel–Starkman 1998; per #395, not verified by us). Uniform flux is fixed by germ+Γ. Here G3 means re-readable.

## 4. Open problems

1. **Symmetry vs uncountability.** Generic handles have isolated gluings or a unique one, so they are discrete but have no known exact example. The exact examples (flat, Misner) are symmetric, and their gluings form a continuum, so they are uncountable (#398 F7/F8; #394 F8).
2. **Test-field limit.** Under the lock, $T_{ab}=G_{ab}/8\pi$ is fixed on the tube. Written records (matter or flux next to handles, #388) are exact only in the test-field limit. Pre-existing gluings avoid this for the ledger state itself, since nothing is written (#393), but not for the read-out: a reply whose arrival time, direction or Doppler differs between worlds brings world-dependent energy onto the tube, which the lock forbids, so the beacon and its replies are test fields too, and so is the observer (#393 L66 for lattices). The lock also forces this for any localized body surrounded by vacuum, since an analytic $T_{ab}$ that vanishes on an open set vanishes everywhere.
3. **Read-out window.** Reading needs the tube to inject ($t\sinh(\beta/2)>\rho$); re-readability holds only within that window (#398 F10). In Misner the window closes before the reader's proper time runs out, so under #390's grades the gluing is G1 there (every entry correct on the whole window), not G3; G3 holds where there is no window, as in the flat quotients.
4. **Persistence grades** (#390). The persistence of a written entry is defined over the interval $[t_i,t']$ with a fixed, gauge-invariant read-out. Designs are compared by a preorder on persistence sets; grades G0–G3; losses are downgrades, not fatal. Persistence is judged per world, requires a read-out that, at every time in the interval, is correct and separates the alternatives written at $t_i$ (for each alternative, some world agreeing up to $t_i$ wrote it and is read correctly), and takes the reference, slot and mouth labels from the record itself (mouth re-identification included). Designs are compared on the same writes, and G3⊂G2⊂G1, with G0 = no entry ever persists, G1 = some entry persists on some positive interval, G2 = some entry persists for the rest of the observer's proper-time domain, and G3 = every entry does. The handle-flux slot is G2 in the test-field limit, in worlds where no later charge crosses that mouth's throat: flux changes by ±q per signed passage (A +1, B −1, graze 0, read as a difference from the reference). The Gauss surface must enclose exactly one mouth, with no charge between it and the throat when it is read. Every charge crossing the throat counts, and only throat charge changes the flux. The flux is an integer only as a difference with one carrier species. The threading orbit is unstable (trace 2(1+L/a) per passage), and stabilization by the mouths' gravity is open. A Hadley-type carrier reads −q.
5. **Carried from #372.** Round-trip open points (a)–(d) for round trips through a CTC region (the read-outs of §3 settle all four: light suffices, no region G is needed, $g\ne1$, and $s>t_e$), Hadley hosts (#372 §4 item 10), and counting as a labelled conjecture only (the uncountable families need an extra input). A static time-shift gluing has no CTCs if the shift is below the outside light time between the mouths, and CTCs at all times if it is above (then a reply can arrive before it was sent, #372 §2.2 (d)), so modelling Morris–Thorne–Yurtsever-style formation needs a growing shift.

6. **Handle vs CTC round trip (Lead ruling).** The exact read-outs (flat quotients, Misner before the horizon) are handle round trips with no CTC. An exact read-out through a geon's CTC region, David's 4:17 PM target, is still missing (#372 §2.2 (a)–(d); #388 needs an interacting pulse there).

## 5. Next: two-slit (future work, not argued)

A toy with two routes for the beacon (e.g. round either side of a handle) and **no record** of which route was taken. Three things must be fixed first. (i) A classical wave beacon takes both routes in every world, while a test-particle beacon takes the one route its launch fixes; say which. (ii) #372's record entry includes the deck element $g$, so two routes with different $g$ are recorded by definition; "no record" then needs routes with the same entry (arrival time, direction, $g$) or a coarser record, named as such. (iii) Any other bodies the toy needs (source, slits) are test matter and must be added to the definition of a world in §3. The question for later is what the reply class looks like when the routes are not separately recorded. Framed only; nothing is claimed here.
