## 1. For David (one page)

**The picture.** Inside any one world, free fall is deterministic and never forks. The observer can't tell at first which world it's in, because many worlds look exactly alike near it. It sends out a signal (the beacon). The signal goes round a nearby handle or geon and comes back. What comes back depends on the world, so each reply rules some worlds out. **That narrowing is the fork**, and a fork is the discovery of which world you're in (your 3:19 PM and 11:42 PM points, in paraphrase).

**Where the difference sits.** Under the "analytic world" rule that we keep, everything near the observer, and everything you reach by following space outward, is the same in every compatible world, including the far side of a nearby handle. The only thing that can differ is **how the handle is glued back on itself**: with a twist, a time shift, or a boost. That gluing is the ledger state. It was there from the start; nothing has to write it.

**Reading it.** The cleanest example is Misner space, the standard model of the time-loop region around a geon. It is flat space glued with a boost. A light signal sent round it comes back blueshifted by an amount that tells you the boost. That works until shortly before the time-loop horizon, while the wrap is still wider than the observer.

**Persistence.** The gluing never changes, so a new signal can read it again at any time while reading is possible. Separately, a diary of stored replies can keep entries well or badly. Better ledgers keep more entries; losing some is not fatal (your 10:37 PM point).

**What's open.** A generic handle allows only isolated gluings, often just one. The exact examples we have are symmetric, and those allow a continuum, so there is no counting there yet. Writing to matter next to the handles (#388) is exact only in the test-field limit. Probabilities are deliberately not touched.

## 3. The main line

- **Analytic lock** (kept; Lead ruling after David's 9:28 PM answer, #388). The germ fixes the simply connected analytic development along every path (#393 L65). Compatible worlds differ only by the deck group Γ and the extension past Cauchy horizons (#370).
- **Host.** Geon/handle hosts (David 4:06 PM on geons; 4:16 PM allows CTCs and non-time-orientability there as exploration only; "main line stays geon/handle hosts" is a Lead ruling, #393). World-dependence sits in the **gluing isometry of a nearby handle**: the deck element closing it, an isometry of the fixed cover such that the quotient is free and the tube injects (#396 L70, #398 F1).
- **Measurement = beacon round trip** (David 4:17, 4:28, 4:37 PM; #372 §2.2). Only Type O returns read or write (#364, #381). A time-reversing gluing gives Type F, a correlation only (#396).
- **Read-out.** Misner: in the Milne wedge $\{T<-|X|\}/\langle B_\beta\rangle$, returns arrive at $t_1e^{-n\beta}$, blueshifted by $e^{\beta}$. The world data is the boost with its centre and axis; Doppler is the clean read-out. Valid only strictly before the horizon, while $\beta t>2\rho$; the delay needs the boost centre; readers co-moving into the horizon follow the fixed-point/incomplete geodesics of #375, which are incomplete in every union, as are Taub–NUT's p∥=0 geodesics (Hajicek unreconciled), so non-Hausdorff extensions stay parked (#396 L71; #397 M3/M4; #398 F3/F4).
- **Branching.** The record grows (#372 §2.1). A branch is the record class among worlds reaching τ: many worlds per class, refining as replies arrive, nested and never crossing (#372 §2.6; #393 L67). Under the lock the observer's worldline germ is shared, and the fork is in the record (#372 R2). **Fork = discovery.**
- **Before the horizon.** Γ is present on S, so replies that fit in D(S) read it before $H^+(S)$ (#398 F9).
- **Exact proof of concept.** Flat lattice quotients give one observer state with many exact ledger states, no ledger matter, and no CTCs (#393 L66). The observer and flashes are test bodies (otherwise their own gravity tells lattices apart), and flashes are isotropic. Compatible worlds include moving lattices (9 parameters) and the other flat quotients; rotated returns occur only in non-torus quotients. Uniform flux is fixed by germ+Γ. Here G3 means re-readable.

## 4. Open problems

1. **Symmetry vs uncountability.** Generic handles have isolated gluings or a unique one, so they are discrete but have no known exact example. The exact examples (flat, Misner) are symmetric, and their gluings form a continuum, so they are uncountable (#398 F7/F8; #394 F8).
2. **Test-field limit.** Under the lock, $T_{ab}=G_{ab}/8\pi$ is fixed on the tube. Written records (matter or flux next to handles, #388) are exact only in the test-field limit. Pre-existing gluings avoid this (#393).
3. **Read-out window.** Reading needs $\beta t>2\rho$; G3 re-readability holds only within that window (#398 F10).
4. **Persistence grades** (#390). The persistence of a written entry is defined over the interval $[t_i,t']$ with a fixed, gauge-invariant read-out. Designs are compared by a preorder on persistence sets; grades G0–G3; losses are downgrades, not fatal. Persistence is judged per world, requires a correct read-out over the whole interval, and takes the reference, slot and mouth labels from the record itself (mouth re-identification included). Designs are compared on the same writes, and G3⊂G2⊂G1, with G1 = correct on some positive interval. The handle-flux slot is G2 in the test-field limit: flux changes by ±q per signed passage (A +1, B −1, graze 0, read as a difference from the reference). The Gauss surface must enclose exactly one mouth. Every charge crossing the throat counts, and only throat charge changes the flux. The flux is an integer only as a difference with one carrier species. The threading orbit is unstable (trace 2(1+L/a) per passage), and stabilization by the mouths' gravity is open. A Hadley-type carrier reads −q.
5. **Carried from #372.** Round-trip open points (a)–(d), Hadley hosts (OP10), and counting as a labelled conjecture only (the uncountable families need an extra input). A static time-shift gluing has CTCs at all times, so modelling MTY-style formation needs a growing shift.
