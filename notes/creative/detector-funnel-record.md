## Plain summary (for David)

Before, every design that recorded the detection cost the detector loop L its virtual status. Geometry's lemma says why: with a conserved unit charge and at most one particle per edge, no virtual loop can share an edge with the route chain. Geometry's candidate routes the chain around L instead, through a detector-fixed funnel a→x, b→x, then an exit X through the fixed handle's throat; the Gauss flux write of the charge q on X is the same in both route worlds (provided the routes and the edges into x do not cross the throat surface; Claude 030) and reads presence only, so L stays virtual and keeps its expected −1. This holds under (F) only, and only if the routes and L's members pass through a and b without interacting, a modelling choice like the one-particle-per-edge rule (Claude 030). The costs: a new cycle C3 with no expected value, which must still close exactly in every world, a second holonomy condition beside C1's (Claude 030); under (W) it reduces to Reading A; the funnel is detector design, not a law; and #427's weight gap is untouched. Geometry also flags that a virtual pair threading the throat leaves flux too, so #428 §5's criterion ("a virtual pair writes no entry anywhere", citing §4 item 2) needs a qualifier (Claude 030: under that criterion a pair that threads the throat is not virtual, while under David's 5:14 PM definition it is virtual and writes, so the qualifier is David's choice between the two, #445; the result keeps L's −1 under both only if L does not thread the throat, and any pair that threads it leaves the same ±q as a detection, #448); that is recorded as a flag for the stack, and #428 is not edited.

### Result in one paragraph
**Outcome (i): a smallest candidate exists, but only under (F).** The record and the detector's −1 don't have to share edges. Keep L as a closed virtual circulation that never carries the routes' flow. Add a detector-fixed exit: a vertex x joined to the arrival points a and b by edges Ya and Yb that avoid L's arcs, and an outgoing edge X from x that passes once through the fixed handle's throat. P's chain then writes a Gauss-flux record of ±q on X, the same in both route worlds. L keeps its virtual status and its expected −1. Under (W) the record still exists, but the detector then constrains nothing (Reading A). The mechanism doesn't touch #427's gap: nothing here gives exact-closure worlds weight.

### 1. Why #431/#436 lost L (lemma)
**Setting:** conserved unit charge, and at most one particle per edge (Geometry N1, a modelling assumption).

**Lemma.** If an edge carries the route chain, no virtual loop can use that edge.

**Proof.** A virtual loop is a pure circulation c on its arcs. On a shared arc the value would be 1 + c, with both 1 + c and c in {0, 1}, so c = 0. ∎ [Claude 030: the proof takes c in {0, 1}, but a circulation can run either way round L, so c is in {−1, 0, 1}. With c opposite to the route chain on the shared arc, 1 + c = 0 there. If N1 bounds the number of particles (P or P̄) on an edge, that case is still excluded, because the route's particle and L's member would make two. If N1 bounds the net flow, the sum is the route chain rerouted round L's other arc, with no separate pair: #436 §4a (W)'s "0 or 2", and the case of Claude 026's #431 R2 (Claude 030 check script, §2). Either way c = 0, so the lemma holds.]

So any design where the route chain leaves through L's arcs (#431 wiring, #436 zigzag) costs L its virtual status. The only escape is for the route chain to avoid L's arcs entirely. That is a geometric fact, not a choice. [Claude 030: it follows from N1 and charge conservation, and N1 is a modelling assumption (§1), so it is not a choice once N1 is granted.]

### 2. The candidate (smallest graph)
- **Vertices:** a and b (on L, where routes 1 and 2 arrive), x (the exit junction), X's far end, and the source.
- **Edges:** routes 1 and 2 (E0 = route 1 reversed, then route 2), A1, A2, Ya (a→x), Yb (b→x), X.
- **Cycle rank:** 5 vertices (source, a, b, x, X's far end) and 7 edges (route 1, route 2, A1, A2, Ya, Yb, X) give 7 − 5 + 1 = 3. A basis is C1, C2 and a new C3 = E0 · Yb · Ya⁻¹; L = C1 · C2⁻¹ in cycle space (taking A1, A2 as L's two arcs from a to b), so {C1, L, C3} is an equivalent basis. [Geometry's correction, #444 comment 6273048224. Claude 030: with A1 and A2 taken from a to b, L = C1 · C2⁻¹ holds up to inversion, which changes neither the basis nor any sign at ±𝟙. The graph has six cycles: C1, C2, L, C3 and the two route-free triangles that A1 and A2 each form with Ya and Yb (Claude 030 check script, §1).] X lies on no cycle.
- **Flow in the route-1 world:** route 1 → a → Ya → x → X, value 1. A1, A2 and Yb carry 0 route flow. L carries its own circulation, created and annihilated as a pair, so it stays virtual under the 5:14 PM definition. The route-2 world is symmetric through b and Yb. [Claude 030: with at most one unit per edge the route-1 world has five unit flows (Claude 030 check script, §2): this path plus a circulation of −1, 0 or +1 round L (0: no pair on L), and two in which P runs along an arc of L to b and leaves on Yb, where L is not a separate pair (the lemma). Where a virtual L is present its members occupy A1 and A2, so N1 keeps P on Ya. So the funnel admits worlds in which L is virtual and the record is written, which is the compatibility §6 claims; it does not force them.]
- **Detector-fixed:** x, X and the throat are part of the detector, fixed before phases are compared, as #436 condition 1 requires. a and b remain solved per world (#428 §6a(c)2).
- **Well-posedness item:** a and b are vertices shared by L's members and the route chain. The model must say that sharing a vertex is not an interaction (no annihilation at a or b). Here it is taken not to be. Otherwise the design falls back to #436. [Claude 030: this rule carries the result. With a circulation round L, the 1-chain at a has two units in (route 1 and one arc of L) and two out (Ya and the other arc), and it is the same chain as P running once round L before leaving on Ya (Claude 030 check script, §2). Only a rule that the worldlines pass straight through a and b, with no annihilation and no exchange, makes it a path plus a separate pair. The rule is an input chosen so that L stays virtual, like N1, so it belongs with the costs, with a T2 flag unless it is motivated independently. And with nothing happening at a or b, nothing draws the route to L: in 3+1 two worldlines meet only on a codimension-2 set, so the occupied route's meeting with L is itself a condition on the per-world solution.]

### 3. The record (#388 write, classical)
**Charge without charge (Wheeler).** When a charge q crosses a non-separating throat surface S of a fixed handle and doesn't return, the flux through S changes by q. By Gauss's law it stays at that value: a topological, append-only entry next to the fixed handle. That is #388's write, as in Claude 026 #431 R3.

The record is placed on X, so it is:
- **Route-independent:** X is common to both worlds and crosses S once, so the record is +q in both. There is no which-way information. [Claude 030: X's single crossing is not enough by itself. The two route worlds' chains differ by the cycle C3, so their records differ by q times C3's signed crossing number with S (Claude 030 check script, §3). Route independence needs that number to be zero: route 1 with Ya must cross S, net, as often as route 2 with Yb (in the design, none of them crosses it), for every attachment solution, since a and b are solved per world (#431 §4, Claude 026's bracket). L and any other pair must also cross S equally in both worlds (§5). This is a further design condition, stated here rather than derived.]
- **Presence-only:** it reads P's charge and presence, not the arrival time, Ya versus Yb, or P's frame or sign. This meets #436 condition 2 and Claude 026 S2. [Claude 030: Claude 026 S2 is batch 23's kill rule (#431 §5): since 10:48 AM a which-route record can lift the exclusion only by changing which loops the world has, and a write that leaves the loop through the detector in place does not lift it. The funnel's write leaves every loop in place, so it does not lift the exclusion. That fits a record with no which-way content; it is consistency with S2, not a condition met. The converse is a question for David, not a result: under (F) a which-route version (separate exits from a and from b, each writing at its own fixed handle) would also leave every loop in place, so by the same reasoning it would not lift the exclusion either, and the kill rule would need which-way devices that change the world's loops, for example by ending the route.]
- **Persistent:** it uses charge conservation (Maxwell, once matter is coupled), not species conservation. It lasts as long as P doesn't pass back through S, which meets the "more than never" bar. [Claude 030: the flux through S changes whenever net charge crosses S (#388: "every charge that crosses counts, not just the carrier"), so it lasts as long as no later net charge crosses S: not P, not any other charge, and not a pair that threads S (§5). It is a running count, so it is append-only in #388's sense (each write to a fresh body or handle) only if this throat takes one write, or if every later crossing has the same sign.]
- **Meets #436 condition 3:** the signed throat crossing is the same in both worlds.
- **Condition 4 (4-momentum):** Ya and Yb must write nothing that differs between them. That is a design condition on the detector, stated rather than derived. [Claude 030: P's 4-momentum also changes where P turns, at a or b (into Ya or Yb) and at x (into X), so the condition covers whatever balances it there. On condition 2: the frames that the two route worlds deliver at x differ by a conjugate of Hol(C3) (Claude 030 check script, §4), so under 1(a) their sign difference is sign(C3), the value §4 leaves open. The flux record does not read it; P on X carries it. And x and X are "the same" in both route worlds only under a cross-world identification (#418 (e)), as for c in #436 §4a (V-d). If x is a place rather than an event, P crosses S at different times in the two worlds unless their transit times agree, so "presence-only" means the flux value read after both crossings, not the time of the change.]

### 4. What each reading gives
- **(F) frame graph:** every edge is a transport path, so C1, C2, L and C3 all bind under the 10:48 AM ruling. L is still virtual, so its expected −1 survives (#431 §3b), and with it Hol(C1) = −Hol(C2) up to conjugation (#428 §6a(c)1). This is the first design in the stack that keeps the −1 and has a detector-fixed record. **New cost:** C3 needs an expected value under 11:17 AM, and nothing supplies one yet. It is world data, like the solved attachment points. [Claude 030: C3 is also a closure condition, not only a missing value. Given C1, C3 closes exactly if and only if the triangle through a, b and x along A1, Yb and Ya does, since at a Hol(C3) is that triangle's holonomy times Hol(C1); the triangle carries no route. With Hol(L) = −𝟙 the triangles along A1 and along A2 differ by exactly −𝟙, as C1 and C2 do, so one of them closes at −1 and the other at +1 (Claude 030 check script, §4). Under the lock no open set is exactly flat (#421, Geometry X2), so this is a second holonomy condition of the kind C1 already meets (codimension 4 in the flag form, 6 for the full holonomy, #428 §6a(c)2). It falls on the solved a and b, or on the curve of the empty funnel edge (Yb in the route-1 world), which, like the empty route's (#432 §3a (T-c)), is still to be said. If the curves of empty edges were free, every cycle through one could be closed by choosing it, and (F) would ask no more of the routes than (W) does. The routes' mismatch in C1 is still compensated as in #428 §6a(c)2, by a loop population whose existence is open (#427 §3b). Any exit that both route worlds reach without L's arcs adds such a cycle, so this cost comes with the merge at x, not with the smallest graph. Against #428 §6a's design (L at −1, C1 exact, C2 following; the lemma rules it out under N1), a detection world meets one more holonomy condition; #436 under (F) also had two, C1 and C2, without the −1. This bears on #427's gap (§6) without changing it.]
- **(W) carried loops only:** the carried subgraph is the open path route → a → Ya → x → X, plus L's own carried circulation. L binds itself, but no carried cycle contains a route, so the detector asks nothing of the routes. The record exists, but this is Reading A (#431 §3 (ii)). [Claude 030: as for #436 under (W) (Claude 028 R1), a detection world then has one carried route, not a pair (#428 §2 item 1). L binds only itself, so the exclusion behind any dark spot cannot sit in the detector, and batch 23's kill rule would have to be met by virtual pairs away from it.]
- **R-geon versus R-frame:** nothing in §1–§3 depends on the choice. The record is a flux datum, not a sign. If P is topological, L's pair vertices contain Geroch CTCs (#436 R3), and pair creation always passes #434's rule.

### 5. A flag the stack should check (Geometry, not a ruling)
A virtual pair whose loop threads the handle class (crosses S net once) leaves throat flux ±q after it annihilates. That is the same charge-without-charge mechanism. So #428 §5's "a virtual pair writes none" holds only if L does not thread S. If L does thread S (one route to its −1, #431 §3 (iii)), L writes flux in every world whether or not P arrives. That entry is not a detection record. The detector's ledger then has a baseline set by L, and P's record is the extra ±q on X. So the record criterion must read the change due to X, or L must be kept from threading S, with its −1 coming from enclosed curvature instead. [Claude 030: the quoted words are Claude 028's paraphrase (#436 §4a (V-b)). #428 §5 reads "a virtual pair writes no entry anywhere", citing §4 item 2 (Claude 024). Under that criterion a pair that threads S is not virtual, which is how #431 §3(iv) (Claude 026 R3) already treats a charged L in the handle's class; under David's 5:14 PM definition (#427 §3a) it is virtual and writes. So the qualifier is a choice of definition, which is David's (#445). In the first branch above, L keeps its expected −1 only under the 5:14 PM definition; under §4 item 2 it is not a virtual pair, as in #431. Only the second branch keeps the result under both. Through the handle's class, L's sign is the spin-structure value, world data (#431 §3 (iii)), not a fixed −1. The flag also covers any pair whose loop crosses S net n ≠ 0 times, not only L: in a world where P does not arrive it writes nq, so the flux alone does not tell a detection from such a pair (#448).]

### 6. What this doesn't do
- It doesn't give exact-closure worlds weight (#427's honest gap). No count or measure is proposed.
- It doesn't fix C3's expected value.
- The funnel (x, Ya, Yb, X through the throat) is detector design, which the detector must be shown to have. It is not a law.
- (F) versus (W) stays David's call. This design only shows that under (F) the record and the −1 are compatible.
