## For David (plain summary)

- **Can the far side of a nearby handle differ by world?** Not its shape: under the analytic lock, following space through the handle gives one answer, so the far mouth looks the same in every world. What **can** differ is **how the far mouth is glued back**: with a twist, a time shift or a boost. Near the observer, every world is identical; the gluing is the ledger state.
- **Reading it.** Send the beacon through the handle. It comes back after a delay that includes the gluing's time shift, and rotated by its twist. Different gluings give different replies, so the reply tells you which world you're in. That *is* the fork (your 3:19 PM and 11:42 PM point). At first, many worlds share a branch, and each reply narrows it down.
- **Link to time loops.** If the time shift is larger than the light-travel time between the mouths, the handle becomes a time machine (Morris–Thorne–Yurtsever). Misner space, the simplest model of the time loops around a geon, is exactly a "boost gluing", and its boost amount is a world-dependent ledger state that a round trip reads before the time loops appear.
- **Persistence.** The gluing is topology plus symmetry. Nothing writes it and nothing erases it, so it stays readable forever.
- **The catches.** The gluing amounts form a continuum (twist angle, time shift, boost), not a list. An exact one-universe handle with enough symmetry for these gluings is not known to us, except in flat or Misner-type models. And thin-shell handles are not analytic, so they sit outside the lock.

## Set 1: gluing freedom of a nearby handle (deck element of the handle)

**Plain summary.** The far mouth is glued back with a twist, a time shift or a boost. The near region is identical, the world differs, and a beacon through the handle sees the difference.

| Gluing variant $h$ | Exact? | Near region identical? | Readable by Type O reply? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rotation $R$ about the mouth axis | exact iff $\tilde M$ has that rotational isometry | yes (if the tube embeds) | **yes**: the returning image or polarization is rotated by $R$ | time-orientable, so Type O |
| Time shift $T$ (static $\tilde M$) | exact iff $\tilde M$ is static | yes | **yes**: the reply's delay is traversal + $T$ | $T>$ outside light time gives **CTCs** (MTY 1988; Frolov–Novikov 1990; V-text abstracts per #386), exploration only |

- **Exactness.** It needs an exact one-universe handle $\tilde M/\langle g\rangle$ whose cover has the isometries $h$. Flat models (#393) and Misner-type models have them. A static, axially symmetric, analytic, two-mouth, one-universe wormhole solution is **not known to us** (unchecked).
- **Status: live (conditional on an exact symmetric host)** (L70).

## Set 2: Misner space as the geon-host example (exact)

**Plain summary.** Misner space is flat space glued to itself with a boost. The boost amount is a world-dependent ledger state. Before the time-loop horizon, a light signal going round the closed direction comes back with a delay and Doppler shift set by that boost. This is exact, and it is the standard model of the geon's time-loop region.

- **Host** [A; Misner 1967 (unchecked); Hawking–Ellis §5.8 (V-meta per #377)]. The Taub region of 2D Misner space is $\{X^2>T^2,\ T>0\}/\langle B_\beta\rangle$, with $B_\beta$ a boost of rapidity β; a 4D version uses $\times\mathbb R^2$. The metric is flat, so the germ is the same for every β, provided the observer's tube injects: its boost-image distance exceeds $2\rho$ at $\tau=0$ [A].
- **Ledger state:** β, the deck element of the closed direction. Different β are non-isometric worlds: the length of the closed spacelike circle at given $T$ is $\beta T$ [A].
- **Read-out:** a null ray sent round the closed direction returns to a co-moving observer after a delay, and with a Doppler factor $e^{\pm\beta}$ [A, sketch], before the Cauchy horizon $T=0$ is reached from the past. Type O (time-orientable). No CTC is used in the read-out.
- **Beyond the horizon:** CTCs (exploration only). Survival is fixed per extension (#367, #375), and the ledger state β is the same in both extensions.
- **Status: live (exact, low-dimensional model)** (L71).

## Read-out, branching and persistence

- **Beacon read-out** (#372 §2.2). A round trip through the nearby handle returns with (delay, direction, rotation or Doppler, deck label). The read-out map is fixed in advance and gauge-invariant (#390, #392 F1–F3).
- **Branching.** At finite τ a branch is the record class of all worlds (gluings) consistent with the replies so far. It contains many worlds and refines as replies arrive. **The fork is the discovery** of which gluing one is in (David 3:19, 11:42). Classes nest (#372 §2.6).
- **Persistence.** The gluing is part of Γ, which is constant (no topology change for reasonable sources in a CTC-free, time-orientable host; per #380). As re-readability by new beacons, it is **grade G3** (#390, #394 F7). Stored replies in the diary are graded separately.
- **Status: live** (L74).

## Weak points (strongest first)

1. **A continuum of gluings (strongest).** Rotation angle, time shift and boost are continuous parameters, so the compatible worlds are uncountable. Discreteness needs the cover's isometry group to be discrete, or a further input (#394 F8).
2. **No known exact symmetric one-universe handle** beyond flat and Misner-type models. The gluings need isometries of the cover that generic analytic handles lack.
3. **Thin-shell handles are not analytic.** The simplest handle models fall outside the lock.
4. **Time shifts give CTCs** above the light-travel threshold, and generic nearby masses drive the shift (Frolov–Novikov). So the CTC-free regime is non-generic.
5. **Misner is 2D (×ℝ²)** and only a model of the geon's time-loop region, not a Hadley geon.
6. **The extension choice** is readable only by survival (nested prefix).
