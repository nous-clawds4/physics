## 1. For David (plain language)

**What we have.** An observer that can remember (your 3:53 ruling) keeps a diary of signals it receives. It sends a signal out; the signal goes into a particle's tiny time loop (a Hadley geon) and comes back (your 4:17 idea). We call this **backscattering** (your 4:37 "Yes, I'm happy with that."; the precise term is "round trip"). In full we write **geon backscattering (radar sense)**[^bs]; "geon" and "radar sense" are the Lead's qualifiers, to keep it apart from curvature backscatter. What comes back is written in the diary, and that is the measurement. We do not assume the usual particle-physics in/out description: the model is classical, and with time loops even a classical in-to-out map can fail to exist or to be unique.

Free fall stays perfectly predictable inside each possible world. The branching comes from *which world we are in*. Worlds that look identical to the observer now can send back different replies, so the diary forks. With memory in the state, that is "a fork in the observer's future", the target you named at 4:28. The observer's motion does not fork: under the analytic lock, two worlds that both carry it past the horizon give it the same germ (#367), and in Misner space it gets past the horizon in at most one of the two continuations. In short: **the fork is in the record (what the observer receives), not in its motion.** The fundamental group (the handle structure) is part of what distinguishes those worlds (L28/L29). Keeping the model classical is your 5:00 PM guiding constraint (§3).

**Some details that survived review.**
- Only signals that come back "the right way round" in time count as replies (Type O). The others are just correlations (Type F) (#364).
- Where the geon forms after a period with a Cauchy surface, replies can only arrive after the observer has passed that horizon (#364 (d), sketch). Beyond it, whether the observer continues is fixed by its own motion within each world (Misner: Geometry's own computation, #367; Taub–NUT unverified). Stopping is not a branch (#367, #370, #371).
- What the observer has recorded so far may still cover several possible worlds, and it narrows toward one as more replies arrive. For worlds with a closed repeating structure (cocompact Γ in a known cover) and exact displacement records, it narrows to exactly one in a finite time (#370, sketch; homogeneous covers need care). A single geon in an open universe is not of this kind.
- Worlds are compared through the observer's state and diary. That still works when the geon has always existed and there is no "starting moment" (#370).

**What is not done.**
- Probabilities are deliberately parked. Counting worlds is only a labelled conjecture.
- How the memory is physically stored is open.
- The time-loop puzzle has infinitely many consistent solutions, which needs sorting out.
- A plain light signal is probably too simple: it seems to need to interact with something in the loop.
- Some Taub–NUT details are unverified.
- The nearest earlier ideas in the literature are Hadley's 1997 4-geon causal link between apparatus and preparation (for the measurement) and Belnap's 1992 branching picture (for outcomes as classes of branches).

## 2. The model

### 2.1 State: germ + record
- The state is $\hat O(\tau)=(O(\tau),\rho(\tau))$: the oriented germ (modulo isotropy, #324) plus a record of received signals. Each entry has an arrival proper time, an emission stamp and isotropy-invariant direction data.
- The record only grows (L28, L29; batch 08; David's 3:53 memory working assumption).
- **Well-posedness (#355).** It needs a beacon or body and exact-real records. Records are continuous arrival curves per deck element. A definite beacon switch-on time is needed for the split at $\tau^*$. The isotropy-angle gauge is valid but lossy.

### 2.2 Measurement = beacon round trip through the geon's CTC region (plain name: backscattering)
- **Naming (David 4:37; verbatim, relayed by Physics Lead, Physics Lead chat, not signed).** Physics Lead proposed "backscattering" as the plain-language name and "round trip" as the precise defined term; David: "Yes, I'm happy with that." **Caveat:** no in/out S-matrix is assumed. The model is classical, and with CTCs even a classical in-to-out map can fail to exist or to be unique (§4.3, §4.5). Where the geon forms after a Cauchy slice, the return also lands beyond the observer's Cauchy horizon (§2.4); eternal geons have no Cauchy surface (§2.8).
- **S-matrix (Literature, relayed; FPS, KRW and Friedman et al. 1990 abstracts V-text, the rest V-meta).** Context only; the model uses none of this. For classical linear fields an in-to-out map exists on a restricted class of CTC spacetimes and is unique among solutions that die off in time (Friedman & Morris, PRL 66 (1991) 401 and CMP 186 (1997) 495, V-meta; abstracts read by external review); Friedman et al. 1990 argue that wormhole spacetimes are "benign" (data on past null infinity seem unconstrained; abstract V-text, #365). Free quantum fields have a unitary S-matrix on a class of asymptotically flat CTC spacetimes, and interacting ones do not (Friedman–Papastamatiou–Simon 1992, abstract read; Boulware 1992; Politzer 1994; Hawking 1995). The two-point function of any Hadamard state is singular at the horizon's base points, so the renormalised stress tensor is ill-defined or singular there (Kay–Radzikowski–Wald 1997, abstract read).
- **Definition (round trip; David 4:17; L36; batch 11; #364).** Here $\gamma$ is the observer's worldline by proper time, in a host $\tilde M/\Gamma$ with deck group Γ (batch 08). The beacon emits at $\gamma(t_e)$. A round trip is a signal path $c$ from $\gamma(t_e)$ to $\gamma(s)$ that passes through the geon region $G$, which contains CTCs (Hadley 1997, V-text per #362), and arrives as a reception: the loop formed by $c$ and $\gamma$ between $s$ and $t_e$ is Type O (§2.3). The record entry is the arrival time $s$, the emission stamp $t_e$, direction data and the loop's deck element $g\in\Gamma$ (the lift of $c$ runs from $g\tilde\gamma(t_e)$ to $\tilde\gamma(s)$; batch 08; §2.1). The term is well-posed only once four open points are fixed:
  - (a) the carrier: a null curve of a linear test field, or an interacting beacon (§4.5);
  - (b) $G$ itself, so far only "a geon region containing CTCs" (batch 11);
  - (c) whether the loop must be non-contractible (deck element $\neq1$); if trivial-class returns count (#373), the line against curvature backscatter (footnote) is drawn by mechanism, not by a topological invariant;
  - (d) whether $s<t_e$ is admitted: Type O does not order emission and arrival when $\gamma(s)$ lies on a CTC, and beyond the horizon in Misner space and in Taub–NUT every point does (Misner's region is totally vicious), so batch 11's chronological region $U\supset\gamma$ cannot contain the reception point there.
- The definition is new, though its ingredients are known. The closest prior art is Hadley 1997 (#365).

### 2.3 Type O / Type F returns
- The classification is well-posed and homotopy-invariant, set by $w_1$ of the closed loop (the time-orientation character: Type O if carrying a time orientation once round the loop brings it back unchanged, Type F if it comes back flipped). **Type O is the double-cover repair, not new** (#364 (a)).
- Non-trivial Type O homotopy classes exist (topologically) iff ker w1 ≠ {1}; there is no non-trivial Type O class if π1 = Z/2 and its generator reverses time orientation. Whether Hadley geons realise any of them by a causal curve through the CTC region is unchecked (#364 (b)).
- Type F returns are recorded as correlations, not measurements (batch 11).

### 2.4 Branching beyond the observer's Cauchy horizon
- If the host has a slice S before the geon, no reply can reach an observer still inside D⁺(S) (#364 (d), sketch; Hawking–Ellis §6.5 unverified). Measurements then happen beyond the observer's Cauchy horizon, where the extension is not unique. Under the analytic lock the observer's path does not depend on the extension while it runs (only whether it continues does, §2.5); what depends on it is the record (#367 (1); record: #367 (2), case (d)). Without such an S (eternal geons) this timing result does not apply (#370). Hadley's own geon is a persisting world tube (Hadley 2000, $S^2\times\mathbb R$, per #362; batch 13), i.e. the eternal case; the horizon story of §2.4–§2.5 concerns geons that form.
- Where the observer's state diverges there, that **is** the branching sought (Physics Lead's framing, batch 12; David 4:28: "a fork in the observer's future (a branch in observer space) is exactly what we are looking for"). Under the analytic lock the divergence is in the record, not the germ: two extensions that both continue the observer share its germ along its path (#367 (1); v0.10.1 §6.2), so its germ histories across worlds are nested prefixes. Germ divergence needs the smooth case, where the differing extensions form a continuum (batch 12 (1)).

### 2.5 Survival vs dropout

**Misner space.**
- Within each extension, survival is fixed by the geodesic's own data. It is determined, not a fork (Geometry's computation, #367; not a verified source). "Survives" means "crosses the horizon", not "complete": in each extension every timelike geodesic of the Taub region is incomplete, either at the horizon or, after crossing, at the CTC region's second horizon (#375, item 3). So for one observer the two Misner worlds give nested prefixes, not two continuing branches.
- Geodesics through the boost's fixed point are incomplete in both extensions and in **every** union (as a set of geodesics, i.e. of initial data in the covering chart: closed, nowhere dense and Lebesgue-null; no measure on observer space adopted; Lead ruling. As a set of points it is the whole Taub region: every point lies on one of them, the comoving observer through that point). The **two-extension** non-Hausdorff union is **not complete**: generic geodesics entering a CTC region hit its second horizon. Among unions of the four half-plane pieces, only the full quotient $(\mathbb R^2\setminus\{0\})/B$ (Hawking–Ellis's non-Hausdorff extension) is complete modulo the fixed-point geodesics; helicoidal coverings of the punctured plane give infinitely many further non-Hausdorff maximal extensions with the same exception (Rieger, arXiv:2402.09312, preprint; "maximal" only within covering-based extensions, Prop. 5.5, per #377; Hawking–Ellis and Hájíček 1971 stay V-meta, #377) (#367; #375, `reviews/misner-union-completeness-geometry-literature.md`).

**Taub–NUT.**
- Non-isometric maximal extensions exist (Chruściel–Isenberg 1993, V-meta, abstract only, per #332/#368). The abstract also notes that many familiar extensions are isometric.
- The dichotomy and the imprisoned geodesics are in Misner–Taub 1969 (relayed from external review (Claude job 013); text V-meta, not read by Literature (#377); the sign rule and the zero-momentum case confirmed by Literature's own computation, #377 item 6; JETP 28, 122, §II B–C, §III): the sign of the momentum along the closed generators decides which extension a geodesic crosses into, the others circle "infinitely many times" near the horizon at bounded affine parameter, and the comoving ones cross in neither.
- Taub–NUT has no boost fixed point (#375), but its zero-momentum geodesics play that role: the comoving ones "cannot be continued across [the horizons] in either of the two space-times" (Misner–Taub 1969, §II C; quotation relayed from external review, Claude job 013, V-meta for us; content confirmed by Literature's own computation, #377), so they stay incomplete in any union of the two extensions (#371 S2–S3: "incomplete in every union"). A non-Hausdorff maximal extension is reported complete (Hájíček 1971, via Clément–Gal'tsov–Guenouche 2015; unchecked); this conflicts with the comoving exception above and is unresolved. Literature #377 confirms the comoving exception by its own computation (geodesics with $p_\parallel=0$ continue into neither extension, for any $p_\perp$), so #375's Taub–NUT completeness reading (its "Taub–NUT vs Misner" paragraph as first filed, 2f294a6) is superseded; #375 has since been corrected to match (b5aa8a0).

**General.**
- Non-unique extension is the general Cauchy-horizon fact (#371).
- **Dropout**, i.e. ending or non-existence of an extension, is **not an arm** (v0.10.1 §6.2; #370).
- Survival is determined within each world and varies between worlds (L38, L42; Misner by Geometry's computation, #367; Taub–NUT and Hadley hosts unverified). Here survival means crossing the horizon once. A crossing observer can still end: in Misner always (above). In Taub–NUT no geodesic crosses the horizon twice (Misner–Taub 1969, §III; relayed from external review, Claude job 013; V-meta for us); whether a crossing observer then ends is unchecked.

### 2.6 Record branch vs state branch
- The state branch $[W]^S_\tau$ sits inside the record class $[W]^R_\tau$ (the outcome). The partitions nest and do not cross (#369, L41). Under the analytic lock the two coincide among worlds in which the observer reaches $\tau$ (same germ, #367 (1)): the fork is in the record, not the motion (§2.4; inference by external review, Claude job 013, R2, from #367 (1) and batch 13 (A)'s definitions; adopted by Creative Lead).
- At finite $\tau$ the outcome is an equivalence class of branches, refining as $\tau$ grows (#369; #355; #359). Both classes are taken among worlds in which the observer reaches $\tau$; worlds where it has already ended are dropout, not an arm (v0.10.1 §6.2), and need a stated convention, since in a forming-geon host every reply arrives where survival varies between worlds (§2.4, §2.5).
- Defining "branch" as the record class makes outcome = branch true by definition. That is a convention, not evidence (LT2). Both notions stay named (L41).
- Prior art: Belnap 1992 is closest (#371).

### 2.7 Certifying Γ (a single branch where the extension is not open)
- For any cocompact Γ in a known cover with exact displacement records, Γ is fixed, and certifiably so, once $\tau$ exceeds a generating set's displacements and about twice the quotient diameter, provided the record identifies each deck element from its arrival (#370, sketch; this supersedes #359's 'n ≤ 3').
- This fails for non-cocompact Γ and for angle-only records. Homogeneous covers need care (the stabiliser) (#370). A single geon in an open universe has non-cocompact Γ, so this result does not cover the geon replies of §2.2. It also needs the lock (a known cover), and it fixes Γ; it fixes the branch only where the extension is not also open, as in the crystal covers it was derived for (#359, #370).
- Under the analytic lock, the germ fixes the cover at $\tau=0$ except past non-unique extension points. What stays open is Γ and the extension (#370). In the smooth case (relaxed lock), geometry outside J⁻(γ(τ)) is never fixed by germ and record (#346, #370).

### 2.8 Comparing worlds
- $E_{\mathcal W}$ and $E_M$ are defined by germs (v0.10.1 §4), so worlds are compared by germ + record.
- This works for eternal geons, which have **no Cauchy surface** (#371). Only #346's agreement-region argument and #364 (d)'s timing need $D(S)$ (#370).

## 4. Open problems (as flagged in the reviews)

1. **Where the memory is stored.** Is it part of the state itself? (David 3:53; ledger open item.) The non-local state breaks the germ-only definition (#352).
2. **Probabilities are parked** (David 4:03). Counting appears only as "conjecture: count gives Born (to be derived, not assumed)" (ledger standing rule). (M1) is unchanged at L3. Counting results stand: degenerate 0/1 shares in crystal covers (L30, L31; #358, #359).
3. **EKT infinity.** EKT found infinitely many self-consistent solutions (#365).
   - Countable generically, by isolated fixed points in traversal classes (L40, sketch).
   - Continuous families arise for data invariant under a continuous symmetry, and possibly from continuous internal degrees of freedom such as spin or inelasticity (unverified) (#367). Generic isolation assumes transversality of the actual EKT map (unverified).
   - Lossev–Novikov "jinn" loops are a second source of multiplicity (#368).
4. **Exact-real records and a beacon/body** are needed for well-posedness (#355).
5. **An interacting beacon is needed.** Friedman et al. 1990 is verified from the abstract only (#364 (c)): linear fields behave benignly in the wormhole examples, and interacting systems can have multiple solutions. Linear uniqueness (Friedman & Morris) was unverified in #364; its status in #365 is as recorded there. Non-benign spacetimes may misbehave even for linear fields. L36 needs matter (#364 (c)).
6. **Taub–NUT:** the dichotomy is in Misner–Taub 1969 (relayed from external review (Claude job 013); text V-meta, not read by Literature; sign rule confirmed by Literature's own computation, #377 item 6); its reading for observers, and Hadley hosts, remain unverified (#370).
7. **Outcome vs branch at finite τ:** the outcome is coarser. Branch-without-outcome can occur only in the smooth case, or as dropout, which is not an arm (L39, L41). Under the lock, worlds that both continue the observer to τ give it the same germ (#367 (1)), so among them the state branch and the record class coincide.
8. **Split rule vs Paper 1:** the record split conflicts with Paper 1's $\sim$ ("Topology labels do not enter"). It is accepted under David's 3:53 working assumption 2, not resolved (batch 08 (b)).
9. Eternal geons: #346's first-divergence argument and #364 (d)'s timing do not apply (no D(S)); replies are not barred before any horizon. Ensemble comparison by germ + record is unaffected (#370).
10. **Hadley survival:** unanalysed for Hadley hosts (L42).
11. **Framing hazards:** keep clear of Everett-style branching (#332 N1; Sebens–Carroll flagged in #350). The non-Hausdorff union is a low-priority note only (#367): the Misner union forks no single geodesic; under the lock the branches share germs along the path (#367).
12. **Prior art:** Belnap 1992 (outcomes as classes of not-yet-split histories). Also Hájíček 1971, McCabe 2005, Earman 2008, Placek–Belnap / Müller (#368, #371).
