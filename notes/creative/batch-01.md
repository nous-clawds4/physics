## Set 1: "Sheets" (L3 + L2) — live, ranked first

**In a minute.** Spacetime stays perfectly smooth and predictable almost everywhere. The exception is a thin "seam", like where the sheets of a spiral parking ramp meet. Go around the seam and you can come back on a different level. Your path is fixed between seams. At a seam there are a few choices, and their number is a whole number (2, 3, …) fixed by the shape, not picked by us. That is the "mostly deterministic, discrete branches" picture. The catch: the seam is something we place in the universe, most paths never touch it, and we still need a rule for which level you land on.

**Tweak.** "analytic" → "analytic off a codim-2 branch set" (#331 Candidate A).
**Taste.** TT1: needs $\Sigma$'s causal type, field equations on $\Sigma$ (not a Geroch–Traschen distribution, #332 R3), and a sheet rule. TT2: $n$ is topological (good); the locus is a host choice (bad). TT3: cosmic-string-like, physically familiar. TT4: no measure, unless equal weight per sheet is added. Watch: amplitude hook is borrowed QM (#332 R4); Everett-style branching framing (#332 N1).
**Next check (L2).** Before adding $\Sigma$, ask whether existing $\pi_1$/holonomy structure in the record already gives the sheet count.

## Set 2: "Jet, not germ" (L5 reframed → L8) — parked

**Verdict.** Parked: collides with Paper 1's "smooth is not enough" lock (#335); the continuum switches on at every positive proper time, not at points (#334). Reopening is David's call.

**In a minute.** David liked the Gevrey idea: two universes identical in one place and different somewhere else. Literature (#332) showed this is already standard in relativity, with ordinary smooth functions, so Gevrey isn't the special ingredient. And it makes different *universes*, not different *futures for one traveller*. This set keeps the good part and moves it. Say an observer "is" only the infinitely detailed snapshot at a point, not the whole neighbourhood. Then many neighbourhoods share that snapshot, and the traveller's next step isn't settled. That is branching of motion. The catch: there are infinitely many, a continuum of options ("anything goes"), unless we find a natural reason to count only some.

**Tweak.** Observer state = infinite jet (not germ), in a non-quasianalytic host class (#331 §4 split; v0.10.1 §8.1).
**Taste.** TT1: must redo §8.1's (E1)/(E2) singleton argument; unchecked. TT2: the jet/germ split is forced by the class, not tuned. TT3: small; one equivalence relation changes. TT4: high risk; any discreteness rule could smuggle a count.
**Credit.** #331 C, corrected by #332 R1/R2.

## Set 3: "Loops in observer space, not spacetime" (L7 + L9) — parked

**Verdict.** Parked: under free fall a loop in $V$ has only one consistent history, so there is nothing to choose between (#334).

**In a minute.** In the known billiard-ball time-machine puzzles, the ball obeys ordinary laws, yet there are several consistent histories. The multiplicity comes from requiring the whole story to hang together. David wants that kind of branching without time machines. The idea is to put the loop in the space of observer states instead of in spacetime: a state is allowed to "come back to itself", and we require consistency around such returns. Local physics stays deterministic, and branching comes from a whole-history condition with a few solutions. This is the most speculative set: we have not yet written down what "consistent" means.

**Tweak.** "CTC" → "closed curve in $V$" (cf. self-loops $(O^u,O^u)$, v0.10.1 §11), carrying an EKT-style consistency condition.
**Taste.** TT1: undefined so far (fails until a condition is written). TT2: risk of becoming a disguised law of $R$. TT3: borrows a known GR pattern (EKT 1991). TT4: none, if solutions are only counted and not weighted.
