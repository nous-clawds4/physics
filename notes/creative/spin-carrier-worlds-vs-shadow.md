You then proposed (5:26 PM) that actor-on-a-stage models belong to shadow spacetime, while Worlds are all geometry: the FS geon lives in Worlds and is approximated in shadow space as a Dirac (MTW) box within a box. This note records that proposal and Geometry's readings. Geometry agrees that in a World the frame is geometry, not an actor, and lays out two readings for you to choose between, without picking one: spin comes from topology (particles are spinorial primes), or every path carries a spin frame and particles are extra, tensorial topology added to it. It also says how each fits #434's parity result, and flags one open question: what makes the clock tick. You settled the naming at 5:37 PM: the FS geon names the World object, and the Dirac box names its shadow-space stand-in. Nothing here is a ruling.

## 4. Two readings for David (Geometry readings; neither is picked)

**(R-geon) Spin from topology.** In Worlds spinoriality (the class of the 2π rotation in the slice's mapping class group; not the frame's ±1 lift, Claude 028, see the end of this paragraph) comes from topology, so particles must be spinorial primes (e.g. the Poincaré sphere); half-integer spin states are a quantization result (Friedman–Sorkin 1980). Handles and lens spaces are excluded: they are the only non-spinorial primes (D. Giulini, "On the configuration space topology in general relativity", Helv. Phys. Acta 68, 86–111 (1995), arXiv gr-qc/9301020, Theorem 2; D. Giulini, "Mapping-class groups of 3-manifolds in canonical quantum gravity", in Quantum Gravity, Birkhäuser, pp. 161–201, doi:10.1007/978-3-7643-7978-0_9, §3, Thm 3.1 (Hendriks) with Friedman–Witt, Topology 25, 35 (1986); also Giulini, Int. J. Theor. Phys. 33, 913 (1994), doi:10.1007/BF00672824, Table, column S). The Dirac box copies the geon's sign and adds none of its own. [Claude 028: that needs the World's spin structure (§3) to add no sign of its own, and it adds one on every loop: the frame's ±1 lift, whatever the loop's tube carries (#432 §3a (T-d)), which is the sign exact closure uses (#431 §3a–§3c). A spinorial core's 2π class is a second ℤ₂ beside it, as R-frame says for a spinorial P. That class is classical (for a spinorial slice the asymptotic symmetry group is the double cover, whose origin "is purely topological and has nothing to do with quantum theory": Giulini, Quantum Gravity (Birkhäuser), §3), but how it relates to the frame lift is #434 §7's open item. So R-geon has to say which ℤ₂ is the fermion's −1. [Geometry: the shadow-space Dirac box's −1 is the frame lift by construction (flat-space spin cover), so "the Dirac box copies the geon's sign" requires the far-field shadow map to send the core's 2π class to the frame lift; that is #434 §7's open item, and it is what R-geon needs if the core's class is chosen.] If the frame lift, the core supplies a label, not the sign. If the core's class, closure, a holonomy condition, does not see it (#432 §3a (T-a)), and the closure rule and the content of the 11:24 AM adoption would need restating, which is David's call.]

**(R-frame) Spin frame on every edge** (Geometry's confirmation of David's 4:44 PM question). Worldtube reading: an empty edge carries the frame only; a P edge carries the frame plus a tube whose cross-section is a ball containing P.
- Species = tensorial topology (lens spaces L(p,q), S¹×S²). Fermion vs boson = frame type.
- A spinorial P would add a second, independent ℤ₂ and could flip the composite to boson-like. ("Boson-like" is a quantization label. Classically the composite simply carries both ℤ₂ classes, and which combination counts is a choice; Claude 028.) So species are restricted to tensorial primes, or the reading must say which rotation counts.
- Every edge, empty ones included, carries a fermion-type frame, so bosons need a different frame type. Possible hook for David's arbitrary-spin hope (a hope, not a ruling): spin = which representation the frame carries (Geometry reading).

**Fit with #434's rule.** Gibbons–Hawking (CMP 148, 345 (1992), doi:10.1007/BF02100864) conserves the Kervaire semi-characteristic, not spinoriality (#434 §3(c)); for connected slices that is the parity of the number of primes with odd dim H₁(ℤ₂).
- L(p,q): dim H₁(ℤ₂) = 1 for p even, 0 for p odd. S¹×S²: 1. Poincaré sphere: H₁ = 0, so unconstrained.
- So both readings are only partly constrained (Geometry, 5:34 PM review, fix 1, merged with Literature D8; both correct the 5:31 PM brief's "no parity conservation at all"; this agrees with #434 §3(c)). R-geon: a spinorial prime counts toward the conserved mod-2 total only if its dim H₁(ℤ₂) is odd, e.g. the 3-torus (d = 3), the prism manifolds S³/D*₄₍₂ₙ₊₁₎ with n ≥ 1 (H₁ = ℤ₄, d = 1; n = 0 is the lens space L(4,1)) and S³/O* (H₁ = ℤ₂, d = 1) (Giulini 1994, Table, columns S and N); spinorial primes with even d are unconstrained, e.g. the Poincaré sphere (H₁ = 0) and S³/Q₈ (dim H₁(ℤ₂) = 2). R-frame: even-p lens spaces and S¹×S² count toward the conserved mod-2 total; odd-p lens spaces are unconstrained.
- In both readings species conservation remains a postulate.
- Neither reading forces (F) or (W) (#436 §4a). Under (W) the empty-edge frame sits idle.
- **Fit with #423** (Claude 028). A prime summand with d = dim H₁(ℤ₂) adds d to dim H¹(M; ℤ₂), so it multiplies the World's spin structures by 2^d, and a loop threading one of its ℤ₂ classes picks up the spin-structure value, which is world data; choosing it to fit would be LT2 (#423; #428 §6(c)). R-geon: the Poincaré sphere adds none, S³/Q₈ two, T³ three, the odd-d prisms and S³/O* one each. R-frame: even-p lens spaces and S¹×S² one each, odd-p lens spaces none. Neither reading fixes a spin structure, and neither may pick one.

## 5. Open item: clock rate (Geometry reading)

Parallel transport does not turn the frame on a flat edge (#428 R5/Y1). A tick needs one of: holonomy; a tangent-slaved (helicity-type) frame (#428 R3); or a proper-time rate. [Claude 028: a rate needs a frequency scale. The Compton rate (mc² over Planck's constant) brings in Planck's constant, and #419 R1 found that a clock hand read as the phase cannot both stay aligned and cancel. A classical scale brings in none, for example c³/(Gm) from the mass, c/ℓ from a geon's own size, or a spinning body's own angular velocity against Fermi–Walker transport. But nothing adopted fixes such a scale (choosing one, like λ_E, is David's call), and tuning it to the Compton value would bring Planck's constant back in.] φ = θ/2 fixes the relation between phase and angle, not what drives θ.
