Sorkin, "Spin ½ from Gravity", PRL 44, 1100 (1980), doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.44.1100; a topological geon in Sorkin's sense, handles S¹×S² treated separately; the spin-½ there is a property of quantum states).

No topology.

## 4. Two readings for David (Geometry readings; neither is picked)

**(R-geon) Spin from topology.** In Worlds spinoriality (the ±1 lift) comes from topology, so particles must be spinorial primes (e.g. the Poincaré sphere); half-integer spin states are a quantization result (Friedman–Sorkin 1980). Handles and lens spaces are excluded: they are the only non-spinorial primes (D. Giulini, "Mapping-class groups of 3-manifolds in canonical quantum gravity", in Quantum Gravity, Birkhäuser, pp. 161–201, doi:10.1007/978-3-7643-7978-0_9, §3, Thm 3.1 (Hendriks) with Friedman–Witt, Topology 25, 35 (1986); also Giulini, Int. J. Theor. Phys. 33, 913 (1994), doi:10.1007/BF00672824, Table, column S). The Dirac box copies the geon's sign and adds none of its own.

**(R-frame) Spin frame on every edge** (Geometry's confirmation of David's 4:44 PM question). Worldtube reading: an empty edge carries the frame only; a P edge carries the frame plus a tube whose cross-section is a ball containing P.
- Species = tensorial topology (lens spaces L(p,q), S¹×S²). Fermion vs boson = frame type.
- A spinorial P would add a second, independent ℤ₂ and could flip the composite to boson-like. So species are restricted to tensorial primes, or the reading must say which rotation counts.
- Every edge, empty ones included, carries a fermion-type frame, so bosons need a different frame type. Possible hook for David's arbitrary-spin hope (a hope, not a ruling): spin = which representation the frame carries (Geometry reading).

**Fit with #434's rule.** Gibbons–Hawking (CMP 148, 345 (1992), doi:10.1007/BF02100864) conserves the Kervaire semi-characteristic, not spinoriality (#434 §3(c)); for connected slices that is the parity of the number of primes with odd dim H₁(ℤ₂).
- L(p,q): dim H₁(ℤ₂) = 1 for p even, 0 for p odd. S¹×S²: 1. Poincaré sphere: H₁ = 0, so unconstrained.
- So both readings are only partly constrained (Geometry, 5:34 PM review, fix 1, merged with Literature D8; both correct the 5:31 PM brief's "no parity conservation at all"; this agrees with #434 §3(c)). R-geon: a spinorial prime counts toward the conserved mod-2 total only if its dim H₁(ℤ₂) is odd, e.g. the 3-torus (d = 3), the prism manifolds S³/D*₄₍₂ₙ₊₁₎ (H₁ = ℤ₄, d = 1) and S³/O* (H₁ = ℤ₂, d = 1) (Giulini 1994, Table, columns S and N); spinorial primes with even d are unconstrained, e.g. the Poincaré sphere (H₁ = 0) and S³/Q₈ (dim H₁(ℤ₂) = 2). R-frame: even-p lens spaces and S¹×S² count toward the conserved mod-2 total; odd-p lens spaces are unconstrained.
- In both readings species conservation remains a postulate.
- Neither reading forces (F) or (W) (#436 §4a). Under (W) the empty-edge frame sits idle.

## 5. Open item: clock rate (Geometry reading)

Parallel transport does not turn the frame on a flat edge (#428 R5/Y1). A tick needs one of: holonomy; a tangent-slaved (helicity-type) frame (#428 R3); or a proper-time rate, which needs Planck's constant and was rejected for alignment in #419 R1. φ = θ/2 fixes the relation between phase and angle, not what drives θ.
