## Plain summary (for David)

You proposed that a detection is itself a pairing. Two routes run from the source to detector i and are joined, but through the detector's matter rather than directly at a vertex. A virtual pair is joined directly, must match exactly (your exclusion rule), and leaves no record. A detection pair is joined through the detector, so its phases need not match, and it leaves a record there. The observer learns only that detector i fired, not which route, and branches so that every route compatible with the record occurs in some world. **This is not a probability rule. Nothing has been chosen to count, if anything is counted at all, and no Born claim is made.** Batch 34's leftover floor still applies, now to the routes that pair through the detector: at detector i at least as many of them remain as the length of the arrow sum (Claude 024, correcting Geometry's first reading). The main open item is to describe the detector joining in geometry, fixed by the detector's own physics and not tuned.

**Update (11:48 PM, §6):** you stated the next problem: the two routes in a world arrive nearly parallel, so the detector must supply the extra turn that closes the loop. Geometry agrees with the statement and says the needed thing is a relative −1, which for a spin-½ particle is a 2π frame rotation. The half-angle arrows then point π apart, so your "pi" and "2pi" describe the same −1 (Claude 025). The first candidate, the spin factor picked up at the turnaround, was tested and does not supply it: it marks every loop, not just detections, and in 3+1 it is a continuous angle set by the loop's shape, exactly −1 only when the solid angle swept by the tangent is exactly 2π (mod 4π): a thin, codimension-1 set of loop shapes, planar loops among them. No fixed −1 from the detector is identified yet. Claude 025 adds: the loop through the detector must close exactly (the premise of your 11:48 PM statement, not a ruling) [Superseded by David's 10:48 AM ET 2026-10-15 ruling, recorded in #431 §3a: exact closure on all loops within a world, detector loops included; approximate in shadow space.], so the detector must supply the exact compensating turn, not only a −1; and a frame carried like a gyroscope picks up no turnaround factor at all on slow routes, so that candidate fails more strongly than first stated.

**Update (12:10 AM, §6a):** you proposed that the detector holds its own virtual loops, and that the two routes wire into one loop at points just far enough apart to supply the missing turn; the change to that loop is what sets off the detector. Geometry accepts #427 (rare bears only on weight under μ, not on whether a world is allowed). Its reading: if the loop closes at −1 (spin-½, not adopted), the needed −1 is exact for any separation; the separation can be solved rather than tuned, but in 3+1 that needs a loop population with at least four free parameters (six if the full holonomy must match), not yet shown, which must change what curvature or topology the joining arc runs through, since in this model the turn comes from that and not from distance; and 'virtual becomes real' as the record does not work as stated. ## 2. The proposal (Geometry's paraphrase of David)

1. Every detection event is a pairing of two routes from source to detector, like a virtual pair.
2. A **virtual pair** is glued directly at a vertex. Its phases must match exactly, which is David's exclusion rule (#424 §4a and #427: 3:33/3:40 PM argument, ruling at 3:58 PM), and it leaves no record.
3. A **detection pair** is glued indirectly, through the detector's matter. The detector interaction affects the phases, so they need not match, exactly or approximately. The gluing leaves a record in the detector (ledger model, #388). The mechanism is not yet described.
4. The return route is not the visible particle. There is no which-way measurement: the observer knows detector i fired, not which route was taken.
5. On detection the observer branches in observer space. Right after the branch, the ensemble contains, for every route compatible with the record, at least one world in which that route occurs.
6. David corrected his earlier word "unpaired": the routes are paired, but through the detector.

- **Candidate tested; does not supply it: the turnaround spin factor.**

  - Carrying the frame Fermi–Walker is independently motivated away from the turnarounds, as the standard transport for a torque-free spinning body (MTW §6.5; text not read by Literature). 2. **The separation of a and b is solved, not tuned, conditionally.**

Along a nearly flat stretch of L, moving a or b changes Hol(γ) only by the curvature the move sweeps (#421 §3), moving L rigidly changes nothing, and turning it only conjugates the arc's holonomy, which leaves the arc's trace unchanged.
