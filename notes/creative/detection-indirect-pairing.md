## Plain summary (for David)

You proposed that a detection is itself a pairing. Two routes run from the source to detector i and are joined, but through the detector's matter rather than directly at a vertex. A virtual pair is joined directly, must match exactly (your exclusion rule), and leaves no record. A detection pair is joined through the detector, so its phases need not match, and it leaves a record there. The observer learns only that detector i fired, not which route, and branches so that every route compatible with the record occurs in some world. **This is not a probability rule. Nothing has been chosen to count, if anything is counted at all, and no Born claim is made.** Batch 34's leftover floor still applies, now to the routes that pair through the detector: at detector i at least as many of them remain as the length of the arrow sum (Claude 024, correcting Geometry's first reading). The main open item is to describe the detector joining in geometry, fixed by the detector's own physics and not tuned.

## 2. The proposal (Geometry's paraphrase of David)

1. Every detection event is a pairing of two routes from source to detector, like a virtual pair.
2. A **virtual pair** is glued directly at a vertex. Its phases must match exactly, which is David's exclusion rule (#424 §4a and #427: 3:33/3:40 PM argument, ruling at 3:58 PM; the 3:58 PM time is from Creative Lead's record, where David confirmed the ruling in Creative Lead chat, not relayed by Geometry), and it leaves no record.
3. A **detection pair** is glued indirectly, through the detector's matter. The detector interaction affects the phases, so they need not match, exactly or approximately. The gluing leaves a record in the detector (ledger model, #388). The mechanism is not yet described.
4. The return route is not the visible particle. There is no which-way measurement: the observer knows detector i fired, not which route was taken.
5. On detection the observer branches in observer space. Right after the branch, the ensemble contains, for every route compatible with the record, at least one world in which that route occurs.
6. David corrected his earlier word "unpaired": the routes are paired, but through the detector.

## 3. David's framing (Geometry's paraphrase, relayed via Geometry, not signed)

## 4. Geometry's notes (reviewer analysis, not rulings)

1. **Floor.** The #424 leftover floor (leftover ≥ resultant) still applies, to the routes left for detection pairs (Claude 024, correcting Geometry's first reading). Exact virtual pairs add nothing to the resultant, whatever happens to the other routes, so in a world w at least R_w of the routes to d_i are left to pair through the detector. In a world with no exact closure all routes to d_i pair through it, so the number of detection pairs there is half the number of routes to d_i, whatever R_w is (that path set is still undefined, #424 §6). What changes is only that the leftovers pair through the detector instead of staying unpaired, and none stays unpaired only if their number at each detector is even. *Superseded, kept as history (Geometry's first reading): "The #424 leftover floor (leftover ≥ resultant) does not apply, because no routes are left unpaired."*
2. **The open item.** Define the detector gluing in geometry. It should be local to the detector's matter, leave a lasting record, and be fixed by the detector's own physics, not tuned (T2). Here "record" should mean a #388 ledger write: an append-only entry in matter or flux next to fixed handles, naming the detector and not the route (§2 item 4). A virtual pair is then one that writes no entry anywhere (Claude 024).
3. **Lineage (analogy and prior art, not adoption).** It resembles Cramer's transactional interpretation (offer and confirmation waves; Wheeler–Feynman absorber lineage), done in geometry. TI's offer–confirmation echo (ψψ*), which TI offers as the source of the Born rule, is not imported. No Born or |a|² step is taken here.
4. **Time direction.** If both routes run forward from source to detector (§2 item 1), neither runs backward: only the closed loop they form runs backward along one of them, as #421's closure loop γ does, so the loop is not a causal curve. If instead the return route is a physical route from detector to source, it runs backward in time, and in a time-oriented host it must be something no detector records, or be read as an antiparticle (Feynman–Stueckelberg). Which reading is meant is open (Claude 024).

## 5. Open items

- The detector-gluing mechanism (§4 item 2). Until it is given, "phases need not match", "leaves a record" and "no which-way" are part of the proposal, not consequences. Under the lock a passive carried frame meets no self-consistency condition (#421 (N1)), and the detector's matter enters a loop's holonomy only as part of the geometry, like any other curvature. So "need not match" is the scope of the exclusion rule (it covers direct gluing only), not something the detector does to the phases. For the detector to act on the phases, the frame must couple to its matter: the same open question as #421 open item 1 at v_a (Claude 024).
- Consistency with exact closure within a world: the detector-glued pair is a different object from a virtual pair under David's 5:14 PM definition (#427 §3a; a virtual pair appears and disappears together), so the exclusion rule need not apply to it; Geometry's reading. Read loosely, the 5:14 PM words fit a detection pair too: both its routes leave the source together and end together, in the detector. What separates the two in §2 is the gluing, directly at a vertex or through the detector's matter, and that criterion has to be fixed by the geometry before any phases are compared. If a pair counted as a detection because its phases do not match, no world would ever be excluded, and the exclusion rule would be empty (LT2). The criterion must also place routes that end in matter that records nothing, such as slit walls or the barrier (Claude 024). Whether the two fit together in one world is to be checked.
- Whether μ is affected. μ is still open, and (M1) is untouched at L3.
- Time direction of the return route (§4 item 4).
- What a which-route record does to pairing (batch 23's kill rule; #421 §4, "Which-path"): for example, whether routes recorded at one slit are thereby removed from cross-slit virtual pairs. Any dark spot must be lifted by such a record (Claude 024).
