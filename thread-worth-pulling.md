The spirit of this program is based on the idea that physics lacks a precise explanation of what the observer is. Progress requires that we supply that explanation.

Specifically:
- a precise definition of the observer as a physical object
- a precise mathematical definition of the space of all possible observers
- equations of motion of the observer through observer space; equations that restrict what motion is allowed without being fully deterministic; movement through observer space is a local process
- a precise specification of how the internal state of the observer contains a complete representation of the external universe
- a model of measurement as branches in the allowed movements through observer space
- a probability interpretation that is entirely classical and requires no additional assumptions

That's it. That's the thread worth pulling. The question should not be: why is this a thread worth pulling. The question should be: how in the name of all that is holy should this NOT be considered a thread worth pulling? Certainly, there is an aesthetic appeal to its ontology. So why has this program not been attempted? (If it has been -- what is its name?) The best explanation for the failure to pull on this particular thread is that it feels like a local hidden variable theory of the sort that is excluded by Bell's Inequality. Our first task is to present the case that this ontology is NOT excluded by Bell's Inequality. Make no mistake: we will not argue that quantum mechanics is wrong, or that Bell's Theorem is wrong. On the contrary: we take QM and Bell seriously.

Our argument is simply that the proposed model is not excluded by Bell. It is a LHV theory, but it is local in observer-space, not in classical spacetime. It is almost trivially true, once the distinction between observer space and spacetime are made mathematically precise.

Much confusion will be avoided by constant reminder that observer space and spacetime are not the same. Most mistakes will be the result of applying results in one of those spaces and applying those results without justification to the other space.
